The challenge

Where They Started: The Contract Manufacturing Trap

Sophia’s Garden Artisan makes cannabis chocolate under a Health Canada licence. For N years they made it for other people.

Producing to spec for dispensary brands means somebody else’s name goes on the box. Somebody else sets the retail price. Somebody else owns the relationship with the store and the customer, and somebody else keeps the margin that comes with owning it. The chocolate was theirs. Everything else belonged to a client.

That is a structurally weak position and it gets weaker over time. Contract manufacturing is a price-taker’s business: you compete on cost against whoever quotes lower next quarter, you carry the production and inventory risk, and you have no pricing power because what is being bought is capacity, not brand. Revenue concentrates into a small number of accounts. Losing one is not a bad month — it is an existential event.

What actually triggered the decision to go own-brand? A lost account, a price squeeze, a contract not renewed, or a deliberate long-term plan? This is the single most important paragraph on the page and it needs the real story from Cruz.

No pricing power

The buyer is purchasing capacity. Capacity is comparable, so the only lever either side has is price.

No brand equity

Every batch shipped built somebody else’s name. Ten years of production leaves you exactly where you started.

Concentration risk

A handful of accounts carry the whole business. One non-renewal takes a meaningful share of revenue with it.

The constraint

And The Obvious Escape Route Was Closed

The normal way out of contract manufacturing is to build a consumer brand. In licensed cannabis, that route is largely blocked by law.

Under the Cannabis Act, a licence holder cannot promote to consumers the way an ordinary confectionery company can. There is no lifestyle imagery, no testimonials about how a product feels, nothing that could appeal to young people, and no depiction of consumption. On top of the statute, the major advertising platforms prohibit the category outright in their own policies — so even compliant consumer advertising has almost nowhere to run.

For most brands in most categories, marketing spend is the lever you pull to build recognition. Here that lever does not exist. Any plan that assumed consumer advertising was going to fail before it started.

What is off the table

Lifestyle imagery. Effect and benefit claims. Consumer testimonials. Anything with youth appeal. Depictions of consumption. Paid social and most paid search aimed at consumers.

What remains

Communication with the licensed trade, informational and educational content about the company and its capability, the packaging itself, and being findable by a buyer who is already looking for a supplier.

What we ran

The Play, Step By Step

The restriction decided the strategy. If you cannot market the product experience to consumers, you market the company and its know-how to the people who decide what goes on the shelf.

Point the entire go-to-market at the retail buyer

Step 1

Consumer promotion was unavailable, so the target changed. The people who determine whether Sophia’s Garden reaches a customer at all are licensed retailers deciding what to stock. Every decision after this one followed from it: the brand had to look like something a store wants on its shelf, and the company had to look like a supplier worth carrying.

Build the brand the product needed

Step 2

Identity, colour system, naming, domain and the packaging system. In own-brand confectionery the package is not decoration — it is the only promotional surface that reaches a consumer legally, reliably and every single time. It has to do the work an advertising campaign would normally do, from a few square inches, inside a regulated format.

Make them credible before the first sales conversation

Step 3

A full website designed and built from scratch, then optimised every month rather than left to age. A retail buyer researching a potential supplier finds a real company with a real operation behind it, not a manufacturer with a phone number and a page that has not been touched since launch.

Be findable by the buyer already looking

Step 4

A complete monthly SEO programme and Google Search campaigns, both run education-first. The searches that matter here are not consumers looking for chocolate — they are trade buyers and partners looking for a licensed producer who can actually deliver.

Sustain a content programme that is compliant by construction

Step 5

Social accounts built and managed on a rolling content plan. The governing rule: market the company and its know-how, never the product experience. Applied at the point of writing rather than as a compliance review afterwards, because a review step does not survive contact with a monthly publishing cadence.

The discipline

What “Compliant By Design” Actually Means

In a regulated category, compliance is either built into how work is produced or it is a bottleneck that quietly stops work shipping.

The common approach is to create marketing normally and then have somebody check it. That fails at volume. Reviews become a queue, the queue becomes a backlog, and the backlog becomes a reason not to publish. Worse, it puts the compliance judgement at the end of the process, where the only options are approve or throw away finished work.

The alternative is a content rule applied before anything is made. Here that rule is a single sentence: we market the company and its know-how, never the product experience. Everything follows from it. Process, craft, sourcing, standards, the people — all publishable. How it tastes, how it feels, who should use it and why — not.

Publishable

The company, the process, the craft, the standards, the licence, the people, the capability, the category itself.

Never published

Product experience, effects, benefits, consumption, lifestyle framing, anything that could read as appealing to young people.

Confirm whether Sophia’s Garden runs its own additional legal review, and whether any content has needed to be pulled or amended. If nothing has ever been rejected, that is itself worth saying.

Why it matters

The Chocolate Did Not Change. The Position Did.

Most marketing engagements optimise a business that already works. This one changed what the business was — from selling manufacturing capacity to selling a product with its own name on it. That is a different kind of project, and the marketing was downstream of a commercial decision rather than a substitute for one.

The part worth paying attention to is the constraint. This happened in one of the most restrictive advertising environments in the country, where the usual lever — spend money, buy attention — was not available. What was left was being the obvious choice for the person doing the buying: a credible company, a product that looks like it belongs on the shelf, and enough visibility that a trade buyer researching suppliers actually finds you.

That is a go-to-market problem rather than an advertising one, and the answer transfers to any business selling through a channel it does not own.
