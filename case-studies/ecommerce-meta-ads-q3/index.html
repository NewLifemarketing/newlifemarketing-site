The Quarter At A Glance

The setup

A Small Campaign, Built To Find Buyers

I ran this campaign myself, and I want to be clear about one thing up front: nothing special happened this quarter. No big launch, no sale weekend, no change in strategy. Just one prospecting campaign with a deliberately small footprint.

One campaign, three ad sets

I split a single prospecting campaign into three ad sets, one per Canadian market, and gave each its own daily budget so every market got a fair, equal test.

Lookalike audiences

Each ad set targeted a lookalike audience in its market, people who look like the brand’s existing buyers, rather than cold interest targeting.

One video per ad set

Three videos in total, one per ad set. We filmed them in person and edited them ourselves. No stock footage, no recycled creative from another brand.

Why $2.49 a purchase is cheap here

The average purchase was worth about $201 ($401,260.68 across 1,994 purchases). When someone comes to this brand, they are not buying a $10 product, so I am more than happy to spend $2.50 to win that customer. Every dollar we put into the ads came back as roughly $81 in reported sales.

The insight

Same Budget, Very Different Results

I gave all three markets the same treatment. One of them pulled far ahead, and it was not the one I expected.

The third market got the most impressions of the three, so you might expect it to win. It didn’t. On almost exactly the same spend as the strongest market, it produced 237 purchases against 1,386. More reach did not mean more buyers.

That is exactly why I test markets side by side: the data tells me where the next dollar should go. Next quarter I’m moving more of the budget into the strongest market. It is clearly ready to scale.

The proof
