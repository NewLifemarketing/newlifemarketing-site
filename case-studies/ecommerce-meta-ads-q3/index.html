80.96x ROAS On Meta In One Quarter

Elite Cards Toronto, a sports card shop selling online, reaching buyers in three Canadian markets through one Meta (Facebook & Instagram) prospecting campaign in Q3 2026.

Services Used:

  • Meta Ads
  • Creative Production
← All case studies

Published October 22, 2026 · By Cruz Lee, Founder & CEO, who ran this campaign

Meta Ads/Creative Production/E-Commerce

The Issue

One prospecting campaign on Meta (Facebook & Instagram) with a deliberately small footprint: three ad sets, one per Canadian market, at $15–20 a day each.

Nothing special happened this quarter. No big launch, no sale weekend, no change in strategy.

The Goals

Find new buyers

Reach people who look like the brand’s existing buyers, not cold interest targeting.

Give every market a fair test

One ad set and one daily budget per Canadian market.

Learn where to put the next dollar

Test markets side by side and let the data decide where budget goes.

Snapshot

The Quarter At A Glance

Client: Elite Cards TorontoPeriod: Q3 2026, July 1 – September 30Platform: Meta (Facebook & Instagram)Campaign: 1 prospecting campaign, 3 ad setsBudget: $15–20 a day per ad set, set at the ad set levelAudiences: Lookalike audiences in three Canadian marketsCreative: 3 videos, filmed in person and edited by our teamAttribution: Meta default, 7-day click, 1-day view or engagement

The Approach

Test the markets side by side on equal budgets, and let the data say where the next dollar should go.

  1. One campaign, three ad sets. A single prospecting campaign split into three ad sets, one per Canadian market.
  2. Equal budgets. $15–20 a day per ad set, set at the ad set level, so every market got a fair, equal test.
  3. Lookalike audiences. Each ad set targeted a lookalike audience in its market: people who look like the brand’s existing buyers, rather than cold interest targeting.
  4. One video per ad set. Three videos in total, filmed in person and edited by our team. No stock footage, no recycled creative from another brand.
  5. Left to run. No big launch, no sale weekend, no change in strategy from July 1 to September 30.

The insight

Same Budget, Very Different Results

I gave all three markets the same treatment. One of them pulled far ahead, and it was not the one I expected.

The third market got the most impressions of the three, so you might expect it to win. It didn’t. On almost exactly the same spend as the strongest market, it produced 237 purchases against 1,386. More reach did not mean more buyers.

That is exactly why I test markets side by side: the data tells me where the next dollar should go. Next quarter I’m moving more of the budget into the strongest market. It is clearly ready to scale.

The Results

The average purchase was worth about $201 ($401,260.68 across 1,994 purchases). When someone comes to this brand, they are not buying a $10 product, so I am more than happy to spend $2.50 to win that customer. Every dollar we put into the ads came back as roughly $81 in reported sales.
