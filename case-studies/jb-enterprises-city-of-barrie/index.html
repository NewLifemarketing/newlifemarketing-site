Snapshot

The Project At A Glance

Client: JB Enterprises Ltd.Industry: Municipal infrastructure · Sewer and watermainEnd client: The City of BarrieService: Creative production — on-site video and photographyLocation: Downtown Barrie, OntarioShot: July 2026, across two full daysCrew: Cruz and Keegan, on site for the durationDelivered: 7 finished videos, wide and vertical cuts, plus stills

The brief

Document A Build That Only Happens Once

JB Enterprises Ltd. was constructing a new storm drainage system in downtown Barrie for the City. They brought us in to document it properly — not a few phone clips from the foreman, but a real record of the work, shot to a standard they could put on their own website.

It is worth being clear about who is who here, because it shapes everything about how the shoot ran. JB is our client. The City of Barrie is theirs. We were on that site as JB’s production crew, documenting work being delivered for the municipality — which meant operating inside the City’s safety requirements while staying out of the way of a contractor who had a schedule to hit.

Infrastructure contractors do not usually hire a film crew. The work is underground within days, the site is restored, and the only record is a set of as-built drawings and a few photos on someone’s phone. A storm drainage system gets buried and is never seen again. If it is not filmed while it is open, it is gone.

Who they are

JB Enterprises Ltd.

A Central Ontario municipal infrastructure contractor, incorporated in Ontario in 1996 — thirty years of sewer, watermain and road building.

What they build

Municipal infrastructure construction, infrastructure restructuring and replacement, I.C.I. site planning, and commercial and residential land development. Their stated specialties are municipal infrastructure, earthworks and land development, and stormwater management — the last of which is exactly the job we were there to film.

The scale of the company

A unionized team of 40 employees fielding four full-time crews, with more than 70 projects completed. The company is led by president John Bravakis, who brings a civil engineering background and over twenty years in the industry.

Where they work

Central Ontario — Newmarket to Muskoka, and Owen Sound to Peterborough. That takes in Barrie, Alliston, Bradford, Innisfil, Midhurst, Orillia, Midland and Collingwood, working directly with municipal engineers, private developers and commercial project builders.

Why that matters on camera

A company that works with municipal engineers is judged on precision and process, not on marketing polish. The footage had to reflect that. Shaky handheld clips of a hole in the road would have undersold a thirty-year contractor delivering infrastructure for a city.

The challenge

Filming A Live Job Site You Do Not Control

This was the hard part, and it is the whole reason the shoot took two full days rather than an afternoon.

There were roughly forty people working on that site at once. It was a big enough job that the architects flew in from Ottawa to verify the work was being done to spec. Excavators, cranes and heavy equipment were moving continuously, and the crew had a schedule that did not pause because a camera was present.

On top of that, the City of Barrie’s safety regulations governed the site. We had a limited set of zones and areas we were cleared to move through — and everything we needed to capture was happening outside of them, or moving between them.

Three constraints at the same time

Never be in the way

Forty trades working to a schedule. A production crew that slows a municipal build down does not get invited back, and the client pays for the delay.

Never be unsafe

Heavy equipment with real swing radii and blind spots, under City safety requirements. Staying clear was not a preference, it was a condition of being there at all.

Still get the shot

The build only happens once. There is no second take on a drainage structure being lowered into place, and no reshoot after the trench is backfilled.

Most production problems are solved by asking people to move, waiting for better light, or going again. None of those were available here. The solution was anticipating the sequence of the build, positioning ahead of it, and shooting from the zones we had rather than the ones we wanted.

Two days of that, and we got the job start to finish.

How we shot it

Working Ahead Of The Build

Read the sequence first

Excavation, structure, concrete, backfill — a drainage build runs in a fixed order. Knowing what came next meant we could be set up before it happened instead of chasing it.

Shoot from the cleared zones

We worked the angles available inside the areas we were cleared for, using longer focal lengths to reach into the trench rather than walking into it.

Put the drone where people cannot go

Aerial work covered what ground positions could not — the full shape of the excavation, the cranes working, and the scale of a forty-person site in a downtown street.

Cover it twice, two ways

Everything was captured for both a wide edit and a vertical cut, so JB got footage that works on their website and on a phone without a second shoot.

What we delivered

Seven Videos, Two Formats, One Buried Build

The full job, start to finish: the excavation opened, the drainage structure built and set, the concrete installed, and the street put back.

Wide cuts for the website

Landscape edits built for JB’s own site and for showing the work to municipal clients — the format that reads as a finished project record rather than social filler.

Vertical cuts for phones

The same material recut for a vertical frame, so the footage works where most people actually watch it, without going back to a job site that no longer exists.

Aerial coverage

Drone work establishing the scale of the build in a downtown street — the shot that makes the size of the job legible to someone who was never there.

Stills alongside the video

Photography captured through both days, usable for proposals, the website and project documentation independently of the edits.

All of it filmed in-house by our own crew. No stock, no subcontracted shooter, no footage of someone else’s job site standing in for theirs.

The trench is backfilled. The footage is not.

A storm drainage system is visible for about a week in its entire service life. JB now has a permanent record of how theirs was built — and a reason to show it to the next municipality that asks what they can do.
