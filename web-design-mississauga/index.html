Buying A Cheap Website Properly

This section is genuinely not a sales pitch. If you are a Mississauga business in the first group, here is how to avoid the ways a cheap build goes wrong.

Non-negotiable

Own the accounts

Domain, hosting and analytics in your name, with the passwords. This is free to insist on at the start and expensive to resolve later. It is the single most common unpleasant surprise in a cheap build and it has nothing to do with price.

Replaceability

Use a platform others know

A wordpress website design mississauga developers can hand to anybody else is worth more than a slightly better bespoke one only its author understands. Whether you hire a mississauga web design company or a single mississauga web designer, you are buying something somebody else may need to pick up.

Maintenance

Insist you can edit it

Prices, hours and the team page are where every update request comes from. If changing those means emailing somebody, the site will go stale and the saving evaporates within two years.

Quality floor

Get the basics measured

It must work properly on a phone and load quickly. Cheap does not have to mean slow, and a web design company mississauga businesses rehire at any price will have checked both before handover.

Get those four right and a modest budget produces something that will serve a brochure business for years. Mississauga web design at the affordable end is a genuinely functioning market with capable people in it, including more than one competent mississauga web design agency, and the failures are almost never about the design.

Questions

Web Design In Mississauga: Common Questions

Is a cheap website a bad idea?

Not at all, for a lot of Mississauga businesses. If customers reach you by referral, if the site mainly confirms you are real, and if the purchase gets discussed in person, then a competent WordPress build from a capable local developer is the correct purchase and the money saved is better spent elsewhere. The mistake is buying cheaply when the website is where customers actually decide, which is a different situation and one most businesses have never been asked to distinguish.

How do we tell which kind of business we are?

One question does most of the work: does the decision happen before anybody speaks to you. If a customer phones and is then persuaded, the site did its job by existing. If they decide from the site and phone to arrange it, the site is your salesperson. The second question is what one customer is worth, because a business where that figure is a few hundred dollars and one where it is fifty thousand should not be buying the same website.

Should we use WordPress?

For most Mississauga businesses, yes, and for a reason that has nothing at all to do with the platform being best. It is one that any other developer can pick up, which matters far more than a marginally better bespoke alternative only its author understands. You are buying something you may need somebody else to maintain, possibly at short notice, and replaceability is worth more than elegance for almost every business at this end of the market.

What makes a website expensive if the design is similar?

Writing and photography, almost always. A site that genuinely sells needs real proof, real prices and a page per service, and producing those takes time that has nothing to do with the build. That is also why a business arriving with photographs and its own copy gets a dramatically cheaper quote. If a proposal does not change when you offer to supply those, it is not itemised properly and you should ask what it is priced on.

Our cheap site is slow. Is that because it was cheap?

Usually not. The causes are a heavy theme loading features you never use, images uploaded straight off a camera, plugins added over years and never removed, and nobody ever agreeing a speed target so it was never measured. All four are avoidable at any budget and all four are fixable without a rebuild. Compressing the images alone resolves it on a surprising proportion of the sites we are asked to look at.
