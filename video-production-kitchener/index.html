Video Production Kitchener

The Film Nobody Commissions

Almost every video production kitchener brief is about selling to customers, and in a region where Communitech and Google's engineering office compete for the same people as everybody else, the film that would earn the most is the one about working there.

The under-used case

Video Production Kitchener: Recruitment Is The Unspent Budget

Hiring is a marketing problem in this region and it is almost never given a marketing budget.

A job advertisement is a list of requirements. It tells a candidate nothing about what the work is actually like, who they would sit with, or what the building smells like at four in the afternoon, and in a region where strong candidates have several offers that is the information that decides.

Ninety seconds of the real place — people working, somebody explaining what a normal week looks like, the thing being made — does more for a vacancy than any amount of copy, and it is reusable for every vacancy afterwards.

It is also the cheapest film on this list to make, because the subject is already there and nobody has to be persuaded to be filmed doing their own job.

The reason it goes unmade is organisational rather than financial: recruitment sits with one department and film sits with another, and nobody owns the overlap.

The customer-facing case

Two Audiences, Two Different Films

This city holds a technology sector and a much older manufacturing and service economy, and they do not watch the same thing.

For the manufacturing and service half, the valuable film is evidence: the line, the install, the finished job, the quality step. A customer cannot visit and the film substitutes for the visit. It needs coverage rather than presenters and it tolerates the place looking like a working place.

For the technology half, the valuable film is the problem rather than the product. A screen recording shows an interface to somebody who already understands why they would want it; filming the mess the software replaces — the spreadsheet at eight in the evening, the clipboard, the phone call to find out where something is — is what makes a stranger care.

Those are genuinely different productions with different costs and different permissions, and a single brief trying to serve both produces an office montage that serves neither.

So the first conversation is about which audience, and the answer is usually clearer to the business than it expects once it is asked directly.

What to make first

Three Films That Pay For Themselves Here

Each does a job written material does badly, which is the only test worth applying.

The real week, for hiring

People working, somebody explaining what a normal week looks like, the place as it actually is. Cheapest to make, reusable for every vacancy, and almost nobody in this region has one.

The work being done, for customers

The install, the line, the finished job. It answers the question that otherwise needs a site visit and it can be sent to the people in the buying group who will never travel.

The person who would answer a technical question

Not the owner reciting capability. The engineer, the lead, the operator. In a technically literate region this is far more persuasive than polish and is easy to shoot.

The question everybody asks

A Video Production Kitchener Piece About What It Costs

The film nobody makes, answering the question every customer has.

Two minutes explaining how a job is priced — what moves it up, what moves it down, what a typical one runs to — does more work than any other film a business can make, because it is the conversation every enquiry begins with.

It does not require publishing a price list. It requires explaining the logic, which is reassuring precisely because most competitors will not.

It also filters. People for whom the answer is impossible stop before ringing, and the enquiries that arrive are from people who already accept the range.

Put it on the page where the question arises and send it in reply to the people who ask by email. Within a year it will have saved more time than it took to make, several times over.

Where it gets watched

Design For The Forward, Not The Homepage

In a long purchase, one person speaks to you and several others decide.

Those others never visit your website and never take a call. What reaches them is whatever your contact can forward, and in most companies that is a brochure nobody reads.

A three-minute film survives being watched at one and a half times speed by somebody who did not ask for it, which is a real design constraint: the first twenty seconds have to say what the company does and why this film exists, because that is all a forwarded viewer gives you.

It also means the film belongs in the sales process rather than only on the site. Linked from a proposal, sent after a meeting, embedded in a follow-up. Those placements produce most of the value and cost nothing extra.

The same logic applies to the recruitment film. It is not for the careers page; it is for the message somebody sends a candidate who is weighing two offers.

One practical consequence of designing for the forward is length. A film made to be watched on a homepage can wander; a film made to be sent cannot, because a forwarded viewer is deciding within twenty seconds whether to keep watching. Cutting a three-minute piece down to ninety seconds is frequently the highest-return edit in a project and the one nobody schedules.

Our part

We Film It And We Spend Behind It

The reason we do both is that footage sitting on a website is a cost rather than an asset.

We film, cut for placement, and run paid distribution where there is a case for it. We manage over $70,000 a month in advertising spend for Ontario businesses and the owner runs the paid media personally.

How a video production kitchener project should begin

A video production kitchener project should begin with who has to see the film and what they are deciding, because that settles the subject, the length and whether it needs to be shot anywhere other than your own premises.

We are based in Minesing rather than Kitchener, so filming here involves travel, which we price as its own line. Where what you need is a screen-recorded product demonstration and nothing else, that is a task rather than a production and you should not pay production rates for it.

Would a recruitment video actually help us hire?

In a region where strong candidates hold several offers, the deciding information is what the work is actually like and who they would sit with, and a job advertisement cannot convey either. A short film of a real week does, it is the cheapest thing on any production list to make, and it is reusable for every vacancy afterwards.

How much does a shoot cost here?

The swing is set by whether anybody speaks on camera, where it is shot, how many days of preparation are involved and how many finished pieces come out. A quote that prices only the filming hours has either absorbed the preparation or will discover it. Deciding the number of finished pieces before the day is what reduces cost most.

Can you film inside a plant?

Yes, with the access sorted beforehand. The client’s own safety regulations govern where a camera may stand, which means inductions and agreed positions in advance rather than negotiated on the day. We have filmed on an active job site under exactly those conditions, around roughly forty trades and engineers across two days, and delivered seven finished videos.

Who owns the footage?

Agree it in writing before anybody films, and be specific: the finished pieces, the raw footage and the right to use both in advertising are three separate things. Take the raw files on your own storage at handover. Owning enough to re-cut next year without a second shoot is most of what makes the first one pay.
