LinkedIn Ads Agency Toronto

You Are Buying A List

A LinkedIn ads agency Toronto businesses hire should start with a list of companies rather than a budget, because this platform charges a premium for precision and that premium is only worth paying when the precision is doing something.

What you are actually paying for

What A LinkedIn Ads Agency Toronto Account Is Really Buying

The platform costs a multiple of what the same impression costs elsewhere, and the reason is the targeting rather than the audience.

Everywhere else you buy an audience and hope the right people are inside it. Here you buy the right people by job title, company, company size and seniority, and you pay a premium per thousand impressions for the privilege.

That trade is excellent when the right people are a defined, finite set — forty companies, six titles — and terrible when they are not. Pointed at a broad audience, the platform is simply an expensive way to buy impressions, and the targeting you paid extra for is doing no work at all.

So the first question for a LinkedIn ads agency Toronto client is not what to spend. It is whether you can name the companies. If you cannot, the money is better spent somewhere cheaper while you work out who you are actually selling to.

Where the money goes wrong

Four Ways A LinkedIn Ads Agency Toronto Account Wastes Budget

Targeting a title instead of a company

Every operations director in Ontario is an audience. The forty operations directors at companies that could plausibly buy from you is a list. The first costs more and converts worse, and it is the default.

Audience expansion left on

A setting that quietly widens the audience beyond what you specified, which is the thing you are paying the premium to avoid. Check it on every campaign.

Sending a cold audience to a demo request

The ask is too large for the relationship. On a platform this expensive, a click wasted on a form nobody will fill in is expensive twice.

No retargeting layer

The people who engaged are the only genuinely warm audience you will build here, and they are cheap to reach again. Accounts without this are paying full price for every impression forever.

What a LinkedIn ads agency Toronto proposal should open with

A list. Company names, titles, and an estimate of how many people that actually is. If the proposal opens with a budget and a creative concept, the expensive part of the platform has not been thought about.

What to say to a list that small

Small Audiences Need Different Creative

An audience of two thousand people sees the same thing repeatedly, which changes what is worth saying.

At broad reach, a single strong message can run for months because new people keep arriving. Against a named list, everybody has seen it within a fortnight, and after that each additional impression is either reinforcing or irritating.

The format that holds up is a sequence rather than a campaign: something that establishes the problem, something that shows the method, something that makes a small ask. Each piece is cheap to make and the sequence is what gives the budget somewhere to go after week two.

The format that does not hold up is a single advertisement for the company. It says everything once and has nothing to follow it with.

This is also why production capacity matters more than it appears to. An account aimed at a narrow list exhausts creative faster than a broad one, not slower, and that is the opposite of most people's intuition.

The uncomfortable part

This Only Works If Sales Picks Up The Other End

Advertising to forty named companies produces signals, not leads, and a signal nobody acts on is a cost.

What the platform gives you is a list of people at target companies who engaged. That is genuinely valuable and it is not a lead. Somebody has to decide what happens next, and in most engagements that decision was never made.

The version that works has a named person on your side who receives the engaged list weekly and does something specific with it. The version that fails sends the list into a shared inbox.

So we ask about this before quoting, and occasionally the honest answer is that there is nobody to do it. In that case the advertising should wait, because running it first produces an expensive demonstration that the channel does not work.

None of this is a LinkedIn problem. It is what happens when a targeting product is bought as a lead product, and it is the most common failure we see in this channel in this city.

Formats

Which Ad Types Earn Their Cost

The platform offers several and they are not interchangeable at this price.

A single image in the feed is the cheapest to make and the easiest to ignore, and it works when the message is one sentence a stranger can act on. It does not work as a general advertisement for the company.

Document and carousel formats hold attention longer because they require a deliberate action to consume, which makes them suited to anything that has to teach before it can sell. For a technical proposition they are frequently the only format worth running.

Message-style formats delivered to the inbox have the highest cost per send and the highest irritation risk. They are defensible against a short list where the ask is genuinely small and the sender is a real person who will answer a reply. They are indefensible as a volume play.

Video sits between the two: expensive to make, strong at establishing that real people do real work, and reusable everywhere else. Where footage already exists from another project, it is usually the cheapest strong option on this platform rather than the dearest.

What we do

Narrow, And Said Plainly

We run this channel as part of a defined motion, not as a standalone media buy.

We build the account list with you, set the targeting against companies rather than titles alone, produce the sequence of creative it needs, and hand the engaged list back in a form somebody can act on. We manage over $70,000 a month in advertising spend for Ontario businesses, and the owner runs paid media personally.

We do not run outbound messaging, we do not sell lead lists, and we do not run this channel for businesses who cannot name their target accounts. The third one costs us work and it is the most useful sentence on the page.

What is the minimum budget for LinkedIn advertising?

Enough to reach your named list several times within a month, which is a calculable number once the list exists rather than a rule of thumb. Against a small list that figure is often lower than people expect. Against a broad audience it is high enough that the money is usually better spent elsewhere, which is the more useful finding.

Is LinkedIn better than Google Ads for B2B?

They answer different questions. Google captures people already looking for a solution, which is a smaller group than your market and arrives ready. LinkedIn reaches people who are not looking yet but are the right people, which is a larger group that arrives cold. Most B2B businesses should buy the first until it is exhausted, then use the second to grow the pool.

Can we use this to reach a specific list of companies?

Yes, and that is the use case the premium pricing is actually for. You can upload a list of companies and advertise only to people at them, which makes the platform behave like an addressable channel rather than a broadcast one. It is also the point at which coordination with whoever handles sales stops being optional.

How long before this produces anything?

Engagement within weeks; pipeline on whatever your sales cycle is, which in B2B is frequently a quarter or more. The thing to agree before starting is what evidence at the eight-week mark would count as progress, because without that the review happens against revenue the cycle cannot yet have produced.
