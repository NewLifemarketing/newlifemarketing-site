Social Media Marketing London Ontario Has No Single Audience

Over 420,000 people, a large university population, established neighbourhoods, newer suburbs and a rural catchment that shops here. They have very little in common.

422,000+
people in London

Statistics Canada, 2021 Census

A town of 30,000 genuinely has one audience. Post something good and the people who might buy from you share a feed with everybody else, and a single voice works because there is only one room. That is the model nearly every Ontario social media plan is built on, and London breaks it.

London is several rooms. The student population, the families in the newer north-end suburbs, the long-established households in Old South and Byron, the rural customers from the surrounding counties who drive in. Content written to appeal to all of them is written to appeal to none of them, which is why so much social media marketing london ontario businesses pay for reads as oddly bland.

The blandness is not a writing failure. It is the arithmetic of addressing four London audiences in one post. Nothing specific enough to land with one group survives the requirement to be acceptable to the other three.

Pick one

Narrow Content Outperforms Broad Content Here

The instinct is that speaking to everybody reaches more people. In a city the size of London the opposite happens, and the mechanism is straightforward.

The mechanism is sharing. A post that names a specific street, a specific situation or a specific kind of customer gets forwarded by the people it describes, and that forwarding is where reach actually comes from. A post calibrated to offend nobody gets forwarded by nobody, so it reaches only the followers you already had.

Which means narrowing is not a sacrifice of reach in London. It is the mechanism for getting any. A social media agency london ontario firms hire that proposes broad, inoffensive content is proposing the version that cannot travel.

The four rooms

Who Is Actually In London, And What Reaches Them

Not demographics for their own sake. Four London groups that respond to visibly different things.

The newly arrived are worth most

Somebody who moved into a new north-end London subdivision last year is choosing a dentist, a mechanic, a landscaper and a hairdresser in the same twelve months. Reaching them once is worth years of trade, and almost nobody in London targets them deliberately.

Established neighbourhoods need proof, not reach

London households with existing habits change supplier on a recommendation rather than an advertisement. For this group the account exists to survive being checked, not to persuade, which is a very different content brief.

The counties need the practical details

Hours, parking, whether it can be done in one visit. A social media marketing agency london ontario clients retain rarely thinks about the drive, and for a meaningful slice of the market it decides everything.

Straight answers

Social Media Marketing FAQ

Straight answers before you ever book a call.

Will narrow content not reach fewer people?

It reaches more, and the mechanism is sharing. A post that names a specific neighbourhood, situation or kind of customer gets forwarded by the people it describes, and in a city of this size that forwarding is where almost all reach comes from. A post written to be acceptable to everybody is forwarded by nobody, so it only ever reaches the followers you already had. Narrowing feels like giving up reach and is actually the only way to generate any.

Which audience should we pick?

Usually whichever makes the most money, but the newly arrived are worth a second look. Somebody who moved to London last year is choosing a dentist, a mechanic, a hairdresser and a landscaper within about twelve months, so reaching them once can be worth years of trade. They are also the group least likely to be targeted deliberately, because they look like everybody else in a demographic report and behave nothing like them.

Can we not just run several audiences at once?

Eventually, and not at the start. A second audience is a second account in everything but name: different content, different references, often different timing and a different platform. Running two badly produces worse results than running one well, and the common failure is a calendar that alternates between them, which reads as inconsistent to both. Get the first producing, then add the second with its own plan and its own measurement.

Our engagement is fine but nothing sells. Why?

Frequently because the content reaches people who were never going to buy. In a London with several distinct audiences it is entirely possible to build a following made mostly of the wrong group, which produces healthy-looking numbers and no revenue. The useful check is not how many people engaged but whether the people engaging look like your actual customers. If they do not, the audience was never chosen, it accumulated.

How often should we post?

Less often than most plans propose, and more specifically. Two genuinely targeted posts a week will outperform five written to be acceptable to the whole of London, because the narrow ones travel and the broad ones do not. The constraint is almost never writing time; it is the willingness to publish something that visibly is not for most of the people who will see it, which is a harder decision than it sounds.
