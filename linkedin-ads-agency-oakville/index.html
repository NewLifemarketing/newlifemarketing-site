LinkedIn Ads Agency Oakville

They Live Here. They Work Elsewhere.

The first thing a LinkedIn ads agency Oakville businesses hire should change is the geography, because a large share of the professional audience in this town is employed somewhere else, and a campaign drawn around the municipality is aimed at where they sleep rather than where they decide.

The geography problem

Why A LinkedIn Ads Agency Oakville Campaign Should Not Target Oakville

Everywhere else, location targeting approximates a market. Here it approximates a commute.

Statistics Canada counted 213,700 residents at the 2021 Census. A professional platform lets you target by location, and the obvious move is to draw a circle around the town. That circle catches people whose employer, budget and buying authority sit in Toronto, Mississauga or Hamilton.

Whether that is right depends entirely on what you sell. If you sell to the person, their home address is relevant. If you sell to their employer, it is nearly irrelevant and you have just paid a premium for a filter that excluded most of your market.

So the question to settle before any budget is which of those two you are doing, and it is not a targeting question. It is a question about who signs.

What the premium is for

You Are Buying Precision, So Use It

This platform costs a multiple of the alternatives per thousand impressions, and that trade only pays when the precision is doing work.

Everywhere else you buy an audience and hope the right people are in it. Here you buy by job title, company, company size and seniority, and you pay for it. Pointed at a broad audience the platform is simply an expensive way to buy impressions, and the thing you paid extra for is idle.

The use that justifies the price is a named list: the companies you want, the roles inside them, and a campaign that reaches nobody else. For an Oakville business selling into GTA industry, that list is frequently a few dozen companies and a few hundred people — small, expensive per impression, and nothing wasted.

What a LinkedIn ads agency Oakville clients keep asks for first

The list. Companies and roles, with a rough count of how many people that actually is. A proposal that opens with a budget and a creative concept has not used the expensive part of the platform.

Creative against a small list

A Sequence, Not A Campaign

Against a few hundred named people, everybody has seen your advertisement within a fortnight.

Something that establishes the problem

Written for somebody who does not yet agree they have it. This is the piece most often skipped, and skipping it is why the later pieces land on people who are not listening.

Something that shows the method

How the work is actually done, in enough detail that a competent person could find fault. Document and carousel formats hold attention for this because consuming them is a deliberate act.

Something with a small ask

Not a demo request. A short piece of research, a benchmark, a teardown — something worth the click that does not require a meeting. The meeting comes from the relationship, not from the advertisement.

The handover

What A LinkedIn Ads Agency Oakville Account Actually Produces

What this platform produces is a list of people at target companies who paid attention, and that is not the same as somebody asking to buy.

The value of the engaged list is real and it decays quickly. Somebody who read a document last week is a different prospect from somebody who read one in March, and the difference is entirely in how fast a human follows up.

So the arrangement that works has a named person on your side receiving the list weekly and doing something specific with it, and the arrangement that fails sends it to a shared inbox. We ask about this before quoting because it decides whether the spend is worth making at all.

Where the honest answer is that nobody has time, the advertising should wait. Running it first produces an expensive demonstration that the channel does not work, and that conclusion then outlives the circumstances that caused it.

This is not a LinkedIn problem. It is what happens when a targeting product is bought as a lead product, and it is the most common failure we see in this channel.

The cheaper alternative

When Not To Use This Platform At All

Three situations where the premium is money burned, and all three are common among businesses based in this town.

If your buyers are already searching for what you sell, buy that first. Search captures people who have decided to act, at a lower cost per outcome, and no amount of targeting precision beats somebody typing the problem into Google. A LinkedIn ads agency Oakville businesses hire before exhausting that is being asked to do the harder job first.

If the product is bought by individuals rather than organisations, a professional network is an expensive way to reach them. The same people are reachable for a fraction of the cost on platforms where they are not at work, and the job title filter you are paying for is irrelevant to the purchase.

If the sales cycle ends in a transaction rather than a conversation, the economics rarely work. This channel justifies its price when a single closed deal is worth a great deal, because the cost per conversation is high and the number of conversations is small by design.

None of that is an argument against the platform. It is an argument for checking three things before anybody quotes, and for being told the answer even when it loses the agency the work.

Our scope

What We Run And What We Will Not

A short list, honestly drawn.

We build the account list with you, set the targeting against companies rather than titles alone, produce the sequence the small audience needs, and hand the engaged list back weekly in a form somebody can act on. We manage over $70,000 a month in advertising spend for Ontario businesses and the owner runs the paid media personally.

We do not run outbound messaging on your behalf, we do not sell lead lists, and we will not run this channel for a business that cannot name its target accounts. The third one costs us work and it is the most useful sentence here.

We are based in Minesing rather than Oakville. For an advertising account that changes nothing.

Should we target Oakville at all?

Only if the buying decision happens at a home address, which for most business-to-business selling it does not. If you sell to employers, target the companies and let the people live wherever they live. If you sell to the individual — professional services bought personally, for instance — then the residential geography is exactly right and the premium is worth paying for it.

How small can the audience be?

Smaller than feels comfortable. A few hundred named people at a few dozen companies is a workable programme and often outperforms a broad one, because every impression lands on somebody who could actually buy. The cost per impression looks alarming and the cost per outcome frequently does not.

Is this better than Google Ads for us?

They do different jobs. Google captures people already searching, which is a smaller group that arrives ready to talk. LinkedIn reaches the right people before they are looking, which is a larger group that arrives cold. Most businesses should take all the search demand they can get first, then use this to grow the pool.

What budget does this need?

Enough to reach your named list several times in a month, which is calculable once the list exists and is often lower than expected. If there is no list, the number is unanswerable and any figure quoted is guesswork — which is why we would rather spend the first session building the list than discussing spend.

Those two answers settle the geography, the targeting and most of the budget. Send them and we can be specific in one call.
