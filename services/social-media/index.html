The problem

Posting Is Not A Strategy

Most businesses are doing social media admin. Almost nobody is doing social media marketing.

The 4pm scramble
Somebody remembers at four o'clock that nothing went out today, so a stock photo and a caption go up. Repeat until the account reads as a chore that somebody lost interest in.
The content drought
Three good posts in January, then nothing until March. The algorithm treats a dormant account as a dormant account, and the reach does not come back to where it was.
The same thing, five times
One square graphic pushed to every platform at once. Each algorithm rewards format fit, so a post built for nothing performs on nothing.
Measuring followers
The one number that feels like progress and predicts almost nothing about revenue.

None of this is a discipline problem

Businesses do not post consistently because they do not have anything to post. It is a production problem wearing a scheduling problem's clothes — and it is solvable with a camera and a plan rather than with more reminders.

Which is why this service starts with a shoot day rather than a calendar.

02

What it is

What Social Actually Does

Strong at three things, weak at three others. Buying it for the wrong one is how people end up disappointed.

Genuinely strong at

  • Being found — your profile is a search result and a credibility check before anyone calls
  • Staying in front of people who already know you, so you are the name they remember when the need arrives
  • Proof — showing the work, the team and the results in a way a website structurally cannot

Weak at

  • Direct response on its own, detached from paid or a real offer
  • Producing predictable lead volume in week one
  • Replacing a sales process

Judged before you are contacted

By the time somebody calls you, they have already looked. The profile is the last thing checked before a decision and the cheapest place to lose one — a dead feed undercuts every other dollar you spend.

That is the job this service is actually doing, and it is why we measure profile visits rather than applause.

Six things owners reasonably believe that are not true in 2026

“Organic reach is dead”

Reach moved rather than died. Feeds now distribute on watch time, saves and shares instead of who follows you — which is why a brand-new account can out-reach an established one, and why follower count stopped predicting anything.

“We just need to post more”

Frequency without format fit does nothing. Five identical squares pushed to five platforms is five posts built for none of them.

“Followers are the goal”

Followers are the number that feels like progress and predicts the least. Saves, shares and profile visits are the ones that sit closest to revenue.

“Social will bring us leads next month”

Sometimes, but that is not what it is strongest at. It is strongest at being found, being remembered, and being believed. If you need volume this month, run ads and put social underneath.

“Someone internal can handle it”

They can handle publishing. What collapses is supply — by month two there is nothing left to post, because nobody scheduled a camera.

“A viral post would fix this”

One spike against a flat month is a spike, not a trend. The account that compounds is the one that was never dormant.

The destination

Mass Reach Needs A Destination

The chapter that turns this from a content service into a revenue channel.

Everything above this chapter builds attention. A webinar is what converts it — and it is the piece almost no social media agency offers, because it requires producing and running a live session rather than scheduling posts.

The push
Every platform in the stack drives registrations. This is where mass organic volume finally has a specific job rather than existing for its own sake.
The session
Free, genuinely valuable, no gate. The content gives away real methodology rather than teasing it — which is what makes the audience trust the offer at the end.
The qualification
Attendance does the filtering. Somebody who registers, shows up and stays to the end has demonstrated more intent than any form fill can.
The ask
The offer is made at the end, to people who now understand what it is. The sales conversation afterwards starts warm, informed and much shorter.

Why this changes the economics

Social on its own is judged on reach, engagement and follower growth — all real, none of them revenue. A webinar gives the entire content system a destination: every reel, short, carousel and story now has somewhere to send people, and the month ends with a number you can actually act on. Registrations, attendance, calls booked.

Typically one session a month, filmed once and run more than once, with registration open continuously in between, a registration page on your site and the whole thing wired into your CRM for reminders and follow-up. We scope whether it fits your offer before we build it — it is a layer we add when the maths supports it, not a box every account gets.

Community management

The Half That Is Not Posting

Half of social media is not publishing, and it is the half that usually goes unstaffed.

Replying to comments and DMs, responding to reviews, and answering the questions that arrive in the inbox rather than through the form.

Algorithms in 2026 weight conversation density and retention over raw impressions, so an account that replies genuinely outperforms one that broadcasts. It is also where leads actually arrive on social — and where most businesses lose them, because nobody owns the inbox.

17

Social SEO

Your Posts Are Now Search Results

The reason captions are written rather than generated.

A meaningful share of younger buyers search inside TikTok and Instagram rather than Google, and YouTube has always been a search engine. So captions, on-screen text, spoken words and hashtags all get written with real search terms in them.

They are the same intent keywords that drive the SEO work — one keyword set, working across search and social at once, rather than two teams guessing separately.

  • You can let a crew in once a month — That single day is what makes the other twenty-nine possible.

It is the wrong call when

The honest line: social is strongest on awareness, credibility and retention. If the immediate need is lead volume this month, we would point you at Google or Meta first and run social underneath it.

Straight answers

Social Media FAQ

What owners ask before handing over their social — who makes the content, how often, what it costs, and who owns it — answered straight.

How often do you actually need to film?

Once a month is the standard rhythm, and it is what the whole system is built around. One production day on site produces the long-form, the short-form and the stills for the next thirty days. Some clients add a second day when there is a launch or a seasonal push, but one is enough to keep every platform fed without the business doing anything between shoots.

Do I have to be on camera?

Somebody usually should, because people follow people rather than logos — but it does not have to be you and it does not have to be often. We can build a month around the work itself: process, product, hands, finished jobs and the space. It is a weaker version of the account than one with a face in it, and we will tell you that honestly rather than pretend the two perform the same.

Which platforms should my business be on?

Fewer than you think. A local service business or trade starts with Instagram and Facebook, then adds YouTube once there is a library. E-commerce starts with Instagram and TikTok. B2B starts with LinkedIn and YouTube. We would rather fully own two platforms for you than have you forgettable on six, and we decide which two on the call.

How long before social media produces business?

The account looks alive from week two, which matters more than it sounds because that is what people see when they check you. Real momentum takes about ninety days: month one buys consistency, month two buys a library, and month three is the first month where we are steering from evidence. If you need booked jobs inside thirty days, run Google Ads and let social compound underneath.

What happens if I hate a piece of content?

You see the whole month before it goes out. The calendar is built, written and approved in advance precisely so nothing is a surprise — if something is wrong it gets changed at the planning stage rather than argued about after it is published. And if a piece still misses once it is live, we pull it.

Do you write the captions too?

Yes, all of them, and they are written rather than generated. Captions carry real search terms because posts are now search results, so the same intent keywords driving the SEO work go into the words on social. Hashtags are chosen the same way rather than copied from a list.

Who owns the content and the accounts?

You do, both. We publish into accounts in your name using your logins — we do not create agency-owned pages that you would have to leave behind. The footage is yours too, raw and finished. If we stop working together, the audience and the library stay with you.

Can you run ads off the same content?

That is the point of filming it this way. The same shoot day feeds Meta and Google, so the production cost is shared across organic and paid instead of being charged to one of them. It is also why our ad creative does not look like stock — it is the same footage, cut for a different job.

What if we already have someone posting internally?

Then the gap is usually supply rather than effort, and we can fill just that: the production day and the asset library, with your person keeping the publishing and the community management. We would rather scope to what is actually missing than replace somebody who is doing fine.

Do you do podcasts and webinars, or just posts?
