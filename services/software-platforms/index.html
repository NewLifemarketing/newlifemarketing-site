The problem

Software That Fits One Business

Most businesses run a specific operation on software designed for somebody else's.

The spreadsheet operation
The real business runs in five spreadsheets, three chat threads and one person's head. It works until it does not, and it cannot be handed to anybody.
The subscription pile
A different tool for every function, none of them talking to each other, each charging monthly for a fraction of what is needed.
The long document
Account research living in Google Docs and sheets that nobody can sort, search or keep current, so the list is stale the week after it is built.
The monthly PDF
Clients waiting for a report somebody compiled by hand, when the numbers were available the whole month.

This is a fit problem, not a technology problem. Nothing on this page is about replacing tools that already work. It is about the gap between what your business actually does and what you can buy off a shelf.

Overview

Three Platforms We Have Built

Software we build, run and rely on ourselves.

Platform 1

ABM platform

Builds the account list for an account-based program, then researches the decision makers at every account until the file is complete and sourced.

Platform 2

HQ

Runs a team day to day: who is doing what, what is done, what is late, and where the hours went.

Client reporting

Each one was built to solve a real problem inside our own work first. The ABM platform runs on a live client program, the HQ runs our own company, and the reporting platform is what our clients sign in to.

Platform 1

The ABM Platform

From a spreadsheet of company names to a ranked, researched, sourced account list.

It replaces the spreadsheet account lists and the long account write-ups with one screen per program, a sortable list of every account, and a one-page file on each one.

Tiered by design
Every program is split into one-to-one, one-to-few and one-to-many accounts, each tier with its own columns, its own scoring model and its own research checklist.
Three numbers, never blended
Fit (should we want them), Research (do we know enough about them) and Warmth (do they know us yet). Each is calculated separately so one cannot hide another.
A one-pager per account
The company, its buying centre, its triggers and its next step, written up in sections and kept next to the evidence behind them.
Built to feed campaigns
The output is an account list ready to be targeted, not a raw dump somebody has to clean first.

What it feeds: account-based marketing and LinkedIn Ads against the named list.

Platform 1

Lists Built From Public Registers

Source, group, tier, then research. In that order.

A list is only as good as where it starts. The platform builds a list from the regulator's own public register where one exists, rather than from a directory or a search result, and keeps the source, the date and the row count on record.

Group by the company that decides
Several storefronts are often one licensed company under different banners. The list is grouped by the company, not the shop name, so one buyer is never counted as five.
A parent check
Companies behind more than one banner are found and merged, with the evidence, before anything is researched.
Tier and trim, with reasons
Accounts are tiered by footprint and cut only for a reason the brief gives, and the reason is written down.
Approved before it is researched
The setup is signed off by a person before any research effort is spent on it.

We say data pipeline in full on this page. On the CRM and ABM pages, “pipeline” means the sales pipeline. They are different things.

Platform 1

Research That Cannot Skip A Step

Accuracy is enforced by the software, not left to whoever is researching that day.

Research agents gather the information, and the platform decides when an account is done. A file reaches 100% only when every checklist item is filled and has a source. “Not found” does not count.

Decision makers, fully
Each person needs a name, an exact current title, a role in the buying centre, a dated source, a LinkedIn profile, a work location and a published business email or direct phone.
Published contact details only
No guessed email patterns and no home addresses. A contact detail is accepted only with the page it was published on.
Goes stale on purpose
Items expire after a refresh window, so a record that nobody has checked in months drops back out of 100%.
People decide what is uncertain
Conflicts, unverified people and proposed score changes wait in a review queue for a person to accept or reject. The platform cannot overwrite a human edit, delete a record or contact anyone.

Platform 2

HQ: The System A Team Runs On

Built for our own team first, and now something we can build for yours.

Not a project management subscription bent into shape

An HQ is one place where the whole operation is visible and the whole team works. Everyone logs in, sees their own work and ticks it off. Recurring work reschedules itself, and the manager sees what is done, what is late and who is overloaded without chasing anyone for an update.

The point is that the day's hours go to the work that moves the business. When time and tasks are visible, drift is visible too.

Platform 2

What An HQ Tracks

The common set, and why it works as one system.

Each person's day
Their tasks, what is done and what is outstanding, in one view.
Time
Hours logged against the work, so you can see where the day actually went.
Recurring work
Weekly, fortnightly and monthly tasks reappear on schedule instead of relying on memory.
Briefings
Work assigned into the platform ahead of time: who owns what, what is due, what is blocked.
Goals and leaderboard
Targets and team performance made visible rather than assumed.
Live reporting
Pipeline and results in the same place the team works, so nobody waits for a monthly report to know how the month is going.

The modules are assembled per business. The common set lives in one place instead of six subscriptions that do not talk to each other.

Platform 3

The Client Reporting Platform

What our clients actually log into.

Our own software, built in-house and not licensed. A live client dashboard where Meta, Google, Business Profile, SEO and organic numbers land in one place and refresh as the month runs. No waiting for a PDF.

One login per client
Each client signs in and sees their own numbers and only theirs.
Every channel together
Paid, search, local and organic results side by side, not in five separate tools.
Refreshed as the month runs
The picture moves during the month, not after it.
Role-based access
Who can see what is enforced on the server rather than hidden in the interface.

See it in the software behind our service.

Qualify yourself out

Who It Is For, And What We Talk People Out Of

The honest version, on the service where honesty is most expensive to skip.

This fits when

  • You run account-based marketing: And need the research engine behind it rather than a list you rebuy every quarter.
  • Nobody can see what the team is doing: And the day's hours are the one resource you cannot get back.
  • The operation lives in spreadsheets: And in somebody's memory, which is a risk rather than a system.

  • Nobody internally will own it: An unused platform is worse than none: it is the same problem plus a bill.
  • The requirement is a website or a login: That is a websites and portals job.

The process

Scope To Launch

Five phases, and the first one is where most of the value is decided.

Scope
We map how the business actually works before deciding what to build. Most of the value is decided here, because building the wrong thing well is the expensive failure.
Design
Screens and permissions designed together, so who sees what is decided before a screen is built.
Build
Developed in stages, with working software to look at early, rather than a long silence followed by a reveal.
Launch
Data loaded, users onboarded, and the team actually trained on it. A platform nobody was shown how to use does not get used.
Iterate
It changes as the business changes. Ours are maintained and extended.

The scope

What Is Included, And What It Costs To Run

What comes with a build, and the running costs most vendors leave until the invoice.

  • Scope: mapping how the business actually works before anything is designed
  • Custom design and full build, in-house
  • The permission model designed before the interface, for anything with a login
  • CRM, tracking and reporting connected at build time
  • Data loading, user onboarding and actual training for the team who will use it
  • Hosting and infrastructure, stated up front rather than discovered later
  • Maintenance, support and ongoing development as the business changes
  • Ownership: the code, the data and the accounts stay with you

Custom software carries running costs a template does not: hosting, the database and the automation running in the background. Those numbers go in the proposal rather than on this page, because they depend entirely on what gets built.

Straight answers

Software & Platforms FAQ

Who owns it, what it is, and whether any of this is AI, answered plainly.

What platforms have you built?

Three so far. An account-based marketing platform that builds account lists and researches the people who decide. An HQ that runs a team day to day. And a client reporting platform that shows live results. The first is on a live client program, the second runs our own company, and the third is what our clients log into.

Do I own what you build?

Yes. The code, the data and the accounts stay with you, which is not the norm in this category and is worth asking every vendor you talk to.

Can I use one of these platforms for my business?

Possibly. We scope that on a call. A platform we have already built is a faster starting point than a blank page, and anything that has to change is quoted plainly before work starts.

Is any of this AI?

The ABM platform uses research agents to gather and check information about accounts and decision makers, then holds every finding for review wherever it is uncertain. People decide what goes in, and nothing is invented to fill a gap.
