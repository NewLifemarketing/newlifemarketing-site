Intent Based Marketing

Intent & Signal Marketing

Buying research happens anonymously. Signal-based programmes tell you which of your target accounts are in-market now, so effort goes where the timing is right.

The problem

Why This Exists

Most buying research is anonymous. By the time an account identifies itself, the shortlist is usually written. Signal-based programmes are how you find out which named accounts are looking while it still matters.

What we do

What Intent & Signal Marketing Actually Involves

Define the signals that matter

Not every signal is worth acting on

Which research behaviours, site actions, hiring moves and technology changes actually correlate with your deals: decided before buying any data.

Wire the signal into the CRM

So it reaches a human

Account-level alerts routed to the right rep with context attached, rather than a dashboard nobody opens.

Act on it with a play, not a blast

Signal without a response is noise

Defined plays per signal type, what marketing does, what sales does, and within what window.

Be honest about the data

Intent data is directional

It tells you where to look, not what will close. We use it to prioritise effort, never as a substitute for qualification.

Where it genuinely works

Three Situations Worth The Expense

This is an expensive discipline and it suits a narrow set of businesses. Outside them, the money buys a list.

A finite, nameable market. Where the total number of companies that could ever buy is in the hundreds rather than the millions, knowing which forty are moving this quarter changes what a small sales effort does with its week. Where the market is everybody, the same information is noise.

A long cycle with a visible trigger. A contract renewal, a funding round, a new site, a regulatory date. Where the trigger exists and is published, the timing problem is solvable and the reward for solving it is large.

Somebody available to act. The whole value is in the speed of a human response. A business whose sales effort is one person already at capacity cannot convert a timing advantage into anything, and should spend the budget on the capacity instead.

Where none of the three holds, the honest recommendation is to put the money into being findable when those companies start looking. That is a smaller engagement than this page describes and it is frequently the right one.

Before anything is bought

Four Things To Settle First

Each of them decides whether a signal programme produces meetings or produces a dashboard.

Who acts, and how quickly. A signal has a half-life measured in days. If the route from a flagged account to a human conversation runs through a weekly meeting, the programme is buying information it cannot use. Decide the response time before the data.

What the first message says. Acknowledging a signal without being unsettling is a genuine skill. “We noticed you were researching” reads badly; a message about the problem itself, sent at the right moment, does not. The difference is the whole craft.

What happens to accounts that never convert. Most flagged accounts will not become customers this quarter. Without a rule for releasing them, the list grows until the same companies are contacted repeatedly by people who do not know it has happened before.

What would make you stop. Written down at the start, in a sentence: the cost per conversation at which this is no longer worth running. Agreed afterwards, the decision gets made by whoever argues hardest at renewal.

None of the four is about data. All four are about what the business does with it, which is where every programme of this kind actually succeeds or fails.

The uncomfortable part

Most Intent Data Is Wrong About Most Accounts

Not useless — wrong at a rate nobody measures, which is a different and more expensive problem.

Third-party intent works by inferring. Somebody at a company read something on a site in a network, the company is matched to an address range, and the account is reported as researching your category. Each of those three steps is probabilistic, and the error compounds.

That does not make it worthless. It makes it a prioritisation tool rather than a trigger: useful for deciding which fifty of five hundred accounts get attention this month, and dangerous as a reason to contact somebody who has shown no interest in you specifically.

The test nobody runs is simple. Take last quarter’s flagged accounts, and count how many turned into a conversation. Compare that against a random sample of accounts from the same list that were never flagged. If the two numbers are close, the feed is being paid for and is not doing anything.

Run it once before renewing. It is the only evidence either side of that conversation can offer, and it takes an afternoon with records the business already has.

The signal you already own

Your Own Site Is The Better Feed

Before buying somebody else’s inference, use the behaviour you can observe directly.

A named contact returning three times in a week, a pricing page read twice, a document downloaded and then forwarded inside the same company — these are first-party signals, they are accurate, and they are free. Most businesses capture none of them because nobody connected the site to the record system.

The second source is the enquiry itself. What somebody typed into a search box before arriving, which page they landed on, and what they asked about first are three pieces of information that describe the problem better than any firmographic score, and all three are discarded by default.

Where third-party data earns its place is in the gap: accounts that match your profile and have shown you nothing. That is a genuinely useful list and it is a fraction of the size of the one most feeds deliver.

So the order is first-party, then enquiry context, then bought data — and the budget should follow the same order rather than the reverse, which is how it is almost always arranged.

Our methodology

The Signal Engine Behind It

Intent work runs through six stages in order. The first three decide whether any signal is worth acting on; the last three decide whether acting on it produces anything.

01 · Sources

The places a buying signal is actually visible: searches, site behaviour, third-party intent data, a job posting, a funding announcement. Each has a different accuracy and a different cost, and most programmes buy one and trust it entirely.

02 · Validation

Checking a signal against what actually happened. A share of every intent feed is noise, and a programme that has never measured its own false-positive rate is acting on data it has not tested.

03 · Scoring

What combination of signals justifies an approach, written down as a rule rather than held as a judgement. Without it, the definition drifts and nobody can say why one account was contacted and another was not.

04 · Routing

Which signals reach a person and which trigger something automatic, with a stated response time. A signal that decays in two days and reaches somebody in five has been bought and thrown away.

05 · Plays

What actually happens when a signal fires: the message, the channel, and how it acknowledges what prompted it without being unsettling about how much you know.

06 · Review

Which sources produced meetings and which produced activity, read quarterly. Intent feeds are expensive and the honest question is whether the ones you are paying for beat watching your own site.

Measurement

What Gets Reported

The numbers

  • In-market accounts identified
  • Signal-to-engagement rate
  • Response time on high-intent accounts
  • Pipeline from signal-triggered plays
  • Account coverage of the target list
  • False-positive rate

How we report them

  • Platform-reported values labelled as such
  • The attribution window stated on every view
  • Early-stage data flagged as early-stage
  • No cross-platform summing presented as revenue
  • Nothing published we cannot show you the source for

The house rule does not change with the service.

Straight answers

Intent & Signal Marketing FAQ

Do we need to buy an intent data platform?

Not necessarily. First-party signals, your own site, CRM, email and sales engagement data, are under-used at most companies and cost nothing extra. We would exhaust those before recommending a purchase.

Is intent data accurate?

It is directional. It is good at telling you an account is researching a category and poor at telling you they will buy from you. Treated as prioritisation it is valuable; treated as qualification it wastes sales time.

Is this compliant?

Account-level intent works on aggregated, company-level signals rather than identifying individuals. We keep programmes to that level and to the platform's own permitted use.
