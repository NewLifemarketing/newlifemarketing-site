Meta Ads: Creating The Demand That Comes Next

Nobody opens Facebook looking to book a contractor. So the job is to stop them scrolling with real footage of real work in their area, often enough that when they do need the job done, you are the name they already know. Google catches the people searching this week. Meta creates the people who will search next month. The system runs both, always.

How the creative moves

New creative goes live

Every two weeks, in prospecting.

It works

Jobs coming in at a cost that makes sense. After a few days it is pushed to scaling: the budget goes up.

It does not

No enquiries, or too expensive. After a few days it is killed, and the next creative takes its place.

Why every two weeks. Ads fatigue. The same video shown to the same area for two months stops working: the cost per enquiry climbs and most businesses never notice until the phone goes quiet. Swapping creative every fortnight keeps performance flat instead of sliding, and it is the whole reason we film on your sites every month. Four of the six videos from each shoot day are built as ads from the start. The other two are for the feed.

Everything starts with the keywords

SEO is not a channel sitting next to the others. It is where the whole system gets its direction. We use Semrush to find the keywords that carry buying intent in your area: what homeowners and builders type, and what they are ready to act on. Those keywords then run through every single thing we produce: the video scripts, the captions, the blogs, the ad copy, the Google Business Profile updates and the website pages. One set of research, pointed at every surface. It is why the content and the ads reinforce each other instead of pulling in different directions.

The research is monthly because search behaviour moves. New terms appear, old ones fade, competitors shift and seasons change what people look for, which matters more in the trades than almost anywhere else. A keyword set from six months ago is a plan for a market that no longer exists.

Why the map pack is the prize. When a homeowner searches for your trade near them, the map results sit above everything, above the ads and above the websites. That position is earned, not bought, which is why the Google profile gets worked on daily and the website gets a new page every day.

Account warmth tracking

Knowing When A Big Account Is Ready, Before Anyone Picks Up The Phone

For a contractor doing 25 or more builds a year, one account can mean a huge amount of work every single year. Those accounts do not come in off a form. They take months of being seen before anyone is ready to talk. Smaller jobs skip this step entirely and book straight away.

ColdAwareWarmingWarmHot

We track when the SEO, the organic content and the ads have warmed an account up: who has been reading, watching and coming back, and how often. When an account crosses the line from interested to ready, it is passed to a sales rep to close.

Who has been looking

Which companies have been on the site, and which pages they went to.

How often

One visit is nothing. Five visits in a fortnight is a conversation waiting to happen.

What warmed them

Whether it was the ads, the content or the search work that did it.

When to call

A spike in activity is the moment to reach out. Without tracking, nobody knows it happened.

Why it is worth doing. A rep calling cold is guessing. A rep calling a builder who has been on your project pages three times this week is doing something else entirely, and on a contract worth a year of work, that difference is the whole deal.

Account warmth · The machinery

How We See Who Is Warming Up

This is the part nobody can do from a spreadsheet. It runs on three pieces working together: visitor identification, a second pipeline in the CRM and a platform we built ourselves to score every account.

  1. 1

    Identify the companies visiting

    Website visitor identification and IP reverse lookup tie a visit to the company behind it, so we can see which businesses are on your site, which pages they read and how often they come back. The same logic follows engagement across your social channels.

  2. 2

    Fill in who they are

    Sales intelligence software adds the company's size, what it does and who works there, so a spike in activity comes with the context to act on it.

  3. 3

    Log it in a second pipeline

    Your main pipeline tracks jobs. A second pipeline in the CRM tracks accounts. Each target company sits in its own warmth stage and moves from Cold toward Hot as the signals add up and fade when they stop.

  4. 4

    Hand it over at the right moment

    When an account crosses from interested to ready, the sales rep gets the account, what it has been looking at and what warmed it, before the first call is made.

Account warmth · The platform

The Account Platform We Built To Run It

We did not rent a black-box tool. We built our own account platform: every target company in one ranked list, with how well it is researched and how warm it is side by side, and a one-page brief on each. It keeps three numbers separate because they answer three different questions.

Fit: should we want them?
A 0 to 10 score built from ideal-customer fit, current need, opportunity size and buying history. It only shows once every part is scored, so a half-known account never looks like a sure thing.
Research: do we know enough?
The share of a checklist that is actually filled with a sourced fact. Not found counts as zero, so no account can fake 100%.
Warmth: do they know us?
A 0 to 100 score from pipeline stage and logged touches, banded Cold, Aware, Warming, Warm and Hot. Touches fade to zero over 90 days, so it measures the relationship now, not its history. Replies and meetings count the most.
Research first, then warming. An account is only worth warming once you know who the buyer is and what the company needs. That order is built into the platform.
