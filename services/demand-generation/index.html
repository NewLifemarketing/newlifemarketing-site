Demand Generation

Demand generation exists because most B2B programmes only capture the small share of the market already searching. Demand generation builds the share that isn't, so there is something to capture next quarter.

The problem

Why Demand Generation Exists

Demand capture reaches the small slice of your market already searching. Everyone competes for that slice, which is why cost per lead climbs every year while the pipeline stays flat. Demand generation builds the share of the market that will search next quarter.

What we do

What Demand Generation Actually Involves

Demand generation creates demand, it does not only capture it

Where the buyer is not yet looking

Education, point-of-view content, video and executive presence aimed at the problem your product solves, so buyers arrive already understanding why it matters.

Capture the demand that exists

Where intent is already visible

Search, retargeting and review-site presence built to convert the buyers who are actively looking, without pretending that is the whole market.

Route it to the right motion

So demand becomes pipeline

Demand that fits a target account goes into the account programme; the rest goes to self-serve or nurture. Nothing is thrown at sales indiscriminately.

Measure creation separately from capture

Because they behave differently

Capture converts this month and creation compounds over quarters. Reporting them as one number hides which is actually working.

The hard part

Demand Generation Is Judged On A Lag

The work that builds future demand produces nothing you can point at in the month it happens, and that is where most programmes are cancelled.

Capture is satisfying because the numbers arrive the same week. Somebody searched, clicked, enquired, and a report can draw a line between the three. Demand generation has no such week: the people reached today are not in the market today, which is the entire reason they were cheap to reach.

So the measurement has to be agreed before anything starts, and it has to be the slow kind. Branded search volume over a quarter. Direct arrivals that no campaign claims. The share of enquiries that already knew the name before they typed it. None of those move in a fortnight and all of them move for real.

The honest version of this conversation includes a minimum term, because a programme stopped at month three has bought the cost and none of the return. If that term is not affordable, the correct recommendation is to spend the same money on capture and come back to this later — which is a smaller engagement and the right one.

Our methodology

The Demand Engine Behind It

Demand generation runs through six stages in a fixed order, and the order is the whole method: a message tested before the budget is widened, and capture built before attention is bought.

01 · Category

The problem the market would recognise before it recognises you. Named in the buyer’s words rather than the product’s, because a category nobody can state is a category nobody searches for later.

02 · Audience

The people who will have this problem next quarter, defined by situation rather than by job title. Most of them are not looking yet, which is the entire reason this work exists.

03 · Message

One claim, carried consistently across every piece for long enough to be remembered. Programmes fail here more often than anywhere else, because changing the message feels like progress and resets the clock.

04 · Channels

Where that audience already spends attention, bought deliberately rather than spread across everything. A channel earns its place by producing recognition that shows up later in search demand.

05 · Capture

The page, the form and the follow-up that catch somebody at the moment the problem becomes urgent. Built before the spending starts, because demand arriving at nothing is demand given to a competitor.

06 · Measurement

Branded search volume, direct arrivals and pipeline created rather than sourced, read over a quarter. The lagging half is the honest half, and it is the half that gets cut from most reports.

Measurement

What Demand Generation Reporting Should Contain

The numbers

  • Pipeline created and influenced
  • Cost per opportunity, not cost per lead
  • Demand creation vs capture split
  • Branded search volume over time
  • Win rate by source
  • Sales acceptance rate

How we report them

  • Platform-reported values labelled as such
  • The attribution window stated on every view
  • Early-stage data flagged as early-stage
  • No cross-platform summing presented as revenue
  • Nothing published we cannot show you the source for

The house rule does not change with the service.

Straight answers

What Demand Generation Is Not

Before the distinctions below, one practical note. The two halves are usually bought from the same budget and judged by the same report, which is how the slower half ends up being cut every time a quarter tightens. Separating them on the report — different targets, different timescales, different evidence — is the single administrative change that keeps a programme alive long enough to work.

It is not lead generation with a longer name. Lead generation competes for the small share of a market that is already looking; the channels are search, directories and referral, the competition is everybody, and the ceiling is the size of the in-market group.

Demand generation works on the much larger group who have the problem and are not yet looking. The job is to make the problem legible and to be the name attached to it when the looking starts. It is slower, it is harder to attribute, and it is the only way past that ceiling.

It is also not a content calendar. A programme can publish for two years, produce a great deal of material and create no demand at all if nobody saw it or if everything it said was agreeable. The test is not output volume; it is whether more people know what you do and have a view about it.

Why It Cannot Be Attributed Cleanly

Before the mechanics, the sum that makes this argument commercial rather than philosophical. A business capturing in-market demand competently will see cost per lead fall for a while and then flatten. That flattening is not an execution failure; it is the market saying you now reach most of the people currently looking.

What usually happens next is that the budget is increased, because that is the lever everybody knows. Cost per lead rises, because the extra money bids for the same finite group against competitors doing the same thing, and quality falls because the additional reach sits further from the centre of the market. Somebody then concludes the channel has stopped working.

It has not. The pool has stopped growing, and nothing in the capture half can grow it. The tell to watch for is the ratio between branded and unbranded search: if unbranded volume is flat while spend climbs, more spend is a tax rather than an investment.

A search click that becomes an enquiry the same week is easy to attribute, easy to report and easy to defend in a budget meeting. A piece of work that made somebody aware of a problem eleven months before they searched is invisible to every reporting tool in use.

So the half with clean measurement gets the budget and the half without it gets cut first in a difficult quarter. The predictable consequence arrives two years later as a pipeline that has stopped growing while cost per lead climbs, because everybody is bidding for the same unchanged group.

There is no attribution model that fixes this; the ones that claim to mostly reallocate credit among channels that were all late. What works is agreeing in advance that one part of the budget is measured on leading indicators — branded search volume, direct traffic, what arriving customers say when asked how they heard of you — and accepting that those are softer.

Any supplier promising clean attribution for this half is selling the other half with a different label on the invoice.

Demand Generation FAQ

How is this different from lead generation?

Lead generation optimises for the number of enquiries. Demand generation optimises for qualified pipeline and treats creating demand as a separate job from harvesting it. In practice that means spending on education that will not convert this month, which is exactly what most lead-gen budgets cut first.

How long before it works?

The capture half can produce inside weeks. The creation half compounds over two to four quarters and shows up first as branded search and inbound quality before it shows up as pipeline.

How do you measure something that does not fill in a form?

How many people look you up by name, direct traffic to key pages, what customers say on the form about how they found you, sales acceptance rate and win rate by source. None is perfect alone; together they describe the trend honestly.
