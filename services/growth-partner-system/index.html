The Growth Partner System: How We Run Marketing And Sales Together

The full breakdown of the system behind every Growth Partner engagement: what runs, in what order, how it scales across four levels and why each part is there. This is how we work, not a sales page.

One Set Of Research. Every Surface. Four Levels Of Intensity.

SEO and authority

SEO: Where The Whole System Gets Its Direction

SEO is not a channel sitting next to the others. We use Semrush to find the keywords that carry buying intent in your market, and those keywords run through the video scripts, the captions, the content plan, the ad copy, the blogs, the Google profile and the website pages. One set of research, pointed at every surface. It is slow to start, it compounds, and it stays yours even if the ads stop.

One blog, two surfaces

Thirty blogs a month becomes thirty indexed pages and thirty profile updates, from one piece of writing a day. The research is monthly because search behaviour moves: new terms appear, old ones fade and competitors shift. A keyword set from six months ago is a plan for a market that no longer exists.

From Level 2: authority building

Rankings are not only about pages. They are about how many other sites point at yours. Two things are added at this level, and the first one is the asset the second is built on.

BlogsArticles
The jobCollect people searching a keywordDemonstrate authority on a topic
How they are builtOne page, one search, written to rankInformation pulled from as many sources as we can find, compiled into one report
How many30 a month, one a day4 a month, one a week
Why they workVolume: each one catches a different searchDepth: the kind of page other sites want to cite
  1. 1

    Articles give people a reason to link

    Nobody links to a blog post about service pricing. They do link to original, data-fuelled research on a single subject.

  2. 2

    Manual backlink outreach

    We find sites that plausibly link to a business like yours, then ask directly, leading with the article. It goes out from your domain, from a mailbox you provide, because it is your business asking.

  3. 3

    Directories and citations

    A proper listing on every directory that makes sense: industry, local and regional, trade bodies and review platforms. A consistent name, address and phone number in many credible places is one of the strongest local ranking signals.

  4. 4

    No shortcuts

    No bought links, no link farms and no directories that exist only to sell links. A link from a site Google already trusts is worth more than ten from sites it does not.

At Level 3: three new ranking pages a week

On top of the daily blogs we publish three new pages a week built for local rankings: service pages, area pages and the combinations of the two that bring in nearby towns one at a time. Blogs catch people researching. These catch people searching for a service in a specific place, which is the search that converts.

Paid media

Meta Creates The Demand. Google Collects It.

Meta puts your work in front of people who were not looking for you yet, often enough that you are the name they know when the need arrives. Google catches the people searching today, which is the highest-intent traffic available and the fastest to convert. One without the other leaves half the market untouched. Paid media is also the only part of the system that produces in month one, and the only channel where we choose exactly who sees it.

Every ad has to earn its budget

A new ad goes live

On a small daily budget, in prospecting. Nobody has seen it yet.

It works

Results at a cost that makes sense. After a few days it moves to scaling: the budget goes up.

It does not

Nothing, or too expensive. After a few days it is killed and a new ad takes its place.

Nothing gets real money until it has proven it works. We are buying information in prospecting, not results. What produces is promoted, what does not is cut quickly.

Campaign typeWhat it does
ProspectingCold audiences. Where every new ad starts and where most of them end.
ScalingThe proven winners, given real budget and left to run.
RetargetingPeople who visited, watched or engaged and did not convert. Consistently the cheapest results in the account.

Why there is a ceiling on spend. Each level has a most we will run, and it is not because the budget could not go higher. Managing spend properly past that point is a materially different amount of work: more campaigns, more creative, more testing, more daily management. We would rather cap it honestly than take the spend and manage it thinly. Ad spend is always paid to Meta and Google directly. It never sits inside the management fee.

Email and CRM

Selling To People Who Already Know You, And Catching Every Enquiry

Email reaches people who have already bought, enquired or put their hand up, so there is no ad spend between the business and that audience. Most businesses have years of addresses sitting in a system doing nothing. We turn that into revenue, and we run all of it: the writing, the design, the scheduling, the sending and the reporting.

Email marketing (Level 2 and up)Detail
Written, designed and sent by usFour a month at Level 2 and V2, six at Level 3, using the photography and video from the shoot day.
Automated sequencesWelcome, thank-you and follow-up, always live.
List managementSegmentation, deliverability and list health.
Tracked for profitOpens and clicks, what was clicked, and revenue per send. The last number decides whether it stays in the plan.

The CRM, and how it grows with the level

Everything upstream produces enquiries. The CRM is where they either get worked or get lost. It is in your name, every team member is trained, we run it day to day, and there is a 24/7 line rather than a ticket queue.

LevelWhat the CRM does
Level 1GoHighLevel, stood up in your name and run by us: pipeline, setup, a walkthrough for every team member, day-to-day management and a 24/7 support line.
Level 2Adds drip campaigns and custom automation, built to your process rather than a template.
Level 3 and V2AI-native. Calls are recorded, transcribed and summarised, and the contact record organises itself.
It only applies where there is a conversation. A CRM matters when the sale happens through enquiries, quotes or bookings: trades, service businesses, dealerships, training centres and B2B. Where someone can buy straight from the website, there is nothing to manage and we say so rather than sell it.

Account warming (Level 3 and V2)

Knowing How Interested Someone Is Before Anyone Picks Up The Phone

When there is a sales process between interest and purchase, most teams call a list in whatever order it arrived. We set up tracking across the website, the emails and the social platforms so individual people can be recognised across all of it, and every interaction adds to a picture of how interested they are.

ColdAwareWarmingWarmHot

Website behaviour

Who visited, what they looked at, how long they stayed and how many times they came back.

Email engagement

What they opened, what they clicked and how consistently.

Social views

How many times they have seen the content across platforms: the signal almost nobody captures.

Identity matching

Cookies from email clicks and site visits tie the activity to a named person instead of anonymous traffic.

The warming data flows into the CRM and the reporting. It is also what finally makes inbound measurable: instead of knowing a campaign produced traffic, we can see which accounts that traffic belonged to and what happened to them afterwards.

It is worth it whenIt is not when
There is a sales team calling peoplePeople buy directly off the website
Deals take weeks or months to closeThe decision happens in one session
The purchase is considered or high-valueIt is a low-value repeat purchase
You sell to businesses, not consumersIt is mass-market e-commerce
What it changes. A rep calling cold is guessing. A rep calling the account that read the pricing page three times this week already knows what that person has been looking at before saying hello. The warmest accounts get called first, a spike in activity is the moment to reach out, and someone who never engages stops consuming a rep's time.

What account-based marketing actually is

Normal marketing casts wide and waits for the right people to come forward. Account-based marketing works backwards: you decide which specific companies you want, then aim everything at them.

Normal marketingAccount-based marketing
Starts withA marketA list of named companies
The adsReach whoever fits the targetingReach the people at those specific accounts
Success isVolume of leadsWinning the accounts you chose
Best forHigh volume, lower valueFewer deals, each worth a lot
Why it works with account warming. The tracking shows which accounts have been reading, watching and returning, and the sales manager reaches out at the moment that activity spikes, already knowing what the company has been looking at.

Questions

Before You Ask

What is the Growth Partner System?

Our full-service model. One set of keyword research feeds a monthly shoot day, paid media, daily SEO, a rebuilt website, email, a CRM we run and reporting you can see, delivered at four levels.

What changes between the levels?

How much of the system runs and how hard it runs. Levels 1 to 3 build on each other, so moving up adds and never swaps. Version 2 is a separate fork that adds a sales manager instead of more marketing volume.

Which level does a business start at?

It depends on the problem. If nothing is running properly, the first level. If it works but is not reaching enough people, the second. If reach is fine and more output would turn into revenue, the third. If reach is fine and nobody is closing, Version 2.

Do I have to supply the photos and video?

No. We come to you and film. One shoot day covers a month of content, two at the top level.

Do I need a website or a CRM first?

No. We take over the website on day one and rebuild it if it is not good enough, and where the business needs a CRM we set it up in your name and run it.

Who pays for the ad spend?

You do, directly to Meta and Google. It never sits inside the management fee, and each level has a ceiling on how much we will manage.

How do I see what you are doing?

A live dashboard, a written and video report every week, a weekly call, a Monday and Friday text, a group chat with the team and a monthly in-person review.
