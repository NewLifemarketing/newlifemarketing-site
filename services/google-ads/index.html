The problem

Spending On The Wrong Searches

Most accounts are not underspending. They are spending on searches that were never going to become a customer.

Google’s own setup flow
It defaults to broad match, auto-applied recommendations and one campaign covering everything. It is designed to spend the budget, not to spend it well.
An account with no negative keywords
Paying for “jobs”, “salary”, “DIY”, “free” and competitor research clicks for months, because nobody has read the search terms report.
Conversion tracking nobody verified
The account optimises toward a conversion that is not firing correctly, so Google confidently spends the budget chasing the wrong signal.
The agency that reported clicks
Traffic went up, the phone did not ring, and nothing in the report connected the two.

The money is not lost finding them

Somebody is already searching for what you sell today. Finding them is the easy part. The budget is lost on everything else the account also pays for — the job seekers, the students, the competitors, the people looking for a free version.

Which is why the first thing we do on a new account is read what it actually bought last month, not what it reported.

02

The auction

The Highest Bid Does Not Win

The single most useful thing a business owner can understand about Google Ads.

Ad Rank
Your position is bid multiplied by quality, not bid alone. A competitor with a better-matched ad and landing page can outrank you while paying less per click.
Quality Score
Google’s rating of how relevant your keyword, ad and landing page are to each other. Improve it and your cost per click falls without touching the bid.
Why it matters
The cheapest route to a lower cost per lead is usually a better landing page, not a bigger budget — which is a very different lever than paid social gives you.

Why almost nobody explains this

Because it makes the obvious lever — spend more — look like the wrong one. Ad Rank means relevance is compounding: a better page raises quality, quality lowers cost per click, and the same budget buys more clicks.

It is also why we will sometimes tell you the highest-return work this month is on your website, not in your ad account.

Six things owners reasonably believe that are not true

“Whoever bids most wins the top spot”

Position is bid multiplied by quality. A better-matched ad and landing page can outrank a higher bidder while paying less per click.

“Our cost per click is too high, so Google Ads does not work”

Cost per click is not the number that matters — cost per booked job is. A higher click cost with a page that converts is cheaper than a bargain click that bounces.

“Broad match gets us more reach, so it is better”

Broad match without a negative keyword list is the fastest way to spend a budget on searches that were never going to buy.

“We should accept Google’s recommendations”

Auto-applied recommendations optimise toward spend, not toward your margin. Some are genuinely useful; they should be a decision, not a default.

“The homepage is fine as a landing page”

Quality Score is partly landing page relevance, so a page that matches the search lowers your cost per click directly. Sending every campaign to the homepage is the most common unforced error we see.

“More budget will fix it”

If the account is spending on the wrong searches, more budget buys more of the wrong searches. Waste scales faster than results do.

Current as of this month

What Google Changed In 2026

All of these are live right now, and most agency pages have not been updated for any of them.

AI Max for Search
Left beta in April 2026. Layers keywordless matching, automatic text customisation and final URL expansion on top of standard Search campaigns.
Auto-upgrades
Began September 2026. Automatically created assets and campaign-level broad match settings are being folded into AI Max, and the Dynamic Search Ads sunset begins February 2027.
Display is retiring
Standalone Display campaigns are being folded into Demand Gen. Migration opened June 2026; anything not moved manually is auto-migrated through 2027.
Language targeting removed
Campaign-level language targeting was removed from Search and AI Max in late September 2026 — Google now matches language automatically.
Limited Ad Serving
Google will deliberately restrict visibility for advertisers with unclear business identity or poor site quality, regardless of budget.

Our position

Automation is not the enemy. Unsupervised automation is.

The controls Google is removing are exactly the ones that used to protect budget. So the ones that remain — negative keywords, brand exclusions, conversion quality and landing page relevance — now carry more weight, not less.

Campaign type 01

Search — The Intent Channel

The channel where the customer comes to you already knowing what they want.

What it is
Text ads against active searches. The only channel where the customer initiates — they have already decided they want the thing and are choosing who provides it.
When it is right
Almost always, if anyone searches for what you sell. It is the first campaign we build and the last one we would ever cut.
Where it leaks
Broad match with no negatives. Match type is the single biggest lever on waste, and most accounts have never had a negative list built properly.
How we run it
Exact and phrase preferred, broad only where it is earning. A negative list built before launch and extended weekly from the real search terms report. One ad group per tight keyword theme, so the ad can genuinely match the search.
The AI Max question
AI Max expands beyond your keyword list and can send clicks to a page you did not choose. On a site with one generic services page that amplifies the weakness. We enable it only where the landing pages are strong enough to support it — and we say so rather than switching it on by default.

Match types and negatives, in a real account

The difference between a profitable Search campaign and an expensive one is usually not the keywords you chose. It is the searches you refused to pay for.

Exact and phrase give you control. Broad gives Google latitude. Both are useful — but broad without a maintained negative list is just an open tab.

06

Campaign type 02

Performance Max — And Where It Leaks

The most powerful campaign type Google offers, and the easiest one to let flatter itself.

What it is
One campaign running across Search, Shopping, YouTube, Display, Discover, Gmail and Maps, with Google allocating between them.
When it is right
Accounts with real conversion volume and a strong asset library. It needs signal to work with.
Where it leaks
Brand terms. Left unfenced, Performance Max will happily spend on people already searching your name and report those conversions as incremental performance. If you also run a brand Search campaign you are paying twice for the same customer and congratulating yourself in the report.
How we run it
Brand exclusions from day one, with account-level and campaign-level negatives maintained. We use the channel performance timeline added in April 2026 to check whether the cheap channels are claiming conversions the revenue does not reflect — that gap is where budget quietly leaks.
On auto-assets
For an account with a real creative library, Google’s auto-generated assets dilute more than they help. We supply our own and turn them off.

Campaign type 03

Demand Gen — The Mid-Funnel Channel

Where Google borrows from social — and where an agency that cannot film quietly opts out.

What it is
The mid-funnel channel that replaced Discovery — running across YouTube, Shorts, Discover, Gmail and now Display inventory as standalone Display campaigns retire into it.
When it is right
When you need to create demand rather than harvest it, and you have creative worth putting in a feed. This is the closest Google gets to how paid social behaves.
Where it leaks
Weak creative. It is a visual, interruption-based channel, so the same rules that govern social apply — and most Google-only agencies have no way to produce for it.
How we run it
Fed from our own shoot days. This is the clearest overlap between our Google and creative work, and the reason we treat Demand Gen as a real channel rather than an afterthought.

Campaign type 04

Shopping — The Feed Is The Campaign

The one campaign type where the work that matters happens outside the ad account.

What it is
Product listings with image, price and merchant name, served against product searches. Retail and e-commerce only.
The core idea
You do not write ads for Shopping — Google builds them from your product data. So the optimisation work happens in the Merchant Center feed, not in ad copy.
Where it leaks
Incomplete or poorly written product titles and attributes. A feed with thin titles is a campaign that cannot match searches properly, and no amount of bidding fixes it.
How we run it
Merchant Center setup and feed optimisation first — titles, descriptions, attributes, images, pricing accuracy. Then campaign structure and bidding on top of a feed that is actually ready.

Campaign type 05

YouTube & Video

The channel we can actually produce for, and the one we will talk you out of if it is not the right first move.

What it is
Video ads across YouTube — in-stream, in-feed and Shorts.
Why most agencies avoid it
They cannot make the asset. A YouTube campaign needs actual video, and an agency that outsources creative either passes on the channel or runs something weak.
How we run it
We film it. The same production days that feed your social and paid social creative produce the YouTube assets, cut for the placement rather than repurposed sideways.
The honest note
Video works best as a demand-creation and retargeting layer, not as a direct-response first touch for most local businesses. We would rather tell you that than sell a channel that will not pay back.

Campaign type 06

Local Services Ads

Only available to some businesses — and worth checking before you spend anywhere else.

What it is
The Google Guaranteed listings that appear above everything else for eligible service categories — paid per lead rather than per click.
Who qualifies
Eligible trades and service businesses that pass Google’s background and licence verification.
Why it matters
It sits above the map pack and the regular ads. For a qualifying trade it is frequently the highest-intent placement available on the entire page.
What the work involves
Verification and profile setup, then ongoing lead management and dispute handling — because you can and should dispute leads that do not qualify.

Scoping, not selling

You Probably Do Not Need All Six

Which of the six your business actually needs, and in what order.

A local service business or trade
Search first. Local Services Ads if you qualify. Demand Gen later, once there is creative to run.
E-commerce or retail
Shopping and Search together, with Performance Max once there is conversion volume to feed it.
B2B with a long sales cycle
Search on high-intent terms only, plus YouTube and Demand Gen for the long middle where nobody is searching yet.
Brand new, no conversion history
Search alone until the account has data. Performance Max before there is signal is an expensive way to gather it.

The rule we hold to: a campaign type earns its place by being the best answer to a specific problem — not because it exists, and not because a report looks fuller with it in there.

How we run an account

The Five Controls

Five things decide whether an account works, and they are not the ones most people adjust.

01 · Conversion tracking
Installed, tested and verified firing correctly before a dollar is spent — call tracking, form tracking and offline conversion import where the sale happens off-site. An account optimising toward a broken conversion spends confidently in the wrong direction.
02 · Keywords & negatives
Match type discipline, one tight theme per ad group, and a negative list built before launch then extended every week from the real search terms report.
03 · Bidding strategy
Manual or maximise clicks while gathering data, then a conversion-based strategy once there is enough signal to support it. Switching too early is one of the most common causes of a stalled account.
04 · Landing pages
Where the cheapest wins live. Quality Score means a better-matched page lowers your cost per click directly — and with AI Max expanding URLs, page relevance matters more than it ever has.
05 · Budget allocation
Moved toward what converts, at the level the decision actually happens — by campaign, by device, by location, by time of day.

In the order we set them up

These five decide whether an account works. Not the keyword list, not the budget, and not which bidding strategy has the best name.

Four of the five are unglamorous and none of them appear in a platform report as a line item — which is exactly why they are the ones most often skipped.

Qualify yourself out

Who It Is For — And Who It Is Not

Naming who should not buy is what makes the rest of this page worth reading.

Google Ads fit when

  • People already search for what you sell — Search is demand capture. If nobody is looking, this is the wrong channel and we will say so.
  • A new customer is worth real money — Click costs are what they are. The maths only works when the customer value clears them.
  • You can answer the phone — High-intent leads go cold fastest. Speed to lead decides half the outcome.
  • You will let us touch the landing page — It is usually the cheapest win available, and we cannot make it from inside the ad account.

It is the wrong call when

  • Nobody is searching for it yet — A genuinely new category has no demand to capture. Demand Gen, YouTube or paid social create it instead.
  • The margin cannot absorb the click cost — In some categories a click is expensive enough that the maths never closes. We would rather run that calculation on the call.
  • Conversion tracking cannot be installed — Without it we are guessing, and you are paying for the guess.
  • Follow-up is broken — More leads into a broken process produces more wasted leads. Fix the CRM first.

The process

The First 90 Days

What actually happens, and what it is reasonable to expect at each stage.

Week 1
Account audit, conversion tracking built and verified, keyword and competitor research, negative list built, campaign structure mapped. Nothing launches until tracking is confirmed.
Weeks 2–4
Search live first. Daily search terms review, negatives added continuously, bids adjusted as data arrives. Search can produce leads in week one — and often does.
Month 2
Structure expands into the additional campaign types the mix calls for. Bidding moves to conversion-based once there is enough signal.
Month 3
Enough conversion data to steer properly — budget reallocated toward what is producing, landing pages improved against real search terms, wasted spend eliminated rather than estimated.

The honest timeline

Search can produce inside week one. Performance Max and Shopping need two to four weeks of learning before their numbers mean anything, and real optimisation lands around 60 to 90 days.

We would rather tell you that up front than have you judge the account in week two on data that cannot support a decision.

Deep dive

The Search Terms Report

One report decides whether an account wastes money. This is how we use it.

What it shows
Every actual search that triggered your ads and what each one cost — as opposed to the keywords you chose, which is a different and much more flattering list.
How often we read it
Weekly, and daily in the first month. It is the single highest-value half hour in account management.
What becomes a negative
Job seekers, students, DIY, “free” and “cheap” modifiers, competitor research, and anything adjacent enough to trigger but not to buy.
What gets promoted
A search converting well inside a broad ad group gets its own keyword, its own ad and a page that matches it.
Why it matters most now
As Google removes manual controls, the negative list is one of the few levers left that still protects budget directly.

Measurement

What We Report — And What We Refuse To

The half of reporting most agencies leave out, which is the half that tells you whether to trust the rest.

What we report

  • Cost per conversion, with the conversion defined
  • Booked jobs or sales where the CRM can trace them
  • Search terms added as negatives, and what that saved
  • Wasted spend eliminated, not estimated
  • The platform named and the attribution window stated

What we refuse to report as an outcome

  • Clicks and impressions — an account can produce more of both while producing fewer customers
  • Conversions without a definition — a “conversion” counting a page view is not a lead
  • Platform value presented as profit — Google’s reported figure divided by spend is not audited revenue, and is never summed with Meta’s as though they were the same number
  • Impression share as achievement — a useful diagnostic and a meaningless headline. We target 90–99% on brand search and deliberately never 100%, because the last few points cost more than they return

Straight answers

Google Ads FAQ

The real questions owners ask before spending on Google — answered honestly, including the budget one.

How much should I spend on Google Ads to start?

Enough to buy meaningful data in your category, which depends on what a click costs and what a customer is worth to you. In a competitive trade a few hundred dollars a month will not clear the learning period; in a quieter niche it might. We run that arithmetic on the call against your average job value rather than quoting a number that sounds reasonable and is not.

Why is my cost per click so much higher than I expected?

Usually Quality Score. Your position is bid multiplied by quality, so a poorly matched ad or a landing page that does not answer the search raises what you pay for the same position. The other common cause is match type — broad match with no negative list buys a lot of expensive, irrelevant clicks. Both are fixable, and neither is fixed by bidding more.

Should I let Google auto-apply its recommendations?

Not by default. Some are genuinely useful and some quietly widen your targeting or raise budgets. They optimise toward spend, not toward your margin. We review them and apply the ones that make sense — which is a decision rather than a setting left switched on.

What is Performance Max actually doing with my money?

Running across Search, Shopping, YouTube, Display, Discover, Gmail and Maps, with Google deciding the split. The honest risk is brand terms: unfenced, it spends on people already searching your name and reports those as new performance. We apply brand exclusions from day one and read the channel performance timeline to check whether the cheap placements are claiming conversions the revenue does not reflect.

Do I need a new landing page, or will my homepage work?

The homepage will work, badly. It is built to serve everyone, so it matches no specific search particularly well — which lowers Quality Score, raises your cost per click and converts worse once the visitor arrives. A page that matches the search is usually the cheapest improvement available in the whole account, and it helps your organic rankings at the same time. We build or recommend the page and wire it into your CRM so no lead is lost.

Should I bid on my own brand name?

Usually yes, and it is a fair question because it feels like paying for traffic you would have had anyway. Brand clicks are cheap, they let you control the message above the fold, and if a competitor is bidding on your name, not showing up is a decision too. We target 90 to 99% impression share on brand and deliberately never 100%, because the last few points cost more than they return.

What happens to my account when Display campaigns retire?

Standalone Display is being folded into Demand Gen — migration opened in June 2026, and anything not moved manually gets auto-migrated through 2027. Moving deliberately means you control the audiences, creative and exclusions that carry across. Being auto-migrated means Google decides. If you have Display running, that migration should be on someone's list now.

Google Ads or SEO — which should I do first?

If you need customers inside 60 days, Google Ads. It captures demand that already exists and Search can produce leads in week one. SEO compounds over quarters and keeps paying after you stop spending, which is the better long-term asset but the wrong answer to an urgent problem. Most of our clients run ads first and add SEO once the pipeline is steady.

Can I run Google Ads and Meta Ads at the same time?

Yes, and they do different jobs. Google captures people already searching; Meta creates demand among people who did not know you existed. If you sell to other businesses, LinkedIn is the third leg — it reaches a named buying committee rather than an audience. The one rule we hold to is that we never add the platforms' reported conversion values together and present the total as revenue — they count differently and they overlap.

What does AI Max change for my account?

It layers keywordless matching, automatic text customisation and URL expansion on top of standard Search. The upside is reach your keyword list would never have found. The risk is that Google also picks the landing page, so a site with one catch-all services page gets that weakness amplified rather than hidden. We turn it on campaign by campaign, only where the pages behind it can carry the traffic, and we tell you which ones it is running on.

Who owns the ad account?

You do. It stays in your business name with us added to it, along with the conversion tracking, the keyword and negative lists and the campaign structure. If we stop working together, the account and everything built in it stay with you.

How is this different from just boosting posts or running it myself?

Boosting optimizes for likes; a managed account optimizes for booked jobs and sales, with conversion tracking, negative keywords and weekly search-term mining behind it. The platform is easy to spend money on and hard to spend it well — that gap is the whole job.

What are Local Services Ads, and are they worth it for my trade?

LSAs are the pay-per-lead “Google Guaranteed” units above the regular results for local trades — you pay per lead rather than per click, and ranking is driven heavily by your review profile. They are only open to certain categories and require passing Google’s background and licence verification. On the call we’ll tell you whether your category is eligible and whether the lead price makes sense next to Search, rather than assuming it does.

What do I get in reporting?

A monthly report in plain English: what you spent, how many leads or sales it produced, and your cost per result — plus what we changed and what’s next. You’ll never need a data-science degree to read it.
