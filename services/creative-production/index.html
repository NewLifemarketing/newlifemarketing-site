01

The problem

Your Footage Is Selling You Short

Most businesses are better than their footage makes them look, and it is costing them more than they think.

Phone footage
Fine for a story, wrong for an ad. Flat, badly lit, unusable audio — and it quietly tells people your business is smaller than it is.
Stock video
The visual equivalent of a form letter. Nobody believes it, everybody recognises it, and it cannot show your actual work.
The one shoot, two years ago
A single expensive production day used everywhere until it was exhausted. The team is in different clothes now and the product has changed.
The freelancer who vanished
A good videographer who booked out, moved on or raised rates — and the footage stopped with them.

This is a media-buying problem, not a branding one

Creative is now the single biggest lever on ad performance, because with modern targeting the creative is what decides who the platform shows it to. Weak footage does not just look worse — it reaches fewer people, and it costs more to reach them.

Which is why this page sits underneath the Meta and Google pages rather than beside them.

The kit

Owned, Not Rented

Six categories, all on our own shelves. The reason that matters to you is scheduling and cost, not bragging rights.

We are not a marketing agency that books a freelancer. The cameras, the lenses, the lighting, the audio and the drone are ours, and the people operating them are on our team. That is the whole reason we can shoot your business every month instead of once a year.

The practical effect is scheduling. We can shoot you next week because the kit is on our shelf rather than on a rental calendar, and we can shoot you again next month without rebuilding a quote — which is what every other service we run depends on.

The glass

Cameras & Lenses

What we run, and the reason for each — because a spec list without a reason is just a receipt.

10-bit cinema bodies
The reason is colour. 10-bit holds far more tonal information than standard 8-bit, which is what lets footage survive a real grade without falling apart.
Fast prime lenses
The shallow depth of field and low-light performance that reads as cinematic. This is the single biggest visual difference between professional and prosumer footage.
Zoom lenses
For run-and-gun coverage where the moment will not wait for a lens change — events, walkthroughs, live process.
Multiple bodies
So interviews run multi-angle and nothing is lost to a single point of view.
05

Lighting

Everyone Sees It, Nobody Can Name It

The difference everybody can see and almost nobody can describe.

Key, fill and back
The structure behind every interview and talking head that looks professional rather than merely filmed.
Modifiers
Softboxes, diffusion and flags. Hard light is what makes footage look like security-camera footage; softening it is most of the job.
Colour temperature control
Matching the space, or deliberately overriding it, instead of letting mixed lighting turn skin tones green.
Practical and location lighting
Warehouses, kitchens, workshops and sites — where our clients actually operate, rather than a studio that looks like nowhere.

Two identical cameras in the same room produce completely different results depending on who lit it. Lighting is the most under-appreciated and most decisive part of the kit, and it is the thing viewers register instantly without ever being able to name.

06

Audio

Bad Audio Makes Good Video Look Cheap

The cheapest way to make expensive footage look amateur.

Wireless lavalier systems
The clean, close dialogue that makes an interview usable at all.
Shotgun and boom
For natural room sound, and for anything where a visible mic would break the shot.
Dedicated recorders and monitoring
Audio is recorded properly and checked on headphones during the take — not discovered in the edit when everyone has gone home.
Room treatment
Most real workplaces are reflective, echoing boxes. That has to be handled on site, because it cannot be fixed afterwards.

Viewers forgive imperfect visuals and abandon bad sound within seconds. On muted-autoplay platforms it matters even more than that: poor audio kills the one moment somebody chooses to unmute.

Motion

Movement & Aerial

Two kit categories, and one judgement call that separates a crew from a camera operator.

Gimbals and sliders
Smooth motion where motion is motivated — a walk through a space, a reveal, a process in progress.
The experienced call
Knowing when not to move the camera. Unmotivated movement reads as amateur, and a locked-off shot is very often the stronger one.
Aerial
Drone work for scale and context — sites, properties, builds and landscapes. The shot that shows the size of a job in one frame.
On regulation
Commercial drone operation is regulated in Canada, and construction and industrial clients are right to ask. We will confirm exactly what applies to your site before we fly anything.

The shoot day

What Actually Happens When We Turn Up

Demystifying the day removes the main objection to booking one.

The crew arrives ahead of anyone being on camera. Lighting and audio are built and tested before a single person is asked to perform.

The planned shot list — interviews, pieces to camera, the scripted material. The part that needs your people.

Most of a shoot day does not require your team at all. We schedule the people-facing parts tightly and shoot everything else around your operation — which is usually the real objection underneath “we are too busy for this.”

The philosophy

The Shot You Cannot Plan

The honest centre of this page, and the reason the planned material is only half the job.

A shot list gets you the footage you knew you needed. The material that actually makes people stop — the genuine reaction, the moment of concentration, the unscripted exchange between two people who work together — cannot be scheduled. It can only be caught, and catching it requires a crew that is set up, watching, and already rolling.

We stay rolling between setups
The most usable material frequently happens in the minute after somebody thinks the take has ended and their shoulders drop.
We shoot the process, not just the result
A finished job is a photograph. The work becoming the finished job is a story.
Real people over performances
We would rather spend ten minutes making somebody comfortable than get a technically perfect take of someone visibly reciting.
Being early is the whole technique
Pre-lighting before anyone is on camera exists precisely so that when a real moment happens we are ready, instead of adjusting a light.

This is what the service is actually about

Everything above this chapter — the kit, the lighting, the pre-light hour — exists to buy the one thing you cannot buy directly: being ready when something real happens.

Five stages, and the one people underestimate is the first.

Edit
Structure, pacing and story. The decision that matters most and the one that takes longest.
Colour grade
Where 10-bit footage pays off — consistent skin tones, a deliberate look, and shots matched across setups so a sequence feels like one film rather than four clips.
Sound
Dialogue cleaned and levelled, music selected and licensed, effects placed. The layer nobody notices unless it is absent.

Titles, lower thirds, captions and brand elements — designed in-house rather than dropped in from a template pack.

Every asset cut for where it is going: 16:9, 9:16, 1:1, long, short, captioned, silent-safe.

Captions as standard

A large share of social video is watched muted. Burned-in captions are not an accessibility afterthought here, they are a performance requirement — so they ship on everything rather than on request.

Qualify yourself out

Who It Is For — And Who Does Not Need Us

Naming who should not book a production day is what makes the rest of this page worth reading.

This fits when

  • Your work is visual — You build, make, serve, treat, transform or sell something somebody can see happening.
  • Creative is the bottleneck — You are already running ads and the thing holding them back is what is in them.
  • You need volume, not one film — A month of content rather than a single hero asset.
  • You want the same crew over time — People who know where the good light is in your building and who on your team is comfortable on camera.

It is the wrong call when

  • There is genuinely nothing to film — And nobody to film. It happens, and we will say so.
  • You need one video, once — Hire a freelancer — honestly. This is built around repetition and you will not get the benefit.
  • Nobody will go on camera — Under any circumstances. There are formats that work around it, but not many, and not as well.
  • The budget supports one day and nothing behind it — A production day with no distribution behind it is an expensive asset with nowhere to go.

Straight answers

Creative Production FAQ

The questions we get before a shoot — what we make, the gear, ownership, timelines and cost — answered straight.

How much does a production day cost?

It depends on the day — how many setups, how many locations, whether there is scripting and how much post is attached. What we will not do is quote a number on a page that turns out to mean something else on the call. The useful framing is that a day priced against one video looks expensive and a day priced against a month of content does not, so we scope it against what the footage has to feed.

How long does a shoot take, and how disruptive is it?

Most shoots are one day on site. The honest answer on disruption is that most of that day does not need your team at all — the crew arrives early to build lighting and audio before anyone is on camera, and the parts that need people are scheduled tightly. We shoot the b-roll, the space and the process around your operation rather than through it.

Do we have to be on camera?

Somebody usually should, because a real person is the single most effective thing in the frame. But it does not have to be everybody, and it does not have to be the owner. We can build a shoot around process, product and hands if nobody is comfortable on camera — it is a weaker version, and we will tell you that rather than pretend otherwise.

How far will you travel?

Further than most regional crews. We are based in Barrie and shoot across Ontario as standard, and we have filmed client productions in Aruba, Thunder Bay, Tampa and Washington DC. If the work is somewhere else, ask — travel is a line item, not an obstacle.

Who owns the footage — raw and edited?

You do, both. The finished assets and the raw footage are yours, archived and handed over on request, during the engagement and after it. We state that plainly because plenty of agencies hold assets as leverage, and you should know which kind you are hiring before you book.

How many videos do we get from one day?

More than most people expect, because the day is planned for volume rather than for one hero asset. A typical day produces a long-form piece, a set of vertical cuts, ad variants in more than one orientation, and a stills library — all from the same setups. The exact count depends on how many setups and locations the day covers, and we agree that in pre-production rather than afterwards.

How long until we see the finished work?

First cuts come back for review, then one structured revision round, then final assets in every format needed. The edit is the longest part and the one worth not rushing — structure and pacing are what decide whether anyone finishes watching. We agree the delivery dates in the brief so they are fixed before the camera comes out.

Can you shoot vertical and horizontal in the same day?

Yes, and it is planned into the shot list from the start rather than solved in the edit. Vertical work is framed natively for 9:16 instead of cropped down from a horizontal master — the difference is visible instantly, and it is one of the clearest tells of a crew that understands where the footage is actually going to be watched.

Do you write the scripts?

Where a script is needed, yes — and because the ad strategy and the film are the same conversation here, the script is written against the campaign rather than in isolation. A lot of our best material is not scripted at all: interview prompts and a shot list produce more usable footage than a page somebody is visibly reciting.

What if we don't like the first cut?

That is what the review round is for, and it is built into the process rather than charged as an extra. If the first cut is wrong, it is almost always because the brief was wrong — so we would rather spend the time up front on what the footage is for than rebuild an edit twice.

Can you film a podcast or live-stream an event?

Podcasts, yes — multi-camera with individual audio channels per speaker, delivered as the long-form video, an audio version and a set of vertical clips. For live streaming, tell us what the event is and we will confirm what we can run for it before you plan around it.

Do you do photography as well, or just video?
