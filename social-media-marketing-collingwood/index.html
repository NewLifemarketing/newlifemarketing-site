Social Media Marketing Collingwood

Three Audiences, One Account

Social Media Marketing Collingwood fails in a specific and avoidable way: a single account tries to speak to the people who live here, the people who visit, and the people who own property here and are somewhere else, and ends up saying something general enough to interest none of them.

The three audiences

Social Media Marketing Collingwood: Three Groups Who Want Different Things

They overlap on a map and almost nowhere else.

Observed across Collingwood client work

Residents want reliability, availability and price. They are deciding between you and the other people who do this locally, and they will remember how it went for a long time.

Visitors want certainty within a short window. Are you open, can they get in, is it worth the trip. They are deciding days rather than months ahead and they are frequently not here when they decide.

Owners who live elsewhere want somebody trustworthy to act without supervision. Access, reporting and what happens when something is found matter more to them than price, and none of it interests the other two groups at all.

Those are three different messages and they are cheap to separate, because paid distribution can target them separately even when one account posts for all three.

What makes the separation worth insisting on is that the three convert at completely different rates and at completely different costs. A single blended figure averages them into something that cannot be acted on, and the usual result is that the cheapest audience to reach gets credited with the performance of the most valuable one.

How to split it

One Account, Three Audiences, Three Budgets

The posts can be shared. The money should not be.

Residents

Reached cheaply and continuously, with the work itself as the material. This is where reviews and recommendations compound, and where a borrowed voice is most obviously detectable.

Visitors

Reached before they arrive rather than while they are here, which means targeting where they live and timing it to when plans get made rather than when people appear.

Absent owners

The smallest audience and frequently the most valuable per customer. Reachable by location targeting on where they actually live, with material about reliability rather than about the view.

Split that way, each budget has a test of its own and a bad month in one does not hide a good month in another, which is what a single blended spend reliably does.

What it runs on

Everybody Films The View

In a town that photographs well, the scenery is the least distinguishing material available.

Every business here has access to the same water, the same escarpment and the same sunset, and most of them post it. A visitor scrolling past sees a general impression of somewhere nice and no reason to choose anybody.

What almost nobody posts is the work: the job going in, the room being finished, the person who answers the phone. That material is unglamorous and it is the only thing a competitor cannot replicate.

It also travels better to the two audiences that are not here. Somebody deciding from two hours away is looking for evidence of competence rather than for a view they have already seen.

One planned shooting day produces months of material, and a phone with a clip microphone covers the gap between visits. A posting retainer with no production budget attached is an arrangement for distributing material that does not exist.

There is a second reason real material matters more here than it would elsewhere. Growth means a rising share of the people seeing your account have no history with the town and no way to tell an established business from a new one. Photographs of your own premises, your own vehicles and your own finished work settle that question in a second, and no amount of well-designed graphics does.

The three products

Posting, Strategy And Distribution Are Separate

Quotes rarely say which is included, and the difference decides what the money buys.

Posting

Somebody makes and schedules content. The most visible line on an invoice and the one that produces least on its own, because it reaches the people who already follow you.

Strategy

Deciding what is worth saying, to which of the three audiences, and when. Cheap, occasional, and what makes the other two worth anything.

Distribution

Money spent reaching people who do not follow you, split by audience. This is where enquiries come from and the line that quietly shrinks when a blended monthly fee is squeezed.

Ask for the three priced separately before comparing anything. A supplier who cannot split them has not decided which of your three audiences the work is for, and the split also makes the arrangement reversible: you can stop paying for posting without stopping the part that reaches anybody new.

Measuring it

What A Social Media Marketing Collingwood Report Should Contain

Two numbers that can be acted on, and several that cannot.

Engagement rate is a percentage of an audience the platform chose. Reach is purchasable, so a reach figure with no spend beside it says nothing. Followers are the least predictive number on the page.

What works is cost per enquiry by audience, which is only possible if the three were separated in the first place, plus the question at the counter: how did you hear of us, and do you live here.

That second question is the one that cannot be bought from a platform and is worth more than anything that can. Kept year on year it also tracks how the mix of the three audiences is shifting, which in a fast-growing town is the most useful trend a business can own.

Read all of it over a rolling quarter. At these volumes one month cannot distinguish a good month from a bad one, and creative fatigue in a town of 24,811 arrives in weeks rather than months.

How we structure social media marketing collingwood

We split social media marketing collingwood by audience rather than by platform, with a separate budget and a separate test for residents, visitors and owners who live elsewhere. Blended into one line, a good result in one of the three reliably hides a poor one in another.

We do not sell daily posting retainers, community management or inbox monitoring. We are in Minesing, about an hour away, which keeps filming travel modest and we price it as its own line regardless.

Why separate the audiences if one account posts for all of them?

Because the money should be split even when the posts are shared. Residents, visitors and absent property owners want different things, and a blended budget means a good result in one hides a poor one in another. Split, each has its own test and its own cost per enquiry.

How do we reach people who own property here but live elsewhere?

By targeting where they actually live rather than where the property is, with material about reliability rather than about the view. They are the smallest audience and frequently the most valuable per customer, and almost nobody addresses them directly.

Should we post the scenery?

Sparingly. Every business here has the same water, escarpment and sunset, and a visitor scrolling past sees somewhere nice and no reason to choose anybody. The work itself — the job going in, the finished room, the person who answers the phone — is the only material a competitor cannot replicate.

What should a quote separate?

Posting, strategy and distribution, and then distribution again by audience. Distribution is where enquiries come from and it is the line that quietly shrinks when a blended monthly fee is squeezed. A supplier who cannot split them has not decided which audience the work is for.
