Video Production Ottawa: The Brief Is Not The Approval

A deep market, and a process that kills more films than any creative disagreement.

63,400+

Invest Ottawa, 2025

In most markets a film is approved by the person who asked for it. Here it is reviewed by communications, by somebody responsible for accuracy, sometimes by legal, and frequently by a senior person seeing it for the first time in its final form.

Every one of those people can ask for a change, and a change requested after the edit is locked costs real money or gets refused, which is how a project ends badly for both sides.

The fix is procedural rather than creative and it is the single most useful thing a supplier can insist on: name every approver before filming, and get them to approve the script rather than the film.

A script approval takes an afternoon. The same changes requested at the edit take a week and an invoice, and the version that ships is usually worse than either.

What Corporate Video Production Ottawa Buyers Should Specify

Who approves, and when

Named people, approving the script before the shoot. Corporate video production goes over budget at the approval stage far more often than at the filming one.

How long it has to last

A film naming a programme, a figure or a job title ages the moment any of them changes. Say what has to be evergreen and what can be dated.

Where it will be shown

A room with a projector, a phone, a trade stand and an internal portal need different versions. Deciding afterwards means re-editing afterwards.

What happens to the rushes

The raw footage is the asset, and for an organisation that will reorganise within two years it is the only part that survives the reorganisation.

Those four belong in the quote rather than in a conversation. A corporate video production proposal that names all four is comparable with another one that does; a proposal naming a day rate is not comparable with anything.

Video Marketing Ottawa Teams Can Actually Use

Video marketing in Ottawa fails most often not because the film is bad but because it is finished and then has nowhere to go. One three-minute piece is delivered, posted once, and never appears again.

The useful version is decided before the shoot. The same day that produces the main film should produce the short answers to questions you are asked weekly, the cutaways, the stills, and a version short enough to sit at the top of a page.

Those are the pieces that keep working: embedded on the page a prospect lands on, attached to a proposal, sent in reply to a specific question. The reply is the use nobody budgets for and frequently the strongest.

So the brief should name the destinations, not just the film. A day planned against four destinations costs almost the same as a day planned against one.

What a day should produce

Plan The List, Not The Film

Once a crew is set up, another piece costs minutes. Setting up and getting approved is what costs.

The main film, cut to the length the approval chain agreed. Short answers to the questions you are genuinely asked, each ninety seconds, each useful for years. Pieces to camera from the people who do the work rather than only from the senior person.

Then the unglamorous bank: cutaways, room tone, and a set of photographs. A crew produces stills almost incidentally and those run a website, a report and a presentation template for years, so they belong in the deliverables rather than being discovered to be missing.

Deciding all of that in advance is what separates a day that produces fifteen usable pieces from a day that produces one. A crew improvising on a site it has never seen makes a good film and nothing else.

And in a market with an approval chain, the list also has to be approved. A piece filmed that nobody authorised is a piece that cannot be published.

Between the shoots

A Phone Covers More Than People Expect

Not everything needs a crew, and a supplier who says so is telling you where the value is.

A phone with a clip microphone handles a progress update, a reply to a specific question, and anything that happened unexpectedly. For internal audiences it is frequently better than a produced film, because it reads as current rather than as a campaign.

What it cannot do is light a room, record clean audio in a noisy one, or produce the piece that sits at the top of a page and has to look deliberate. Those need planning, equipment and a day.

So the honest division is: the crew captures what needs equipment and a schedule; somebody internal captures what needs only presence. Two things make the second half work — a microphone, and a habit of recording the answer the first time somebody asks rather than the fifth.

Keep those recordings somewhere findable, named for the question they answer. Within a year that folder is frequently the most useful asset the communications function owns.

In writing

What To Put In Writing Before A Video Production Ottawa Crew Arrives

Five things, agreed before the day rather than discovered on it, and all five are free to agree.

Who is on camera, and have they agreed. Not "somebody from the team". Named people who know the date, know roughly what they will be asked, and have cleared it with whoever needs to clear it. A day spent finding a volunteer is a day lost.What may appear in the background. Screens, whiteboards, name badges, client paperwork, anything on a wall. In a regulated or security-conscious workplace this is the thing that causes a reshoot, and a ten-minute walk-through beforehand prevents it.Who approves the cut, and in how many rounds. One named person with the authority to overrule a colleague, and two rounds. An approval chain of five people is not an approval process, it is a delay with a schedule attached.Where it will be published. This decides the shapes and lengths before anything is filmed, and a film cut for one place and then forced into another always looks like exactly that.What happens to the raw footage. Who holds it, for how long, and what it costs to have something recut from it in a year. Agreeing that afterwards is agreeing it from a weak position.

The shoot, which is days you can count, from the edit, which is where most of the hours actually go, from versioning, which is what decides whether the material gets used anywhere. Video Production Ottawa FAQ

Straight answers before you ever book a call.

Why do these projects go over budget?

Almost always at approval rather than filming. The person who commissions a film is rarely the one who approves it, and a change requested after the edit is locked costs a week and an invoice. Name every approver before the shoot and get them to approve the script; the same changes then cost an afternoon.

What should a corporate quote specify?

Who approves and when, how long the film has to stay accurate, where it will be shown, and what happens to the raw footage. Those four belong in the quote rather than in a conversation. A proposal naming a day rate is not comparable with anything.

We have a film nobody uses. What went wrong?

It was planned as one piece rather than against destinations. The same day should produce the main film, short answers to questions you are asked weekly, cutaways, stills and a version short enough for the top of a page. A day planned against four destinations costs almost the same as a day planned against one.

Do we need a crew for everything?

No, and a supplier who says otherwise is protecting a day rate. A phone with a clip microphone handles progress updates, replies to specific questions and anything unexpected, and for internal audiences it frequently works better. A crew is for lighting, clean audio and the deliberate piece at the top of a page.
