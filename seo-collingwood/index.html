A moving contest

The Competing Set Is Not Fixed Here

Collingwood has 24,811 residents and a visitor economy that swings hard twice a year. The businesses competing for a search in January are not all there in July.

Collingwood seasonal operators open and close. Listings go dormant and come back. Businesses that serve visitors push hard for six months and stop. A search result is a snapshot of who is currently trying, and in this town who is currently trying changes twice a year.

Everywhere else in Ontario a monthly position chart is a reasonable way to watch progress. Here it is averaging two different contests and presenting the mean as a trend. A number that improves from February to April may be measuring departures rather than your own work.

That cuts both ways, which is the useful part. It means the seo collingwood position that is hard to hold in August may be genuinely easy in November, and a business that only needs to be found in one of those seasons is paying for both.

What the chart hides

Two Contests, One Line On A Graph

The same Collingwood ranking, read two ways. One of them is what happened; the other is what gets reported.

The Collingwood fix is not complicated and almost nobody does it: compare each month against the same month last year rather than against last month. In a seasonal market a year-on-year comparison is the only one that holds the contest constant.

It also takes a Collingwood year to produce, which is why it is rarely offered. A chart that can only be drawn in month thirteen is a poor thing to sell in month one.

Questions

Search In Collingwood: Common Questions

Why does seasonality change the reporting?

Because the Collingwood businesses you are ranked against are not the same all year. Seasonal operators open and close, listings go dormant and return, and visitor-facing businesses push hard for six months and stop. A month-on-month position chart in that market is averaging two different contests and presenting the mean as a trend — a number improving from February to April may be measuring departures rather than your own work.

What should we measure instead?

Each month against the same month last year. In a seasonal Collingwood market that is the only comparison that holds the field constant. The catch is that it takes thirteen months to produce, which is why it is rarely offered — a chart you cannot draw until month thirteen is a poor thing to sell in month one. Until then the honest position is that the trend is not yet readable.

When should the work actually start?

Count backwards from the Collingwood season you need. A page that has to be ranking in July is built in February, because three to six months is how long a new page takes to settle. Starting in May for a summer season is the most common scheduling mistake in a resort market, and it is a scheduling mistake rather than a quality one — the same work started in February would have landed.

There are only ninety searches. Is it worth doing?

For a Collingwood business the profile is, definitely. Ninety searches still produce the same three map slots as nine hundred would, so in a small market the map is a far larger share of the whole opportunity than it is in a city. The website work depends on whether the real demand — which here is in the job people need doing, not the name of the service — turns out to be there. That is one research pass, and it should happen before any writing.

Semrush returned no secondary phrases. What does that mean?

That nobody in Collingwood types the service name in more than one way, which is a finding rather than a dead end. It tells you the head term is the whole of that channel and the ceiling is ninety a month. It does not tell you there is no demand — people in a town this size search the problem they have rather than the trade that fixes it. The temptation is to invent a long tail and write thirty pages against it, which is the doorway pattern. Research it or build nothing.
