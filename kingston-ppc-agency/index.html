What the money is for

Buy Answers, Not Reach

Where a page can rank without a budget, money is best spent answering questions quickly that search work would answer slowly. Does this offer land. Does this page convert. Which of the labels do people who actually enquire use. Is this service worth a page of its own.

Each of those is settled in a fortnight for a modest sum, and each answer then directs months of unpaid work that would otherwise have been guesswork.

Used as a permanent substitute for the pages, a kingston ppc agency account is an expensive rental in a market you could have bought outright. At these volumes that is not a close call.

The corollary is uncomfortable for an agency to write: once the pages rank, the right recommendation is usually to reduce the account rather than grow it, and a supplier paid as a percentage of spend will find that hard to say.

Account design

Four Decisions That Do Most Of The Work

In a small account these matter and bids barely do.

Match type, tight

In a sixty-search cluster, broad match finds adjacency rather than volume. The search-terms report shows within a month how much of the budget went somewhere unrelated, and in an account this size that can be most of it.

Geography drawn to the catchment

The city is 132,485 and the metropolitan area 172,546. A radius around the downtown excludes a real share of the people who could buy. Draw where you actually go and bid down outside it.

One page per campaign

A specific search sent to a general page converts badly at any price. In a market where the pages are being written anyway for search, this costs almost nothing extra.

A schedule and a season

For a large share of businesses here the year is decided in a small number of weeks. A flat budget spends most of itself when nobody is buying.

The underspending trap

Why A Kingston PPC Agency Budget Often Goes Unspent

And why widening the targeting to fix it is the most destructive move available.

A correctly targeted account in a sixty-search market will not spend a large budget, because there are not enough relevant searches to buy. That shows up in a monthly report as a campaign that is not working, and somebody reasonably proposes widening the match types to use the money.

Widening converts a precise, cheap, high-converting campaign into a broad, expensive, irrelevant one. The budget gets spent, the clicks arrive, and the enquiries do not. It happens most months somewhere, because an unspent budget looks like a failure to whoever reads the report.

The better response is to accept the ceiling. Let the campaign spend what the market contains, and put the remainder into the pages that capture the same demand without a click charge.

An agency paid as a percentage of spend will find that recommendation hard to make, which is worth knowing when you read one that does not make it.

Measurement

Cost Per Enquiry, On A Rolling Quarter

Two numbers, and the reporting period matters as much as the numbers.

Cost per click is an input. Conversion rate is a property of the page. Cost per enquiry is the first figure that means anything, and even it is half a question, because the platform does not know what a customer is worth to you.

So before quoting we ask what a customer is worth and what proportion of enquiries become customers. With those, the maximum defensible cost per enquiry falls out arithmetically and the budget conversation becomes short.

At this volume, report on a rolling quarter rather than a calendar month. Five enquiries a month cannot distinguish a good month from a bad one, and an account judged monthly at this scale gets cut and restarted on noise.

Where a business cannot say what a customer is worth, that is the first project and it is a week rather than a quarter.

One further column belongs on whatever list the business already keeps: what each enquiry turned into. A minute per enquiry to record, and it is the only thing that turns an advertising argument into an arithmetic one. In a market with a concentrated year it also tells you which weeks were worth the money, which is the question that sets next year’s budget shape.

The media budget, which goes to Google, and the management fee, which goes to the agency. A single number a month tells you neither, and an agency taking a percentage of spend is paid more for spending more. Ask for both figures in writing, ask what happens to the account if you leave, and ask whose name it is in.

Straight answers

Kingston PPC Agency FAQ

Straight answers before you ever book a call.

What should we budget?

Enough that the account gathers a readable amount of data inside a month rather than a quarter. In a cluster this small that is a modest figure and the constraint is rarely the money; it is that there are only so many relevant searches to buy, and beyond that point the budget has nowhere useful to go.

Our budget never gets spent. Should we widen?

Almost never. An underspending account in a market this size is usually a correctly targeted one that has run out of relevant searches. Widening spends the budget on people who were not looking for you. Put the remainder into pages instead, where in a market this lightly defended it buys a position rather than a visit.

Should we advertise or build pages?

Both, in that order, for different reasons. Advertising settles within a fortnight whether the offer and the page work and which vocabulary converts. Search work then makes the same traffic stop costing per click, and here it is unusually cheap. A short paid test, then a finite build.

Does seasonality matter to the account?

For a large share of businesses here it decides the year. Look at your own enquiries by week across two or three years before setting any budget shape, and expect the pattern to be sharper than you remember it. Then keep a small floor through the quiet weeks rather than going dark, because an account restarted from nothing spends its first fortnight relearning.
