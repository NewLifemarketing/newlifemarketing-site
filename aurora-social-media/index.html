Aurora Social Media

An Audience That Keeps Arriving

Aurora Social Media has a feature no settled town shares: the town grew 11.9% between the last two censuses, so a share of the people seeing your account every year have never seen it before, and the post explaining who you are is the most valuable thing you own.

What growth does

Aurora Social Media: Introduce Yourself On A Cycle

Standard advice treats a following as something that accumulates. In a growing town it also has to be re-earned.

In a settled town the post explaining what you do, where you are and how it works is run once and then feels repetitive. Here a meaningful share of the audience arrives every year without having seen it, so the same post is new to them.

That inverts the usual instinct. Republishing the fundamentals — the service, the area, the process, the price range — on a cycle is not lazy here; it is the single highest-return habit available, and almost nobody does it because it feels like repeating yourself.

It also means the arrival moment is worth targeting deliberately. Somebody who has just moved is choosing a dentist, a mechanic, a landscaper and a clinic in a short window, with no recommendation to lean on.

A business that is visible during that window gets chosen by default. One that is not waits for a referral that may be years away.

What actually reaches people

Organic Reach Is Not Distribution

The thing sold as free stopped being free a decade ago, and in a town where the audience keeps changing that matters twice over.

An account with a few thousand followers reaches a small fraction of them on any given post, and the fraction is set by the platform rather than by the quality of the work. A new arrival does not follow you, so organic reach does not touch them at all.

What reaches a newcomer is paid distribution against a defined local audience, which in a town of 62,057 is affordable in a way it is not in a city: you can put something in front of most of the relevant population, several times, for a knowable sum.

So the useful split is organic for the people who already know you — proof you exist, reason to stay — and paid for everybody else. Treating organic as an acquisition channel is the commonest and most expensive misunderstanding in this category.

Twenty searches a month name a supplier for this work in the whole market, which tells you nobody here goes looking for somebody to do it. That is a statement about how it is bought, not about whether it works.

The three products

Posting, Strategy And Distribution Are Separate

Quotes rarely say which is included, and that silence is where the disappointment comes from.

Posting

Somebody makes and schedules content. Production, priced by output. On its own it will not reach a newcomer, because a newcomer does not follow you.

Strategy

Deciding what is worth saying, to whom and when — including the cycle question above. Cheap, infrequent, and the thing that makes the other two worth anything.

Distribution

Money spent reaching people who do not follow you, which in a finite growing town means most of the relevant population for a knowable sum. Where the enquiries come from, and the line that shrinks when a blended fee is squeezed.

What is scarce

What Makes Aurora Social Media Cheap To Run

Relevance lowers the cost of attention, and relevance comes from material nobody else can post.

Every supplier in York Region has the same stock library and the same templates. What almost none of their clients have is footage of the actual work: the job going in, the room finished, the thing being made, the person who answers the phone.

For a first-time buyer with no recommendation to lean on, that footage is doing the job a neighbour would otherwise do. It is evidence rather than decoration, and it is the reason a small budget can outperform a larger one.

One shooting day produces months of material and the marginal cost of each additional cut is small when it is planned before the shoot. That matters especially here, because the cyclical republishing above needs a library rather than a single piece.

What an aurora social media quote should include and usually does not

A line for making something. A posting retainer with no production budget is an arrangement for distributing material that does not exist.

Measurement

Ask People How They Heard Of You

At this scale platform numbers are noise and the oldest question is the best instrument.

A business receiving six enquiries a month cannot tell a good month from a bad one. Engagement rate is a percentage of a population the platform chose; reach is purchasable, so a reach figure with no spend figure beside it says nothing at all.

What works is asking every arriving customer how they heard of you and recording the answer. In a town of this size a large share of new business arrives by routes no tracking parameter survives — a neighbourhood group, a recommendation at a school gate, a post somebody screenshotted and sent on.

Across a rolling quarter that list is more informative than any dashboard, it costs a minute per enquiry, and it is the only report line that comes from your business rather than from a platform.

Ask any supplier for a report containing such a line. The reaction tells you how the relationship will run.

Our part

Distribution, And The Footage To Feed It

A short offer, honestly bounded.

We run paid social against cost per enquiry and film the material it runs on. We manage over $70,000 a month in advertising spend for Ontario businesses and the owner runs the paid media personally.

How an aurora social media engagement usually starts

Aurora Social Media work with us starts with a shooting day and a calendar built around the arrival cycle rather than with a content plan, because the plan is only as good as the material behind it and most businesses here have none.

We do not sell daily posting retainers, community management or inbox monitoring. Those are real jobs and there are firms in York Region built for them. We are based in Minesing rather than Aurora, so filming here involves travel, which we price as its own line.

Is it not repetitive to post the same thing every year?

In a settled town, yes. In a town that grew 11.9% in five years, a share of the audience has never seen it. The fundamentals — what you do, where you go, what it roughly costs, what happens after somebody calls — are new information to every arrival, and republishing them on a cycle is the highest-return habit available here.

How much should we spend?

Enough to reach a meaningful share of the relevant local population more than once, which is a calculable figure rather than a guess because the audience is finite. Past the point where everybody inclined to respond has, extra budget buys repetition and wears out the creative you have.

Which platform should we be on?

The one your customers actually use, which in this town splits mostly by age and category rather than by anything local. Pick one, do it properly, and treat the others as syndication. Spreading a small budget and a small amount of material across four platforms produces four mediocre presences and no useful data.

Why did results drop after a few weeks?

Usually because the audience has seen it. In a finite town the people inclined to respond do so quickly, and the remaining impressions reach people who have already decided not to. Nothing in the account changed, which is why it is confusing. New material and a rebuilt audience are the fix, planned in advance rather than improvised.
