Video Production Thunder Bay

Supply Exists Here

Video Production Thunder Bay is unusual among the northern markets we work in because there is genuine local supply — seven different phrasings in the local search data name companies — which turns the question from whether anybody can do it into when a southern crew is worth the 1,400 kilometres.

When a cluster this small contains seven phrasings that all name companies — production company, production companies, videography — it tells you people here expect to find one locally, and that they do.

That is genuinely different from the smaller northern markets, where the realistic options are a phone, a southern supplier who travels, or nothing. Here there is a third answer, and it is frequently the right one.

What follows is that distance stops being the deciding factor and the usual questions apply: how many finished pieces, who owns the rushes, what happens to the stills, and how many rounds of changes are included.

A southern crew earns the journey only for something a local one genuinely cannot do, and being honest about which is which is more useful to you than a quote. It is also, from our side, the difference between a client for a decade and a client for one project.

When local is right

Three Jobs For Somebody Who Is Already Here

Proximity is worth paying for in specific cases rather than in general.

Anything that repeats

Monthly or quarterly filming, a series that needs consistency, a project with stages that cannot be predicted. Travel costs compound and a local crew does not carry them.

Anything at short notice

The job that turned up this week, the stage about to be covered over, the person who is only available on Thursday. Distance makes all three impossible to serve.

Anything where the location is the point

Somebody who knows where to stand at what time of day in this city knows something a visiting crew will spend half a day learning.

Against those, the case for a distant supplier rests on one thing only: a specific capability or a specific way of working that is genuinely not available closer. If that cannot be named in a sentence, hire locally and spend the difference on more filming days.

The day it rains

What A Video Production Thunder Bay Booking Should Say About Weather

Anything filmed outside needs an answer to this before a date is agreed.

Three questions settle it: who decides whether the day goes ahead, by what time, and what it costs if it does not. Without them a wet morning becomes an argument between two people who both have a reasonable case.

The usual fair arrangement is a decision made the evening before, by the crew, with a rescheduled day at no extra charge and travel costs covered if they had already set off.

Plan an interior half to the day as well. A schedule with indoor work that can be brought forward turns a poor morning into a rearranged one rather than a lost one.

And be honest about what weather actually suits the subject. Grey light is better than bright sun for a great deal of work, and a crew insisting on a blue sky is frequently protecting an aesthetic rather than the result.

Who turns up

One Person, Two, Or Four

Crew size is the largest line on most quotes and the least explained.

One person can film an interview and cover a process well, provided the schedule allows for them doing things in sequence rather than at once. It is slower on the day and considerably cheaper.

A second person is worth it where sound is difficult, where two cameras would save a reshoot, or where somebody has to manage the people being filmed while the camera is running.

Beyond that, each additional person is buying speed or production value rather than coverage, and for most business video neither is the constraint.

So the question to ask of a quote is what the second and third people are for, in plain terms. There is usually a good answer and it should not have to be requested twice.

What a venue will ask

Somebody Will Want To See A Certificate

A routine request that stops an unprepared shoot at the door.

Public buildings, industrial sites, shopping centres and most places with a facilities manager will ask for proof of insurance before letting a camera in, sometimes naming a minimum figure.

Ask whoever you hire for the certificate at the point of booking rather than the morning of the shoot. A freelancer without one is not necessarily a problem for a private site and is a problem for anywhere with a procedure.

Where the location is yours, check your own position too. Visitors working on the premises are usually covered and occasionally are not, and the time to find out is not afterwards.

Filming in a public place is generally permitted, and the practical limit is whoever is standing there rather than the law. A word with the nearest person in a high-visibility jacket prevents most of what goes wrong.

Where a shoot involves a road, a car park or anywhere a vehicle moves, somebody should be watching for traffic rather than looking through a lens. That is the one genuine safety requirement in most business filming and it is the one most often met by assuming everybody will be careful.

Our scope

What We Film, And When We Would Not

Stated plainly, because the honest answer here is frequently to hire closer.

We shoot and edit for Ontario businesses and run the paid media the footage feeds, with over $70,000 a month in advertising spend under management and the owner running that media personally. Our published work includes two days on an active City of Barrie site with around forty trades and engineers, and seven finished videos.

When video production thunder bay should be bought locally

Video Production Thunder Bay that repeats, that needs somebody at short notice, or where knowing the city is the point is work for a crew already here, and we would say so rather than quote 1,400 kilometres of travel against it. Local supply exists in this market and that changes the honest recommendation.

Where a project genuinely needs something we do, we price the travel as its own visible line rather than folding it into a fee, and we plan the day as a list so a single journey produces a year of material.

Should we hire locally or bring somebody in?

Locally, unless you can name in a sentence a capability or a way of working that is genuinely not available here. Seven phrasings in the local search data name companies, which means supply exists. Travel costs compound on anything that repeats, and a crew already here can come at short notice.

What should any quote contain?

How many finished pieces rather than how many days, who owns the raw footage, what happens to the stills, and how many rounds of changes are included. Those four apply equally to a local crew and a distant one, and most quotes are missing at least two.

How many pieces should one day produce?

Far more than most people ask for. Once a crew is set up another piece costs minutes: a main film, the questions you answer weekly, a short piece from each person present, cutaways and stills. An unplanned day yields one film at the price of a day.

What can we do ourselves?

A good share of it. A phone plus a clip microphone covers social, progress updates and replies to specific questions. The microphone is the highest-return purchase in this subject because audio is the one thing a phone cannot manage on its own.
