Everywhere else you buy an audience and hope the right people are inside it. Here you buy by company, job title, seniority and size, and you pay several times the going rate per thousand impressions for it.

That trade pays when the right people are a finite, nameable set. It does not pay against a broad audience, where the platform becomes an expensive way to buy impressions and the filter you paid extra for is idle.

For a LinkedIn ads agency Mississauga businesses engage, the nameable set here is unusually easy to build, because the companies are concentrated and the sectors are legible. Manufacturing, logistics, distribution and professional services cluster in ways you can see on a map, and a target list of eighty firms here is a morning of work rather than a research project.

Building the list

Start From What You Have Already Won

The best source for a target list is your own closed deals, not a profile written in a workshop.

Take every deal closed in the last three years. Sort by what it was worth and by how little trouble it caused. Look at the top fifteen and write down what they had in common that was visible from outside: size, sector, what they had just done, who held the budget.

That last column is the one that is usually missing and the one that matters. Companies do not buy because they match a description; they buy because something changed — a new line, a plant, a regulation, a departure, a system that stopped coping. Those events are observable and they are what makes a company worth approaching this quarter rather than in principle.

With roughly 300 fewer establishments recorded than the previous year, this is also a market where change is happening: consolidation produces integrations, system migrations and new reporting obligations, all of which are purchase triggers.

The part that takes time is the people. Two to four roles per company, with the person who feels the problem distinguished from the person who signs. A list of companies with no people in it is not usable.

What the sequence says

Four Messages, In Order, To The Same Companies

A single advertisement shown repeatedly to a short list is the commonest way this budget is wasted.

The first piece establishes that the problem exists and is worth attention. It makes no offer at all, which is precisely why it is read.

The second shows that you have solved it for somebody recognisable — a named situation, a figure, an outcome. The third answers the objection the sales team hears most.

Only the fourth asks for anything, and it asks for something small: a conversation, a short document, a specific question answered.

The order is doing real work here. Asking on the first contact converts the small number of people who happened to be ready and teaches everybody else to ignore you, which on a list this short is an expensive thing to teach.

Run them in order against the same list over several weeks rather than all at once. The list is finite, so the sequence is the only thing that stops the campaign becoming repetition.

What it costs to build the list

What A LinkedIn Ads Agency Mississauga Does Before Spending Anything

The list is the campaign, and assembling it is real work nobody quotes for.

Somebody has to decide which companies qualify, find them, check they are still trading and still the right size, and work out which job titles actually make the decision.

That is days rather than hours for a list worth running against, and it is the part most likely to be skipped in favour of a broad filter that lets the platform decide.

It is also the part that survives. A verified list of two hundred companies is an asset the business keeps and reuses — for post, for calls, for events — long after any campaign ends.

So treat it as a deliverable rather than a preparation step. Ask who builds it, how long it takes, and who holds it afterwards, before agreeing a media budget at all.

You should. Any LinkedIn Ads Agency Mississauga businesses hire can build it, and the one worth hiring will hand it over in a form you can read rather than keeping it inside their own account.

Cheaper routes to the same people

Two Hundred Companies Can Be Reached Without A Platform

Worth comparing honestly, because the alternatives frequently win on arithmetic.

A list that short can be written to directly, by post, by somebody who has read something about each one. The response rate to a hundred genuinely personal approaches routinely beats a quarter of advertising aimed at the same companies.

It can also be reached through the places those people already are: a trade association, a supplier’s customer list, an event, a mutual client willing to introduce you.

The platform’s advantage is repetition without effort — being present in front of the same people for months without anybody doing anything. That is genuinely valuable and it is what you are paying the premium for.

It is also the only one of the three routes that works while nobody is available to do anything. For a business whose sales effort stops entirely during its busy season, that is a stronger argument than any comparison of response rates.

The honest answer is usually both, with the advertising making the direct approach warmer rather than replacing it. Sold as a substitute for the work, it underperforms the work.

Straight answers

LinkedIn Ads FAQ

Straight answers before you ever book a call.

How many companies should we target?

Sixty to eighty is a workable first programme: small enough to personalise, large enough that a few non-responses do not end it. In a city with 23,700 establishments the temptation is to go much wider, and a list of several hundred is ordinary demand generation with a more expensive media buy attached.

What budget does this need?

Enough to reach your named list several times within a month, which is calculable once the list exists and is often lower than people expect. Without a list the number is unanswerable, which is why the first session is better spent building one than discussing spend.

Is this better than Google Ads for us?

They do different jobs. Google captures people already searching, which is a smaller group arriving ready. This reaches the right people before they look, which is a larger group arriving cold. Most businesses should take all the search demand they can get first, then use this to grow the pool.

Can we target by company rather than job title?

Yes, and that is the use the premium pricing exists for. Uploading a list of companies and advertising only to people inside them turns the platform into an addressable channel rather than a broadcast one. It is also the point at which coordination with whoever handles sales stops being optional.
