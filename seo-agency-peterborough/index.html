Sixteen Ways, One Page

SEO Agency Peterborough searches arrive in sixteen different phrasings that all mean the same thing, at a difficulty score of 14, which tells you the problem is not who you are competing against — it is that most local sites compete against themselves.

Difficulty 14 means almost nothing substantial is defending these results. In a city where 510 monthly searches go looking for a marketing supplier, that combination is strange and worth pausing on: plenty of demand, very little defence.

The explanation is in the second number. Sixteen phrasings of one question is not sixteen opportunities; it is one opportunity written sixteen ways, and a site that builds a page for several of them ends up with four thin pages splitting one result between them.

Search engines resolve that split by picking one of your pages, usually not the one you would have chosen, and the others contribute nothing except the dilution. Nobody notices, because no error is reported anywhere.

So the first piece of work is subtraction. Decide which single page owns the cluster, point the others at it, and the defence everybody assumed was competition turns out to have been self-inflicted.

Finding the split

How To Tell If Your Pages Are Competing

Three checks, none needing a tool subscription, each taking about ten minutes.

Search your own site

Put your domain and the phrase into a search engine with a site: prefix. More than one page returned for the same search, with no clear winner, is the split in plain sight.

Read the titles side by side

List every page title on your site. Two titles that a customer could not tell apart are two pages a search engine cannot tell apart either, and one of them is costing you the other.

Check which one actually ranks

It is frequently a blog post from four years ago rather than the service page you paid for. That is the clearest possible signal that the service page is not winning its own search.

What to do about it is unglamorous and permanent: pick the page that should win, fold the useful material from the others into it, and redirect them. Nothing is deleted and the result stops being divided.

What the page needs

Specific Beats Comprehensive In A Local Result

The page that wins a local search is rarely the longest one.

A page claiming every service for every customer gives a reader nothing to recognise. A page stating what you do, where you will travel, what it costs to begin and what you will not take on gives them four things, and the fourth is the one that builds trust fastest.

Specificity also solves the catchment problem. The census area holds 128,624 people against the city’s 83,651, and a reader in a township thirty minutes out is asking one question before any other: do they come here. A page that answers it plainly converts a visitor a vaguer page loses.

The same applies to price. A range with the reason it varies reads as competence rather than as a discount, because only somebody who understands their own costs can give one, and it disqualifies the enquiries that waste your week.

None of that is a trick. It is simply that the things which make a page convert are the same things that make it recognisably about something, and recognisably-about-something is what ranks.

The local layer

Where SEO Agency Peterborough Work Should Start

Before any page is written, two free things usually move more than a month of work.

The Business Profile decides a large share of local results and is frequently wrong in ways nobody has looked at: categories chosen once and never revisited, a service area that does not match where you actually go, hours that lapsed, photographs from a previous decade.

Reviews are the second. They are a process rather than a good intention: a specific moment in the job when somebody asks, and a short link that makes it a thirty-second task rather than a favour. Businesses that do this collect steadily; businesses that mean to do it collect nothing.

Both are free, both take an afternoon to set right, and both are finished before the first page is written, because a page sending traffic to a profile that contradicts it is working against itself.

The test for how much weight to give this: of your last twenty customers, how many chose you partly because you were nearby. Most of them, and the profile is most of the result. None of them, and it is housekeeping that still has to be correct.

The season

Build Before The Water Opens

A large share of this catchment runs on a calendar published by the federal government.

Parks Canada ran the 2026 Trent-Severn navigation season from 15 May to 12 October, and the season runs from Victoria Day to Thanksgiving every year. For any business selling to people who arrive by water or own property on it, demand has a start date set by somebody else and known months in advance.

Search work takes weeks to move, which means a page published in June is helping next season rather than this one. Published in March, it is in place when the demand arrives.

That makes the quiet half of the year the productive half. It is when the self-competition gets resolved, the pages get written and the questions people asked all summer get turned into the answers that rank.

It is also the only time of year when the person who knows the work has an hour to spare, and that hour is the genuinely scarce input in every technical page worth having.

Difficulty 14 sounds easy. Why are we not ranking?

Usually because you are competing with yourself. Sixteen phrasings of one question tempt a site into building four pages that split one result between them, and a search engine resolves the split by picking one, often an old blog post. Consolidating onto a single page and redirecting the rest is the first and largest fix.

Should we write a page for every phrasing?

No, and it is the most common expensive mistake in a local cluster. Sixteen phrasings mean one page, written well, that reads naturally and mentions the variations in passing. Sixteen pages mean sixteen thin ones, none of which wins, and a site that is harder to maintain for the trouble.

How much does the Business Profile matter?

It depends on whether your customers chose you partly for proximity. For a business people visit or call locally, the profile and the reviews attached to it decide a large share of the result and both are free to fix. For a supplier whose buyers found them from a desk elsewhere, it is housekeeping that still has to be correct.

When should this work start?

In the quiet half of the year. Parks Canada ran the 2026 navigation season from 15 May to 12 October, and for much of this catchment that is the commercial year. Search work takes weeks to move, so a page published in March is in place when demand arrives; published in June it is helping next season.
