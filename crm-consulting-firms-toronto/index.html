Where the projects fail

Why CRM Consulting Firms Toronto Are Called In Twice

The commonest cause of a failed implementation is a vocabulary problem that looks like a software problem.

Ask four people in a sales team what "qualified" means and you will get four answers. Ask what separates a proposal sent from a proposal in negotiation and the answers diverge further. Build a system on top of that and you have four people recording four different realities in one database.

The symptom arrives months later as a forecast nobody trusts, and the usual response is to blame the tool and consider migrating. The migration carries the same ambiguity into a new product at considerable expense.

The fix is a workshop and a document: each stage named, each stage's entry condition written as something observable, and the list agreed by the people who will type into it. It takes days rather than weeks. It is the single highest-return part of the project and it is the part that gets cut when budgets tighten.

What CRM consulting firms Toronto buyers should insist on first

The stage definitions, in writing, agreed by the team, before any configuration. If a supplier wants to start in the software, they are starting at the end.

Before anything is bought

The Document That Outlives The Platform

One page, agreed by the people who type into the system, and portable to whatever replaces it.

Each stage named. Against each one, an observable condition — something that either happened or did not, with no judgement required. A proposal has been sent or it has not. A site visit is booked or it is not.

Write it before any product is chosen, because this is the artefact a migration carries forward. Firms that have been through two implementations usually have one; firms on their first usually do not, and that is most of the difference in how the two projects go.

The test of it is boring and reliable: hand it to somebody who was not in the room and ask them to classify ten live deals. Where two readers disagree, the wording is wrong, and it will be wrong inside the software too.

Days of work rather than weeks. It is the first thing cut when a budget tightens and the only thing that would have saved the budget.

Marketing is half of this

A System That Cannot Say Where Leads Came From

The gap we meet most often sits exactly between advertising and the CRM, and both suppliers assume the other covered it.

An enquiry arrives. The CRM records a name, a date and sometimes a form. What it almost never records is which campaign, which page or which search produced it, because that information exists at the moment of capture and is discarded by the time the record is created.

The consequence is that every spending decision afterwards is argued from opinion. The advertising platform claims the result, the sales team remembers it differently, and nobody can settle it.

Fixing it is unglamorous: capture the source at the form, carry it into the record, and make it a required field on the report. It is a day of work and it changes the quality of every budget conversation for years.

This is why we do this work at all rather than leaving it to specialists. A marketing firm that cannot see what its spending produced is guessing, and the place that visibility is won or lost is inside the CRM.

Who it has to work for

Configured By People Who Will Never Use It

The commonest reason a well-specified system is abandoned.

The configuration meeting contains an owner, a consultant and occasionally a manager. The people who will type into the thing forty times a day are usually not in the room, and the decisions that most affect them are made there.

What follows is predictable. A required field that is genuinely unanswerable at the moment of entry gets a junk value, because the alternative is being unable to save the record. Within a month that field is worse than empty, because it looks populated.

One person who does the work, in the room, with a veto over required fields, prevents nearly all of it. It is a cheap intervention and it is skipped almost every time.

Where a team is larger, pick the person most sceptical about the project rather than the most enthusiastic one. They will tell you what will actually happen.

Our scope

What Kind Of Firm This Actually Is

The cluster asks for a person, so it is fair to say which one and what they do.

We are a small Ontario marketing company that builds sales systems and buys the advertising meant to fill them. We manage over $70,000 a month in advertising spend and the owner runs the paid media himself, which is why the reporting end of a build gets more attention from us than the feature list does.

Where CRM consulting firms Toronto buyers should use somebody else

Most firms in this cluster either implement software or buy media. We do both, and the projects where that combination is worth paying for are the ones where the live question is why the report and the bank statement disagree. Where the question is purely configuration, a specialist implementer is cheaper and better at it.

We do not do enterprise integration, ERP replacement, regulated data environments or formal procurement, and we are not the right call for a sales team of forty. We are based in Minesing, north of the city, and work with Toronto businesses remotely.

Remote is worth a sentence rather than an apology. Almost all of this work happens in a shared document, a screen share and a weekly call, and the parts that genuinely benefit from being in a room — the session where stages get named and argued over — are a day or two at the start rather than a standing commitment. Where a project needs somebody present every week, a downtown firm is the better buy and we would say so on the first call rather than the fourth.

Which CRM should we use?

Almost any of the major platforms will do what a mid-sized business needs, so the question is usually less important than it feels. What matters more is which one your team will actually type into and which one connects to the systems you already depend on. We would rather spend the first week on your stage definitions than on a product comparison, because the definitions survive a change of platform and the comparison does not.

How long does an implementation take?

The configuration is weeks. The adoption is months, and it is the part that decides whether the project worked. Budget for training, for a period where both the old way and the new way run, and for somebody internally whose job is to chase the records nobody updated. Projects that skip that third item quietly revert within a quarter.

Can you fix the CRM we already have?

Usually, and it is nearly always cheaper than migrating. Most systems we are asked to replace are not broken; they are configured for a process the business has outgrown, with fields nobody fills in and stages nobody agreed. Re-deciding the process and reconfiguring around it costs a fraction of a migration and carries the history with it.

Will this tell us which marketing works?

Only if source capture is built in deliberately, which it rarely is by default. The work is to carry the campaign and page that produced an enquiry all the way into the record and onto the report. Once that exists, the monthly budget argument becomes a short conversation about numbers rather than a long one about impressions.
