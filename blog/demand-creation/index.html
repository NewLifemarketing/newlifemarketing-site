Demand Creation

Nobody Searches for a Problem They Don’t Know They Have

B2B/Demand Generation

by Cruz Lee Posted on October 19, 2026

Every B2B marketing budget has a line for the buyers who are already looking. Search ads, retargeting, review sites, the demo page. Very few have a line for the buyers who will be looking next year, and that second group is almost the whole market.

Demand creation is the work aimed at them. It teaches a problem to people who are not shopping yet, so that when they do start, they already know why it matters and whose explanation they trusted. It is the half of demand generation that cannot be bought with a keyword, which is exactly why it gets cut first. This post covers what demand creation is, how it works, how much of the budget it deserves, and how to keep it alive long enough to pay back.

What Demand Creation Actually Means

Demand creation is marketing aimed at people who are not yet in the market for what you sell. They may not know they have the problem. They may know and not think it is urgent. Either way, they are not searching, not comparing and not filling in forms, so nothing built to catch a buyer in the act will reach them.

Its opposite is demand capture: search, retargeting, comparison pages, the pricing page, the sales follow-up. Capture converts interest that already exists. Demand creation produces the interest that capture will convert later. Most teams run both under one label, out of one budget, and report them as one number. That is where the trouble starts, because the two behave nothing alike.

Demand creationDemand capture
Who it reachesBuyers who are not looking yetBuyers who are looking now
What it asks of themNothing, except attentionA click, a form, a call
When it pays backOver two to four quarters, and it compoundsWithin weeks
First sign it is workingBranded search, inbound qualityCost per opportunity, conversion rate
What kills itBeing judged on this month’s leadsBeing asked to grow past the market’s search volume

The timings in that table are the ones we publish for our own demand generation work: capture can produce inside weeks, creation compounds over two to four quarters. Hold on to the second number. Almost every way a creation programme fails comes back to somebody forgetting it.

It Works on Memory, Not Persuasion

The reason demand creation exists is a piece of arithmetic. Professor John Dawes of the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, writing with LinkedIn’s B2B Institute in 2021, pointed out that businesses replace major suppliers rarely. His example: companies change major providers such as their principal bank roughly once every five years. If that holds, roughly 20% of companies are in the market for a new bank over a year, and only around 5% in any given quarter.

That became the 95:5 rule. At any moment, about 95% of the companies who could buy from you will not buy this quarter. Dawes was clear the percentages are not meant to be read literally. The shape is the point: far more of your market is out than in.

Read the picture as a sequence and the case makes itself. The companies in next quarter’s in-market slice are sitting in this quarter’s out-of-market majority. You cannot push them into the market early; their need changes when their business changes. What you can do is be the name they already associate with the problem when it does.

That is the mechanism. Demand creation is not persuasion. Almost nobody in the 95% is persuadable this quarter, because they are not deciding anything. It is memory: building and refreshing the link between a problem and your name, so the link is there on the day it is needed. Dawes made the same argument, that advertising works by building memory, not by talking people into a purchase they were not planning.

Two practical consequences follow. Reach matters more than response, because the buyers you need are spread across the whole market rather than clustered in this month’s leads. And repetition matters more than novelty, because a memory that is not refreshed fades before the buyer gets there.

Three Things It Gets Mistaken For

Demand creation is easy to describe and easy to misfile. Three near neighbours absorb it in most organisations, and each one quietly changes what gets measured.

Lead generation

Lead generation optimises for the number of enquiries. Put a creation asset behind a form and it becomes a lead generation asset: the people who fill the form in are mostly not ready, the people who would have learned from it never see it, and the report shows a cost per lead that looks bad next to search. The asset did not fail. It was measured as something it is not.

Brand awareness

Closer, but not the same. Awareness asks whether people know your name. Demand creation asks whether they connect your name to a specific problem they will one day have. A logo seen at a trade show is awareness. A clear explanation of why month-end close runs two days late, attached to your name, is creation. The second is worth far more when the buyer goes looking.

Content marketing

Content is one of the main tools of demand creation, but plenty of content creates nothing. A keyword-driven article that answers a question the reader was already asking is capture: it meets existing demand on a search page. Creation content raises a question the reader had not asked yet. Both have a place. Only one of them grows the market you will sell into next year.

How Much of the Budget Belongs Here

The best evidence on the split comes from Les Binet and Peter Field, who studied campaign effectiveness data for LinkedIn’s B2B Institute in The 5 Principles of Growth in B2B Marketing. Their finding was that B2B brands grow best when roughly 46% of the budget goes to brand-building and 54% to activation aimed at an immediate response.

Brand-building in their sense is not identical to demand creation, but the two overlap heavily: both are aimed at people who are not buying now, and both pay back over a longer period than activation. Treat 46% as a direction rather than a target. What it tells you is that a B2B budget sitting at 10% creation and 90% capture is not cautious. It is spending nearly all its money competing for the same 5% as everyone else.

Two adjustments to the number before you use it. A company with no brand at all, entering a category, often needs to lean further toward creation for the first year, because there is nothing for capture to convert yet. A company with strong branded search and a full pipeline can lean the other way. Either way, write the split down and agree it with whoever owns the budget. A split that only exists in the marketing team’s head will be renegotiated at the first bad month.

What a Demand Creation Asset Looks Like

The test for any piece of demand creation work is simple: would a buyer who has no intention of purchasing for a year still find it worth their time? If the answer depends on them wanting your product, it is capture.

Assets that pass the test tend to share a few traits:

  • They are about the problem, not the product. Where the hours go, what it costs, why the usual fix does not work. Your product may never be named.
  • They take a position. “Most teams measure this wrong, and here is why” gives a reader something to remember. “Best practices for X” gives them something to forget.
  • They carry something only you could make. Your own data, a worked example, a benchmark from the accounts you run. Anything a competitor could publish unchanged is not creating demand for you in particular.
  • They come from a person. Executive presence is on our own list of creation tools alongside education, point-of-view content and video, because buyers remember people more readily than logos.
  • They are free to consume. No form, no gate. A gate turns the asset into a lead generation play and cuts its reach to the few people who were already close to buying.
  • They are repeated. One brilliant piece seen once is forgotten. A clear idea seen many times, in different forms, is what builds the memory the 95:5 arithmetic calls for.

What it does not need to be is expensive. A founder posting a sharp observation twice a week, a short benchmark built from your own work, and paid reach that puts both in front of a defined audience is a complete creation programme for most B2B companies under a few hundred staff.

Audit Your Own Programme in an Afternoon

Most teams believe they are doing more of this than they are, because creation and capture sit in the same budget line. An afternoon with last quarter’s spend will tell you.

  1. List every activity and every dollar from last quarter. Campaigns, content, events, tools, agency time.
  2. Mark each one creation or capture, using one question: was it aimed at people already looking? Gated content counts as capture.
  3. Total each side. Compare it with the split you would defend in front of the CFO. If creation is a small fraction of the total, you have found the first problem.
  4. Check what the creation side is judged on. If the answer is leads or cost per lead, it is being measured as capture and will lose every comparison.
  5. Check how long it has been given. Anything stopped inside two quarters never had the chance to work.
  6. Name the problem you want to be known for. If the team cannot agree on one in a sentence, that is the first job, before any channel.

The output is a short page: today’s split, the split you want, what creation will be judged on, and the date of the first honest review. That page is what protects the programme later.

Keeping It Funded Long Enough to Work

Demand creation rarely fails because the idea was wrong. It fails because it was reported after one quarter, next to search ads, on the same measures, and lost. The fix is to agree in advance what it will be judged on, and to report it separately from capture so the two cannot be averaged into one misleading number.

The measures we report for the creation side were chosen because none of them depends on a click or a form:

  • Branded search volume over time. More people searching your name means more people remember it, wherever they learned it.
  • Direct traffic to key pages. People who typed you in rather than clicking an ad.
  • Self-reported attribution. A free-text “How did you hear about us?” field. Buyers will tell you about the podcast or the post that no tracking pixel saw.
  • Sales acceptance rate and win rate by source. Educated buyers arrive better qualified, and these two show it.
  • Pipeline created and influenced, with the creation and capture split shown, and cost per opportunity rather than cost per lead.

None of those is perfect on its own. Together they describe the trend honestly, and in the order creation produces results: branded search and inbound quality move first, pipeline later. Agree that order with finance before the first quarterly review, not during it.

Add the reporting rules we hold to on every programme: platform-reported values labelled as such, the attribution window stated on every view, early-stage data flagged as early-stage, and no summing of numbers across platforms and calling the total revenue. Demand creation is the work most likely to be over-claimed when it is going well and under-credited when it is not. Clean reporting protects it from both.

If you sell to a defined list of accounts rather than a broad market, the same logic applies at a smaller scale. Creation for a named-account programme means reaching the whole buying committee inside your target accounts long before any of them raise their hand, which is where it meets account-based marketing.

The Market You Sell to Next Year Is Forming Now

Every buyer who will be in your market next year is somewhere in this year’s 95%, learning about their problem from somebody. Demand creation is the decision to make that somebody you. It does not need a large budget or a clever channel. It needs a problem you are willing to be known for, a view on it, a person to say it, enough repetition to be remembered, and the patience to judge it on the right measures for long enough to see it work.

  • Demand creation reaches buyers who are not looking yet. Capture reaches the ones who are. Report them separately.
  • It works on memory: about 95% of buyers are out of market in a given quarter, and next quarter’s buyers come from them.
  • Binet and Field found B2B grows best at roughly 46% brand-building to 54% activation. Most teams are far below that.
  • A creation asset is about the problem, takes a position, carries your own material, comes from a person and is never gated.
  • Judge it on branded search, direct traffic, self-reported attribution and win rate, over two to four quarters.

Straight answers

FAQ

What B2B teams ask when they try to fund work aimed at buyers who are not looking yet.

What is demand creation?

Demand creation is marketing aimed at people who are not yet in the market for what you sell. It teaches them a problem, and attaches your name to it, before they start looking for a solution, so that when their need becomes urgent they already understand it and remember who explained it. It is the counterpart to demand capture, which converts buyers who are already searching.

What is the difference between demand creation and demand generation?

Demand generation is the whole job: creating demand and capturing it, then routing it to the right sales motion. Demand creation is one half of it, the half aimed at buyers who are not looking yet. Capture is the other half, aimed at buyers who already are. Most programmes underinvest in creation because it is slower to show results.

How long does demand creation take to work?

Plan on two to four quarters before it shows up clearly in pipeline. It shows up earlier in branded search volume, direct traffic and the quality of inbound enquiries, which is why those are the measures to watch in the first months.

How much of a B2B marketing budget should go to demand creation?

Les Binet and Peter Field, in research for LinkedIn’s B2B Institute, found B2B brands grow best with roughly 46% of budget on brand-building and 54% on activation. Use that as a direction rather than a rule. A new entrant often needs more creation early on; an established brand with strong branded search can need less.

Should demand creation content be gated?

No. A form in front of creation content cuts its reach to the few people already close to buying and turns it into a lead generation asset judged on cost per lead. Keep it free to read and watch, and measure it on branded search, direct traffic and self-reported attribution instead.
