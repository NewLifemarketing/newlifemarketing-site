HVAC Google Ads in Sudbury: How to Get Booked Before the Furnace Dies

Paid Media/Google Ads/Home Services & Trades

by Cruz Lee Posted on September 30, 2026

A Sudbury furnace doesn’t fail in July. It fails at 2 a.m. in January, and the homeowner grabs a phone and types "furnace repair Sudbury" with nobody else to ask. HVAC Google Ads in Sudbury put your company on that screen in the next ten seconds. Done well, that’s the cheapest booked job you’ll buy all year. Done badly, it’s a bill for clicks from people who wanted a DIY video.

Why HVAC Google Ads in Sudbury Work Differently

Sudbury’s heating season is long and unforgiving. Demand doesn’t creep up, it spikes the week the first hard cold arrives, and the customer who calls is rarely shopping. They’re cold, they’re worried about frozen pipes, and they’ll book the first company that answers.

That changes how you buy. Search ads catch people at the moment of need, which is why they suit emergency repair so well. Referrals and word of mouth still matter, but they can’t scale up on the exact morning your phone should be ringing.

Geography matters too. Greater Sudbury is spread across a huge area, with Lively, Azilda, Val Caron, Chelmsford and the downtown core all a real drive apart. If your crews can’t reasonably cover all of it, your ads shouldn’t pretend they can.

Build Separate Campaigns for Emergency, Replacement and Tune-Ups

The most common mistake is one campaign called "HVAC" with every keyword thrown in. Emergency repair, furnace replacement and a spring A/C tune-up are three different buyers with three different budgets and three different urgencies.

Split them:

  • Emergency repair. "Furnace not working," "no heat," "furnace repair near me." High intent, high cost per click, worth it. Run these around the clock if you can answer the phone, and pause them when you can’t.
  • Replacement and install. "New furnace installation," "heat pump Sudbury." Lower volume, much bigger jobs. These searchers compare quotes, so your landing page needs financing details, warranty terms and a quote form.
  • Maintenance and tune-ups. "Furnace tune-up," "duct cleaning." Cheaper clicks, and the goal is booking the slow months.

Separate campaigns let you set separate budgets. Say you have $3,000 a month. You might put the bulk behind emergency and install in winter, then shift toward tune-ups in September and October so the crew stays busy before the cold.

Keywords, Negatives and the Clicks You Shouldn’t Pay For

Start with the words your customers actually type, then spend more time on what to block. HVAC is full of wasted clicks: job seekers ("HVAC technician jobs"), students ("HVAC course"), DIY searchers ("how to replace a furnace filter") and people outside your area.

Build your negative keyword list on day one: jobs, salary, course, training, diy, how to, parts, manual and free. Then review the search terms report weekly for the first month. Every irrelevant term you find is money you can stop spending. Our guide to building a negative keyword list for Google Ads walks through the process.

Target the area you serve, not the whole province. Use location targeting for your real service radius, and exclude the places you don’t reach. Setting it to "people in" your area, rather than "people interested in" it, stops clicks from searchers who are merely curious about Sudbury.

Send Clicks to a Page Built for the Call

An ad that gets the click and a page that loses the call is the same as no ad at all. Most emergency searches happen on a phone, so the page has to work with a thumb.

For emergency campaigns, put a tap-to-call button at the top, your service area in plain words, your hours, and one line about response time you can honestly keep. Don’t promise "within 30 minutes" unless you can do it on a January night with three other calls waiting.

For install campaigns, the page should answer the questions a homeowner has before they’ll let you in the house: what’s included, what the warranty covers, whether you offer financing, and what a quote visit involves. One clear form. No menu with fifteen other links.

Then answer the phone. A missed call on an emergency ad is a lost click and a competitor’s booked job. If your office can’t cover evenings, use call forwarding to a person, or a missed-call text-back so the lead isn’t lost. Our CRM buildout work is built around exactly this gap.

Measure Cost per Booked Job, Not Cost per Click

Clicks are cheap to report and easy to misread. A $12 click that books a $6,000 furnace replacement is a bargain. A $2 click that never calls is expensive.

Track three things and ignore the rest at first:

  1. Calls and form fills from each campaign, using call tracking so you know which ad rang the phone.
  2. Booked jobs from those leads, which means someone on your side marks the lead as booked in the CRM.
  3. Cost per booked job, which is ad spend divided by jobs.

Imagine an HVAC company spending $2,500 in a month and booking 10 jobs from ads. That’s $250 per booked job. If the average ticket is well above that, the ads are working, and you can raise the budget with confidence. If you can’t say how many jobs the ads produced, you aren’t measuring yet. That’s the first thing to fix.

This is how we run Google Ads for trades and home services: tracked against booked work, not impressions. For a look at the same approach in another Ontario market, see HVAC Google Ads in Ajax, or how plumbers in Barrie handle urgent-call campaigns.

The Bottom Line

HVAC Google Ads in Sudbury pay off when you split emergency, install and maintenance campaigns, block the wasted clicks, send traffic to a page built for the call, and judge everything on cost per booked job.

  • Run emergency, replacement and tune-up campaigns separately, each with its own budget.
  • Add negative keywords for jobs, courses and DIY searches before you spend a dollar.
  • Give phone visitors a tap-to-call button and answer, or forward, every call.
  • Track booked jobs in a CRM so you know your real cost per job.

Straight answers

FAQ

What Sudbury HVAC owners ask before they put money into Google Ads.

How much should an HVAC company in Sudbury spend on Google Ads?

Start with a budget your market can absorb and your crew can serve. Many small HVAC companies begin with a few thousand dollars a month and adjust once they know their cost per booked job. Spending more than your team can handle just buys missed calls.

How quickly do HVAC Google Ads start producing calls?

Search ads can produce calls within days of launching, because they reach people already searching. Expect the first few weeks to go into trimming wasted search terms and tuning the pages. Results usually sharpen as that data builds.

Should I run Google Ads or work on local SEO for my HVAC business?

Both help, and they work on different timelines. Ads put you in front of buyers right away, while local SEO builds slower, lasting visibility. Many companies run ads to fill the calendar while their organic ranking develops.
