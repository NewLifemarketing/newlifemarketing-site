Where Can I Get Help With Marketing Attribution

Fix the Plumbing Before You Buy a Model

B2B/Attribution/Strategy

by Cruz Lee Posted on November 22, 2026

Where can I get help with marketing attribution? From whoever fixes the part that is actually broken, and in most B2B companies that is not the attribution model. It is the tracking underneath it, the CRM it should feed, or a report that adds up numbers no one should add together.

The search results for this question are written by companies that sell attribution software, so the answer they arrive at is software. Sometimes that is right. More often a team buys a multi-touch tool, plugs it into conversion events nobody has tested and a CRM where half the leads have no source, and gets a more expensive version of the same argument it was already having.

Below: how to tell which layer is broken, which kind of help fixes which layer, what to ask before you hire anyone, and the report we hand over on the accounts we run.

Attribution Breaks in Four Layers, and Each Needs Different Help

"Our attribution is a mess" usually means one of four things. They sit on top of each other, and a problem in a lower layer cannot be fixed from a higher one.

  1. Tracking. Whether the conversion events fire, and whether each one means something. A "conversion" that counts a page view is not a lead, and an ad account optimising toward a broken conversion spends confidently in the wrong direction.
  2. The CRM. Whether every lead lands in one record with its source attached, and whether that source survives the handoff to sales. If the CRM cannot say where a closed deal came from, nothing above it can either.
  3. The report. Whether the numbers are labelled honestly: which platform reported them, over which attribution window, and whether anyone has summed figures from different platforms and called the total revenue.
  4. Judgement. Whether the business has agreed how to judge the spend that no tool can trace, which in B2B is often the spend that matters most.

Each symptom points to a layer, and the layer tells you who to call.

What you are seeingLayerWho can fix it
Google, Meta and LinkedIn each claim the same deals; their totals add up to more than you soldReportWhoever builds the report, with a rule that platform numbers are labelled and never summed
Platform conversions are up, sales says the leads are worseTrackingWhoever runs the ad accounts: redefine the conversion, import closed deals back
Nobody can say which campaign a closed deal came fromCRMA CRM build or clean-up, before anything else
The brand and content budget gets cut every tight quarterJudgementLeadership, with a separate scorecard for that spend
Every dashboard tells a different storyReportOne report, one source of truth, the window stated on every view

Notice what is missing from the right-hand column: "a better attribution model". A model sits on top of the first three layers. Give it untested events and sourceless leads and it will distribute credit very precisely across data that is wrong.

Where Can I Get Help With Marketing Attribution? Five Places, Five Different Jobs

Once you know the layer, the options sort themselves. Each of these is the right call for something and the wrong call for something else.

Kind of helpGood forNot good for
Your own analyst or RevOps leadOwning the definitions and the report month after month, knowing your sales processFixing tracking inside ad platforms they do not run
A CRM build or admin partnerGetting every lead into one record with its source, stages that reflect how you actually sellDeciding how to judge spend the CRM cannot see
The team running your paid mediaConversion tracking, offline conversion import, labelling platform numbers honestlyGrading its own homework without a report you can check
Attribution softwareModelling credit across touches once the data underneath is soundRepairing that data. It inherits every gap below it
An independent consultantAn outside audit of all four layers, a second opinion before a big purchaseRunning it every month afterwards

The row people skip is the third. If an agency runs your Google, Meta or LinkedIn accounts, most of the tracking layer is already in their hands, and it is fair to ask them to fix it before you pay anyone else. The condition is in the last column: they should hand you a report you can verify, not a number you have to trust.

Where Can I Get Help With Marketing Attribution If We Have No Real CRM?

Then that is where the help goes first. Without a CRM, nothing can be measured past the form fill, so there is no way to tell whether any of the spend worked. Every other option on the list assumes the CRM exists.

The minimum is less than people expect. Every lead from every source routes into one record. The campaign source is attached when the lead arrives, so it survives the handoff to sales. The ad platforms' tags and analytics are wired into it. Then closed-won deals are pushed back out to LinkedIn and Google as offline conversions, which is what lets those platforms optimise toward revenue rather than form fills. That last step is what separates a serious B2B setup from a basic one, and it is the part of our CRM builds that attribution depends on most.

Buy Attribution Software Last, Not First

Software is the most visible answer because it is the one with a marketing budget behind it. It is also the one that can do least on its own, for three reasons.

  • It inherits the layers below it. A tool reading conversion events that count page views reports page views, with more decimal places.
  • Your own data is under-used. Website behaviour, CRM history, email and sales-engagement data are sitting unread at almost every company, and they cost nothing extra. We exhaust those before recommending anyone buy a platform.
  • No model sees the first touch that mattered. Multi-touch models mostly reallocate credit among channels that were all late. The work that made a buyer aware of the problem months earlier is not in any of their data, which is the subject of the last section.

None of that makes tools bad. It makes them the last step. We are not resellers and we take no vendor commissions, so when a tool genuinely is the constraint we will name it and say what it costs. More often the recommendation is less software, configured properly.

One cheap source deserves a mention because tools ignore it: ask. A required "how did you hear about us?" field on the form, or the same question on the first sales call, captures what arriving customers say in their own words. It is softer than a tracked click. It is also the only data source that can name a podcast, a conference conversation or a colleague's recommendation.

Where Can I Get Help With Marketing Attribution I Can Trust? Six Questions to Ask

Whoever you are talking to, an agency, a consultant or a software vendor, these separate help from a sales pitch.

  1. "What counts as a conversion, and who tested it?" Tracking should be installed, tested and verified firing before a dollar is spent. If they cannot say how it was tested, it probably was not.
  2. "Does the source survive into the CRM?" Ask to see one closed deal and the campaign it came from. One real example is worth a demo.
  3. "Do you import closed deals back into the ad platforms?" Otherwise the platforms keep optimising toward form fills while the revenue data that should steer them sits unused.
  4. "Which attribution window is this?" Every number should carry its window and the platform that reported it. A dashboard without one cannot survive a board question.
  5. "Will you add the platforms together?" The right answer is no. Each platform counts the conversions it can claim, and the same deal gets claimed more than once.
  6. "How will you measure what cannot be attributed?" Anyone promising clean attribution for brand and demand creation is selling the other half of your budget with a different label on the invoice.

What a Good Attribution Report Looks Like

Whoever helps you, this is the thing they should leave behind. Here is what we report on the accounts we run, and what we refuse to present as a result.

What we reportWhat we refuse to report as a result
Pipeline created and influenced, in dollarsPlatform numbers summed across channels and called revenue
Cost per opportunity, not cost per leadClicks and impressions as an outcome
Cost per qualified lead: leads sales accepted, not leads the form producedConversions with no definition behind them
Win rate by source, and sales acceptance ratePlatform ROAS presented as profit
The demand creation and capture split, judged separatelyEarly-stage data presented as a trend
The platform named and the attribution window stated on every viewAny dashboard without its attribution window

The ROAS line catches people out. Platform ROAS is the platform's own reported conversion value divided by spend. It is not audited revenue, it is not de-duplicated against your other channels, and it does not know your margins. It is directionally useful, so we report it, labelled as the platform's figure. LinkedIn reports what LinkedIn attributes, and the report should say so in those words.

There is also a discipline that makes attribution possible inside a single platform: change one thing at a time. We space structural changes three to five days apart, because a week with five changes in it produces a result nobody can attribute to any of them.

The Half of Your Marketing No Model Can See

In a long B2B cycle, the enquiry form records the end of a process it cannot see. Somebody reads for weeks, asks two people they trust, looks at three sites and often decides before they ever fill anything in. A campaign that produced the first visit in March gets no credit for the contract signed in June, and a last-click report will quietly recommend cutting the thing that started it.

That is not a tracking failure to be fixed with better software. A search click that becomes an enquiry the same week is easy to attribute. Work that made a buyer aware of a problem eleven months before they searched is invisible to every reporting tool in use. So the half with clean measurement keeps its budget, the half without it gets cut in a hard quarter, and two years later the pipeline stops growing while cost per lead climbs.

The fix is administrative. Split the budget on the report, with different targets, timescales and evidence for each half:

  • Capture (search, retargeting, lead-gen ads) is judged on cost per opportunity and win rate by source, inside a stated window.
  • Creation (content, video, brand, early account engagement) is judged on leading indicators read over a quarter: branded search volume, direct arrivals, and the share of new enquiries that already knew your name.

In an account-based programme the same logic puts coverage before revenue. On a nine-month cycle, revenue in month two is noise. How many target accounts are engaged at all, and how many people on each buying committee you have reached, move first and are the honest early evidence. Our account-based marketing guide sets out that report, and the demand generation guide covers why the two halves have to be budgeted apart.

The Bottom Line

If you are asking where can I get help with marketing attribution, start by finding the broken layer. Tracking and CRM problems are fixed by whoever runs your ad accounts and your CRM, a reporting problem by whoever owns the report, and the judgement problem only by leadership agreeing a second scorecard. Software helps once those hold, and not before.

  • Attribution breaks in four layers: tracking, CRM, report, judgement.
  • A model cannot repair the layers underneath it.
  • No CRM means fix the CRM first; nothing is measurable past the form without it.
  • Import closed deals back so the ad platforms optimise toward revenue.
  • Every number carries its platform and its attribution window; platforms are never summed.
  • Use your own first-party data and a "how did you hear" question before buying a tool.
  • Judge demand creation on leading indicators, on a separate line of the report.

Straight answers

FAQ

What B2B marketing and revenue leaders ask when they cannot say which spend produced the pipeline.

Where can I get help with marketing attribution for a B2B company?

Start with whoever runs your ad accounts and your CRM, because most B2B attribution problems live in the tracking and in leads arriving without a source. Ask them to define each conversion, attach the campaign source to every lead, and import closed deals back into LinkedIn and Google. Bring in an outside consultant for an audit, and software once that data is sound.

Do I need marketing attribution software?

Not to start. Attribution software inherits every gap in the tracking and CRM beneath it, and your own website, CRM, email and sales data are usually under-used. Fix those first. When a tool genuinely is the constraint, it is worth buying; often the better answer is less software, configured properly.

Why do Google, Meta and LinkedIn report more conversions than we had sales?

Each platform counts the conversions it can claim inside its own attribution window, and the same deal is often claimed by more than one. Their figures are not de-duplicated against each other. Report each one labelled as that platform's number with its window, and never add them together and call the total revenue.

What is offline conversion import?

It is pushing closed-won data from your CRM back into the ad platforms. Without it, LinkedIn and Google optimise toward whoever fills in a form. With it, they optimise toward the people who became deals, which is what separates a serious B2B setup from a basic one.

How do you measure marketing that cannot be attributed?

Separately, and agreed in advance. Put brand and demand creation on its own line of the report, judged over a quarter on branded search volume, direct arrivals and the share of enquiries that already knew your name. In an account-based programme, judge account coverage and buying-committee reach before revenue.
