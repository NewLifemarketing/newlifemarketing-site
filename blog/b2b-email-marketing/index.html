B2B Email Marketing

Email the Whole Account, Not a List

B2B/Lifecycle

by Cruz Lee Posted on October 26, 2026

Most B2B email marketing is still run like a newsletter: one list, one message, one send, and an open rate in the report on Monday. That model was built for selling to one person at a time, and a B2B purchase is not made by one person.

The email that moves a deal is narrower than that. It goes to people who have a reason to hear from you, it arrives in the inbox rather than the spam folder, it is written for the role reading it, and it is sent because something changed at the account, not because it is Tuesday. Then it is measured against pipeline, because open rates stopped meaning much a few years ago.

Start With Who Has Said Yes

Before the copy, before the sequence, there is a narrower question: who are you allowed to email? For anyone sending commercial messages into Canada, Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL) answers it, and the answer is stricter than most American playbooks assume.

CASL requires consent, and it recognises two kinds. Express consent is a clear yes, recorded. Implied consent exists only in specific situations the Act lists, and most of them expire:

  • A purchase or contract gives you an existing business relationship for two years from the transaction, or for as long as a written contract is in force and two years after it ends.
  • An inquiry or application gives you six months.
  • A conspicuously published address, such as a director's email on their company's website, counts only if the publication does not say they want no unsolicited messages, and only if your message is relevant to their business role.

Every message also has to identify the sender and carry a working unsubscribe, and the Act gives you no more than 10 business days to honour one. The maximum administrative penalty for a business is $10,000,000 per violation, which is section 20 of the Act and not a figure anyone in your legal team will find amusing.

The practical consequence for B2B email marketing is that a purchased list is close to worthless in Canada. The addresses carry no consent you can prove, and the burden of proving it sits with you. What you can build instead is a list that grows from real contact: event conversations, inquiries, customers, people who asked for something. It is smaller, and every name on it can be emailed.

B2B Email Marketing Starts at the Inbox

The second gate is technical, and since February 2024 it has had teeth. Google's sender guidelines require anyone sending more than 5,000 messages a day to personal Gmail accounts to authenticate with SPF and DKIM, publish a DMARC record, support one-click unsubscribe on marketing messages, and keep the spam rate reported in Postmaster Tools below 0.30%. Google recommends staying below 0.10%.

Those rules are written for personal Gmail addresses, and most B2B mail goes to company domains. That is not a reason to skip them. Corporate mail filters reward the same things: a domain that proves it is who it says it is, a low complaint rate, and recipients who actually engage. A sender who would fail Google's bar is usually failing quieter ones elsewhere and never hears about it, because a message filtered to junk does not bounce.

What to check this week

  1. SPF and DKIM records exist for every domain and tool that sends on your behalf, including the CRM, the marketing platform and any sales-engagement tool.
  2. A DMARC record is published. Google accepts a policy of none to start; move it toward enforcement once the reports show nothing legitimate is failing.
  3. Marketing messages carry one-click unsubscribe in the header and a visible link in the body.
  4. Cold outreach and customer email are not sharing a reputation with each other by accident.

None of this is marketing. All of it decides whether your marketing is read.

Stop Reporting Open Rates

An open is counted when a tiny image in the email loads. Apple's Mail Privacy Protection, an option Apple Mail users can switch on, hides the reader's IP address and downloads remote content privately in the background when the message arrives. The image loads whether anyone reads the email or not.

So the open rate on a modern B2B email marketing report is partly a measure of how many recipients use Apple Mail. It can rise while engagement falls. Optimising subject lines against it is optimising against noise.

Report what a buyer has to choose to do:

  • Replies, and who replied, by role and by account.
  • Clicks through to the pages that signal evaluation: pricing, comparison, implementation, security.
  • Meetings booked from a sequence.
  • Pipeline created and influenced across the accounts the email reached.

That is the same discipline we hold the rest of a programme to: pipeline created and influenced, platform-reported numbers labelled as platform-reported, and the attribution window stated on every view. An email number that cannot survive a board question should not be in the report that goes to one.

Write B2B Email Marketing for Each Role

One newsletter to a whole account assumes everyone in it wants the same thing. They do not. In an account-based programme we write the value proposition separately for the economic buyer, the champion, the technical evaluator and the blocker, because each is answering a different question, and the email that reaches them should do the same.

RoleWhat they need from an emailWhat to send
Economic buyerWhat changes in the business, in their numbersA short note with one outcome and the cost of the status quo
ChampionSomething to forward without rewriting itA one-page summary or business case they can send internally as it is
Technical evaluatorProof it works in an environment like theirsIntegration detail, a specification, a matching reference
BlockerThe risk, named and handledSecurity and procurement answers before they are asked for

Make B2B email marketing forwardable

The champion is the most important reader in the account, and the job you are giving them is to win an argument in a meeting you will not attend. The test we use for sales material applies to email too: can it be sent on without editing first? If the champion has to rewrite your email before forwarding it to finance, most will not forward it.

That rules out most of what clutters a B2B inbox: the long newsletter with eight links, the "just checking in", the case study with the useful number on page four. A forwardable email makes one point, carries the proof for it, and reads as sensible to someone who never saw the message before it.

Email is also one channel among several, not the whole programme. In our account-based work, sequences run alongside paid air cover on LinkedIn, direct mail, executive events and content built for the account. The email lands better when the name in the From line is already familiar.

Send When the Account Moves

Calendar-driven email asks one question: what are we sending this week? Signal-driven email asks a better one: which accounts just did something, and what do we do about it?

The signals that matter most are usually ones you already own. Website behaviour, email activity, CRM history and sales-engagement data are under-used at almost every company, and they cost nothing extra. A target account's technical lead visiting the integration page twice in a week tells you more than any send schedule.

A signal is only useful with a play attached. For each signal worth acting on, write down three things before anything triggers:

  • What marketing sends, and to which roles in the account.
  • What sales does, and whether the email is from a rep or from the company.
  • The window, after which the play stops and the account goes back to its normal cadence.

A signal with no play attached is just a dashboard. Wire the signal into the CRM so it reaches a person with the context already there, rather than into a report nobody opens.

Third-party intent data can sharpen this once the first-party layer is used up. It is directional: good at saying an account is researching a category, poor at saying it will buy from you. Treat it as a reason to look, not a reason to send another email to everyone at the company.

Nurture Is a Destination, Not a Bin

Every B2B programme produces people who are interested but not buying. The usual answer is to drop them into a nurture track and forget them, which is how a database fills up with contacts nobody has thought about in a year.

Routing decides more than copy here. Demand that fits a target account should go into the account programme, where the sequences above apply. The rest goes to self-serve or nurture, deliberately, with a reason to be there. Nothing should be thrown at sales just because it clicked something.

Good nurture email is mostly demand creation: a point of view on the problem your product solves, aimed at people who will buy in a year rather than this month. It behaves differently from a sales sequence, so measure it differently. Creation compounds over two to four quarters, and shows up first as branded search and better inbound quality before it shows up as pipeline. Judging a nurture track on this month's meetings is how good ones get cancelled.

Two rules keep nurture honest. Every contact in it should still have valid consent, which under CASL means the six-month inquiry clock matters. And every track needs an exit: a point where an engaged contact moves to a sales play, or an unengaged one stops receiving email altogether. Mailing people who never engage is the fastest route to the spam rate Google is measuring.

B2B Email Marketing After the Signature

Most of the effort in B2B email marketing goes into accounts that have not bought yet. The cheapest pipeline usually sits with the ones that have.

A signed customer is an existing business relationship in the plainest sense, and the emails that follow the contract do three jobs. Onboarding communication gets the people who use the product to value quickly. Adoption content keeps them there. And account mapping, pointed inward, finds the divisions and buying centres you have not sold to yet.

In our account engine every won account goes back to the start as an expansion objective: new divisions, new products, renewal protection and referenceability. Email is the channel that carries most of that, because the relationship already exists and the inbox is already open to you.

The mistake to avoid is handing customers to the same newsletter as prospects. A customer reading "see why teams like yours choose us" six months after choosing you learns one thing: nobody is paying attention to their account.

Smaller Lists, Sharper Sends

Email reaches a named person at a named account directly, and that is exactly why a lazy version of it gets ignored. Earn the right to send, make sure it arrives, write it for the person reading it, send it when the account gives you a reason, and judge it on what it does to pipeline.

  • Build the list from real contact, and know which consent covers each name and when it runs out.
  • Authenticate every sending domain with SPF, DKIM and DMARC, and keep complaints low.
  • Stop reporting opens; report replies, evaluation-page clicks, meetings and pipeline.
  • Write for each role on the buying committee, and make the champion's email forwardable.
  • Send on signals with a play attached, and keep customers out of the prospect newsletter.

Straight answers

FAQ

What B2B marketing leaders ask before they rebuild an email programme.

What is B2B email marketing?

It is email sent to people at businesses to help them decide to buy from you, and to keep them buying afterwards. In practice it covers newsletters, nurture tracks, sequences aimed at named accounts, signal-triggered follow-up and customer communication. The difference from consumer email is that the purchase is made by a group, so the best programmes write for each role in an account rather than one list.

Is cold B2B email legal in Canada?

Only where CASL's consent rules are met. Without express consent, implied consent covers a purchase or contract for two years, an inquiry for six months, and an address someone has conspicuously published, as long as they have not said they do not want messages and your email is relevant to their business role. Every message must identify you and include an unsubscribe that is honoured within 10 business days.

Do open rates still matter?

Not much. Apple's Mail Privacy Protection downloads remote content in the background, which fires the tracking image whether anyone reads the email or not. Replies, clicks to evaluation pages, meetings booked and pipeline from the accounts you reached are far more reliable.

What do Gmail's sender requirements mean for B2B senders?

Since February 2024, anyone sending more than 5,000 messages a day to personal Gmail accounts needs SPF, DKIM and DMARC, one-click unsubscribe on marketing mail, and a spam rate below 0.30% in Postmaster Tools. Most B2B mail goes to company domains, but corporate filters reward the same practices, so treat them as the baseline.

How often should a B2B company send marketing email?

As often as there is something worth sending to a specific person, and no more. A nurture track might run monthly; a signal-triggered play might send three messages in a week and then stop. A fixed calendar for the whole list is the weakest way to decide, because it ignores what each account is actually doing.
