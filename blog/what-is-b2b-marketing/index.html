What Is B2B Marketing

Six to Ten People Sign, and Most of Them Never Fill In a Form

B2B/Strategy/Go-To-Market

by Cruz Lee Posted on November 26, 2026

What is B2B marketing? It is the work of getting a business, rather than a person, to choose you. That sounds like a small change of customer. It changes almost everything about how the marketing has to work, because a business does not decide the way a person does.

A serious B2B purchase is signed off by six to ten people. Most of them will never fill in a form. They evaluate you over months, against the supplier they already have and against the option of changing nothing. And at any given moment, most of the companies that could buy from you are not looking at all.

Below: what B2B marketing is, who it is really aimed at, the four ways a programme is usually organised and when each one fits, where the channels come in, and what it should be measured on.

What Is B2B Marketing, in One Paragraph?

B2B marketing is everything a company does to make other businesses aware of it, consider it, choose it and keep buying from it. Business-to-business, as opposed to business-to-consumer. The customer is an organisation: a manufacturer buying components, a hospital buying equipment, a software company buying the tools its sales team runs on.

The textbook answer stops there and moves on to a list of channels. The useful answer starts with three things that are true of almost every B2B sale and false of most consumer ones:

  • A group decides. The person who finds you is rarely the person who signs, and neither of them is the person who can quietly kill the deal.
  • The decision is slow. Months, not minutes. The marketing that started a deal and the meeting that closed it can sit in different quarters.
  • The market is finite. Often you could write down the companies worth winning. A consumer brand cannot list its customers before it has them. A B2B company often can, and that changes how the budget should be spent.

Everything distinctive about B2B marketing follows from those three. If you want the side-by-side with consumer marketing, our post on marketing B2B vs B2C walks through it. This one stays on the B2B side.

Who B2B Marketing Is Really Aimed At

Not a lead. A committee. Six to ten people sign off on a serious B2B purchase, each of them asking a different question:

RoleTheir partWhat they actually want to know
Economic buyerSigns itDoes this move a number I am accountable for?
ChampionWants itWill this make my team's work measurably better?
Technical evaluatorTests itDoes it actually do what the deck says?
FinancePrices itTotal cost, payback and what it displaces.
IT, security, procurementClear itWhere the data lives, the terms, the vendor risk.
End usersLive with itWill we actually use this, or work around it?
The blockerStops itWhy change something that already works?

What Is B2B Marketing For, If Not Generating Leads?

For reaching the whole table. Usually only one of those people ever fills in a form, so marketing measured on form fills is speaking to one seat out of eight. The deal that results is single-threaded: it rests on one champion, and it stalls the moment that person goes quiet, changes role or gets overruled.

That is why the content a B2B buyer needs is so uneven. The champion wants the case for change. Finance wants a cost and a payback period. The technical evaluator wants proof the thing works, and the blocker wants a reason the status quo is riskier than it looks. A programme that only produces top-of-funnel awareness content has nothing to hand the people who decide.

In manufacturing the order runs differently again: an engineer shortlists you long before procurement ever calls. If your marketing only reaches buyers once they are buyers, the shortlist was written without you.

Most of the Market Is Not Looking Yet

The second fact that shapes B2B marketing is timing. At any moment, only a small slice of the companies who could buy from you are actively searching. The rest have the problem, or will have it, and are not in the market this quarter.

That splits the work in two:

  • Demand capture reaches the small slice already searching: search ads, review sites, comparison pages, retargeting. It converts this month and is easy to report.
  • Demand creation reaches everybody else, so that when they do start looking, you are already on the list. It compounds over two to four quarters and shows up first as branded search and better inbound enquiries, well before it shows up as pipeline.

Most B2B programmes only do the first. It feels safer because every dollar has a conversion beside it. The catch is the ceiling: capture competes for a finite group of in-market companies, against every competitor bidding for the same group. Add budget and cost per lead rises, because the extra money is bidding against the same rivals for the same buyers. If unbranded search volume is flat while spend climbs, more spend is a tax rather than an investment.

The timing problem gets worse in B2B because most buying research is anonymous. By the time an account identifies itself by filling in a form, the shortlist is usually written. Creation is how you get onto it before that point.

What Is B2B Marketing at the Strategy Level? Four Approaches

At the strategy level the answer is not a channel list. It is a choice about how to go to market, and four approaches cover most of it. They are not rivals; most mature programmes run more than one. The table is about when each one leads.

ApproachThe question it answersLeads when
Go-to-market strategyWho exactly is this for, why you over the incumbent, which channels earn the moneyLaunching, entering a new segment, or when nobody can say why budget sits where it does
Demand generationHow do we reach the market that is not searching yet, as well as the part that is?A broad market, no nameable list, pipeline that depends entirely on search
Account-based marketingWhich named companies are worth winning, and how do we reach their whole committee?Large deals, a list you could write down, sales and marketing willing to work one list
Intent and signal marketingWhich of those accounts is in market right now?A finite market, a long cycle with a visible trigger, and somebody free to act fast

Go-to-market comes first

The other three are ways of executing a decision that go-to-market strategy makes: the segment, the position against the incumbent, the channel plan, the pricing, the first 90 days. When the ideal customer profile is vague, reps spend their time on deals that were never winnable. Without a costed channel plan and a tracked funnel, budget goes to whichever channel had the best month. Our go-to-market strategy guide sets out how that gets decided and written down.

Account-based marketing flips the order

Most marketing generates leads and hopes the right companies turn up among them. Account-based marketing decides which companies are worth winning first, then aims marketing and sales at those organisations as one unit. If you can name the 20 companies that would change your year, ABM is the discipline of going after those 20 on purpose. It runs in tiers, one-to-one, one-to-few and one-to-many, chosen by what each account is worth. It is also a strategy rather than a campaign: if what you want is a three-month burst, you want demand generation instead. Our account-based marketing guide covers the whole method.

Intent data tells you where to look, not what will close

Signal-based programmes try to spot which target accounts are researching your category now. Bought intent data is directional: useful for deciding which 50 of 500 accounts get attention this month, and a poor reason to contact somebody who has shown no interest in you. Your own site is usually the better feed. A named contact returning three times in a week, or a pricing page read twice, is a first-party signal, and it is both accurate and free.

Where the Channels Fit

Channels are where most definitions of B2B marketing begin. They belong after the strategy, because the same channel does a different job depending on which approach it is serving.

  • LinkedIn lets you target by company, job title and seniority, which makes it the natural home for account-based and creation work. Clicks are expensive, and the question is what a customer is worth rather than what a click costs. A $90 lead that closes at 40% beats a $12 lead that never had budget.
  • Search, paid and organic, is capture. It meets the slice of the market already looking, and it is where the shortlist gets checked.
  • Content and thought leadership carry the argument to the people who will never fill in a form: the finance lead, the evaluator, the blocker. In B2B, content is the sales material the committee reads when your rep is not in the room.
  • Email and lifecycle keep a long decision warm and turn the first contract into a second. The first signature is the smallest revenue event in a good account.
  • The CRM is not a channel, and it decides whether any of the others can be measured. A lead that arrives without its campaign source attached cannot be traced back to the money that bought it.

For how those choices get made and sequenced, our post on B2B digital marketing strategy goes channel by channel.

What Is B2B Marketing Measured On?

Not lead volume. A pipeline full of marketing-qualified leads that sales refuses to touch is the most common way a B2B programme hits its targets and misses its revenue. The unit of measurement has to match how the product is bought, which means it moves down the funnel and up to the account.

Instead ofMeasure
Leads generatedPipeline created and influenced, in dollars
Cost per leadCost per opportunity, and win rate by source
Form fills from an accountHow many of the buying committee you have reached inside it
Impressions and clicksBranded search volume and the quality of inbound enquiries
New logos onlyShare of each account's spend, including expansion after the first deal

How the numbers are reported matters as much as which ones. Platform-reported values should be labelled as such. The attribution window should be stated on every view. And conversions from separate platforms should never be added together and presented as revenue, because each platform counts its own share of the same customer. Our post on what CAC in marketing really includes works through the cost side of the same discipline.

Where B2B Marketing Usually Goes Wrong

Most failing programmes are not short of effort or budget. They are running consumer-marketing habits against a business buyer. Five patterns come up again and again:

  1. Optimising for lead count on a product a committee buys. Volume targets met, revenue targets missed, and a running argument between sales and marketing about whose fault that is.
  2. No written answer to who it is for. A vague customer profile spreads the budget across companies that were never going to buy, and every one of them costs sales time.
  3. Two departments, two lists. Marketing works one definition of a good account, sales works another. Where that alignment does not exist, the programme fails and the tooling gets blamed.
  4. Cutting creation when a quarter tightens. Capture always looks better on a short window, so the half that fills next year's pipeline is the half that gets cut. The cost arrives a year later, as a pipeline that stopped growing.
  5. Treating the first contract as the finish line. Expansion is left to whoever remembers to ask at renewal, which in most B2B businesses means the cheapest revenue available has no owner.

None of the five is fixed by a new channel. Each is a strategy decision that was never made, or was made once and not written down.

The Bottom Line

What is B2B marketing, once the definitions are out of the way? Marketing built for a buyer that is a group, a decision that takes months, and a market that is mostly not looking yet. Decide who you are for before you choose channels. Reach the whole committee, not the one person who fills in a form. Build demand as well as capturing it, and measure pipeline and accounts rather than leads. The channels matter, but they are the last decision in the chain, not the first.

  • B2B marketing wins an organisation, and a committee of six to ten signs.
  • Most of the committee never fills in a form, so lead count measures one seat.
  • Most of the market is not searching; capture alone has a ceiling.
  • Go-to-market strategy comes before ABM, demand generation or intent.
  • Channels serve the strategy, not the other way round.
  • Measure pipeline, cost per opportunity and committee reach, not leads.

Straight answers

FAQ

What founders and marketing leaders ask when they are working out how a business should go to market.

What is B2B marketing in simple terms?

Marketing aimed at businesses rather than individual consumers. The company selling has to persuade an organisation to buy, which in practice means persuading a group of people who each care about something different, over a decision that usually takes months rather than minutes.

What are the main types of B2B marketing?

At the strategy level, four approaches cover most programmes: go-to-market strategy, demand generation, account-based marketing, and intent and signal-based marketing. Underneath those sit the channels: LinkedIn, search, content, email and events. The approach decides what each channel is for.

How is B2B marketing different from B2C marketing?

A consumer usually decides alone and quickly. A business decides as a committee, slowly, against an existing supplier and the option of doing nothing. That moves B2B marketing away from volume and toward reaching specific companies and every person involved in their decision.

Is account-based marketing the same as B2B marketing?

No. Account-based marketing is one way of doing B2B marketing: choosing the named companies worth winning first and aiming sales and marketing at them together. It suits large deals and a market you can list. Many B2B companies run it alongside demand generation rather than instead of it.

How should B2B marketing be measured?

On pipeline and revenue rather than lead volume: pipeline created and influenced, cost per opportunity, win rate by source, buying-committee reach inside target accounts, and expansion within existing customers. Platform-reported numbers should be labelled as such, with the attribution window stated.
