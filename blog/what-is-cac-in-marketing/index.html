What Is CAC in Marketing

Most CAC Figures Leave Out Half the Cost

B2B/Measurement/Strategy

by Cruz Lee Posted on November 25, 2026

What is CAC in marketing? Customer acquisition cost: everything you spent on sales and marketing in a period, divided by the number of new customers you won in it. One division, and it is the number a finance team reaches for first when it decides whether marketing is an investment or an expense.

The formula is the easy part. Most CAC figures that reach a board are wrong in the same direction, and not because anyone fiddled them. They count the ad spend and leave out the salaries, they divide this quarter's spend by customers that last quarter's spend produced, and they add creation and capture together into one number that hides which half is working.

Below: the formula, what belongs in it, why B2B makes it harder, how cost per lead turns into CAC, and how to bring it down without cutting a dollar.

What Is CAC in Marketing, and How Do You Calculate It?

The formula fits on one line:

CAC = total sales and marketing cost in a period ÷ new customers won in that period

Spend $60,000 across a quarter, win 12 new customers, and your CAC is $5,000. Those are illustrative figures, not a benchmark, and the point of the example is how little it tells you on its own. Is $5,000 good? That depends entirely on what a customer is worth and how long they take to pay it back, which we come to below.

Two versions are worth keeping apart from the start.

  • Paid CAC. Ad spend divided by customers who came from paid channels. Useful for comparing channels with each other.
  • Blended, or fully loaded, CAC. Every sales and marketing cost divided by every new customer, whatever the source. This is the one finance means, and the one that has to hold up when somebody checks it against the general ledger.

Paid CAC is always the smaller number, which is why it is the one that tends to appear in marketing decks. Reporting it as though it were the whole cost is the most common way a CAC figure goes wrong.

What Is CAC in Marketing Made Of?

The test for any cost is simple: would you still spend it if you stopped trying to win new customers? If not, it belongs in CAC.

Usually countedUsually left out, and should not be
Ad spend on every platformSalaries and commission for marketing and sales, including the founder's time spent selling
Agency and freelancer feesSoftware: CRM, automation, data and enrichment tools, call tracking
Event and sponsorship costsContent and creative production, video and design
Paid lead listsSales engineering and proposal time spent on deals that did not close

The right-hand column is often larger than the left. In a company where the founder still runs most first calls, their time can be the single biggest acquisition cost on the books, and it appears nowhere in an ad-spend CAC.

Existing-customer costs stay out. Account management, support and renewal work belong to retention, not acquisition. Mixing them in makes CAC look worse than it is and hides what expansion is worth.

Why CAC Is Harder in B2B

The formula assumes the spend in a period produced the customers in the same period. In a B2B sale it rarely does.

B2B buying cycles run months and involve several people: a committee of six to ten, evaluating you against an incumbent and the option of doing nothing. Spend in January can produce a signed contract in June. Divide January's spend by January's new customers and you are dividing by customers that last autumn's work bought.

What Is CAC in Marketing When the Sales Cycle Is Six Months Long?

It is a cohort number, not a calendar one. Two ways to make it honest:

  1. Lag the denominator. If your typical deal takes about four months from first touch to signature, divide this quarter's customers by the spend from four months earlier. Crude, and far closer to the truth than no lag at all.
  2. Cost the cohort. Tag every opportunity with the campaign and month that created it, and total the cost of each cohort once its deals have closed or died. Slower to report, and the only version that survives an audit.

Both depend on the source surviving the handoff. Leads need to arrive in the pipeline with their campaign source attached, or attribution dies the moment sales picks them up. For the same reason, anyone reporting week-one return on LinkedIn is measuring noise. The channel is aimed at a cycle measured in months, and so is its CAC.

From Cost per Lead to CAC

Most marketing reports stop at cost per lead, and cost per lead is not a small version of CAC. It is the first term in a chain, and every later step can multiply it.

A $90 lead that closes at 40% beats a $12 lead that never had budget. Run the arithmetic: at a 40% close rate, $90 leads cost $225 per customer in lead spend. The $12 leads that never had budget cost an infinite amount per customer, because there are no customers. The cheap lead looked better on every report until the one that mattered.

The chain, with illustrative numbers:

StepRateCost per unit
Lead—$150
Sales accepts it50%$300 per accepted lead
Becomes an opportunity40%$750 per opportunity
Closes25%$3,000 per customer, before salaries and tools

Each rate is a lever. Raise sales acceptance from 50% to 70% and the same lead spend produces a lower CAC without a single extra lead. That is why we report cost per opportunity, not cost per lead, alongside sales acceptance rate and win rate by source: together they show which step of the chain is costing you. Our post on what a lead in marketing actually is covers how to write the definitions those rates depend on.

One CAC Number Hides Which Half Works

Demand capture and demand creation behave differently, and a single blended CAC averages them into a number that describes neither.

Capture reaches people already looking: search, retargeting, review sites. It converts this month, and its CAC is easy to calculate and easy to defend. Creation reaches the much larger group who have the problem and are not looking yet. It compounds over two to four quarters and shows up first as branded search and better inbound before it shows up as pipeline.

Put both in one number and creation looks like waste in every quarter it is building, because its customers have not arrived yet. So it is the half that gets cut when a quarter tightens, and two years later the pipeline has stopped growing while cost per lead climbs.

Report them separately: capture CAC on a short window, creation measured on leading indicators such as branded search volume, direct arrivals and what new customers say when asked how they heard of you. Our demand generation guide sets out how that split works in practice, and the house rules that go with it: platform-reported values labelled as such, the attribution window stated on every view, and no summing of platform conversions across channels and calling it revenue.

What Is CAC in Marketing Worth Without the Other Half?

Very little. A CAC figure only means something next to what a customer is worth. On its own it is a cost with no return attached.

Finance looks at three things when it prices a marketing decision: total cost, payback, and what it displaces. Two ratios carry most of that conversation.

  • Payback period. How many months of gross margin from a new customer it takes to recover their CAC. A short payback means growth funds itself; a long one means every new customer is a loan until it is repaid.
  • Lifetime value to CAC. The gross margin a customer produces over the whole relationship, divided by what it cost to win them. Below one, you lose money on every customer you win, however good the campaigns look.

The worth side changes which CAC is acceptable. If a closed account is worth five figures, a $12 click against exactly the right person at exactly the right company is cheap. LinkedIn clicks commonly run between $8 and $15, which looks expensive next to other platforms and stops mattering once the question becomes what a customer is worth rather than what a click costs.

Expansion matters as well. In account-based selling the first signature is often the smallest revenue event in the relationship. A CAC judged only against the first contract undervalues every account that grows afterwards, and that is a reason to measure expansion rather than leave it to whoever remembers to ask at renewal.

Lowering CAC Without Cutting the Budget

The reflex when CAC rises is to cut spend, or to add spend and hope volume fixes it. Neither touches the cause. Most of the useful levers do not involve the budget at all.

  1. Sharpen who you sell to. When the ideal customer profile is vague, reps spend their time on deals that were never winnable, and every hour of it lands in CAC. Sharpening who you sell to, and what makes you the obvious choice, lowers cost of acquisition without touching the budget. Our go-to-market strategy guide covers how that decision gets made and written down.
  2. Fix the page before buying more traffic. On search, the cheapest route to a lower cost per lead is usually a better landing page, not a bigger budget.
  3. Stop the leak after the click. Leads you paid for leak out of a pipeline run on memory and good intentions. Route every lead from every source into one CRM record, and respond fast: speed to lead is the biggest single factor in whether a lead converts.
  4. Teach the platforms what a customer looks like. Push closed-won deals back to LinkedIn and Google as offline conversions, so they optimise toward revenue rather than form fills. Our CRM buildout is where that loop gets wired.
  5. Know when more spend is a tax. Once capture reaches most of the people currently looking, extra budget bids for the same finite group and cost per lead rises. If unbranded search volume is flat while spend climbs, the next dollar belongs in creation, not capture.

For what CAC tends to look like channel by channel, our research piece on customer acquisition cost in 2026 breaks it down.

The Bottom Line

Ask what is CAC in marketing and the textbook answer is one division: sales and marketing cost over new customers. The version worth reporting counts every cost, including the people, lags the spend to match your sales cycle, splits creation from capture, and sits next to payback and lifetime value. Get those four right and CAC stops being a number marketing defends and becomes one finance uses.

  • CAC is all sales and marketing cost divided by new customers won.
  • Paid CAC is not fully loaded CAC. Report the one finance means.
  • In B2B, lag the spend or cost by cohort, because the cycle runs months.
  • Cost per lead only becomes CAC after acceptance, opportunity and win rates.
  • Split creation from capture, or the slower half gets cut.
  • Judge CAC against payback and lifetime value, never alone.

Straight answers

FAQ

What founders and marketing leaders ask when finance wants to know what a customer really costs.

What does CAC stand for in marketing?

Customer acquisition cost. It is the total you spend on sales and marketing in a period divided by the number of new customers you won in that period. It measures what one new customer costs you, all in, not what one click or one lead costs.

What is the difference between CAC and CPA?

Cost per acquisition is usually the cost of one action on an ad platform: a form fill, a sign-up, a purchase. CAC is the cost of a paying customer, including salaries, tools and every channel. In a B2B sale the two can differ by an order of magnitude, because most of the actions a platform counts never become customers.

Should salaries be included in customer acquisition cost?

Yes, for the fully loaded figure. Marketing and sales salaries, commission and the founder's time spent selling are acquisition costs, and in many companies they are the largest ones. Leave them out only for a paid CAC used to compare channels, and label it as such.

How do you calculate CAC with a long sales cycle?

Either lag the spend by your typical cycle, dividing this quarter's new customers by the spend from the months that produced them, or cost each cohort of opportunities by the campaign that created it once those deals have closed. A calendar-quarter CAC on a six-month cycle divides by customers that earlier spend bought.

How can I lower CAC without spending less?

Tighten the customer profile so sales stops working deals that were never winnable, improve landing pages before buying more traffic, respond to every lead quickly from one CRM record, and send closed-won deals back to the ad platforms so they optimise toward revenue. Each lowers cost per customer without changing the budget.
