LinkedIn Ads Specs

The Small Numbers Decide the Ad, Not the Big Ones

B2B/LinkedIn Ads/Paid Media

by Cruz Lee Posted on November 19, 2026

LinkedIn ads specs are mostly a list of ceilings, and the ceilings are rarely what goes wrong. An image can be 5 MB, intro text can run to 3,000 characters, a video can last 30 minutes. The numbers that decide whether an ad works are the small ones: 150 characters before the intro text is cut off, 70 before the headline is, and a vertical image that only ever serves on a phone.

Every figure below was checked against LinkedIn's own Marketing Solutions help centre on November 19, 2026. That matters more than it sounds. The spec roundups that sit beside LinkedIn's pages disagree with each other, and several still carry limits LinkedIn has since changed. Where they differ, LinkedIn's page wins, and we say which page.

The LinkedIn Ads Specs That Change the Ad

Most of the published limits are generous enough that you will never meet them. Nobody uploads a 7680 x 4320 image or a 300-page PDF to the feed. The handful that matter are the ones that change what a reader actually sees, and every one of them is a copywriting or a design decision rather than a file-format one.

FieldShown in full up toHard maximum
Intro text (image, video, document)150 characters3,000 characters
Intro text (carousel)150 characters255 characters
Headline (image lead gen, video, document)70 characters200 characters
Description (image lead gen)100 characters300 characters
Carousel card headlinetwo linestruncated after two lines
Message ad subject60 characters60 characters

Read the first column, not the second. A 600-character intro is allowed, and a reader in the feed sees roughly the first 150 of it before a "see more". So the first 150 characters carry the whole ad: the problem, the claim, the reason to stop. Anything after that is for the few who already decided to read on.

Three more specs that quietly change results:

  • Vertical serves on mobile only. LinkedIn's help centre says paid vertical assets only serve on mobile devices. A campaign built entirely in 4:5 has opted out of desktop.
  • Narrow images become thumbnails. A single image under 401 pixels wide displays as a thumbnail, not a full-width ad.
  • Emoji are capped, and symbols get rejected. Intro text allows up to 10 emoji, and LinkedIn's video spec warns that ads with more than four emoji, symbols or repeated characters may be rejected.

Single Image Ads

Single image is the workhorse of sponsored content in the feed, and on our own LinkedIn ads work it is the one format used at all three campaign stages. Per LinkedIn's Single image ads advertising specifications page, the file can be JPG, PNG or GIF, up to 5 MB, and an animated GIF must be 250 frames or shorter.

RatioRecommendedMinimumMaximum
Horizontal 1.91:1 (recommended)1200 x 628640 x 3607680 x 4320
Square 1:11200 x 1200360 x 3604320 x 4320
Vertical 4:5 (recommended vertical)720 x 900360 x 6402430 x 4320

LinkedIn adds a detail the roundups usually drop: a vertical image at 1:1.91 gets borders added to its sides, which is why 4:5 is the recommended vertical. Square and vertical images can also be cropped when the same image is shared organically, so a file built for the ad is not automatically safe for the company page post.

Which LinkedIn Ads Specs to Build to First

Build the horizontal 1200 x 628 first, because it is the only cut that serves everywhere and it is the one LinkedIn lists as recommended. Add a 4:5 when the campaign is meant to win on phones, and treat it as a second asset rather than a crop of the first: anything that sat near the edge of a wide frame will not survive the trip to a tall one. The third-party guides that name one "correct" LinkedIn image size are answering a question LinkedIn does not ask. Three ratios are accepted, and the right one depends on where you want the ad to show.

Video Ads

Video is the format we use most in the prospect and nurture stages, because it carries more trust with a B2B buyer than a still and it is the format most competitors cannot produce. The LinkedIn ads specs for video are stricter than for images, and two of them catch people out: the ratio ceiling and the upload route.

SpecLimit
FileMP4, 75 KB to 500 MB, H.264 or VP8
Length3 seconds to 30 minutes; 15 to 30 seconds recommended for every feed and Audience Network placement
Frame rateunder 30 frames per second
AudioAAC or MPEG4
CaptionsSRT file, plain text, no custom formatting
Horizontal 16:91920 x 1080 or 1200 x 675 recommended; 640 x 360 minimum
Square 1:1360 x 360 to 1920 x 1920
Vertical 4:5 (recommended)720 x 900; 360 x 450 to 1080 x 1350
Vertical 9:16720 x 1280; 360 x 640 to 1080 x 1920
Thumbnail (optional)JPG or PNG, 2 MB, same ratio as the video

Anything wider than 16:9 is rejected even when the pixel count is inside the limits; LinkedIn's own example is a 1920 x 360 file. Video has to be uploaded to Campaign Manager directly from a desktop, so a YouTube or Vimeo link will not do, and Apple ProRes is not supported. The 500 MB ceiling is the current help-centre figure. LinkedIn's own B2B Marketer's Guide to Every LinkedIn Ad Type, dated September 2025, still lists 200 MB, and several spec roundups quote that instead.

Captions belong in the brief, not the export

LinkedIn accepts captions as a separate SRT file, and the spec allows text only. That means burned-in, styled captions are a creative choice made in the edit, and an SRT is a file somebody has to produce on purpose. Decide which one you are doing before the shoot. A video whose argument only lands with the sound on is relying on every viewer choosing to turn it on.

Carousel and Document Ads: LinkedIn Ads Specs for Swipe and Read

These are the two formats where somebody does more than look. A carousel asks for a swipe; a document asks for a read. Their specs reflect that, and both have a lead generation rule that changes how you build them.

CarouselDocument
What you upload2 to 10 image cardsUp to five files per upload
File typesJPG, PNG, non-animated GIFPDF, PPT, PPTX, DOC, DOCX
Size10 MB per card; 1080 x 1080 recommended, shown at 312 x 312Under 100 MB, 300 pages, 1 million words
Intro text150 shown, 255 maximum150 shown, 3,000 maximum
HeadlineTwo lines per card70 shown, 200 maximum; defaults to the file name
Lead gen ruleEvery card opens the same formPDF only

The carousel rule is the one that shapes strategy. Cards can link to different URLs, but with a Lead Gen Form every card opens the same form, so a carousel built to offer three different things is a carousel that can only collect one of them. Videos cannot be added to a carousel image ad either, and the CTA text is capped at 45 characters.

The document rules are mostly about what survives the upload. Video and animation inside the file show as static images. Hyperlinks only work after the reader downloads it. Layered PDFs need flattening, and pages of different sizes need fitting to one size. The headline also defaults to the file name, so "Q3_guide_FINAL_v4" will appear in front of buyers unless somebody changes it.

On our side, documents sit in the nurture stage: genuinely useful material delivered in the feed to people who have already seen the prospecting ads. That is also where we use Lead Gen Forms, because the ask is small and the form pre-fills from the member's profile. A document that is a sales deck with a cover page will be read as one.

Message and Conversation Ads

Sponsored Messaging lands in the inbox instead of the feed, and the specs are written in characters rather than pixels.

Message adsConversation ads
Subject60 characters60 characters
Body1,500 characters8,000 characters per message; formatting counts toward it
ButtonsOne CTA, 20 charactersUp to five per message, 50 per conversation, 25 characters each
LinksUp to three in the bodyThrough the buttons
RequiredA "Not Interested" option, added automatically"Not Interested" as one of the five intro buttons
ImageOptional 300 x 250 banner, desktop only, 2 MBOptional 250 x 250 image; 300 x 250 desktop banner

The 8,000-character limit on a conversation message is a trap. LinkedIn's own help page says Campaign Manager starts giving feedback past 500 characters because fewer characters can drive higher engagement, and that is the more useful number: an inbox message from a stranger gets read the way a cold email does, which is briefly or not at all.

We use conversation ads in the convert stage, against retargeting audiences only, and message ads sparingly. One caution we already state on our service page applies here too: Sponsored Messaging carries consent restrictions for recipients in the EU, so if your market includes Europe, check what can run before the campaign is built rather than after.

Thought Leader Ads: LinkedIn Ads Specs Borrowed From the Post

A Thought Leader Ad sponsors an organic post from a real person instead of the company page, so the ad carries their name and face. It has no specs of its own. LinkedIn's help page says the member's post has to meet the existing single image, video and document ad specifications, which means the post has to be built to ad specs before anybody decides to sponsor it.

  • Eligible: public text, single image, video, document, article and newsletter posts, from employees, creators and members who are 1st, 2nd or 3rd-degree connections. Eligible collaborative posts and LinkedIn Live event posts from attendees count too.
  • Not eligible: celebrations, polls, multi-image posts and reposts. To sponsor a repost, ask the original author and sponsor their post.
  • Objectives: Brand Awareness or Engagement, plus Video Views for video.
  • Permission: the author has to approve the request, the post has to be public, and their profile has to show their full name.

No CTA button, so the link goes in the copy

LinkedIn's help page is explicit that a call-to-action button is not available on single image, video or document Thought Leader Ads; event ones are the exception. The URL goes in the intro text instead, which puts it back inside the 150 characters a reader sees before the cut. A post written as a person's genuine opinion, with a link wedged into the second sentence, reads as exactly what it is. Write the post so that a link near the top belongs there, or accept that this format builds recognition and leave the asking to the stages after it.

On objective, we default to Engagement rather than Brand Awareness, because practitioner analysis across large Thought Leader Ad datasets consistently finds it delivering a lower cost per click through to the landing page. Older help content also described Thought Leader eligibility more narrowly than LinkedIn's current page does, leaving out document posts, which is the clearest single example of why a spec list needs a date on it.

Matching the Format to the Campaign Stage

LinkedIn ads specs answer "will it upload". They do not answer "should we build it". That decision follows the campaign structure. We run LinkedIn in three stages, prospect, nurture and convert, as separate campaigns, with the ask escalating only as the audience warms. Each format has one job in that sequence:

FormatStage we use it inThe spec that bites
Single imageAll three150-character intro; vertical is mobile only
VideoProspect, nurtureNothing wider than 16:9; desktop upload; captions as SRT
Thought LeaderProspect, nurtureNo CTA button; author approval; post must meet ad specs
DocumentNurturePDF only for lead gen; links work only after download
CarouselNot one we build the stages aroundOne Lead Gen Form for every card
ConversationConvert, retargeting only500 characters is the useful length; EU consent
MessageConvert, sparinglyOne 20-character CTA

The thing the table cannot show is that most accounts need three of these done well, not all of them done badly. Pick the formats by stage, then build each one to its specs. Working the other way round, starting from a list of every format LinkedIn offers, is how a campaign ends up with a carousel nobody needed and no video for the stage that needed one. The same thinking about who sees what, and when, is behind our account-based marketing work.

The creative itself matters more than any spec. Polished stock imagery reads as an ad and gets scrolled past; real photos, real numbers and a specific claim do better. That is a production problem as much as a design one, which is why we film, edit and build the video, document and single-image assets in-house through our creative production team.

A Pre-Launch Check for LinkedIn Ads Specs

Run this before anything goes to Campaign Manager. Each line is a spec from the sections above that turns into a rejected ad, a truncated message or a wasted placement when it is missed.

  1. The first 150 characters of every intro say the whole thing on their own.
  2. Headlines that need to be read are under 70 characters.
  3. There is a horizontal cut of every image, not only a vertical one.
  4. No video is wider than 16:9, every video is MP4 under 500 MB, and it is being uploaded from a desktop.
  5. Captions are either burned in or supplied as an SRT, decided before the edit.
  6. Document headlines have been renamed from the file name, and lead gen documents are PDFs.
  7. A lead gen carousel offers one thing, because every card opens the same form.
  8. Thought Leader posts are public, approved by their author, and do not rely on a CTA button.
  9. Messaging is cleared for any EU recipients before it is built.
  10. Every figure you are working from was checked on LinkedIn's help centre this month, not copied from last year's roundup.

If you want the platform's wider logic before the specs, our Reddit vs LinkedIn comparison covers when LinkedIn is the right channel at all.

The Bottom Line

LinkedIn ads specs are easy to meet and easy to misread. The big ceilings rarely matter. The small limits, 150 characters of intro, 70 of headline, a vertical cut that only serves on phones, a Thought Leader Ad with no button, decide what a buyer actually sees, so they belong in the creative brief rather than in the upload checklist.

  • Write every intro so the first 150 characters stand alone; the 3,000-character limit is not a target.
  • Build the horizontal 1200 x 628 first; add 4:5 for mobile as its own asset.
  • Video: MP4, up to 500 MB, nothing wider than 16:9, uploaded from a desktop.
  • A lead gen carousel collects one thing; a lead gen document must be a PDF.
  • Thought Leader Ads borrow the image, video and document specs and have no CTA button.
  • Choose formats by campaign stage, then build each to spec, and check the help centre's date before trusting a number.

FAQ

What paid media leads and marketing directors ask before building creative for LinkedIn.

What size should a LinkedIn single image ad be?

LinkedIn recommends 1200 x 628 pixels for the horizontal 1.91:1 ratio, which serves on desktop and mobile. Square ads are 1200 x 1200, and the recommended vertical is 4:5 at 720 x 900, which serves on mobile only. Files can be JPG, PNG or GIF, up to 5 MB.

What are the LinkedIn ads specs for video?

MP4, between 75 KB and 500 MB, from 3 seconds to 30 minutes long, under 30 frames per second, with AAC or MPEG4 audio. LinkedIn recommends 15 to 30 seconds so the ad is eligible for every feed and Audience Network placement. Ratios run from 16:9 to 9:16, and anything wider than 16:9 is rejected.

How many characters can a LinkedIn ad's intro text have?

Up to 3,000 characters on single image, video and document ads, and 255 on a carousel. LinkedIn advises keeping it to 150 characters to avoid truncation, and that is the number to write to: it is roughly what a reader sees before the text is cut off.

Can Thought Leader Ads have a call-to-action button?

Not on single image, video or document Thought Leader Ads, according to LinkedIn's help centre; those borrow the regular LinkedIn ads specs for their format and leave the button out. Event Thought Leader Ads include one. For the rest, put the URL in the intro text, ideally within the first 150 characters if the link is the point of the ad.

Do LinkedIn document ads work with Lead Gen Forms?

Yes, but only with PDF files. Other document ads accept PDF, PPT, PPTX, DOC and DOCX, under 100 MB and 300 pages. Hyperlinks inside the document only become clickable after the reader downloads it, so the form, not the file, should carry the ask.
