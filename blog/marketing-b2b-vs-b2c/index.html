Know Who Signs Before You Spend

B2B/Strategy

by Cruz Lee Posted on October 29, 2026

Most advice on marketing B2B vs B2C stops at one line: businesses buy on logic, consumers buy on feeling. It is the most repeated part of the comparison and the part the evidence supports least. The difference that actually changes your plan is simpler and harder to fix: who the buyer is, and how many of them there are.

We run both kinds of account. In the same quarter, one of our Meta campaigns sold to individual shoppers at $2.49 a purchase, and one of our account-based builds spent its first job working out that four store banners were really a single 98-store buyer. Same agency, same month, almost nothing in common. The tactics overlap. The arithmetic underneath them does not.

Marketing B2B vs B2C Starts With Who Counts as the Buyer

In consumer marketing the buyer is a person. One person sees the ad, wants the thing, and pays. Every number in the account describes that person: cost per purchase, average order value, how many come back.

In business marketing the buyer is an organisation, and an organisation is not a person. It has a budget owner, a user, somebody in finance and somebody whose job is to say no. The unit you are selling to is the account, and the first piece of work is counting the accounts correctly.

That sounds trivial until you try it. In an account research build we ran for a brand selling into Ontario's licensed cannabis stores, the market was 1,903 stores. Grouped by the licensed company that actually decides what goes on the shelf, it came to 625 buyers. Four banners that looked like four prospects were one 98-store chain. A plan that treated those as four leads would have sent four different messages to the same buyer and reported four times the reach it had.

Compare an e-commerce campaign from the same season: one prospecting campaign, three ad sets at $15 to $20 a day, lookalike audiences in three Canadian markets. It produced 1,994 purchases in a quarter. Nobody needed to know who those buyers were by name. The platform found them, because each one could decide alone.

Those two are the honest centre of marketing B2B vs B2C. One is a volume problem where the platform does the finding. The other is a list problem where you do.

One Signature or Six to Ten

A meaningful business purchase is approved by a committee of six to ten people, and most of them never fill in a form. That single fact changes almost every line of a marketing plan:

Selling to consumersSelling to businesses
Who decidesOne person, often in one sittingA committee, over weeks or months
What stalls itFriction at checkout, a weak offerThe champion going quiet, procurement, a security review
What the ad has to doStop the scroll and sellMake the name familiar before a rep calls
The unit you countPurchases and customersAccounts, and people reached inside each one
When money arrivesDaysOn your sales cycle, often two or three quarters

Why Single-Threaded Deals Die

A consumer sale with one decision-maker has one point of failure, and it is the moment of purchase. A business deal that runs through one contact has a different one: that contact. Single-threaded deals die when the champion goes quiet, changes role or gets overruled. So business marketing has to reach the rest of the committee, and arm the champion with something they can forward without rewriting it.

This is the job account-based marketing exists to do: pick a finite list of named companies, map who sits on each committee, and measure how many of those people you have reached. A deal where six of eight committee members already understand you is a different deal from one where only the champion does.

The Emotion Myth

The comfortable version says business buyers are rational, so business marketing should be specifications, comparisons and ROI calculators. The best evidence available says otherwise.

Les Binet and Peter Field, best known for mining the UK IPA Databank of advertising effectiveness cases for consumer brands, ran the same analysis on the B2B cases for LinkedIn's B2B Institute. Their report, The 5 Principles of Growth in B2B Marketing, found that the rules which drive consumer growth largely hold for business too, including the one most B2B teams resist: emotional campaigns outperform rational ones for long-term brand building.

Where they found a real difference was in the budget. In consumer marketing, efficiency is typically maximised with around 60% of the communications budget on brand and 40% on activation. In B2B their data suggested roughly 46% brand and 54% activation. They are explicit that this is a guiding principle from a small sample, not a ratio to follow precisely.

So the gap in marketing B2B vs B2C is a matter of degree, not of kind. Business buyers are people who remember names, feel risk and prefer the supplier they already recognise. The rational material still matters, because a committee needs something to circulate. It just is not the part that gets you shortlisted.

Most of the Market Is Not Buying Yet

Consumer categories with frequent purchases always have buyers in the market. Somebody is buying groceries, shoes and phone cases today. Business categories mostly do not work like that.

John Dawes of the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, writing for the B2B Institute in 2021, noted that companies change providers such as their principal bank around once every five years, so perhaps 5% of a market is buying in a given quarter. He was clear it is not a precise rule. The direction holds for most business purchases: at any moment, most of the companies that will buy in the next two years are not looking.

That is why capture alone runs out sooner in B2B. Search ads, review sites and retargeting reach the small slice already searching, everybody bids for the same slice, and cost per lead climbs while the pipeline stays flat. Demand generation is the work aimed at the rest: naming the problem in the buyer's words so that when it becomes urgent, they already know who solves it. We go further into the mechanics in our post on demand creation.

Where the Channels Overlap

Neither kind of business owns a channel. Both use search, social, video and email. What changes is the job each channel is given.

  • Meta. For consumer brands it is a sales channel. Broad audiences are the default and the creative now does most of the targeting, which is why we film our own: an account that runs one ad until it fatigues has nothing to rotate in. For business buyers it does a smaller job, retargeting the people on a named list who have already shown up.
  • LinkedIn. The reverse. Rarely worth its price for a low-cost consumer product, and the most useful paid channel for most B2B programmes, because it is the one platform that can target a named company and the roles inside it. That is exactly how a target account list and a buying committee are defined. See our LinkedIn Ads work.
  • Search. Captures intent in both. The difference is volume and value: a consumer category may have thousands of searches a day at a few dollars of margin each, a business category a handful worth a five-figure contract.
  • Content. For consumers it sells or entertains. For businesses it also has to survive being forwarded: the comparison against the incumbent, the business case, the security answers a blocker will ask for.

Marketing B2B vs B2C on the Same Platform

The common waste is running a business campaign with consumer settings. A lead-form campaign on a broad audience will produce cheap forms, because the platform is very good at finding people who fill in forms. Many of those people will never be buyers. The platform did what it was told; it was told the wrong thing. If the buyer is a committee, the campaign has to be aimed at the list, not at the cheapest available click.

Measuring Marketing B2B vs B2C

Consumer measurement is close to the money. Purchases, cost per purchase, return on ad spend and repeat rate arrive within days, and the platform can see most of them. In the e-commerce account above, every dollar of spend came back as roughly $81 in platform-reported sales, and the figures arrive while the campaign is still running.

Business measurement has to survive a long gap between the first touch and the signature. Reported the consumer way, it rewards the wrong thing: cost per lead is cheapest where the leads are worst. The measures that hold up are different:

  • Pipeline created and influenced, not lead count.
  • Cost per opportunity, not cost per lead.
  • Committee reach per target account: how many of the people who sign off have you reached.
  • Win rate by source, so a channel is judged on the deals it produces.
  • Branded search volume over a quarter, the clearest sign that more of the market knows your name.

The house rules are the same on both sides. Label platform-reported numbers as platform-reported, state the attribution window, and never add three platforms' figures together and call the total revenue. Platforms attribute independently and their claims overlap.

When You Sell to Both

Plenty of companies do. A manufacturer sells to distributors and to the public. A software product has a self-serve plan and an enterprise contract. A consumer brand wins its shelf space from retail buyers before a shopper ever sees it.

That last one is the cannabis build again. The product is a consumer good, but the sale that matters is won with the people at each licensed retailer who decide what to stock, and the province's rules on inducements mean the plays had to be built around education, training and samples. The consumer side needs creative and reach. The trade side needs a ranked list, a researched brief per key account and a reason for a buyer to take the meeting.

Run those as two plans with two reports. Blend them and the consumer numbers, which arrive fast and look good, will crowd out the trade work every time a quarter tightens. A go-to-market plan that names the motion for each customer type, and the budget each one gets, is the cheapest protection against that.

Marketing B2B vs B2C Comes Down to the Buyer

The tone of the copy is the least important difference between the two. What separates them is the unit you are selling to, how many people have to agree, how much of the market is buying right now, and how long the money takes to arrive. Get those four right and the channel choices mostly make themselves.

  • Decide whether your buyer is a person or an account, and count accounts by who actually signs.
  • Plan for the whole committee, not the contact who filled in the form.
  • Do not strip the emotion out of business marketing; the evidence says it works there too.
  • Fund demand creation separately, because most business buyers are not in the market today.
  • Measure business programmes on pipeline, committee reach and win rate, not cost per lead.

Straight answers

FAQ

What marketing and revenue leaders ask when one plan has to serve businesses, consumers or both.

What is the main difference between B2B and B2C marketing?

Who the buyer is. Consumer marketing sells to a person who can decide alone, often in one sitting. Business marketing sells to an organisation where six to ten people sign off over weeks or months. That changes what you count, which channels do which job and how long results take.

Is B2B marketing less emotional than B2C?

Less than people assume. Les Binet and Peter Field's analysis of B2B effectiveness cases for LinkedIn's B2B Institute found emotional campaigns outperform rational ones for long-term brand building in B2B, as they do in consumer marketing. Business buyers still need rational material to circulate internally.

How should a B2B budget split between brand and activation?

Binet and Field's B2B data suggested roughly 46% brand and 54% activation, against about 60/40 in consumer marketing. They describe it as a guiding principle from a small sample, so treat it as a starting point and adjust for your category and sales cycle.

Can the same channels work for marketing B2B vs B2C?

Yes, with different jobs. Meta is a sales channel for most consumer brands and a retargeting channel for business buyers on a named list. LinkedIn is costly for consumer products and the most useful paid channel for most B2B programmes, because it can target a named company and the roles inside it.

How do you measure B2B marketing if leads are the wrong number?

Pipeline created and influenced, cost per opportunity, how many people on each target account's buying committee you have reached, win rate by source and branded search volume over a quarter. Agree them before spending, so slower measures are not judged on a fortnight of data.
