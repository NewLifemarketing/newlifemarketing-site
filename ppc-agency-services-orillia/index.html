A difficulty of 7 means the unpaid results are held by directory listings and sites nobody has worked on. Ordinary work done properly takes them, which makes a permanent paid presence a strange purchase for a local business.

That does not make advertising useless here. It makes it narrow: an instrument for answering questions quickly, and a way of reaching the townships where a map result cannot place you.

Both of those have natural end points, which is the awkward part for a monthly arrangement: a well-run account in this market needs less work as it matures, and the fee ought to reflect that.

Saying so at the first call is more useful than anything that could be said afterwards about bidding, and it is the conversation most proposals avoid having.

None of which is an argument against paid media generally. It is an argument about sequence: in a market where the unpaid ground is cheap, buying the ground first and renting only what the ground cannot reach is the order that costs least over a year, and it is almost never the order a monthly proposal suggests.

The honest uses

Two Jobs Worth Paying For

Both are testable and neither is permanent.

Buying an answer

Does this offer land, does this page convert, which words do people who actually enquire use. A fortnight settles each, and the answers direct unpaid work that would have been guesswork.

Reaching the townships

Proximity is weighted heavily, so a town address cannot appear in the map for somebody fifteen minutes out. Paid reach has no such constraint and that is a specific, testable job.

Retargeting, underneath both

The audience has already visited and demonstrated interest, reaching them again costs a fraction of reaching a stranger, and it is the most common thing missing entirely from small accounts.

Each needs a stated end or a fixed job: a number, a date, or an answer. Without one of those three a test quietly becomes a permanent line item, which in a ten-search market is how a small budget becomes a large one with nothing to show.

If you run one

Four Settings Every PPC Agency Services Orillia Account Needs

All four are defaults, and all four default the wrong way in a thin market.

Geography drawn by hand, as named places rather than a radius, and reviewed against where paying customers actually came from. A circle drawn from the town either excludes the townships or reaches a great deal of nothing.

Match types narrowed. Broad matching in a market with little inventory will find something to match, and what it finds is rarely what you sell. In a thin market this is the single largest source of waste.

Automatic placements off, or constrained. Campaign types that choose their own placements inside a budget will always find cheap impressions, and cheap impressions here are cheap for a reason.

And a daily cap that reflects reality. If there are a handful of relevant searches a day, a budget built to spend every day is a budget built to find something else to buy, and nothing in the reporting will flag that as a problem.

Before any budget

Three Numbers, One Afternoon

They decide whether this channel makes sense for you at all.

What a customer is worth

Over the whole relationship rather than the first job. Without it, cost per enquiry is a figure with nothing to compare it against.

What share of enquiries convert

The bridge between the two, and the number most likely to be guessed. A guess here moves the defensible budget by a factor of two.

The maximum defensible cost per enquiry

Which follows arithmetically, and which in a town this size frequently comes out below what a click costs — a useful answer rather than a disappointing one.

Reaching that third outcome deliberately is far cheaper than discovering it after a quarter of spending, and it redirects the money to the profile and the pages, which carry no click charge at all.

What to fix first

The Free Things Outperform The Paid Ones Here

Two afternoons, and in a market at difficulty 7 they usually beat a quarter of advertising.

The Business Profile decides a large share of local results and is almost always wrong somewhere: categories chosen once, a service area that no longer matches where you go, lapsed hours, photographs from a previous decade.

The landing page is the second. Advertising magnifies whatever the page already does, so paid traffic over a page that loads slowly or buries the number is paying to lose people faster.

Test it the way a customer does: open it on a phone away from the office, time how long before anything is readable, try to call without pinching, and submit the form to check it reaches somebody who reads it.

Ten minutes, and it catches most of what a campaign would otherwise pay to discover slowly over a quarter it could not afford.

The third free thing is knowing what one customer is worth, without which no cost per enquiry can be judged at all. Those three take an afternoon between them, and an account launched before they are settled is not really an experiment, because nothing it reports can be interpreted.

Should we be advertising here at all?

Narrowly, and with an end in sight. At difficulty 7 the unpaid positions are held by directories and neglected sites, so paying every month for the same visibility is renting ground you could take. A budget earns its place answering a question in a fortnight and reaching the townships the map leaves out.

Our budget gets spent but nothing happens. Why?

Usually the account found somewhere else to spend it. A budget is an instruction, and in a thin market the defaults — broad matching, automatic placements, a radius nobody drew — will reach for whatever is available. The clicks are real and the enquiries are not, and nothing reports it as a problem.

What should we change first?

The geography, drawn as named places rather than a radius and checked against where paying customers actually came from. Then narrow the match types and constrain automatic placements. Those three take an afternoon and are worth more than any bidding change applied afterwards.

From three numbers: what a customer is worth over the relationship, what share of enquiries convert, and the maximum defensible cost per enquiry that follows. In a town this size the third frequently comes out below what a click costs, which is a useful answer reached deliberately rather than after a quarter of spending.
