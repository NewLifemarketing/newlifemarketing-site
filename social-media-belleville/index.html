One market, two boundaries

Social Media Belleville: The Census And The Map Disagree

Statistics Canada counts this as a single market. Every targeting tool offers it as two.

111,184
people in the Belleville–Quinte West CMA

Statistics Canada, 2021 Census of Population

$2.38
cost per click in the local web cluster

Semrush, Canada database, October 2026

The census treats Belleville and Quinte West as one metropolitan area because the people in them behave as one: they shop, work and hire across the boundary without noticing it exists.

An advertising account does notice. Municipal targeting is the unit every platform offers, so an account set up quickly ends up pointed at whichever name the business happens to use for itself, and the other half of its own market never sees anything.

Nobody reports this. The campaign runs, the figures look ordinary, and the gap is invisible because a platform only ever reports on what you asked it to reach.

At $2.38 a click the correction is cheap, which is the frustrating part: the most expensive mistake available here costs almost nothing to undo once somebody notices it.

Who is already following

Your Following Is Mostly Past Customers

Which makes it a poor guide to whether anything is working.

People follow a local business after they have used it, not before. The account therefore accumulates exactly the audience that needs no persuading, and every number the platform reports describes them.

Posting to that group is worth doing — it keeps you present, it answers small questions, and it is nearly free. It is simply not marketing, and treating the two as one is how a budget disappears without producing a customer.

The separation is a line on an invoice. Money that reaches non-followers is the growth half; everything else is maintenance, and maintenance should cost a fraction of what it usually does.

One question settles which you are buying: of the people reached last month, how many had never bought from you? If nobody can answer, nothing in the arrangement is aimed outward.

The three products

Social Media Belleville Is Three Products Under One Name

Quotes rarely say which is included, and in a small market the difference is stark.

Posting

Somebody makes and schedules content. Production, priced by output. On its own it will not reach anybody who does not already follow you, which in a town this size is most of the market.

Strategy

Deciding what is worth saying, to whom and when, including the burst-versus-drip question above. Cheap, infrequent, and the thing that makes the other two worth anything.

Distribution

Money spent reaching people who do not follow you, which in a finite audience means most of the market for a knowable sum. Where enquiries come from, and the line that shrinks when a blended fee is squeezed.

What you are left holding

The Account Should Be In Your Name

This is an administrative detail for as long as the arrangement lasts, and the whole asset on the day it ends.

An account opened by a supplier, audiences built inside their business manager, and finished material that exists only as published posts are three separate ways to be locked out of your own marketing.

None of it is malice. It is simply what happens when nobody specifies otherwise at the start, and it is impossible to unwind afterwards because audiences do not transfer and learning does not either.

So the arrangement to insist on is dull: the advertising account under your company, the audiences in your own business manager, and every finished file delivered to you as a file.

It costs nothing at the beginning. Asked for at the end, it is a negotiation you will lose.

Measurement

If The Phone Rings, Nothing Counts It

Most local businesses take enquiries by a route no report can see.

Calls, messages and people walking in are invisible to every advertising platform, and in a lot of trades they are the majority of the response. An account judged on form submissions alone is grading itself on the smaller half.

The fix is not a dashboard. It is a note beside the telephone: who called, what they wanted, and where they say they heard of you.

Three months of that beats any report, because it is the only record covering the routes nothing else sees — a mention at a counter, a screenshot sent between neighbours, a vehicle somebody noticed.

It also survives a change of supplier, platform and fashion, which no dashboard has ever managed.

Production, which is filming and editing and happens on days you can count, from management, which is planning, posting and replying and happens every week. Straight answers

Social Media Belleville FAQ

Straight answers before you ever book a call.

What should we spend?

Work backwards from a customer rather than forwards from a budget. If a customer is worth four hundred dollars and one enquiry in four becomes one, a hundred dollars an enquiry is break-even and anything below it is profit. Without those two figures, a monthly number is somebody else’s benchmark.

Why did results drop after a few weeks?

Almost always because the audience has seen it. In a finite market the people inclined to respond do so quickly, and the remaining impressions reach people who have already decided not to. Nothing in the account changed, which is why it is confusing. New material and a rebuilt audience are the fix, planned in advance rather than improvised.

How often should we post?

As often as there is something worth showing, which in a small market is less than a daily schedule implies. Two posts a week of real work beats five of stock graphics, because the second pattern teaches a small audience to scroll past you and there are no new people arriving to replace them.

Should we target Quinte West too?

If you serve it, yes, and the gain is proportionally larger here than it would be in a city. The two municipalities are counted as one metropolitan area of 111,184 because they function as one labour market, so adding the second can be a double-digit increase in reachable audience for no extra spend.
