How Much Shopping Has Really Moved Online? 19 Sources, Six Years After the Surge

E-commerce/Retail/Research

NewLife Research Posted on November 2, 2026

This analysis draws on data from 19 credible sources, including government statistics, industry reports and company data.

In September 2026, 28.3% of British retail sales were made online.10 In Canada, the August figure was 7.6%.8 The United States sat between them at 17.1% in the second quarter.1 Three wealthy, connected, English-speaking markets, and the share of retail spending that happens online differs between them by almost four times.

What they have in common is the shape of the last six years. Online spending jumped in 2020. In Britain and Canada a large part of that jump has since been given back. In the US it was not, but the climb since has been slow. And in the US, Germany and China, the latest year-over-year figures still have online growing faster than retail as a whole.

We'll explore:

  • The pandemic peak, and what came after
  • The US kept its gains, and then slowed
  • China: a plateau at about a quarter
  • Germany and France: about one euro in eight
  • Australia: two numbers, both correct
  • Nearly every shopper is online. Most spending is not
  • Online still grows faster than stores
  • What the data does and does not show

The pandemic peak, and what came after

The UK publishes the longest clean monthly series. The Office for National Statistics measured internet sales at 20.2% of retail in January 2020. By November 2020 the figure was 37.2%, and in January 2021 it reached 37.8%.10 That is the highest reading in the series.

It did not hold. On an annual basis, the online share went from 19.2% in 2019 to 28.1% in 2020 and 30.7% in 2021, then fell to 26.6% in 2022.10 Since then it has crept up a few tenths of a point a year: 26.7% in 2023, 27.1% in 2024, 27.5% in 2025.10 Every month of 2026 so far has landed between 27.3% and 28.7%.10

So the UK is well above where it started. On annual figures the share rose 11.5 points from 2019 to 2021. By 2025, 8.3 of those points remained.

Canada's curve is the same shape at a lower level. Statistics Canada measured online at 2.4% of retail sales in 2016 and 4.0% in 2019.9 In April 2020 the share hit a record 11.4%, up from 3.8% a year earlier.9 Between February and May 2020, total retail sales fell 17.9% while retail e-commerce sales nearly doubled, up 99.3%.9 By August 2026, seasonally adjusted e-commerce sales were $5.6 billion, or 7.6% of total retail trade.8

That is nearly double the pre-pandemic share, and about two-thirds of the peak.

Figure 1: Before, at the Peak, and Now: Online Share of Retail (%)

Source: 9, 8, 10, 7, 6, 1. Canada: 2019, April 2020, August 2026. UK: 2019, January 2021, September 2026. US: Q4 2019, Q2 2020 as first published, Q2 2026. Canadian 2020 figures are unadjusted; the 2026 figure is seasonally adjusted.

These measures are not built the same way. Canada's retail figures leave out several common online purchases, including travel and accommodation bookings, tickets and financial transactions.8 The 2020 Canadian proportions are unadjusted, while the August 2026 figure is seasonally adjusted, so the comparison is close but not exact.9,8 The gap between countries is real. Its exact size depends on the definitions.

The US kept its gains, and then slowed

The American series did something the British and Canadian ones did not. On annual figures, it never went back down.

The Census Bureau put e-commerce at 11.4% of seasonally adjusted US retail sales in the fourth quarter of 2019, and at 11.0% for the full year.7 In the second quarter of 2020, as first published, online sales jumped 31.8% in a single quarter to $211.5 billion and reached 16.1% of retail, while total retail sales fell 3.9%.6 For 2020 as a whole, e-commerce grew 32.4% to $791.7 billion, or 14.0% of retail.5

After that the share held, then rose slowly. It was 14.6% in both 2021 and 2022.4 It reached 15.3% in 2023 and 16.1% in 2024.3 In 2025 Americans spent an estimated $1,233.7 billion online, 16.4% of all retail sales.2

The most recent quarter is the strongest in a while. US online sales reached $340.2 billion in the second quarter of 2026, 12.2% higher than a year earlier, against 6.7% growth in total retail.1 That took the share to 17.1%.1

Put the endpoints side by side. In six years the US share has moved from 16.1% at the 2020 peak to 17.1% today.6,1 One point. Between 2019 and 2020 it moved three.5

The Census Bureau notes that its annual revisions have since replaced the second-quarter figures, and revised numbers are due with the third-quarter release on November 19, 2026.1 The 17.1% here is the figure as published in August.

Source: 7, 5, 4, 3, 2, 10, 14, 13, 12. Annual figures as published by each agency. China: online retail sales of physical goods as a share of total retail sales of consumer goods; 2019, 2023 and 2025 shown.

China: a plateau at about a quarter

China's online share has stopped rising.

The National Bureau of Statistics recorded online retail sales of physical goods at 8,523.9 billion yuan in 2019, 20.7% of total retail sales of consumer goods.14 By 2023 they were 13,017.4 billion yuan and 27.6%.13 In 2025 they grew 5.2% to 13,092.3 billion yuan, but the share slipped to 26.1%.12

The 2026 releases no longer print the share. They do print both inputs. From January to September 2026, online retail sales of goods were 9,506.0 billion yuan, up 3.8%, and total retail sales of consumer goods were 37,000.1 billion yuan, up 1.1%.11 Divided out, that is 25.7%. The NBS also renamed the measure from "physical goods" to "goods" this year, with the definition and coverage unchanged.11

So the Chinese share is moving sideways at a level just under the UK's. The movement is now inside retail formats. In the first nine months of 2026, sales at larger convenience stores and supermarkets rose 5.2% and 4.1%, while department stores fell 2.9% and brand-exclusive stores fell 10.3%.11

Germany and France: about one euro in eight

The two largest economies in the EU sit well below the UK.

Germany's retail federation, the HDE, puts online at 13.5% of the German retail market in 2025, up from 13.4% the year before.17 German online retail grew 3.9% to EUR 92.3 billion, faster than offline retail at 2.9%.17 Online revenue is EUR 19.3 billion higher than in 2020.17 In groceries and drugstore goods the share is far lower, 5.0%.17 Marketplaces carried 56.7% of German online revenue in 2025.17

France's e-commerce federation, Fevad, estimates that online sales of products made up 12% of French retail in 2025.18 Product sales grew 4% to EUR 76.1 billion.18 Most French online spending is not retail at all: services grew 9% to EUR 120.3 billion, taking total French e-commerce to EUR 196.4 billion.18 The French made 3.2 billion online transactions in 2025, up 10%.18

That split is a reminder of why the headline figures differ. Canada's and France's retail-share figures both leave services out.8,18 Travel bookings, tickets and other services bought online sit outside them.

Australia: two numbers, both correct

Australia shows how much the method matters in one country.

The Australian Bureau of Statistics put online at 12.7% of retail in June 2025, in original terms, up from 11.6% a year earlier.15 For food retailing the share was 7.2%. For everything else it was 19.0%.15 That June release was the last: the ABS has ceased the Retail Trade publication, so there is no newer official figure.15

Australia Post reports that Australians spent $82.6 billion online in 2025, up 14% from $72.5 billion, and that 24% of all retail spend is now made online.16

12.7% and 24% are not a contradiction. They are different denominators and different data. The ABS figure comes from retailers' reported sales. Australia Post's comes from its own parcel data and extrapolation, on a wider base.15,16 If you quote either one, quote where it came from.

Source: 10, 11, 16, 1, 17, 15, 18, 8. The latest published figure from each source. Definitions differ: China is derived from two NBS totals, Australia Post uses parcel data, France covers products only.

Nearly every shopper is online. Most spending is not

The share of people who shop online is no longer the constraint.

Eurostat found that 78% of EU internet users bought or ordered goods or services online in 2025, up from 62% in 2015.19 Internet use itself covers 95% of Europeans aged 16 to 74.19 In Australia, 82% of households, 9.8 million of them, shopped online in 2025, and 41% of those did so at least every two weeks.16

Set those against the spending figures. Close to four in five European internet users buy online, and online is about one euro in eight of German and French retail.19,17,18 Germany is one of the countries where the share of online buyers barely moved over a decade, up 3 points between 2015 and 2025.19

The gap between those numbers is where the remaining growth sits. It is not about recruiting people who have never bought anything online. Almost everyone has. It is about which purchases they still make in a store.

Online still grows faster than stores

In every market here with a recent year-over-year growth figure, online outgrew retail as a whole.

In the US, online sales rose 12.2% year over year in the second quarter of 2026 against 6.7% for total retail.1 For 2025, it was 5.4% against 3.5%.2 In Germany, online grew 3.9% in 2025 and offline 2.9%.17 In China, online goods sales rose 3.8% in the first nine months of 2026, while total retail rose 1.1%.11 Canada publishes only month-to-month changes, and there August was even: e-commerce sales rose 1.1% while total retail rose 1.2%.8

The exception in this set is worth noting. In 2022, US e-commerce grew 7.7% while total retail grew 8.1%, the one year in the series when stores outpaced online.4 It is also the year the UK share fell 4.1 points.10

Source: 1, 2, 17, 11, 4. Year-over-year growth. Germany compares online with offline retail rather than total retail.

The gaps are narrow outside the US. Germany's is 1 point, and with online at 13.5% of the market, that gap moved the share by a tenth of a point in 2025.17

What the data does and does not show

Taken together, the 19 sources support four findings.

Outside the US, a large part of the pandemic surge was temporary. Canada went from 4.0% to 11.4% and back to 7.6%, giving up about half the jump.9,8 The UK gave back 3.2 of the 11.5 points it gained between 2019 and 2021.10

The underlying trend is slow and still upward. The US gained one point of share in six years after its peak.6,1 The UK has gained about a point since 2022.10 Germany gained a tenth of a point in 2025.17 China has edged down from its 2023 high.13,12

Most retail spending still happens in stores, everywhere measured. The highest current official share in this set is the UK's 28.3%.10 In Canada, more than nine dollars in every ten of retail spending is still offline.8

The figures are not interchangeable. Canada leaves out travel and tickets.8 France's 12% covers products only, while most French online spending is services.18 Australia has an official figure of 12.7% and a parcel-based one of 24%.15,16 China's 2026 share is arithmetic on two published totals.11

What the data does not show is a single global "online share of retail". Each statistic answers a slightly different question. The honest answer to "how much shopping has moved online" is a range: about one retail dollar in thirteen in Canada, about one in six in the US, about one in four in China and a little more than that in the UK.8,1,10,11
