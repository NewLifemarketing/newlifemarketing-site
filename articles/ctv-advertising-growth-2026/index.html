Streaming TV Ad Money Is Still Growing. The Price of Each Ad Is Falling.

Connected TV/Advertising/Research

NewLife Research Posted on October 19, 2026

This analysis draws on data from 18 credible sources, including company financial filings and industry reports.

Roku sold 40% more video ad impressions in the second quarter of 2026 than a year earlier, and earned 12% less for each one.10 Its advertising revenue still rose 25%, to $673 million.9,10 Volume is outrunning price.

That is the connected TV (CTV) market in mid-2026. Viewing is moving to streaming, ad budgets are following, and the platforms are adding ad inventory faster than advertisers are adding money. The filings below show the same pattern at company after company.

We'll explore:

  • Where TV viewing has gone
  • Where the ad money is going
  • The streaming platforms: growth at every company, at very different rates
  • Linear TV is where the decline shows
  • Why more ads are earning less each
  • What the data does and does not show

Where TV viewing has gone

Streaming took 49.0% of all US TV viewing in July 2026, up 0.5 points from June, according to Nielsen's Gauge.5 Broadcast held 19.5% and cable 18.7%.5 YouTube alone took a record 14.2%.5

Advertisers care most about the slice of viewing that carries ads. In the first quarter of 2026, streaming took a record 46.6% of ad-supported TV viewing, ahead of broadcast at 28.2% and cable at 25.2%.4 Ad-supported TV was nearly 73% of all TV viewing.4 Nielsen notes that the Gauge has not yet moved to new ARF DASH-based universe estimates, a change planned for fall 2026, so these shares may be restated.4

Figure 1: Share of US Ad-Supported TV Viewing, Q1 2026

Source: 4

Free, ad-supported services are part of that shift. Fox's Tubi grew total view time 20% in fiscal 2026, to more than 13 billion hours, and averaged about 2.2% of all TV viewing.18 More than 100 million streaming households worldwide used a Roku TV operating system device in April 2026.8

Where the ad money is going

The money is following the audience, though not at one speed. In the IAB/PwC census, US digital video advertising, which includes CTV, social video and online video, generated $78 billion in 2025, up 25.4%.1 That was faster than the 13.9% growth of the $294.6 billion internet ad market as a whole, and faster than video's own 19.2% growth the year before.1

For 2026, IAB's video spend report projects digital video will pass $80 billion, up 11%, nearly 20% faster than the total ad market.2 Digital video is expected to take more than 60% of total TV and video ad spend for the first time.2 The same report expects social video to grow 13% and CTV 11%, the first year social video outpaces CTV.2 IAB says digital video growth is now roughly half its 2022 peak rate.2

IAB's separate buyer survey gives a higher CTV number. In September 2026, 211 brand and agency decision-makers projected CTV spend would grow 15.6% in 2026, up from 13.8% when 205 buyers were asked in January.3 They projected linear TV spend would fall 1.5%, versus a 1.7% decline in January.3 Social media sat just above CTV at 16.5%, and digital video excluding CTV at 9.4%.3 Total US ad spend growth was revised to 12.3%, up 2.8 points from January.3

The two IAB figures, 11% and 15.6%, come from different studies with different respondents and timing. Both put CTV ahead of the total market, and both put linear TV behind it.

Source: 3

The streaming platforms: growth at every company, at very different rates

Netflix is the fastest grower from the smallest base. In 2025, its third year selling ads, Netflix's ad revenue grew more than 2.5 times to over $1.5 billion.6 It forecast a rough doubling for 2026, and in July said it remained on track for about $3 billion.6,7

Roku, which began reporting advertising as its own segment in 2026, grew ad revenue 27% in the first quarter, to $612.7 million from $482.8 million.8 Ad spend through third-party programmatic partners rose more than 40%.8 In the second quarter, ad revenue grew 25% to $673 million, and spend from advertisers outside media and entertainment was more than a third of Roku Experience ad revenue.9 On June 15, 2026, Fox agreed to acquire Roku.9

The legacy media companies grew streaming ads far more slowly. Disney's Disney+ and Hulu advertising revenue rose 3%, to $851 million from $830 million, in the quarter ended June 27, 2026.14 Warner Bros. Discovery's streaming ad revenue rose 9%, to $306 million from $282 million, on more ad-lite subscribers.15 Paramount's direct-to-consumer advertising grew 8%, with Paramount+ ad revenue up more than 30%.16 Comcast's Peacock generated $1.9 billion of revenue, up from $1.2 billion, and posted its first profit, $189 million of adjusted EBITDA against a $101 million loss a year earlier.17 Comcast does not split Peacock's revenue between ads and subscriptions.17

The ad-tech companies that sell and buy CTV inventory report the strongest rates. Magnite's CTV contribution ex-TAC rose 36% in the second quarter, to $97.1 million, while its display and online video business (DV+) grew 2%, to $92.5 million.12 For full-year 2025, CTV was $304.2 million for Magnite, up 17%, and 45% of the total.11 Its management said it was "witnessing spend shift into CTV from various areas of digital advertising."11 MNTN, which sells self-serve CTV ads to performance marketers, grew revenue 21% to $82.5 million.13

Source: 9, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16

Linear TV is where the decline shows

The same filings show linear ad revenue shrinking. Warner Bros. Discovery's Global Linear Networks advertising fell 27%, to $1,429 million from $1,953 million, on 17% lower domestic audiences.15 The loss of the NBA accounted for 20 points of that decline, which leaves roughly 7 points from everything else.15 Across the whole company, advertising fell 22%, to $1,724 million.15

Paramount's TV Media advertising fell 14%.16 About 8 points came from lapping NCAA Final Four advertising and about 3 points from selling Telefe and Chilevision, partly offset by about 2 points of political advertising.16 Disney's total Entertainment advertising, which combines its streaming and linear networks, fell 1% to $1,625 million even as its streaming ads grew.14

Live sports can still lift a TV company's ad line. NBCUniversal's domestic advertising rose 55.0% to $2,163 million in the World Cup quarter, and 23.5% without the World Cup, helped by the NBA and higher revenue at Peacock.17 The sports rights moved between companies. The NBA's absence cut Warner Bros. Discovery's linear ad growth by 20 points in the same quarter.15

Why more ads are earning less each

Streaming has an inventory problem that linear TV never had: it can always add more. Every additional hour watched on an ad-supported tier is more ad slots for sale, and viewing is growing.5,18

Roku's 10-Q spells out the result. Its second-quarter ad revenue rose $133.8 million, or 25%, "primarily due to an increase in video ad impressions delivered."10 Impressions rose 40% and the average price per impression fell 12%.10 For the first half of 2026, impressions rose 48% and price per impression fell 13%, for 26% revenue growth.10

Disney reports the same mechanics on a smaller scale. Its streaming ad revenue rose because impressions added 8%, while lower rates took away 4%.14 Disney attributed the soft quarter to "continued marketplace supply growth," which it said created a softer demand environment.14

Source: 10, 14. Disney reports the effect of impressions and rates on revenue growth, not the change in each.

Falling prices are pulling in new buyers. MNTN counted 4,225 active Performance TV customers over the trailing twelve months to June 2026, up from 3,020 a year earlier and 1,746 two years earlier.13 At Roku, advertisers from outside media and entertainment are now more than a third of its Roku Experience ad revenue.9

What the data does and does not show

Thirteen of these 18 sources are companies reporting their own results. The filings are audited or filed with the SEC, but each company defines its own ad line. Magnite reports contribution ex-TAC rather than revenue, Comcast does not separate Peacock's ads from its subscriptions, and Fox does not report Tubi revenue at all.11,17,18 The three IAB sources are forecasts and surveys, not audited totals, and they disagree on CTV's 2026 growth rate by more than four points.2,3

Within those limits, the sources agree on three things. Streaming now holds the largest share of ad-supported viewing.4 Every streaming ad line in this set grew in the second quarter of 2026, while each separately reported linear ad line fell.9,12,14,15,16 And at the two companies here that disclose pricing, the price of a streaming impression went down.10,14
