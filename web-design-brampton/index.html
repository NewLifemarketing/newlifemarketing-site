Questions

Web Design In Brampton: Common Questions

Why does our site get traffic but no calls?

The most common reason in Brampton is that the site is built for people who already know you, in a market where most do not. A slogan, a services list and stock photography confirm a reputation; they do not create one. A visitor who has never heard of your business is looking for how long you have operated, who works there, what you have actually finished and whether anyone vouches for you. If those four are missing, the traffic is arriving and finding no reason to act, and no amount of redesign fixes that.

Do we need a rebuild or just fixes?

More often fixes, and the test takes about an hour. Open your site on a phone and ask whether a newcomer could tell what you do, where you work, how long you have been doing it and whether anybody vouches for you. If those are missing, that is content work on the site you already have. A rebuild is genuinely the answer when the platform is unsupported, the structure cannot carry the services you now sell, or you do not own the accounts it is built on.

Should the site mention specific Brampton neighbourhoods?

Where you genuinely serve them, yes. Brampton people describe where they are as Bramalea, Springdale, Castlemore or Heart Lake rather than as the city, and somebody new to the area genuinely does not know whether they fall inside your service area. Naming them is useful and honest. What to avoid is a near-identical page per neighbourhood, which is a doorway pattern and risks the pages you already have. If an area does not have something specific to say about it, it belongs as a line on an existing page.

How much should a website cost here?

The honest driver is how much writing and photography is needed, not the design. A Brampton business that arrives with finished-job photographs and its history written down is a genuinely cheaper project, and any quote that does not move when you supply those is not itemised properly. In Brampton specifically, the proof material is usually the missing piece rather than the design, so that is where we would expect the budget to go first.

Will a new site lose our Google rankings?

It can, when addresses change without redirects, copy is cut for visual reasons, or the new build is slower on a phone. All three are decisions rather than accidents. Every existing address and what it ranks for gets mapped before design starts, so whatever is working keeps its address or gets a redirect. If you are comparing quotes, ask each one how they handle this specifically. The answer is one of the clearest signals of what you are actually buying.
