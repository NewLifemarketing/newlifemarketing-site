Facebook Ads Brampton

Too Many People, Not Too Few

A facebook ads brampton campaign faces the opposite problem to one in a small town: with 656,480 residents the audience never runs out, so nothing in the account ever forces a correction and the discipline has to be imposed rather than inherited.

The inverted constraint

Facebook Ads Brampton Is Limited By Exclusion, Not Reach

In a small market the audience saturates and tells you. Here it does not.

In a town of fifty thousand a campaign reaches everybody who matters within a month and then starts paying to repeat itself. Frequency climbs, cost per result climbs with it, and the account complains loudly enough that somebody acts.

Here a broad audience is effectively bottomless. You can spend indefinitely without reaching the same person too often, which sounds like freedom and is mostly a trap: the spend continues, reach climbs, cost per result drifts upward, and no automatic signal says the targeting is wrong.

So the useful work is subtraction. Excluding the people who will never buy, the placements that produce clicks and nothing else, and the geography you cannot serve, until the cost per enquiry moves.

A hundred searches a month name a supplier for this work in the whole city, which tells you almost nobody arrives at this channel by searching for it. That is a fact about how it is bought rather than about whether it works.

What to exclude

Four Subtractions Worth Making First

Each is free, reversible and usually unexamined.

Existing customers

Prospecting campaigns routinely re-advertise to people who already bought. Uploading the customer list as an exclusion costs an afternoon and improves every prospecting number immediately.

Placements that do not produce

Automatic placements put the budget wherever it is cheapest, which is not the same as wherever it works. Check results by placement after a month and remove the ones producing clicks without enquiries.

Geography you cannot serve

A radius drawn from a pin in a city this size spills into Mississauga, Caledon and Vaughan. If you cannot serve them profitably you are paying to reach them; if you can, they should be a separate campaign so the cost is visible.

Audiences nobody can explain

An interest-based audience that cannot be described in a sentence is a guess with a budget. Either you can say who these people are and why, or the audience should be broader and the creative should do the sorting.

What the platform does now

Creative Is Doing The Targeting

The thing agencies sold for a decade has largely been automated, and in a market this large that matters more than elsewhere.

Detailed interest targeting has narrowed and broad targeting with automated optimisation performs well, because the platform tests creative against people faster than any human can. The lever moved: what you give the system to choose between now matters more than who you told it to find.

In a small town that change is softened, because there are only so many people and the system runs out of room. Here it is the whole game. A campaign with one piece of creative is asking the platform to find the right people in a city of 656,480 using a single signal, and it will do it badly.

Several distinct pieces — different arguments rather than different crops of one image — give the system something to sort with. That is a production decision as much as a media one, and it is the line most often missing from a quote.

A proposal that is mostly about audience segments is describing a job the platform now does.

A city that grew 10.6%

What A Facebook Ads Brampton Campaign Should Say To Newcomers

A large share of this audience arrived in the last five years and has no supplier relationship to displace.

That is an advantage and it changes the message. A newcomer is not being asked to switch; they are being asked to choose for the first time, and the obstacle is not loyalty but unfamiliarity.

What works in that situation is evidence rather than persuasion: the work being done, the person they will deal with, the thing answered that everybody asks before committing. A stranger scrolling at home will watch ninety seconds of real work and will not fill in a form asking for a consultation.

So the structure that works is two stages. Cheap, broad attention against something genuinely worth watching, then a much smaller ask retargeted at the people who watched most of it. The enquiries come from the second stage and the first is what makes it affordable.

Judged on the first stage alone the channel looks like a failure, which is why it is so often abandoned a month before it would have started working.

Measurement

The Platform Will Claim More Than It Earned

Every reported number here is produced by the party being paid, which is a fact to plan around rather than a scandal.

Attribution inside the platform counts an impression or a click as the cause of a purchase within a window it chooses. Where somebody saw an advertisement, then searched your name, then enquired, both this platform and your search account will claim it. Add the reports together and you have more business than your bank does.

The defence is not a better attribution model. It is one blunt question asked monthly: what changed in total enquiries and total revenue in the months the spend ran against the months it did not. It lags, it is crude, and nobody has an incentive to inflate it.

In a city where a great deal of business arrives through referral and community networks, add the oldest instrument: ask every arriving customer how they heard of you and record the answer. Across a quarter it captures the routes no tracking parameter survives.

Both habits cost a minute per enquiry and they are the only report lines that come from your business rather than from a platform.

Our part

Distribution, And The Footage To Feed It

In a market constrained by creative rather than money, production is the bottleneck being removed.

We run Meta accounts against cost per enquiry and film the material they run on. One shooting day of real work produces enough cuts to give the platform something to sort with, and the marginal cost of the fifth cut is a fraction of the first.

What a facebook ads brampton account needs from you

A facebook ads brampton account needs two things we cannot supply: what a customer is worth, which sets the ceiling on what an enquiry may cost, and something real to film, because in a bottomless audience relevance is the only thing that makes the impressions cheap.

We manage over $70,000 a month in advertising spend for Ontario businesses and the owner runs the paid media personally. We are based in Minesing rather than Brampton, so filming here involves travel, which we price as its own line rather than folding it into a fee.

How much should we spend here?

Work backwards from a customer rather than forwards from a budget. If a customer is worth four hundred dollars and one enquiry in four becomes one, a hundred dollars an enquiry is break-even. Below that the only other constraint is having enough results each week for the account to settle, which is a smaller figure than most people assume.

Why is our cost per result rising?

Look at how many times the average person has seen it before looking at anything else. In a city this size the number climbs faster than expected, and past three or four the same spending reaches people who have already decided against you. New material fixes it; a larger budget makes it worse.

How many pieces of creative do we need?

More than one, and in a city this size the number is set by what the platform needs to sort with rather than by how fast the audience tires. Several distinct arguments running at once will outperform one polished piece, and that is a production budget decision before it is a media one.

Can you use footage we already have?

Usually, and it is the first place to look. Material that has already run carries a record of how it performed, which is information you paid for and which no new piece has. Anything shot on a phone by somebody doing the actual work is frequently the best of it.
