Sediment

An Old Kingston Business Has An Old Kingston Website

Kingston has been a city since 1846 and was briefly the capital of the Province of Canada. It has an unusual number of businesses measured in decades, and their websites show it.

A Kingston website is not built once. It is built once and then added to, by several people, over years, with no process for taking anything away. Every new service got a page. Every campaign got a landing page. The staff page accumulated people and never lost them. A news section ran enthusiastically for eighteen months in 2019.

None of those additions was wrong at the time. The problem is that nothing was ever removed, so a visitor and a search engine both arrive at a Kingston site describing a business that no longer exists in that shape. Website design kingston projects are nearly always quoted as adding something, because that is what a quote looks like, and a web design kingston ontario proposal priced per page has every reason to keep it that way.

The Kingston pages that still matter are competing for attention with the ones that do not, and losing. That is true for a visitor who cannot find the service they came for, and true in search, where a dozen thin pages about discontinued work dilute the handful that should be ranking.

What to remove

Four Kinds Of Page That Are Costing You

These are on almost every established Kingston site, and removing them is faster and cheaper than anything else available in Ontario.

Removing a Kingston page is not free and it is not dangerous either, provided it is done properly. Anything that still attracts visitors or holds a ranking gets redirected to the nearest thing that is still true. Anything with no traffic and no links simply goes. The difference between those two cases is a morning with an analytics account.

What makes this worth doing rather than merely tidy is what happens to the pages that remain. A Kingston site with twelve service pages spreads its authority across twelve. The same site with four concentrates it, and the four get measurably easier to rank. Kingston web design projects almost never propose this because nobody has worked out how to sell deletion, and a kingston website design retainer has the opposite incentive entirely.

What usually goes wrong in Kingston

A rebuild quoted on the existing page count, which means paying to rebuild forty pages when twelve should not exist. The new site launches with the same dilution in a nicer typeface, and the agency has been paid to migrate content nobody should have carried over. Quotes are priced this way because the page count is the easiest thing to count.

What we do instead

Audit the Kingston site before quoting. Every page listed with its traffic, its rankings and whether what it says is still true. Then delete, redirect, and only afterwards discuss what to build. The rebuild, if there is one, is usually a third smaller and correspondingly cheaper.

Doing it safely

How To Delete Without Losing Anything

The fear is reasonable and the process is well understood. Four checks decide what happens to each Kingston page.

Pull the data first

Traffic, rankings and inbound links for every page, before any decision. Web design in kingston projects that skip this step are the reason people are frightened of deleting, because the one time it went badly it went badly for exactly this reason.

Redirect, do not just remove

Anything with traffic or a ranking goes to the nearest page that is still true. This takes minutes per page and it is the difference between consolidating your authority and discarding it.

Keep a Kingston record

What was removed, where it now points, and why. A kingston web design handover without that list is why the next agency will be too nervous to touch anything, and the sediment starts accumulating again.

Questions

Website Design In Kingston: Common Questions

Is deleting pages not bad for SEO?

Deleting carelessly is. Deleting with a redirect map is one of the most reliable improvements available to an established site. The mechanism is simple: a site spreads its authority across the pages it has, so twelve thin service pages each get a twelfth and four strong ones each get a quarter. Anything with traffic or a ranking gets redirected to the nearest page that is still true, so nothing is thrown away. The only dangerous version is removing pages without checking which ones were earning.

How do we know which pages to remove?

Data rather than judgement. Pull traffic, rankings and inbound links for every page, then ask one question of each: is what this says still true. Four outcomes follow. Still true and earning, keep it. Earning but out of date, rewrite in place and keep the address. Out of date but still visited, redirect. No traffic, no links and no longer true, delete. It takes a morning and it removes the argument entirely.

Do we need a rebuild as well?

Often less of one than you would expect, and the honest answer only becomes visible after the audit. A Kingston site carrying twelve years of additions is hard to judge, because the problem looks like design when it is mostly accumulation. Once the dead pages are gone and the survivors are visible, the remaining question is usually about three or four pages rather than forty. The rebuild that follows a proper clear-out is typically around a third smaller.

Why has nobody suggested this before?

Because it is difficult to sell. A quote to add something is easy to understand and easy to price per page. A quote to remove a third of a site sounds like paying for less, even though it reliably improves what remains and reduces what you maintain. There is no monthly subscription in it either, which means an agency doing this well makes less money from you, not more. That is an uncomfortable thing to write on a page selling web design and it is still the truth.

Can our current developer do it?

Usually, and if they can it is cheaper than bringing anybody in. The work is an audit, a set of decisions and a redirect map, which any competent Kingston developer can implement in a day or two. The part that benefits from outside help is the decisions themselves, because it is genuinely hard to be objective about pages your own team wrote. We are happy to supply the audit and the map and let somebody you already trust do the implementation.
