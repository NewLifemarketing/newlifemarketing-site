The Newmarket market

Three Economies Sharing One Town

Newmarket runs on three engines: a historic Main Street that's one of the GTA's best-preserved downtowns, the Upper Canada Mall retail gravity well that pulls shoppers from across York Region, and Southlake Regional Health Centre — a major hospital that has grown an entire health-services economy around itself.

Each engine makes different businesses. Main Street and the Fairy Lake district reward experience-driven independents — restaurants, cafés, boutiques — that can turn a visit into a ritual. The mall corridor and Davis Drive host the franchise and big-box layer that fights on convenience. And the Southlake cluster fills the surrounding blocks with clinics, specialists, physio, pharmacies and every health-adjacent service imaginable — all competing for the same patients on trust signals.

Around all three sit the family neighbourhoods — from the older tree-lined streets due for renovation to the newer subdivisions pushing toward East Gwillimbury — a commuter population with GO-line habits and York Region expectations. It's a genuinely layered market, and the businesses that win pick their layer and dominate it rather than whispering at all three.

The problems

What Newmarket Businesses Are Up Against

The mall eats the default trip

Upper Canada Mall is the region's reflex destination. Independents survive on intention — and intention has to be built.

Health-cluster saturation

Around Southlake, every second door is a clinic. Patients choose on reviews, search position and how trustworthy the booking path feels.

Main Street needs destination energy

Foot traffic follows events and stories. A quiet feed means a quiet Tuesday, no matter how good the espresso is.

Renovation demand, quote chaos

The older housing stock is a trades goldmine — and every contractor in York Region knows it. Speed and proof decide who books.

York Region ad auctions

Newmarket targeting overlaps the GTA's spend. Weak creative pays full price and loses anyway.

Catchment blur

Aurora, Holland Landing, Sharon — customers flow across town lines, and campaigns drawn to the municipal border miss half the market.

How we help

Across town

From Fairy Lake To The 404

Newmarket's geography sorts its customers neatly — which makes precise targeting unusually rewarding here.

The Main Street and Fairy Lake district draws the experience-seekers: patios, events, weekend strolls that turn into purchases. Davis Drive and the Yonge corridor carry the clinic-and-service economy in Southlake's orbit, plus the transit spine. Upper Canada Mall's quadrant owns the regional shopping trip; the established neighbourhoods — Gorham, College Manor, the tree-lined streets — hold the renovation-age housing stock; and Stonehaven and the newer east side add the young-family layer. Across the borders, Aurora and East Gwillimbury households treat Newmarket as their downtown. Each zone gets its own audience, offer and creative — that's how a mid-size budget beats York Region prices.

Straight answers

Newmarket FAQ

How do you compete with Upper Canada Mall pulling everyone's spending?

You don't out-mall the mall — you out-story it. Main Street and independent Newmarket win on experience, and experience is exactly what filmed content sells: the patio, the owner, the thing the mall can't copy. Pair that with radius targeting of the households already nearby and the destination trip becomes a habit.

We're a clinic near Southlake. Isn't everyone else too?

That's the point — Southlake Regional Health Centre has built one of the densest health-services clusters north of Toronto around itself. Standing out takes the trust stack: high-intent search, a review engine, authority content and a booking funnel that converts research into appointments. All run within platform health-advertising rules.

Is Main Street content really worth paying for?

Newmarket's Main Street is one of the best-preserved historic downtowns in the GTA — it's a set most towns would kill for. Businesses there are sitting on visual assets they've never captured properly. One golden-hour shoot day produces a month of content that looks like a tourism board made it.

What about trades? Half the housing stock here is due for renovation.

Exactly — Newmarket's older neighbourhoods are prime renovation territory while new builds push east. Trades win here the same way they win everywhere we run them: search capture, filmed proof of workmanship, and follow-up automation that answers before the competitor calls back.

Do you serve Aurora and East Gwillimbury from a Newmarket campaign?

Usually yes — the catchment behaves as one market along Yonge and Davis. We build the geographic structure around where your customers actually come from, not the municipal boundary.

What does it cost to start?

Scoped on the call. A Main Street shop's local push, a Southlake-area clinic and a renovation contractor are three different builds — Keegan prices what your situation actually needs and you keep the written plan either way.

Can events and promotions actually be marketed profitably?

Yes — Newmarket's calendar is an asset. Main Street's markets, patio season and holiday windows concentrate intent, and short-burst campaigns built around them outperform always-on spend for experience businesses. We plan the year's pushes in advance so creative is banked before each window opens.
