The split

Social Media Marketing Kitchener: Two Audiences Who Do Not Overlap

They use the same platforms, live in the same postcodes, and respond to nothing in common.

2
anchors of the local technology sector

Communitech and Google’s Kitchener engineering office

The technology half is used to being marketed to well. It discounts polish, notices a borrowed phrase, and rewards a post that teaches something specific. The manufacturing and service half is unimpressed by vocabulary imported from software and buys on evidence of competence and presence.

A single account alternating between the two reads as confused to both. The technology reader finds the posts about a van and a finished job irrelevant; the contractor finds the posts about process and iteration foreign.

It is one of the clearer cases in this region where choosing an audience beats reaching more people. An account aimed at one half, consistently, outperforms one aimed at the city.

The choice is usually obvious once asked directly: look at the last twenty customers and the split is rarely even.

What organic reach actually is

The Free Channel Stopped Being Free A Decade Ago

An account with a few thousand followers reaches a small fraction of them, and the fraction is set by the platform rather than by the work.

This is not an argument against posting. It is an argument against buying posting as a lead channel, which is what most retainers in this category quietly are.

Organic social does three things well: it proves a business exists and is current, it gives people who already know you a reason to stay, and it produces the material that paid distribution needs. None of those is lead generation and none should be sold as it.

What produces enquiries is paid distribution against a defined audience, bought by the impression. In a city with two distinct audiences that is also where the split above becomes operationally useful, because a paid audience can be defined precisely while a follower list cannot.

A supplier who says this before the contract rather than in month four is a supplier worth keeping.

Where the budget goes

Two Lines In A Social Media Marketing Kitchener Quote, One Usually Zero

Strip a monthly fee back and almost every arrangement resolves into production and distribution.

A fee that covers posting and nothing else has neither. It produces a tidy account, an engagement figure and no new customers, and because both missing lines are invisible on a report nobody can say what went wrong.

Ask which of the two your money is buying. If the answer is neither, the arrangement is administration priced as marketing.

What a technical audience ignores

Claims Are Discounted Before They Are Read

An audience that evaluates software for a living applies the same reading to an advertisement.

Superlatives, round percentages and the word leading all register as padding rather than as information, and a post built on them is skipped by exactly the people it was aimed at.

What survives that reading is specificity: a named constraint, an actual number with a source, a thing you will not do. It is harder to write and it is the only register that works on this audience.

The same applies to the format. A polished advertisement signals budget; a person explaining a genuine problem signals competence, and competence is what this market is buying.

None of that costs more to produce. It costs a decision about what the material is allowed to claim, taken before anybody opens an editor.

Measurement

Engagement Rate Is Not A Business Number

Two metrics dominate reporting here and neither appears in anybody’s accounts.

Engagement rate is the proportion of people who saw a post and reacted. Useful as a comparison between two pieces of creative, useless as a business measure, because the population it is a percentage of is chosen by the platform.

Reach is how many accounts saw something once, and it is purchasable, so a reach figure with no spend figure beside it says nothing. A report showing both climbing together is describing arithmetic rather than performance.

The numbers worth a monthly conversation are enquiries, what they cost, and what proportion became work. All three need something from your side of the table, which is precisely why the glossier metrics persist.

Ask for a report where at least one line comes from your business rather than from a platform. The reaction to that request tells you how the relationship will run.

And keep one number that no platform supplies: what arriving customers say when asked how they heard of you. In a region where people move between employers frequently, a share of new business arrives by a route no tracking parameter survives — somebody who saw the work at a previous job, or heard about it at a meet-up. Across a quarter that answer is more informative than any dashboard, and it costs a minute per enquiry to collect.

What A Social Media Marketing Kitchener Quote Should Separate

Production, which is filming and editing and happens on days you can count, from management, which is planning, posting and replying and happens every week. They are different work at different rates, and a single monthly figure hides which one you are actually buying. Ask how many shoot days, how many posts, who writes them, who answers the messages, and what happens to the footage if you leave.

Straight answers

Social Media Marketing FAQ

Straight answers before you ever book a call.

Can one account serve both audiences?

Rarely well. The two halves of this city respond to different registers, and an account alternating between them reads as confused to both. If both genuinely matter commercially, the honest answer is usually two paid audiences with different creative rather than one account trying to average them.

How often should we post?

As often as there is something worth showing, which for most businesses here is less than a daily schedule implies. Two posts a week of real work beats five of stock graphics, because the second pattern teaches your own audience to scroll past you. Frequency should follow material rather than a target.

Does this work for selling to other businesses?

It works through a two-stage structure rather than job-title targeting, which these platforms do weakly. Cheap, broad attention against something genuinely worth watching, then a much smaller ask retargeted at the people who watched most of it. Judged on the first stage alone the channel looks like a failure, which is why it is often abandoned a month early.

We have somebody posting already. What would change?

Usually the distribution and the measurement rather than the posting. The common pattern is a competent internal poster with no budget behind the work and no definition of what a result is, which produces a tidy account and no enquiries. Adding paid distribution and a number to judge it by changes that without replacing anybody.
