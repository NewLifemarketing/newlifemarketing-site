Kingston Video Production

Finished Before The Weeks Arrive

Kingston Video Production runs into a scheduling problem most cities do not have: for a large share of businesses here the year is decided in a small number of weeks, and a film delivered during them is a film delivered after the decision.

The timing problem

Kingston Video Production Has To Be Early, Not Good

Both would be better. Only one of them is available if the booking is late.

A production has a lead time that nobody enjoys discussing: the scout, the permissions, the scheduling around a working business, the shoot, the edit, the revisions. For a straightforward job that is weeks rather than days, and for anything needing access to a site or to other people’s premises it is longer.

Where demand is spread evenly across a year, a late delivery costs a few weeks of use. Where the year is concentrated, it can cost the year.

So the useful first question is not what the film should be. It is what date it has to be live, and then whether the lead time fits behind that date. If it does not, the honest answer is to make something smaller now and the ambitious thing for next year.

That conversation takes ten minutes and it is the one most likely to save a budget from being spent on something that arrives late.

What to make

Three Films That Earn Their Cost Here

Before anything ambitious, these three do the work for most businesses in this city.

The thing you actually do

The job, the install, the room, the process. It answers the question every customer has and that no amount of text answers, which is what this looks like when you do it.

The person they will deal with

Ninety seconds of whoever answers the phone. In a growing market where a share of buyers have no recommendation to lean on, a face does the job a neighbour would otherwise do.

The question asked before every purchase

Whatever customers ask before committing, answered once on camera. The most reused asset you will own: site, advertising, email, and the reply you currently type weekly.

All three come out of the same visit if the coverage is planned for, and none can be retrofitted from footage that was not captured.

What moves the price

Four Things That Change A Kingston Video Production Quote

Two quotes that read identically can differ by a factor of three. None of these is the camera.

Whether anybody speaks

Observational footage with music over it needs one pair of hands. Anything with a person talking needs a plan for sound, because bad audio is the one fault no edit repairs. That plan costs money.

The hours, including the waiting

A two-hour shoot is rarely two hours. Travel, setup, waiting for the work to reach the right stage and packing down are the day. A quote priced at the filming time has either absorbed that or will discover it.

How many finished pieces

One edit or five. Decided before the shoot it costs very little more because the coverage is captured once; decided afterwards it is a second visit.

Usage, and for how long

A clip on your own site is a different purchase from one running as paid advertising for two years, and where people appear on camera it is a consent question as well as a pricing one.

Where it gets watched

Mostly Not On Your Homepage

The placements that do the work are the unglamorous ones.

Sent in reply to an enquiry, so the fifteen-minute explanation happens once rather than weekly. Linked from a quotation, so the person who did not attend the meeting still sees the place. Embedded in a follow-up email, where it is the only thing worth opening.

And cut short for paid distribution, which in a market of this size reaches most of the relevant population for a knowable sum. A finite audience exhausts one piece quickly, so the short cuts are what keep a campaign alive past week three.

Designing for those placements changes the film. The first twenty seconds have to say what this is and why it exists, because that is all a forwarded viewer gives you.

A film made only for a homepage is a film most of your customers will never reach.

Afterwards

Footage Is An Asset, Not A Deliverable

In a market where the next shoot may be a year away, what you keep matters more than usual.

Agree in writing who owns the raw footage as well as the finished pieces, and take the files on your own storage at handover rather than relying on an archive somebody else maintains. The year you want a second cut is reliably the year after that archive was cleared.

Keep the list of what was shot as well. A folder of unlabelled clips is nearly unusable two years later; a one-page note saying what is in each is the difference between re-cutting and re-shooting.

Both cost nothing if they are decided at handover and are close to impossible to recover afterwards.

And plan the second cut before you need it. In a city where demand concentrates, the week you want new creative is the week everybody else does too.

That applies to the production calendar as well as the media one. Crews, edit time and the people who have to appear on camera are all scarcer in the weeks a business is busiest, which is the same reason the film has to be finished before them. Booking the shoot in the quiet months is not a convenience; it is the only time the business can actually give it attention.

Our part

We Film It And We Spend Behind It

The reason we do both is that footage sitting on a website is a cost rather than an asset.

We film, cut for placement, and run the paid distribution where there is a case for it. We manage over $70,000 a month in advertising spend for Ontario businesses and the owner runs the paid media personally.

How a kingston video production booking should start

A kingston video production booking should start with the date the film has to be live rather than with the brief, because the lead time is the constraint in a city whose year concentrates. Everything else can be designed around that date.

We are based in Minesing, a long way west of here, so a shoot in this part of the province carries travel, which we price as its own line. Where what you need is two hours with a camera and nothing else, a local operator is the better buy and we will say so.

How far ahead should we book?

Further than feels necessary, and the test is simple: work back from the date the film has to be live through the edit, the shoot and the access arrangements. For anything needing permissions or other people’s premises, add weeks rather than days. In a city where the year concentrates, late delivery can cost the year rather than a few weeks of use.

What does a shoot cost?

Less than a crewed production and more than the filming hours suggest, because travel, setup and waiting are the real day. The largest swing is whether anybody speaks on camera. The most effective way to reduce cost per usable piece is to decide the number of finished pieces before the day.

We only need one video. Is a bigger brief worth it?

Usually, because the marginal cost of a second and third piece from the same visit is small and the cost of a second visit is not. A finite local audience also exhausts one piece quickly, so a single film gives a campaign nothing to run past week three.

Who owns the footage?

Agree it in writing before anybody films, and be specific: the finished pieces, the raw footage and the right to use both in advertising are three separate things. Take the raw files on your own storage at handover, with a note of what is in each clip. That note is what makes a second cut possible two years later.

Next step

What Date Does It Have To Be Live?

Answer that and we can say immediately whether the ambitious version fits behind it or whether something smaller is the right call this year.
