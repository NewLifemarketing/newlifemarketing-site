Who is on the other end

Barrie Web Design Starts With How People Here Browse

A website gets read in the gaps of someone's day. In Barrie, those gaps are long, mobile and often out of town.

The commute. The 2021 census counted 17,510 workers commuting from the Barrie area down to Toronto, and 97% of them drove. Statistics Canada puts the average trip at 49 minutes by car and 81 minutes by transit, and the GO train from Allandale Waterfront to Union takes well over an hour and a half. That is a lot of phone time for Barrie commuters. The phone number, the area you serve and the way to book have to be on the first screen, and the form has to be short enough to finish before the next stop.

The ring of townships. In December 2024, SWIFT reported that its Simcoe County projects had brought fast internet to more than 12,590 previously underserved homes and businesses, in townships including Springwater, Oro-Medonte and Essa and the Town of Innisfil. Some of the people who hire a Barrie roofer or book a Barrie clinic have only recently stopped waiting for pages to load, and others still browse on mobile data. Nationally, 84% of Canadians had a mobile data plan in 2022. A heavy page with an autoplay slideshow loses those visitors before they see what you do.

A city still filling in. Barrie had 147,829 residents at the 2021 census and plans for about 298,000 by 2051. People who move here check a business's website before they trust a name they have never heard. A site that looks abandoned, or still lists hours from before the pandemic, reads as a business that might not be open.

Where the traffic comes from

Web Design In Barrie Follows The Calendar And The Map

The same site has to work for a July Saturday downtown and a February weekday at a ski hill. Four local patterns shape what we put on the pages.

Downtown on event days

Every business inside the Downtown Barrie BIA boundary is a member automatically, and the BIA, set up in 1974, organizes and markets the downtown events. In 2025 its Open Air Dunlop series ran from an airshow in early June to Eats On The Streets on August 16. The BIA brings the crowd; your site has to show the patio, the special and the hours for that day.

The south-end retail pull

Park Place, at Highway 400 and Mapleview Drive, is the largest retail and mixed-use development in the city, and it moved Barrie's commercial hub from the north end to the south. A business with a Bayfield Street store and a south-end location needs a page for each, with directions that match where the customer is coming from.

Four seasons, four home pages

Horseshoe Resort in Oro-Medonte runs ski runs and Nordic trails in winter, golf in summer, and meeting and banquet space all year, and the businesses around it swing with it. For a caterer, an outfitter or a rental, the home page in January should not be the home page in July.

Room bookings carry a local tax

Since April 1, 2024, Barrie has charged a 6% Municipal Accommodation Tax on stays under 30 nights, and food, beverages and meeting rooms have to be itemized apart from the room. A hotel, motel or inn taking bookings online needs a rate page that shows those lines clearly.

The profile points at the site

Google turned off the free websites built inside Business Profiles in March 2024, and the redirects ended on June 10, 2024. Local profiles still linking to an old business.site address send every click to a dead page. That link is one of the first things to check when a new site goes live.

Rules that reach your website

What A Web Designer In Barrie Should Check Before Launch

A few Ontario rules apply to some Barrie websites and not others, and a designer should know which apply to you before quoting. This is general information, not legal advice.

Accessibility (AODA). Under Ontario's accessibility standards, a business or non-profit with 50 or more employees must make its public website meet WCAG 2.0 Level AA, apart from live captions and pre-recorded audio descriptions. That deadline was January 1, 2021. A business with fewer than 50 employees is not held to that website rule, but it still has to provide accessible formats and communication supports when someone asks, and accessible ways to give feedback. Any business or non-profit with 20 or more employees also files an accessibility compliance report every three years, and the next one is due December 31, 2026. Headcount is the first thing to settle, because it decides which of these rules your build has to meet. Readable text, real headings and forms that work by keyboard help every visitor anyway, not just the ones the law names.

Licence numbers. The Electrical Safety Authority requires a licensed electrical contractor's ECRA/ESA number on its website, not just on its trucks and invoices. For an electrician in Barrie, that number belongs in the footer of every page.

Menus. Ontario's calorie-posting rule covers food-service chains with 20 or more locations in the province. An independent restaurant on Dunlop Street isn't covered, so its menu page can stay a plain, fast list of dishes and prices instead of a PDF nobody can read on a phone.

Industries

Web Page Design Barrie Industries Actually Need

A contractor's site and a restaurant's site do different jobs. Here is what each type of Barrie business usually needs on its pages.

Trades and contractors

Licence numbers where they're required, a page per service, before-and-after galleries, and a quote form a Barrie homeowner can fill in with one thumb. More in our trades playbook.

Home builders

Buyers comparing Barrie-area lots and floor plans want to narrow the options before they call. A browsable plan catalogue, with one page per plan, does that selling while you're on site.

Restaurants and cafés

A menu in text, not a photo of a menu. Hours that change for Open Air Dunlop days and holidays, a map, and a reservation or order button above the fold.

Accommodation and venues

Rate pages that show Barrie's 6% accommodation tax clearly, seasonal packages for ski season and summer, and wedding and meeting pages for the shoulder months.

Clinics and professional firms

Accessibility matters most here. Firms passing 50 staff are covered by the AODA website rule, and patients and clients of every age read the pages. Booking online, without a phone call, is often the main job.

Manufacturers and B2B

Capability pages for buyers who will never visit Barrie, and spec sheets that download cleanly.
