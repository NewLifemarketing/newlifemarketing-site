Who sets the price

Ninety Kilometres Is Inside Everybody’s Radius

Barrie sits at the top of Highway 400, close enough to the GTA that almost every wide targeting setting in southern Ontario includes it by accident.

A Barrie auction price is set by whoever is bidding, not by whoever wants the customer. A Toronto firm targeting the province, or drawing a radius generous enough not to think about it, enters every Barrie auction. It does not especially want a Barrie lead. It is bidding at a rate calibrated to a market where a lead is worth more.

So a Barrie advertiser ends up paying a price built elsewhere, for traffic the other bidder would barely notice losing. That is not a conspiracy, it is laziness in a settings panel, and it is extremely common.

The Barrie instinct is to raise the bid. That is the one response guaranteed to make it worse, and most google ads management barrie engagements begin by doing exactly that.

The wrong response

Broader Is The Instinct, Narrower Is The Answer

When Barrie results are thin the account usually gets wider. In a contaminated auction that walks further into the thing that is causing the problem.

The reason narrowing works in Barrie is specific rather than general. A GTA account bidding here by accident is almost always on broad terms — that is how the accident happens. It is far less likely to be matched to a four-word search naming a specific job and a specific neighbourhood, because nobody writes those keywords by mistake.

So the narrow Barrie searches are both cheaper and less contaminated, and they convert better, because the person typing four words already knows what they want. The only thing they are not is impressive on a volume report.

The One Thing A GTA Advertiser Cannot Copy

A distant advertiser bidding in Barrie by accident has a structural weakness, and it is not price.

In a Barrie account every query you exclude is budget moved to one you want. In an auction full of accidental bidders, a weekly negatives habit is the cheapest performance work available and almost nobody keeps it up.

Say the thing they cannot

Being able to attend this week, from this town, is a claim a distant advertiser cannot make. It belongs in the ad text, not buried on the landing page.

Set the hours honestly

Running Barrie ads at times nobody answers the phone spends budget on calls that ring out. Matching the schedule to reality costs nothing and is the first thing we change on most accounts here.

Questions

Google Ads In Barrie: Common Questions

Why are our clicks so expensive here?

Because the Barrie price is largely set by advertisers who are not trying to reach Barrie. The town is ninety kilometres up one highway from the GTA, which puts it inside almost every lazy radius and every province-wide setting in southern Ontario. Those accounts bid at a rate calibrated to a market where a lead is worth more, and they would barely notice losing yours. You are paying a price built somewhere else.

Should we raise our bids?

It is the Barrie instinct and it is the one response guaranteed to make it worse. Raising a bid buys more of the auction that was already overpriced. The move that works is inward: three and four word searches naming a specific job, exact and phrase match, weekly negatives and a landing page per campaign. Volume falls, cost per result falls further, and none of it needs a larger budget.

Why does narrowing help against a bigger advertiser?

Because an account bidding in Barrie by accident is almost always on broad terms — that is how the accident happens in the first place. It is far less likely to be matched to a four-word search naming a specific job in a specific neighbourhood, because nobody writes those keywords by mistake. So the narrow searches are both cheaper and less contaminated, and they convert better because the person typing four words already knows what they want.

What is the cheapest improvement available?

For a Barrie account, a weekly negatives habit. Every query you exclude moves budget to one you want, and in an auction full of accidental bidders the waste regenerates constantly, so a quarterly clean-up does not hold. Fifteen minutes a week, no cost, and it is the first thing dropped when an agency is busy — which is why checking it is a reasonable way to judge one.

What can we say that a Toronto advertiser cannot?

That you can be in Barrie this week, from this town, and that your reviews are from down the road. Those are claims a distant advertiser bidding by accident simply cannot make, and they belong in the ad text rather than buried on a landing page. It costs nothing, it is true, and it is the one advantage in this auction that no budget can out-bid.
