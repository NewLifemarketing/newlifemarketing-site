Innisfil Web Design

The Site Is The Premises

Innisfil Web Design carries more weight than it would in a town with a high street, because for most businesses here there is nothing for a customer to walk past: the site is the premises, the sign and the window, and it is judged by people who will never see any of the real ones.

What the site is for

A customer who cannot walk past has no way to form an impression before they arrive at your website, which means the site is doing the work a building, a sign and a window would otherwise do between them.

That raises the stakes on the pages most sites treat as filler: who you are, where you are, what you do, what it costs roughly, and what happens after somebody makes contact.

It also means the site has to look current. A copyright year from three years ago or photographs of a vehicle you no longer own reads, to somebody who cannot check any other way, as a business that may not exist.

None of that is a design problem, which is why a rebuild judged on appearance frequently changes nothing at all, and why the useful conversation starts with what a stranger needs rather than with how the home page should look.

What it has to answer

Four Things A Stranger Needs To Decide

All four are text, and most sites in a dispersed town answer fewer than half.

What exactly you do

Specifically, in the words a customer would use. Nobody is going to infer it from a sign, because there is no sign to infer it from.

Where you are and how far you go

A location and a drive time, with what changes beyond it. In a town 262 square kilometres across, this question applies to residents as well as outsiders.

What it costs, roughly

A range with the reason it varies. Somebody who cannot drop in to ask has no other way to calibrate, and its absence reads as something being hidden.

What happens next

Who replies, how soon, and what they will ask. Uncertainty is the commonest reason an enquiry does not get made and one sentence removes it.

Four blocks of text, an afternoon of writing, and in a town with no passing trade they do more for enquiries than a redesign would.

One habit is worth building alongside any new site. Each time somebody asks a question the site does not answer, write it down rather than only answering it. Within a month that list is a page plan drawn from what real customers here wanted to know, and in a town where nobody can walk in and ask, those answers are the whole of what the site is for.

The address itself

The Domain Has A History You Did Not Write

Worth checking before a rebuild, and essential before buying a name somebody else used.

A domain carries whatever it accumulated before you. Most of the time that is nothing; occasionally it is a decade of a previous business, and sometimes it is something considerably worse than nothing.

Look it up in the public archive of old web pages before committing. Ten minutes there tells you what the address used to be and whether the old pages are still being linked to from elsewhere.

Where they are, those links are worth keeping. Pointing old addresses at the relevant new pages preserves whatever they were sending, and that is free value nobody else can take.

For a business changing its name, the same applies to its own former domain. Keep paying for it and keep it pointing at the new one — it is the cheapest marketing expense in the business and the one most often cancelled.

What it is built on

What An Innisfil Web Design Project Should Be Built On

Three honest options with different costs and different ceilings.

A hosted builder is the cheapest to start and the easiest to keep running: nothing to patch, nothing to break, a monthly fee. The ceiling is reached the first time you want something it does not offer, and you cannot take the site elsewhere.

A content system is the common middle: anything is possible, you can move hosts, and in exchange somebody has to apply updates and watch for problems. That maintenance is real work and it is the part most often sold and then not done.

The third option is a simple site with no system behind it, which is fast, extremely cheap to host and effectively unbreakable — and which needs a supplier for every change.

The right answer follows from how often the site will change and who will change it. Answer that first and the platform argument resolves itself without anybody having a preference.

Where it lives

Hosting Is A Small Bill With Large Consequences

Three questions, all of which have been the cause of a site disappearing.

Whose card pays it? A hosting account on a former employee’s personal card is a site with an expiry date nobody has written down.

Is there a backup, and has anyone restored one? An untested backup is a belief. The test takes an hour and it is worth doing once rather than discovering the answer in an emergency.

And is the domain registration separate from the hosting? Keeping them apart means a dispute with one supplier cannot hold the other hostage, which is the situation that turns a disagreement into a crisis.

None of this costs more than a few dollars a month. It is administration rather than technology, and it is the administration that decides whether anything else on this page survives a bad year.

Our scope

What We Build And What We Would Hand On

Narrow on purpose, so a wasted meeting is avoided.

We build and rebuild presentation sites with the structure decided against the searches each page is meant to own, the redirects written before launch, and the content planned as part of the project rather than chased afterwards.

What innisfil web design work should leave behind

Innisfil Web Design work with us leaves you able to add a page and change the important text without us, the registry of which page owns which search, and the redirect map. Where the site is the premises, being locked out of your own building is the risk worth designing away first.

We do not take on open-ended application development. Where something on the site has to compute, that is a specialist job with a different commercial model, and naming it now is cheaper than discovering it in month three.

Why does the site matter more here?

Because there is nothing to walk past. At 165 people per square kilometre across 262 square kilometres, a customer cannot form an impression from a building or a sign, so the site is doing the work all three would otherwise do between them.

What has to be on it?

What exactly you do in the customer’s words, where you are and how far you go, what it costs roughly, and what happens after somebody makes contact. Four blocks of text, and in a town with no passing trade they do more than a redesign would.

Why does speed matter so much?

Because the realistic reader is on a phone at the far end of a large, thinly populated municipality, with a bigger centre twenty minutes up the road as an alternative. Every large image is a delay they pay for, and it is invisible on the machine the site was designed on.

What should we be able to change ourselves?

Text and new pages, at minimum, and you should be shown how before the project ends. Where the site is the premises, a site nobody can edit gets abandoned rather than updated, and an abandoned site damages the impression it was built to create.
