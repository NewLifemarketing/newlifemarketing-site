Social Media Thunder Bay

Buy The Parts You Cannot Do

Social Media Thunder Bay is already being done by most of the businesses that want it done — this city searches for marketing help at nearly three times the rate of one half again its size — which means the honest offer is not posting, but the two parts nobody can do from a desk.

What is already happening

Social Media Thunder Bay: The Posting Is Not The Gap

A market that goes looking for marketing help has usually already started.

Businesses here post. Somebody takes photographs on a job, writes a line and publishes it, and in a city of this size that is frequently better than anything an agency would produce, because it sounds like the person customers will actually meet.

What almost nobody has is the other two parts. Material that needed equipment and planning to capture, and money spent reaching people who do not already follow the account.

Those two are what a supplier can genuinely add, and they are the two most commonly bundled into a monthly fee where the visible deliverable is more posting.

So the useful arrangement here is unusual and smaller than a retainer: you keep the voice, we supply the material and the distribution, and nobody pays for somebody else to write in your voice badly.

The two parts worth buying

Material And Distribution

One is a day a few times a year; the other is a line on a budget.

Material that needs equipment

Lit interiors, clean audio, drone where it is genuinely useful, and a planned list that produces months of pieces rather than one. A phone covers everything else and should.

Paid distribution

Money reaching people who do not follow you. In a city of 108,843 with no commuter overlap, the audience is a defined list and covering a meaningful share of it more than once is a calculable sum.

The decision about what to say

Cheap, occasional and worth buying once rather than monthly. It is the part that makes the other two worth anything and the part most often billed every month.

Notice what is not on that list. Daily posting, community management and inbox monitoring are services somebody in your business is already performing, usually better, and buying them is the commonest way a budget disappears without reaching anybody new. They are also the easiest things to put on an invoice, which is exactly why they end up there.

The ceiling

A Closed Audience Can Be Finished

Isolation makes the audience knowable, and knowable audiences run out.

With the nearest larger city roughly 700 km away there is no commuter inflow and no neighbouring market bleeding in. The people reachable here are the people who live here, which is an advantage for planning and a trap for a permanent campaign.

The pattern is consistent: two or three good weeks, then cost per result climbing while nothing in the account has changed. Nothing is broken. The people inclined to act have acted and the rest have seen it.

So the shape that works is bursts with a reason behind them — a season, an opening, an offer with a date — rather than a permanent drip through a finite audience. Three or four of those a year, each properly supplied with material, will outperform twelve months of steady spending at the same total cost.

And plan the material for the whole burst in advance. Opening with everything you have produces a strong fortnight and then a decline that looks like the market cooling.

What to show

What A Social Media Thunder Bay Budget Should Run On

In a city where people recognise places, stock photography is not neutral.

Every supplier has the same stock library. What almost none of their clients have is footage of the actual work: the job going in, the finished result, the person who answers the phone.

Here that matters more than in a larger city, because people recognise the road, the building and frequently the person. A stock interior sitting where their own town should be is a small, specific moment of distrust that no amount of targeting recovers.

Real material also lasts longer, which is what a finite audience requires. A library is what lets a campaign keep running past week three without people learning to scroll past you.

One planned day produces months of it, and a phone with a clip microphone covers the gap. That accessory is the highest-return purchase in this subject, because audio is the one thing a phone cannot do on its own, and bad audio loses an audience faster than an ordinary picture ever will.

Measuring it

Cost Per Enquiry, And One Question At The Counter

At this scale the platform numbers are decoration.

Engagement rate is a percentage of an audience the platform chose. Reach is purchasable, so a reach figure with no spend beside it says nothing. Followers are the least predictive number on the page, and in a city this size the follower count is mostly people who were always going to know you.

What works is cost per enquiry plus asking every arriving customer how they heard of you. In a closed market a single post that travels can reach a meaningful share of the city without any money behind it, often enough to distort a month.

Read it over a rolling quarter. At these volumes one month cannot distinguish a good month from a bad one, which is how useful campaigns get cut and lucky ones get extended.

And track the share of spend reaching people who do not already follow you. If that number is zero, the budget is a newsletter with worse delivery, and the report will show engagement rising while enquiries stay exactly where they were.

Our part

Distribution, And The Material To Feed It

A short offer, honestly bounded.

We run paid social against cost per enquiry and film the material it runs on. We manage over $70,000 a month in advertising spend for Ontario businesses and the owner runs the paid media personally.

What we would not sell as social media thunder bay work

Daily posting, community management and inbox monitoring are not what we do, and in a city where businesses already post in their own voice they would be the wrong purchase anyway. Social Media Thunder Bay bought from us is the material and the distribution, with the voice staying yours.

We are in Minesing, a long way south, so filming here involves travel, which we price as its own visible line rather than folding it into a fee.

We already post. What would a supplier add?

Two things you cannot do from a desk: material that needed equipment and planning to capture, and money spent reaching people who do not already follow you. Everything else — the posting itself, the replies, the voice — you are probably doing better than an agency would, because it sounds like the person customers will meet.

Why did the campaign fade after three weeks?

The audience has seen it. With no commuter inflow and the nearest larger city 700 km away, the reachable audience is a defined list, and a list you can cover completely you can exhaust completely. Bursts with a reason behind them, and material planned for the whole burst, work better than a permanent drip.

How much should we spend?

Enough to reach a meaningful share of the city more than once, which is calculable here because the audience is defined and nothing adjacent absorbs the overflow. Then stop. Past saturation, extra budget buys repetition and wears out the creative you have.

What should we stop paying for?

Daily posting, community management and inbox monitoring, if somebody in your business is already doing them. They are the visible deliverable on most monthly fees and the reason the distribution line — the only part that reaches anybody new — ends up being whatever is left over.
