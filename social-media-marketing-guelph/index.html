Social Media Marketing Guelph

Followers Depreciate

Social Media Marketing Guelph is usually sold on growing a following, which is the weakest asset available in a city that added 9.1% to its population between censuses — the audience assembled three years ago is not the audience living here now.

Why the following ages

Growth is good for a business and awkward for a follower count.

A following is a snapshot of who was paying attention when it was built. In a market adding people at this rate, a meaningful share of the people who will buy from you next year were not here to follow anything.

They are also the most valuable part of the market, because they have no incumbent supplier. Reaching them requires paying to be in front of people who do not follow you, which is the line most commonly squeezed when a blended monthly fee is negotiated.

None of that makes a following worthless. It makes it a by-product rather than the objective, and a report built around follower growth is measuring the thing that ages while ignoring the thing that works.

The question worth asking a supplier is simple: what share of this month’s budget reaches somebody who does not already follow us. If the answer is none, you are paying to talk to people who had already decided.

The three products

Posting, Strategy And Distribution Are Separate Purchases

Only fifty searches a month name this locally, which means it is sold rather than compared. The questions have to do the work.

Posting

Somebody makes and schedules content. The most visible line on an invoice and the one that produces least on its own, particularly when the audience it reaches is last year’s.

Strategy

Deciding what is worth saying, to whom and when. Cheap, occasional, and what makes the other two worth anything at all.

Distribution

Money spent reaching people who do not follow you. In a growing city this is where the new arrivals are, and it is the line that quietly shrinks when a blended fee is squeezed.

What to show

Real Work Beats A Content Calendar

Especially for somebody who has just arrived and has no idea who anybody is.

Every supplier has the same stock library and the same template graphics. What almost none of their clients have is footage of the actual work: the job going in, the finished result, the person who answers the phone.

For a newcomer with no recommendation to lean on, that material is doing the job a referral would otherwise do. Stock photography does the opposite: it makes a real business look like a placeholder at exactly the moment somebody is deciding whether you are real.

Real material also lasts longer, which matters because creative fatigue in a single city arrives in weeks rather than months. A library is what lets a campaign keep running without the audience learning to scroll past you.

One planned shooting day produces months of material, and the marginal cost of each additional cut is small when the shot list is decided in advance rather than improvised on the morning.

There is a second argument for real material that has nothing to do with performance. A newcomer deciding between three local firms is looking for any signal that one of them is a specific business rather than a category, and a photograph of your own premises is the cheapest such signal there is. It also cannot be copied by a competitor who bought the same template.

Measuring it

What A Social Media Marketing Guelph Report Should Contain

Two numbers that can be acted on, and several that cannot.

What to ignore

Follower count, which ages. Engagement rate, which is a percentage of an audience the platform chose. Reach with no spend figure beside it, which is purchasable and therefore says nothing.

What to use

Cost per enquiry, and the share of spend reaching people who do not already follow you. The second is the number that says whether the campaign is working on this year’s city or the last one.

Read both over a rolling quarter. At the volumes a business this size sees, one month cannot tell a good month from a bad one, which is how useful campaigns get cut and lucky ones get extended.

What goes wrong

The Retainer That Reaches Nobody New

It is the commonest arrangement in this market and the easiest to diagnose.

A monthly fee covers posting, the posts reach the existing following, the following was built some years ago, and the report shows engagement rising while enquiries do not move. Nothing is broken and nothing is working.

The diagnosis takes one question and the fix takes one line on an invoice: separate the distribution budget, state it, and protect it. A campaign reaching only people who already follow you is a newsletter with worse delivery.

The second common failure is narrowing an audience until it runs out. In a single city the temptation is to target only perfect prospects, which produces a flattering first week and then premium prices to show the same person the same thing repeatedly.

A broader audience with sharper material outperforms it almost every time, and in a city filling up with new arrivals the broader audience is the point.

The third failure is subtler and it shows up in the handover rather than the reporting. An account built, grown and run entirely by a supplier is an asset you do not hold, and when the arrangement ends the audience, the creative and the history of what worked frequently end with it. Insist that the accounts are in your company’s name from the first day and that the finished material is delivered as files rather than as posts, and the risk disappears at no cost.

Should we be growing our following?

Treat it as a by-product rather than the objective. Guelph added 9.1% to its population between censuses, so a following built a few years ago excludes a meaningful share of the people who will buy next year — and those are the most valuable buyers, because they have no incumbent supplier. Spend on reaching them instead.

What one question should we ask a supplier?

What share of this month’s budget reaches somebody who does not already follow us. If the answer is none, the campaign is a newsletter with worse delivery: the report will show engagement rising while enquiries stay flat, and nothing in it will explain why.

Why did a tightly targeted campaign stop working?

Usually because it ran out of people. In a single city, narrowing to only perfect prospects produces a flattering first week and then premium prices to show the same person the same thing repeatedly. A broader audience with sharper material outperforms it almost every time.

Do we need to post daily?

No. Post as often as there is something worth showing, which is less than a daily schedule implies. Two posts a week of real work beats five of stock graphics, which teach an audience to scroll past you, and the difference is better spent on reaching people who have just arrived.
