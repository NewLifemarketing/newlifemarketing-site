Timmins Web Design

A Site For Referrals

Timmins Web Design is bought in a market whose population fell between the last two censuses, which means the website is not going to find you people nobody knew existed — its job is to confirm what somebody was already told and to stop the ones you have from drifting.

What the site is actually for

Timmins Web Design In A Market With No New Arrivals

The population fell from 41,788 to 41,145. That changes what a website is bought to do.

In a growing city a website is an acquisition channel: new people arrive, they search, and a page that answers them wins a customer nobody had. That is the model almost every web proposal is written against.

Here the pool is fixed, and a large share of new business arrives by recommendation. The person landing on your site has usually already been told your name by somebody they trust, and the site’s job is to confirm rather than convince.

That changes the brief in a specific way. The hardest-working page is not the one optimised for a search term; it is the one that loads instantly on a phone, shows the actual work, and makes the number tappable, because the visitor is three-quarters sold and looking for a reason not to be.

Forty searches a month across two phrases is the entire local supplier demand. The site is not competing for traffic; it is competing with the possibility that somebody does nothing.

What a phone downloads

Images Are Almost All Of The Weight

Page speed on a phone is mostly one problem wearing several disguises.

A photograph straight from a camera is several megabytes. The same photograph sized for the space it occupies and saved in a modern format is a small fraction of that, visually identical on a screen.

A site with twenty untreated photographs is slow on any connection and unusable on a weak one, and no amount of work elsewhere compensates. It is also the easiest thing on this page to fix.

The second cause is images loading before they are needed. Everything below the first screen can wait until somebody scrolls, which is a setting rather than a project.

Ask for a speed figure measured on a phone over a mobile connection rather than on a laptop over office broadband. The two numbers differ enormously and only one of them describes your visitors.

It is also the part of timmins web design work with the clearest payback, because every visitor benefits from it and nobody has to be persuaded of anything.

After the button

Timmins Web Design: The Ninety Seconds After Somebody Submits

The part of a site that is almost never designed, and where enquiries are lost.

Where does the submission go? To an inbox somebody checks twice a day, or to a phone? Does the sender get an acknowledgement saying when they will hear back, or a bare thank-you line?

An acknowledgement that names a time is worth more than any design decision on the page before it. It stops the person contacting a competitor while they wait, which is otherwise exactly what they do.

Then there is the record. A form that emails somebody is trivial to build and leaves no trail; a week later nobody can say how many enquiries arrived or what happened to them.

Decide all three before the form is built, because the form is the easy part and all three are frequently discovered to be missing in the first busy week after launch.

Launch

The Fortnight Either Side Of Going Live

Two weeks of attention that decide whether the project was worth it.

Before: a list of every address on the old site, the measurement running, and somebody who is not the builder reading every page for things that are simply wrong — an old price, a departed member of staff, a service you stopped offering.

At launch: check the forms actually arrive, check the phone number dials, and load the site on a real phone away from the office rather than on the machine it was built on.

After: watch for error pages for a fortnight. They are the symptom of a missed redirect and they are invisible unless somebody is looking, because the people hitting them simply leave.

None of this is technical work and none of it appears on a quote. It is an afternoon of somebody’s attention spread over two weeks, and it catches most of what goes wrong.

The template question

A Template Is Cheap Until You Need It Changed

Worth understanding before choosing, because the saving is real and so is the ceiling.

A bought template gets a reasonable-looking site live quickly for very little, and for a business that needs a presence rather than a sales tool that is a sound decision honestly made.

The cost arrives the first time you want something it was not built to do: a different page layout, a particular form behaviour, a section arranged the way your work actually divides. Fighting a template to do that costs more than building the piece would have.

So the question is how much you expect to change. A business whose offering is settled can live inside a template for years. One still working out what it sells will hit the edges within months.

There is no wrong answer, only an answer made in advance or discovered later. Made in advance it is a saving; discovered later it is a second project.

Our scope

What We Build And What We Would Hand On

Narrow on purpose, so a wasted meeting is avoided.

We build and rebuild presentation sites with the structure decided against the search clusters each page is meant to own, the redirects written before launch, and the content planned as part of the project rather than chased afterwards.

When timmins web design work should go to somebody closer

Timmins Web Design that genuinely needs somebody who can drop in, photograph the premises next week and sit at the counter while a form is tested is work for a supplier who can be there. We are in Minesing, several hours south, and we would say so rather than take it.

We also do not take on open-ended application development; where something on the site has to compute, that is a specialist job with a different commercial model. Everything else we would rather quote with the travel as its own visible line.

Is a website worth it if most of our business is word of mouth?

Yes, and for a different reason than usual. A referred visitor is three-quarters sold and looking for a reason not to be, so the site’s job is confirmation: load fast on a phone, show real work, make the number tappable. In a market with no new arrivals, protecting the business a recommendation already won is worth more than chasing traffic.

How much should a website cost here?

Less than the same site in a city, and the spread is still set by how much is bespoke, what it connects to and who writes the content. A quick way to narrow it: decide whether anything on the site has to compute something specific to your business. If not, you are in the lower part of the range.

Will a new site bring more enquiries?

Only if the current one is losing them, which is checkable before you spend anything. Look at whether the number is tappable on a phone, whether the form works, whether pages load quickly, and whether there is a page for each thing you sell. If all four are fine, the problem is upstream and a rebuild will not fix it.

What if our supplier is three hours away?

For building and maintaining a site, distance changes very little. For anything needing somebody physically present, it is real and should be a separate line on the quote. The more important question in a remote market is what you would need in order to carry on if they stopped answering — hosting, domain, admin access and source, all in your own name.
