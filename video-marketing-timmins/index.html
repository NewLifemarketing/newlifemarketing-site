What distance changes

Video Marketing Timmins: The Shoot Happens Once

Three or four hours each way turns a routine booking into a planned expedition, and that changes the brief.

41,145
people in Timmins, down from 41,788

Statistics Canada, 2021 Census of Population

In a city a forgotten shot is a half-day to recover. Here it is a return journey that somebody has to justify, so in practice the forgotten shot simply never gets taken and the piece it belonged to never gets made.

That single fact reorganises the whole purchase. The expensive part is not the camera or the edit; it is the travel and the day, and both are already paid for whether the day produces one film or fifteen cuts.

So the question worth arguing about before a crew is booked is not what the video should be. It is what the complete list of things worth filming is, across everything you might want to publish for the next twelve months.

A supplier who takes that brief without asking for the list is selling you one film at the price of a day.

Planning the day

Decide The Finished Pieces Before The Date

A visit this far north happens once, so the list has to exist before anybody travels.

Work backwards from what the year needs: the page that has nothing on it, the question every enquiry asks, the job you wish people could see, the thing that will not exist in six months.

Each of those implies something to capture, and the list of them is the schedule. A day planned from a list produces a library; a day planned from a theme produces one film and a folder nobody opens.

Write it as finished pieces rather than as shots, because a shot list is the crew’s job and a deliverables list is yours. Six named pieces is a normal expectation from a full day with the material prepared.

Then put the one that cannot be filmed any other week first in the running order. Everything else can be caught up; that one cannot, and it is the item most likely to be lost to an overrun.

What it is for

Footage Earns Its Travel On The Pages, Not The Feed

In a market with no new arrivals, the longest-lived use of the material is rarely the one people buy it for.

A social post is seen for two days. The same ninety seconds embedded on the page a referred customer lands on is working every week for years, and in a town where the population fell between the last two censuses, protecting business a recommendation already won is worth more than chasing reach.

Real footage on a service page does a job stock photography cannot: it confirms what the person who recommended you said. Stock quietly contradicts it, and in a small town it is obvious.

The same material carries the paid campaigns, which in a fixed audience means it has to last longer than it would in a city because there are no new people arriving to see it fresh.

And it carries the thing nobody budgets for: the reply to an enquiry. Sending a short film of the exact process somebody asked about closes more than a paragraph does, and it costs nothing after the day is paid for.

The first seconds

Video Marketing Timmins And The First Three Seconds

Wherever it is watched, the decision to keep watching is made before the third second.

A logo animation, an establishing shot and a greeting consume exactly the window in which somebody decides. By the time the content begins, a large share of the audience has gone.

Open with the thing itself: the problem stated, the result shown, the surprising fact. Whatever would have been the most interesting moment at ninety seconds belongs at zero.

This is an editing decision rather than a filming one, which makes it free. The same footage cut two ways performs completely differently, and the version that opens cold almost always wins.

Keep the logo for the end, where it is seen by people who stayed and is worth something, rather than at the start where it is seen by everybody and costs you the ones who would not have.

How long it should be

Length Is Decided By Where It Goes

One film at one duration is a film made for nowhere in particular.

A social feed tolerates fifteen to thirty seconds. A page on your own site, reached by somebody actively researching, will hold two or three minutes. A reply to a specific question can run as long as the question needs.

So the deliverable from a day is not a film; it is a set of cuts at different lengths for different places, planned before anybody shoots so that the material exists to make them.

The long version is made first and the short ones cut from it. Doing it the other way round means filming twice.

And resist the compromise length. Ninety seconds is too long for a feed and too short for a page, which is why so much video sits in the middle performing moderately everywhere.

The exception is a film answering a specific question somebody asked. Length stops mattering entirely once a viewer has chosen to watch something because they wanted that exact answer, and those are the pieces worth making long enough to be genuinely complete.

Straight answers

Video Marketing Timmins FAQ

Straight answers before you ever book a call.

What does a shoot here cost compared with one down south?

The production itself is the same; the difference is travel, which should appear as its own line rather than be folded into a day rate. The way to make the journey worth it is not to negotiate it down but to make the day produce more — a planned shot list instead of a single film.

How many pieces should one day produce?

Far more than most people ask for. Once a crew is standing in your building, another setup costs minutes. A planned day routinely yields a main film, several short answers to common questions, a bank of cutaways and a set of photographs. An unplanned day yields one film.

Can we just film it ourselves?

Partly, and you should. A phone plus a clip microphone covers social posts, enquiry replies and anything that happened unexpectedly. What it does not cover is lighting a workshop, clean audio in a noisy room, or the deliberate piece at the top of a page. Do both and the gap between visits stops being empty.

Who should own the raw footage?

You should, and it is worth settling before the day rather than after. The rushes are the asset: holding them means a different editor can produce something new in three years without anybody travelling north again. Not holding them means paying for the journey twice.
