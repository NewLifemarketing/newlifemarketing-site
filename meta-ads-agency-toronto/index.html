Meta Ads Agency Toronto

A Channel Nobody Searches For

Three hundred searches a month ask for a Meta ads agency Toronto businesses might hire, in a city of 74,560 business establishments where the platform takes a very large share of local advertising money. Almost nobody arrives at this channel by searching for it, and that fact should change how you buy it.

The gap in the numbers

Meta Ads Agency Toronto: Three Hundred Searches, An Enormous Market

Compare that with paid search, where the equivalent Toronto cluster runs to 71 phrases and 5,690 searches. Nearly twenty times the demand for a supplier, for a channel that is not twenty times larger.

The explanation is how the two are bought. Google Ads is a thing people decide to do, then look for somebody to run. Meta buying usually arrives attached to something else: an agency already doing the search work, a studio already making the footage, or an in-house attempt that outgrew itself.

So the buyers reaching this page are unusual, and it is worth saying what that means. You are either comparison-shopping deliberately, which is rare and sensible, or something has gone wrong with an existing arrangement. Both deserve a page that argues rather than one that lists services.

Testing properly

Most Creative Tests Prove Nothing

Two advertisements running at once is not a test unless it is set up as one.

Left to itself the delivery system will give most of the budget to whichever piece starts better, within hours, long before there is enough evidence to know which is actually better.

So a real test needs the spend split deliberately, a decision made in advance about what would count as a winner, and enough results on each side to tell them apart.

Test one thing at a time. Two advertisements that differ in picture, text and offer cannot teach you anything, because whichever wins, nobody knows which difference did it.

And test propositions rather than details. The difference between two headlines is usually noise; the difference between two offers is usually the whole campaign.

What a Meta Ads Agency Toronto should be able to show

A list of what has been tested, what won, and what was concluded. Any Meta Ads Agency Toronto running an account for six months without that document has been optimising rather than learning, and the two look identical on an invoice.

The real constraint

Production Volume Is The Budget Line Nobody Quotes

Testing requires something to test. Most accounts run out of material long before they run out of money.

Footage of the actual work

Filmed once, cut many ways. This is the only genuinely scarce input in local advertising, because every competitor has access to the same stock and the same templates.

Multiple angles on one idea

The same offer said five ways beats five offers said once, because the platform can find who each version suits. This is cheap if it is planned at the shoot and expensive if it is not.

A kill threshold, agreed in advance

A number at which a piece of creative stops running. Without one, underperforming material survives on sentiment, and in an auction that costs money every day.

We manage over $70,000 a month in advertising spend for Ontario businesses, and the owner runs the paid media personally rather than passing it to a desk. We also film, which in this category is less a convenience than the main constraint being removed.

What it is and is not good at

Where A Meta Ads Agency Toronto Account Earns Its Place

Demand that does not exist yet

A new category, a product that needs explaining, an offer nobody is searching for. Search can only answer a question somebody asked. This is the channel that asks it for them.

Retargeting, which is nearly always underused

Small audience, established intent, low cost per result. It is the least glamorous thing in the account and usually the most efficient, and it is the first thing we check is running.

Anything visual

Trades, venues, hospitality, anything where seeing the work is the argument. A clip of the real thing outperforms every adjective available.

Not: capturing existing demand

If people are already searching for what you sell and you are not there, buy that first. Paid social is additive to search, not a substitute, and an agency selling it as a substitute is selling you the channel it prefers.

Proving it

The Only Honest Test Is Switching It Off

Expensive, uncomfortable, and the only method that answers the question directly.

Every reporting method attributes. None of them establishes what would have happened anyway, and for a brand with existing demand that gap is large.

The direct answer is to stop for a defined period and watch what the business does. Four weeks off, compared with the same four weeks a year earlier, tells you more than any dashboard.

It is a real cost and it is worth paying once, particularly before committing to a large annual budget. Most businesses have never done it and could not say what their advertising is actually contributing.

A smaller version works too: hold one region or one audience out of the campaign for a month and compare. Less conclusive, far less frightening, and a great deal better than nothing.

It is also the one experiment nobody being paid to run the account has an incentive to propose, which is exactly why a client should.

Set the terms before starting: how long the pause runs, what will be compared against what, and what result would change the decision. Agreed in advance, the outcome is informative whichever way it falls. Agreed afterwards, it becomes an argument about seasonality that neither side can win.

Run it in a quiet period rather than a peak. Losing four weeks of a slow season costs comparatively little and the comparison against the same four weeks a year earlier is just as readable.

Is Meta still worth it for a Toronto business?

For anything visual, anything with an offer that needs explaining, and anything with an existing audience to retarget, yes. For capturing demand that already exists as searches, no — buy that first. The channel has not got worse; what changed is that the easy advantage of precise interest targeting went away, so the work moved to creative and most proposals have not caught up.

How many ads do we need?

More than one, and the number is set by how fast your audience exhausts a piece of creative. In a market the size of Toronto audiences are large enough that fatigue is slower than in a small city, but testing still needs several versions running at once. A proposal with a single monthly asset has not planned for testing at all.

Can you run Meta without running our Google Ads?

Yes, and sometimes that is the right split, particularly where an incumbent is running search competently. What we would want is visibility of what search is doing, because the two channels are frequently credited for the same enquiry and judging either in isolation produces confident wrong conclusions.

What happens to our existing creative?

We would use it until there is evidence it should be replaced, rather than rebuilding on arrival. Existing material has performance history attached, which is information you have already paid for. The common finding is not that the creative is bad but that there is not enough of it for the platform to test with.
