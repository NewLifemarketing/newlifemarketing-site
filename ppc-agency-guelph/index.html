The boundary problem

What A PPC Agency Guelph Is Actually Managing

The setting that decides the cost is geographic, and it takes thirty seconds to change.

Guelph is close enough to much larger centres that a default radius reaches into them. Those markets have more competitors, bigger budgets and higher click prices, and nothing in the account tells you when you have crossed into one.

What you see instead is a cost per click that drifts upward and a conversion rate that drifts down, because the extra clicks are from people who have ten nearer alternatives and were never going to drive here.

The fix is not a bidding strategy. It is drawing the boundary deliberately, naming the places you genuinely serve, and pricing travel beyond them as its own line rather than absorbing it.

That decision costs nothing, takes an afternoon, and is worth more than any optimisation applied afterwards.

Where the costs come from

Four Things A PPC Agency Guelph Should Fix Before Bidding

None of them is the bid, and all four are usually neglected in a small account.

The boundary

Discussed above and worth repeating because it is first. A campaign reaching a larger adjacent market pays that market’s prices for clicks that will not convert here.

The page the click lands on

Advertising magnifies whatever the page already does. A slow page or a buried phone number raises cost per enquiry more than any bidding change will lower it.

The negative keyword list

The list of what you are not. A handful of irrelevant phrases can absorb a meaningful share of a small monthly budget before anybody notices, and nothing reports it.

Retargeting

The audience has already visited and demonstrated interest, and reaching them again costs a fraction of reaching a stranger. In the small accounts we are shown it is the single most common thing missing entirely.

Those four are worth more attention than the budget figure. Doubling spend over a broken page doubles the waste; fixing the page changes every click that follows.

What growth changes

The Words People Use Keep Moving

A city that added 9.1% to its population produces a stream of buyers with no habits yet.

Somebody who has lived here twenty years knows which firm to call. Somebody who arrived last spring types a description of the problem, and the description they use is frequently not the phrase your account is built on.

That makes the search terms report the most valuable document in the account and the one least often opened. It shows what people actually typed, as opposed to what you asked for, and in a growing market the gap between the two widens every quarter.

Read it monthly and move the useful phrases into the account deliberately. That single habit does more for cost per enquiry than any automated bidding change, because it corrects the account toward the market rather than toward the platform.

It also feeds the unpaid work. The words that convert in a paid campaign are the words the pages should be built on, which is the cheapest research a growing market offers.

One practical note about automated campaign types, because they are the default now and they interact badly with everything above. Left to choose their own placements and their own geography inside whatever boundary they are given, they will find the cheapest impressions available, and next to a much larger market the cheapest impressions are rarely the ones you want. Keeping a manual, reviewable boundary is less sophisticated and it is the difference between a report you can act on and one you can only read.

Your own name

Bidding On Your Own Brand: When It Is Worth It

Defensible in one situation, a waste in the other, and the test takes a minute.

Search your business name and look at the result. If nothing but your own listing appears, paying for that click buys what you already had for nothing.

If a competitor is advertising there, the calculation changes: their click is cheap, the audience has already decided, and letting it go unanswered costs more than defending it.

The defence is inexpensive because the match between the search, the advertisement and your site is as close as it ever gets, which is exactly the situation the auction rewards.

Keep it small and keep it separate from everything else, so that its cost per enquiry does not flatter the rest of the account. Brand clicks convert well by definition and will hide a weak campaign if they are blended in.

Matching the promise

The Page Has To Say What The Advertisement Said

The commonest reason a well-built campaign produces nothing.

An advertisement promising same-week availability leads to a page that does not mention timescales. One naming a service leads to a list of twelve. In both cases the reader arrives, fails to find the thing that brought them, and leaves.

That gap is invisible in the account, where the click looks identical to a good one. It shows only as a conversion rate that nobody can explain.

So the check is to read the advertisement and the page together, in order, as a stranger: does the page immediately deliver what the advertisement offered? If not, the page is the cheaper thing to change.

Do it for each of the main phrases. Twenty minutes of this usually finds two mismatches, and fixing them is worth more than any bid adjustment available.

PPC Agency Guelph FAQ

Straight answers before you ever book a call.

Why is our cost per click rising with nothing changed?

Most often the targeting is reaching a larger adjacent market where prices are set by bigger budgets. Nothing in the account announces the crossing; you see a drifting cost per click and a falling conversion rate. Draw the boundary around the places you genuinely serve and price travel beyond it separately.

What should we fix before raising the budget?

The boundary, the landing page and the negative keyword list, in that order. Advertising magnifies whatever the page already does, so doubling spend over a slow page doubles the waste. Then set up retargeting, which in most small accounts we open is missing entirely.

How do we decide what to spend?

From three numbers rather than a benchmark: what a customer is worth over the relationship, what share of enquiries convert, and the maximum defensible cost per enquiry that follows. With those the budget is arithmetic. A supplier quoting a monthly figure before asking for them is quoting a fee.

Which report should we actually read?

The search terms report, monthly. It shows what people typed rather than what you asked for, and in a city that added 9.1% to its population the gap between the two widens every quarter as new arrivals describe problems in their own words. Moving those phrases into the account deliberately beats any automated bidding change.
