Where it starts

Website Design Guelph Begins With A Site That Stopped Fitting

Nothing is broken. That is what makes it hard to justify, and why it gets left two years too long.

The signals are small and they are all the same signal. A service you now sell has no page. The team page lists somebody who left. A price is wrong and changing it means an email to somebody else. You stop sending people to the site, because the thing you want them to see is not on it.

None of those is a fault. They are the gap between a business and a description of it written when the business was smaller, and that gap only ever widens.

A Guelph business that has grown steadily for five years is usually running on a site built for the first two. The question is not whether it still works. It is whether it still describes what you sell today.

The real trigger

Outgrown, Not Broken

Four signs, and all four are structural rather than visual.

One page for everything you sell

The commonest and the most expensive. A business with five services and a single services page ranks for none of them, and adding the fifth service made it worse rather than better.

Changes require an invoice

If updating a price or a photograph means emailing somebody and waiting, the site is governing the business rather than serving it, and the backlog grows until nobody bothers.

Nobody can add a page

A site where a new page has to be built from scratch by its original author will not keep pace with a growing business. It should be a fifteen-minute job.

The form goes to one person

Who has left, or is on holiday, or filters it. Enquiries disappearing silently is the failure nobody notices, because an absence reports nothing.

None of the four is visible in a screenshot, which is why rebuilds get specified as redesigns and the same problem is purchased again in a nicer typeface.

What to publish instead of news

What Website Design Guelph Should Build That Is Not A Blog

Company news is read by nobody. The alternative is more work and lasts for years.

A resource page answers one question completely: how a particular job is priced, what the options actually are, what goes wrong and how to avoid it. It is written once, revised occasionally, and keeps earning.

A news post about an award is read the week it goes up and never again. The effort is similar and the return is not comparable.

Three or four resource pages, each genuinely useful, do more for a small business site than two years of monthly posting — and they are the pages people send to each other, which nothing else on the site achieves.

Write them from the questions you are tired of answering. That tiredness is a reliable signal that the question is common and the answer is not published anywhere good.

Revisit each one once a year rather than leaving it. A resource page that is still accurate four years later is doing more work than anything else on the site, and the only thing that can undo it is a price, a regulation or a product name that quietly stopped being true.

Keeping a record

What Website Design Guelph Should Leave Behind: A Change Log

A site accumulates small changes from several people, and nobody can reconstruct them afterwards.

A single page or document: date, what changed, who asked for it. Two lines per entry. It sounds bureaucratic until the first time something breaks and the only useful question is what was altered last week.

It also answers the slower questions. Enquiries dropped in the spring — what changed in the spring? Without a record, that is unanswerable and the conversation becomes guesswork.

Include the things that are not on the site: a change of phone number, a new tracking tag, a supplier swapped out. Those are the changes that cause the strangest symptoms and the ones nobody thinks to mention.

It costs a minute per change and it is the difference between diagnosing a problem and rebuilding around it.

Keep it where the next person will find it — beside the list of accounts and passwords rather than in somebody’s own notes. A record that leaves with the person who kept it was never really a record.

Sign-off

One Person Approves, In One Pass

Projects of this size are delayed by approval far more often than by work.

Name the person who can say yes before anything starts, and make sure everybody else’s view reaches them before it reaches the supplier. A project receiving separate opinions from three directors is a project that stops.

Give the approval a deadline in the plan, the same as any other task. “Waiting on client” is the largest single cause of overrun in web projects and it never appears in anybody’s estimate.

Agree what approval covers, as well. Approving a design is not approving the words, and discovering that distinction late is how a finished site goes back two stages.

Where a business genuinely cannot name one approver, that is worth knowing in week one. It usually means the project has two sponsors who want different things, and no supplier can resolve that for you.

For a buyer, a contested supplier market means a wide spread of quotes for work that sounds identical. Two proposals can differ by a factor of three and neither is dishonest; they are describing different projects in the same vocabulary.

What separates them is not the page count. It is how much is designed from scratch, what the site connects to, who writes the words, and what happens on the day something breaks.

Straight answers

Website Design Guelph FAQ

Straight answers before you ever book a call.

How do we know it is time to rebuild?

Four structural signs, none of them visual: one page for everything you sell, changes that require an invoice, nobody inside the business able to add a page, and a form going to somebody who has left. If the site looks dated but none of those apply, you need a facelift rather than a rebuild.

Why do quotes vary so much?

Because they describe different projects in the same words. What moves the number is how much is designed from scratch, what the site connects to, who writes the content, and what happens after launch. Ask every supplier for all four and the quotes become comparable; without them you are comparing one number with a different number.

What should we insist on so it lasts?

A page type rather than a one-off page, a structure matching what customers search rather than your org chart, and somebody inside the business who can actually change things. All three are free to ask for at the start, expensive to retrofit, and routinely absent from a quote competing on price.

What is the biggest risk in the project?

Launching without redirects. A redesign changes URLs, and if the old ones are not pointed at their replacements everything they earned is discarded on launch day, with the symptom arriving three weeks later as an unexplained drop. Ask for the redirect map as a dated deliverable before launch.
