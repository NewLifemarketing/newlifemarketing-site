Video Production Peterborough

Five Months Of Footage

Video Production Peterborough has a constraint no agency brief usually mentions: a great deal of what is worth filming here only exists while the locks are open, and Parks Canada publishes the dates months in advance for anybody who thinks to look.

The calendar

Video Production Peterborough Runs On A Published Season

Victoria Day to Thanksgiving, and in 2026 that meant 15 May to 12 October.

Five months. Outside them the locks are closed and not maintained, the water traffic is gone, and the shots that make this part of Ontario look like itself are simply unavailable until the following May.

Most businesses discover this in February, when somebody asks for a summer film for a spring campaign and the answer is that the footage does not exist and cannot be made for three months.

The fix is a calendar rather than a budget. Decide in winter what next season has to produce, book the days against the season rather than against a quarter, and film the summer material while the summer is happening.

It is the rare planning problem with a published answer: the opening and closing dates are posted by Parks Canada well before anybody needs them.

What one day should produce

Plan The Shot List Before The Season Opens

Once a crew is standing in your building, another setup costs minutes.

The work itself

The job going in, the machine running, the finished result. The only footage a competitor cannot buy from the same stock library you can.

The questions you answer weekly

Each one on camera in ninety seconds. The most reused material a shoot produces, because it goes on the website, into email replies and into social for years.

The seasonal shots

Whatever only exists while the water is open. These are the ones that cannot be recovered in November, so they go first on the list, not last.

Cutaways and stills

The unglamorous bank of detail shots and photographs that lets an editor build ten more pieces later without anybody booking another day.

Decide those in advance rather than on the morning. A crew improvising on a site it has never seen produces a good film and nothing else, and the second film is where the economics of a shooting day live.

How it is watched

Video Production Peterborough Should Assume The Sound Is Off

A large share of views happen silently, and a film that depends on audio simply fails for those people.

Captions are not an accessibility afterthought; they are how most social viewing works. Without them a piece to camera is a person moving their mouth, and the viewer moves on within two seconds.

Which changes how the film is made rather than only how it is finished. The point has to be visible as well as audible: show the thing, put the key figure on screen, let the picture carry the argument.

Burn the captions into the picture for social use and supply a separate caption file for anywhere that accepts one. Both, because the platforms differ and the alternative is a film that works in one place.

Check them. Automatic captions mangle trade terms, place names and prices with great confidence, and an error in text on screen is more noticeable than the same error spoken.

Keep the lines short and leave them on screen long enough to read at a glance. Two lines at a time is the practical limit, and a caption that changes faster than somebody can read it is worse than none, because the viewer stops trying rather than catching up.

Permission

Get It In Writing Before The Camera Is Out

Dull, quick, and the thing that stops a finished film from being usable.

Anybody recognisable on camera should agree, in writing, that the footage can be used. A line on a sheet with a name and a date is enough for most purposes and it takes thirty seconds per person.

It matters most for the people least likely to be asked: a customer who happened to be there, a member of staff who has since left, a child in the background at a public event.

The same applies to the premises. Filming inside somebody else’s building needs their agreement, and a venue will often have its own form, which is worth asking about a week ahead rather than on the morning.

Keep the forms with the footage. Three years later, when a clip is wanted for something new, the question of whether it may be used is answerable in seconds or not at all.

Where somebody declines, that is the end of it, and it is worth saying so out loud before filming starts. People agree far more readily when refusing is visibly allowed, and a reluctant participant is poor material anyway.

The person on camera

What Happens When They Leave

A film built around one person has a lifespan tied to that person’s employment.

Owners are safe. Managers and front-of-house staff are not, and a library of films featuring somebody who left eighteen months ago is a library that has to be reshot or quietly retired.

So for anything intended to last, favour the work over the face: hands, process, finished results, voices over pictures rather than people addressing the lens.

Where a person genuinely is the proposition — and for a lot of small businesses they are — make it the owner, and accept that a staff film is a shorter-lived asset priced accordingly.

It is also a conversation to have with whoever is being filmed. Somebody who understands the footage may outlast their time at the company is giving more informed consent than somebody who does not.

Our scope

What We Would Take On For Video Production Peterborough

Narrow, and easier to say before a meeting than during one.

We film and edit for Ontario businesses and run the advertising the footage goes into, managing over $70,000 a month in spend with the owner on the media personally. The reason we do both is that a film nobody puts money behind is a cost rather than an asset.

How we would plan a video production peterborough booking

We would ask what is only possible in a particular month and build the day around that, because video production peterborough work tied to a season has one date and no second attempt. Everything that can be filmed at any time gets scheduled around the thing that cannot.

We are not a local crew and would not pretend to be one. Where a job needs somebody who can appear at two days’ notice several times a year, a closer supplier is the better buy and we will say so on the first call.

When should we film?

Whatever is seasonal, while the season is open. Parks Canada ran the 2026 Trent-Severn navigation season from 15 May to 12 October, and outside those dates the water-side material simply does not exist. Interior and interview work can happen any month, so it fills the days around the shots that cannot wait.

How many pieces should one day produce?

Far more than most people ask for. Once a crew is standing in your building another setup costs minutes, so a planned day yields a main film, several short answers to common questions, a bank of cutaways and a set of photographs. An unplanned day yields one film at the price of a day.

Can we film it ourselves?

Partly, and you should. A phone plus a clip microphone covers social posts, enquiry replies and anything unexpected. What it does not cover is lighting an interior, clean audio in a noisy room, or the deliberate piece at the top of a page. Do both and the months between shooting days stop being empty.

Who should own the raw footage?

You should, and it is worth settling before the day rather than after. The rushes are the asset: holding them means a different editor can produce something new in three years without booking another day in a season that only lasts five months.
