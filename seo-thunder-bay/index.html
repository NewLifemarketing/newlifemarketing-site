Search In Thunder Bay: Common Questions

Is it a problem that our agency is not local?

Not inherently — a thousand kilometres from Thunder Bay does not change how a page is indexed, and plenty of good work is done remotely. What changes is what can go wrong: accounts created in the agency’s name, pages on a domain you do not control, and local detail nobody checked. Those are all avoidable, and they are avoided by asking four questions before signing rather than discovering the answers in month eight.

What are the four questions?

For a Thunder Bay engagement: who actually does the work, by name. What do I own the day I leave. Who checks that a local fact is true before it publishes. And what will success look like, written down before the first invoice. All four have short answers, none of them appear in a standard proposal, and together they separate the field faster than any amount of comparing packages.

Why does local detail matter so much?

Because it is what makes a Thunder Bay page worth reading, and because it is what a remote writer is least equipped to get right. Someone writing from away reaches for what a search returns — the lake, the bridge, the terminals — and some of it will be wrong in ways only a resident catches. A page that gets a local fact wrong is worse than one that mentions none, because in a market this size being wrong gets mentioned.

Why is demand so much higher here than in Sudbury?

We can measure the Thunder Bay gap rather than explain it. Sudbury has 166,004 people and 90 searches a month for this; Thunder Bay has 108,843 and 260. Per head that is roughly three times. What follows from it is the useful part: with demand that high and few local suppliers, most of those searches end with a firm from away — so the market’s real problem is not finding help, it is buying it well.

What should we own ourselves?

All of it, in the Thunder Bay owner’s hands. The Business Profile, analytics, Search Console, the domain and the hosting, each in an account an owner controls. It takes twenty minutes on day one and it is routinely skipped because somebody else setting it up is faster.
