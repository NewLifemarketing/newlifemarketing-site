Social Media Marketing Oakville

Eleven Dollars A Click

Social media marketing Oakville businesses buy carries a cost per click of $11.10, which is a striking figure for a town of 213,700 people and is not explained by how many people live here. It is explained by what they are worth to whoever is already bidding.

What the price means

Social Media Marketing Oakville Prices Are Set By Your Competitors

An auction price describes the people bidding, not the people being reached.

A click costs what the highest bidder can afford. At $11.10, the businesses competing for attention in this town are, on average, selling something where a single customer justifies that. Professional services, specialist health, larger home improvement, high-value retail.

That is a useful thing to know before you set a budget, because it tells you which auction you are walking into. If your own value per customer is comfortably above that figure, the price is not your constraint and you are probably underspending relative to what you could defend.

If it is well below, you are bidding against people who can outlast you, and the correct response is a narrower audience rather than a larger budget. Narrowing reduces the number of auctions you enter and raises the relevance of the ones you do, which is the only lever that moves the price in your favour.

What does not work is the middle: a broad audience at a modest budget in an expensive auction. That combination spends steadily, reaches thinly, and produces the conclusion that the channel does not work here.

The three products

Management, Marketing And Advertising Are Not The Same Purchase

The seven phrases in this cluster span three different jobs, and quotes rarely say which one is being sold.

Management

social media oakville, oakville social media management. Somebody makes and schedules the posts. It is production, it is priced by output, and it will not by itself produce enquiries in a town where organic reach is a small fraction of a modest following.

Marketing

social media marketing agency oakville, small business social media marketing oakville. Somebody decides what the account is for, who it talks to and what it should say. Strategy, in other words, and the most frequently skipped of the three.

Advertising

The paid distribution that actually reaches people who do not follow you. This is where the $11.10 applies and where enquiries come from, and it is the line that gets squeezed when a single blended fee comes under pressure.

Video, inside the cluster

social media video company oakville carries real volume here, which is unusual. This town is already asking for the production and the distribution together, which is the right instinct and the opposite of how most quotes are structured.

Insist on three numbers in any proposal: production cost, strategy cost, and media spend. One number conceals which of the three absorbed the others.

What is actually scarce

What Makes Social Media Marketing Oakville Campaigns Cheaper To Run

In an expensive auction, relevance is the only thing that lowers your cost, and relevance comes from material nobody else can post.

The work itself, filmed

The installation, the treatment room, the finished build, the thing being made. It cannot be copied from a stock library and it is the single most under-supplied asset in local social media.

The person the customer will meet

Ninety seconds of whoever answers the phone. For a service bought on trust this converts better than anything else and is the cheapest item on the list to produce.

The question asked before every purchase

Answered once, properly, on camera. It becomes the most reused asset you own and it works in advertising, on the site and in a sales conversation.

What social media marketing Oakville quotes almost never include

A line for making anything. A posting retainer with no production budget attached is an arrangement for distributing material that does not exist, and in an expensive auction it is distributing it badly.

One shooting day produces months of material and the marginal cost of each additional cut is small. Deciding that before the shoot rather than after changes what gets filmed: more coverage, more angles, fewer talking heads.

Measurement

Engagement Rate Is Not A Business Number

Two metrics dominate reporting here and neither appears in anybody’s accounts.

Engagement rate is the proportion of people who saw a post and reacted. It is a useful comparison between two pieces of creative and useless as a business measure, because the population it is a percentage of is chosen by the platform rather than by you.

Reach is how many accounts saw something once. In any market it is purchasable, so a reach figure without a spend figure beside it says nothing. A report showing both climbing together is describing arithmetic.

The numbers worth a monthly conversation are enquiries, what they cost, and what proportion became work. All three require something from your side of the table, which is precisely why the glossier metrics persist: they can be produced without asking you for anything.

Ask for a report where at least one line comes from your business rather than from a platform. The reaction to that request tells you how the relationship will run.

The commuter effect

Posting At Nine In The Morning In A Town That Left At Seven

Scheduling is the cheapest unexamined decision in most local accounts.

A large share of this town's working-age population is employed outside it. The practical effect on a social account is that the hours a default schedule treats as prime are hours when a good part of the audience is commuting, in an office, or not looking at a personal feed.

The evidence is in your own data rather than in a best-practice article. Look at when the posts that actually produced something were seen, and when enquiries arrive, and weight accordingly. For several categories here the useful window is the evening and the weekend, which is also when advertising is cheaper because fewer advertisers have bothered to look.

The same observation changes format. Somebody glancing at a phone between meetings and somebody on a sofa at nine in the evening want different things, and an account producing only one of them is reaching half its market with the wrong thing.

None of this requires a strategy document. It requires one look at the hourly data, which takes a few minutes and is almost never done.

Our part

Distribution, And The Footage To Feed It

A narrow offer, said plainly.

We run paid social against cost per enquiry and film the material it runs on. We manage over $70,000 a month in advertising spend for Ontario businesses and the owner runs the paid media personally. We are based in Minesing rather than Oakville, so filming here involves travel, which we price rather than fold into a fee.

We do not sell daily posting retainers, community management or inbox monitoring. They are real jobs, there are local firms that do them well, and buying them from us would be worse for you than buying them from somebody built for it.

Why is the cost per click so high here?

Because of who is bidding rather than how many people are available. An auction price reflects what the highest bidder can afford per customer, and this town’s advertisers are weighted toward categories with a high value per customer. If yours is not one of them, narrow the audience rather than raising the budget.

How often should we post?

As often as there is something worth showing, which for most local businesses is less than a daily schedule implies. Two posts a week of real work beats five of stock graphics, because the second pattern trains your own audience to scroll past you. Frequency should follow from how much material exists, not from a target.

Do we need to be on every platform?

No. Pick where your customers actually are, do that properly, and treat anything else as syndication. Spreading a small budget and a small amount of material across four platforms produces four mediocre presences and no useful data about any of them.

Can you work with our existing content person?

Yes, and it often works well: they know the business and make the material, we handle distribution and measurement. The thing to settle at the start is who decides what gets promoted, because the best-performing piece is frequently not the one anybody internally was proudest of.
