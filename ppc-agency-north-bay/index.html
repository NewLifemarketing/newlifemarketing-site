PPC Agency North Bay

Less To Manage

A ppc agency north bay runs into an awkward commercial fact: there is not much here to manage, the honest account is small and temporary, and the fee that follows from that is smaller than the one most proposals are built around.

The second number is the one that should shape any paid proposal here. Where unpaid positions are effectively unclaimed, renting the same visibility every month is a strange purchase, and a supplier who does not say so is not looking at the market.

That does not make advertising useless. It makes it narrow: a tool for answering questions quickly and for reaching the parts of a wide catchment where a map result cannot place you.

Both of those have a natural end. The question gets answered; the catchment campaign settles into a small, steady line that needs very little attention.

Which brings the awkward part forward rather than leaving it to month seven: in a market like this, a well-run account genuinely requires less work as it matures, and the fee should reflect that.

The part of the cluster that names it

What Google Ads Management North Bay Businesses Pay For

The phrase is searched for specifically. Here is what it should contain in a market this size.

The search terms report, read

What people actually typed rather than what you asked for. In a thin market a handful of irrelevant phrases can absorb a meaningful share of a month, and nothing reports it as a problem.

Negative keywords, maintained

The list of what you are not, growing from the report above. It is cumulative: an account two years old with no negatives has been paying for the same irrelevant clicks since launch.

A hand-drawn boundary

Named communities rather than a radius, reviewed against where paying customers actually were. In a catchment over an hour wide a default circle is wrong in both directions at once.

A named exception trigger

What has to happen for somebody to call you between reports. Without one, the first anybody hears of a problem is a month after it started.

Google Ads management in a market this size is those four done reliably, not a weekly round of bid adjustments. Ask what happens in the weeks when nothing needs changing — the answer tells you what the fee is really buying.

What the budget is for

Two Honest Uses, Both With An End

Neither is permanent, and both are testable.

The first is buying an answer. Does this offer land, does this page convert, which words do the people who actually enquire use. Each is settled in a fortnight for a modest sum, and each answer then directs unpaid work that would otherwise have been guesswork.

The second is reaching the catchment. A map result places you relative to where the searcher is standing, so a business in town is largely invisible to somebody searching from forty minutes out. Paid reach has no such constraint, and that is a specific job with a specific test.

Retargeting belongs inside the second. The audience is small, it has already visited, and reaching it again costs a fraction of reaching a stranger — and in the small accounts we are shown it is the most common thing missing entirely.

Agree what would end a test before it starts: a number, a date, or an answer. Without one of those three nothing ever switches off.

Before any budget

Three Numbers, One Afternoon

They make the fee conversation short and the reporting meaningful.

What a customer is worth

Over the whole relationship rather than the first job. Without it, cost per enquiry is a figure with nothing to compare it against.

What share of enquiries convert

The bridge between the two, and the number most likely to be guessed. A guess here moves the defensible budget by a factor of two.

The maximum defensible cost per enquiry

Which follows arithmetically. With it, the budget is a calculation rather than a benchmark borrowed from somebody else’s business.

A supplier who quotes a monthly figure before asking for those three is quoting a fee rather than proposing a plan.

What goes wrong

How A PPC Agency North Bay Account Goes Wrong

A correctly targeted account here will not absorb a large budget, and that looks like failure in a monthly report.

Somebody reads the report, sees unspent budget, and reasonably proposes broadening the match types or the radius to use it. The budget then gets spent, the clicks arrive, and the enquiries do not.

In this geography there is a second version: widening until the radius covers communities you cannot profitably reach. Those clicks are real and the jobs are not, because the travel arithmetic makes them unprofitable even when they convert.

There is a quieter version too, and it survives every review: automated campaign types choosing their own placements and geography inside whatever boundary they are given. In a thin market they will find the cheapest impressions available, and the cheapest impressions here are the ones nobody wants.

The better response is to accept the ceiling. Let the campaign spend what the market contains, and put the remainder into the profile and the pages, which keep earning after the card is declined.

The reporting habit that prevents all three versions is the same one: read the search terms and the geographic report side by side, monthly, and ask one question of each line. Would I have chosen to pay for this. Anything that fails that test becomes a negative keyword or an excluded location that week, and the account gets tighter every month instead of looser.

Should we advertise here at all?

Narrowly, and with an end in sight. Unpaid positions in this market are effectively unclaimed at difficulty 3, so renting the same visibility every month is a strange purchase. A budget earns its place answering a question in a fortnight and reaching the parts of the catchment a map result cannot place you in.

What should management actually include?

The search terms report read, negative keywords maintained from it, a hand-drawn boundary reviewed against where paying customers were, and a named trigger for contacting you between reports. Ask what happens in the weeks when nothing needs changing — that answer tells you what the fee buys.

Our budget never gets spent. Should we widen it?

Almost never. An underspending account in an eighty-search cluster is usually a correctly targeted one that has run out of relevant searches. Widening spends the money on people who were not looking for you, and here it also reaches communities the travel arithmetic makes unprofitable.

How do we decide what to spend?

From three numbers: what a customer is worth over the relationship, what share of enquiries convert, and the maximum defensible cost per enquiry that follows. With those the budget is arithmetic. Without them any figure is borrowed from a business that is not yours.
