Dryden Consulting And Marketing

Advice, Then Your Own Hands

Dryden Consulting And Marketing is searched for as one phrase and it describes two purchases, and in a town of 7,388 people the first one is almost always the right buy: somebody to tell you what to do, after which the work itself is small enough to do yourselves.

Why advice beats execution here

Dryden Consulting And Marketing: Two Purchases, One Phrase

The town is small enough that the doing is not the hard part.

Consulting means somebody tells you what to do. Marketing means somebody does it. In a city the second is worth buying because the volume of work genuinely exceeds what a business can absorb.

Here it rarely does. A town of 7,388 needs a correct Business Profile, a handful of pages, reviews collected on purpose and somebody answering the phone properly, and all of that is a few days of work spread over a year rather than a monthly engagement.

What is genuinely scarce is knowing which of those things matters and in what order, which is advice, and advice is cheap, infrequent and the input everything else depends on.

So the useful first question for any supplier is which of the two they are quoting. A proposal answering “both, monthly” is selling execution and including the advice as a meeting.

What the advice should settle

Four Decisions Worth More Than A Year Of Activity

Each is settled once, takes under an hour, and governs everything spent afterwards.

How far you actually serve

In the northwest this is not a formality. A customer two hours out at the same price is a different job, and the answer decides every targeting setting and every quote.

What a customer is worth

Until this number exists, no budget can be judged and no cost per enquiry means anything. It takes an afternoon with your own invoices.

Which page owns which search

Written down, one page per cluster. It prevents the commonest silent failure, which is two of your own pages splitting one result.

What you will not take on

The jobs outside your range, stated publicly. It costs nothing, disqualifies the enquiries that waste your week, and is the fastest credibility signal a small-town page carries.

None of those requires a retainer and all four survive a change of supplier, which is the test worth applying to anything bought in a market this size.

What to do with your own hands

The Work Itself Is Smaller Than It Sounds

Four jobs, none of them technical, all of them repeatable by somebody already on the payroll.

Correcting the Business Profile is an afternoon: categories, service area, hours, photographs that are actually current. It decides a large share of the local result and it is free.

Asking for reviews is a habit rather than a project. A specific moment in the job when somebody asks, and a short link that makes it a thirty-second task, is the whole method, and businesses that build it collect steadily.

Writing one page per thing you sell is easier for the person who does the work than for any agency, and it produces five results instead of the none a single services page produces.

And asking every arriving customer how they heard of you, written down, is the only report in a town this size that comes from your business rather than from a platform.

What to stop doing

What Dryden Consulting And Marketing Should Usually Recommend Cutting

Most small businesses are doing too many things badly rather than too few things well.

Six channels maintained by one person who also runs the business produces six half-efforts. Three done properly beats it, and the decision about which three is the advice being bought.

The ones to question first are the ones nobody can point at a result from: a social account posted to monthly, a directory listing, a tool bought for a problem that was solved another way.

Dropping something feels like going backwards and almost never is. The attention released goes into whatever remains, and the remaining things improve visibly within a quarter.

Write down what was stopped and when. If something turns out to have been carrying more than anybody realised, that shows up in the following quarter and can be restarted knowingly.

Expect almost nothing to be missed. The usual outcome of stopping two or three activities is that no measurable change occurs at all, which is itself the most useful finding available and the one no supplier being paid for those activities will ever volunteer.

Distance

What To Ask A Supplier A Long Way South

Every supplier answering these searches is remote, including us.

What is done at a distance

Search work, advertising, reporting and strategy, by almost everybody. A southern supplier is not worse at these than one down the road, and most of the work is in this column.

What is not

Filming, photographing a site, sitting with a team. These carry travel, and a supplier who does not price it separately has either absorbed it into the fee or has not planned to do it.

So the questions are specific: how many days on site does this proposal contain, who answers when the person who sold the work is unavailable, and what would you hand over if we stopped. A supplier whose answer to the last one is a login has kept the plan in their own head, and there is no second supplier down the road to pick it up.

Our scope

A Narrow Offer, Said Plainly

Including the honest version, which here is usually the smaller engagement.

Paid media on Google and Meta managed against cost per enquiry. Search work built one cluster at a time so two of your own pages never compete. Video and photography of real work. And the systems underneath, so an enquiry can be traced to what produced it. We manage over $70,000 a month in advertising spend for Ontario businesses and the owner runs the paid media personally.

What dryden consulting and marketing should hand back

A dryden consulting and marketing engagement with us should leave you holding the four decisions in writing, the profile audit with its corrections applied, the registry of which page owns which phrase, and a short list of what to do next yourselves. In a town of 7,388 that is frequently the whole job.

Not public relations, not events, not brand identity from scratch. We are in Minesing, a long way south, which changes nothing for this work and matters for filming, where travel appears as its own visible line.

Should we hire somebody to do this or just get advice?

In a town of 7,388, advice almost always. The work — a correct profile, a handful of pages, reviews collected on purpose — is a few days spread over a year, and the person who does the job can write about it better than an agency can. What is scarce is knowing which of those things matters and in what order.

What should the advice actually settle?

How far you genuinely serve, what a customer is worth, which page owns which search, and what you will not take on. Each takes under an hour, none needs a retainer, and all four survive a change of supplier. Everything spent afterwards is measured against them.

Is 70 searches a month worth anything?

It depends entirely on what a customer is worth. A handful of enquiries a month is transformative for a business selling large jobs and marginal for one selling small ones. That is why the value of a customer is the first number to work out, before any budget is discussed.

Can a supplier hundreds of kilometres away do this?

For search work, advertising, reporting and strategy, yes — those are done at a distance by almost everybody, and most of the work is in that column. For anything needing somebody present the travel is real and belongs on its own line. Ask what you would be handed if you stopped.
