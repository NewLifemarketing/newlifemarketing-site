The arithmetic

Google Ads Kitchener: What An Expensive Click Does To The Maths

Cost per click is an input nobody controls much. What it controls is where effort is worth spending.

$10.69
cost per click on the regional marketing cluster

Semrush, Canada database, October 2026

A hundred clicks at $10.69 costs $1,069 before anybody enquires. At a two per cent conversion rate that is two enquiries at over five hundred dollars each; at five per cent it is five at two hundred and fourteen. Nothing in the bidding interface moves a number by that much.

The page does. Which is why the first thing we ask to see is not the account but whatever the ads point at, and why a proposal that covers management and excludes landing pages is a proposal that has fenced off the main lever.

The structural reason this happens is not dishonesty. Improving a page is a one-off writing job that is hard to bill monthly; managing bids is a recurring activity that is easy to. The fee model quietly chooses the work.

Ask where the landing pages sit in the agreement. If the answer is "outside", ask what the plan is, because somebody has to do it.

Who you are bidding against

Two Anchors And A Lot Of Software Money

Communitech and Google’s Kitchener engineering office anchor a technology sector that bids differently from a local trade.

A software business with a customer worth tens of thousands over several years can justify a click price that a local service business cannot. In a shared auction, that sets the floor for everybody.

The second response is to be ruthless about geography and schedule. A technology company selling nationally does not care which hour or which postcode; a local business very much does, and those two settings are free.

Done together, those moves take a small account out of the auction it cannot win and into the one it can, usually inside a month.

What the fee actually buys

Separate The Platform Bill From The Management Bill

Conflating them is how a business loses track of what it is paying for what.

One amount goes to the platform for clicks and one goes to whoever manages the account. A single blended monthly figure hides which is which, and it hides it in the direction that suits the supplier.

Ask for them separately on every invoice, and ask what the fee covers in hours or in tasks. “Ongoing optimisation” is not a list.

The useful ratio is fee against enquiries produced, not fee against spend. An account spending a little and producing well deserves its fee; one spending a lot and producing nothing does not, regardless of how the percentage looks.

And ask what happens when the account improves. Under a percentage arrangement, efficiency reduces the supplier’s income, which is a fact worth naming out loud rather than discovering across a year.

The fee question

What A Google Ads Kitchener Fee Arrangement Rewards

The standard model in this market is a percentage of spend, and it leans in a predictable direction.

Under that arrangement the agency earns more when you spend more and nothing extra when the same spend produces twice the result. Everyone working under it can be conscientious and the incentive still points one way, which predicts which conversations you will have.

The alternatives each carry their own problem. A flat fee is honest and makes a small account unattractive to service properly. Performance pricing sounds ideal and founders on attribution, because the two sides rarely agree on what counts. A flat fee against a defined scope, reviewed quarterly, is the least bad arrangement we have found.

Whatever the model, one question settles a lot: under this arrangement, what would make you recommend we spend less? An agency that cannot answer has not thought about the incentive.

And in a market at this click price, "spend less and fix the page" is the right recommendation often enough that it should be available.

Closing the loop

Tell The Account Which Enquiries Became Work

The step that separates an account optimising for enquiries from one optimising for revenue.

Left alone, the system optimises toward enquiries, all of which it treats as equal. In reality a quarter of them are worth a great deal and the rest are worth nothing, and the account has no way of knowing which.

Feeding that information back — which enquiries became customers, and roughly what they were worth — changes what it goes looking for. It is the single largest improvement available to an account that is already working.

It requires the business to keep that record, which is the real obstacle. A column in a spreadsheet marking each enquiry as won, lost or junk is enough, and nobody can do it except the people answering the telephone.

Even without feeding it back, the record is worth keeping. It is the only way to tell whether a campaign producing more enquiries is producing more business, and those two things diverge more often than anybody expects.

What A Google Ads Kitchener Quote Should Separate

The media budget, which goes to Google, and the management fee, which goes to the agency. A single number a month tells you neither, and an agency taking a percentage of spend is paid more for spending more. Ask for both figures in writing, ask what happens to the account if you leave, and ask whose name it is in.

Straight answers

Google Ads Kitchener FAQ

Straight answers before you ever book a call.

What is the minimum budget worth running here?

At around ten dollars a click, a budget that buys fewer than a few hundred clicks a month is not enough data to optimise against, which makes managing it largely guesswork. Either find a narrower, cheaper corner of the auction where the same money buys more volume, or wait until the budget clears that floor.

Should we bid on our own brand name?

Often yes, defensively, because competitors can bid on it and a cheap click protecting an existing customer is usually worth it. The important part is reporting it separately: brand clicks convert at several times the rate of everything else, and blending them conceals how the rest of the account is performing.

Our competitors are software companies with bigger budgets. Can we compete?

Not in the same auctions, and you should not try. Specific terms describing exactly what you do are quieter, cheaper and convert better, and in a cluster this size they are most of what is worth having. Add a tight geography and a schedule that matches your buyer, and a small account stops competing with a national one.

Can you take over an existing account?

Yes, and read access with an assessment beats rebuilding, because an account with history carries learning that is expensive to recreate. The usual finding is not poor construction but an unread search-terms report, a geography set once, and brand spend blended into the headline number.
