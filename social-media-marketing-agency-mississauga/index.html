A City This Size Breaks Organic Social

Mississauga has 718,000 people. An organic post reaching a few hundred of them is not a small win, and treating it as one is how a Mississauga year disappears.

In an Ontario town of twenty thousand, an organic post reaching four hundred people reaches two percent of the market, and that is a genuine result. The same four hundred in Mississauga reaches roughly one twentieth of one percent, which is indistinguishable from nothing however good the post was. Social media mississauga accounts are judged against the first number and operating under the second.

This is not an argument about content quality. In Mississauga a brilliant post and a mediocre one both reach a few hundred people organically, because that is what the platforms give an account without a budget. The difference matters enormously at small scale and is swamped at this one.

Which inverts the usual recommendation. In most Ontario places we would say fix the free part first and consider paying later. A social media marketing agency mississauga businesses hire should be saying the opposite: the free part exists to survive being checked, and Mississauga reach has to be bought. Any social media marketing company mississauga owners interview that leads with an organic calendar has not done the arithmetic.

The upside

Paid Targeting Gets Cheaper As The City Gets Bigger

The same Mississauga density that makes organic hopeless makes paid unusually efficient, and the two facts are the same fact.

Density is the reason. Delivering an advertisement to ten thousand relevant people costs less where those ten thousand live close together than where they are spread across a county, and Mississauga is about as concentrated as Ontario gets outside Toronto. Social media advertising mississauga businesses run is genuinely cheaper per useful impression than the same campaign in a dispersed market.

The targeting is also better in Mississauga simply because the population supports it. Narrowing to a specific age, a specific set of postcodes and a specific interest leaves an unusably small audience in a town of twenty thousand. In Mississauga it still leaves tens of thousands of people, which means a social media agency mississauga firms hire can be precise without running out of market.

Questions

Social Media In Mississauga: Common Questions

Should we stop posting organically?

No, but stop expecting reach from it. In a Mississauga of 718,000 an organic post reaching a few hundred people is a rounding error however good it is, and that is a function of scale rather than of quality. The account still has three real jobs: surviving the click your advertising paid for, proving the business is current, and acting as a cheap test for what is worth promoting. Fund it for those and buy reach separately.

Is paid social expensive?

Less than most people expect in a Mississauga this dense, and the density is the reason. Delivering an advertisement to ten thousand relevant people costs less where those people live close together, and Mississauga is about as concentrated as Ontario gets outside Toronto. The targeting is also better here because the population supports it: narrowing by postcode, age and interest still leaves tens of thousands of people rather than an unusably small audience.

How much should we spend?

Start from what a customer is worth rather than from a percentage of revenue. If one new customer is worth two thousand dollars and a campaign produces an enquiry for forty, the budget question answers itself and the only limit is how many such enquiries exist. The mistake is picking a monthly figure first and then asking what it buys, which is how budgets get set at a level that cannot produce a measurable result either way.

Which platform should we advertise on?

Wherever your customers are, which for most consumer-facing Mississauga businesses means Facebook and Instagram, and for firms selling to other businesses means LinkedIn. The more useful question is usually not the platform but the audience definition, because at this city’s scale you can afford to be genuinely specific and most campaigns are not. A badly defined audience on the right platform performs worse than a precise one on the second choice.

Our organic engagement is good. Does this still apply?

It applies more, in a sense, because good organic engagement at this scale means you have something worth putting money behind and are not doing it. The posts people respond to without any budget are the ones most likely to work as advertising, and you already know which they are. That is a considerably better starting point than most campaigns have, and it is being wasted if the content never reaches beyond the few hundred people who see it free.
