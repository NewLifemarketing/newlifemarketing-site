Affluent, Diverse, And Ferociously Competitive

Richmond Hill is 200,000 people stacked along the Yonge Street spine — one of Canada's most affluent and most multicultural cities, where every category from dentistry to dumplings is contested block by block. The demographics are the headline: household incomes well above provincial averages, and a population where a majority of residents speak a language other than English at home — with large Chinese and Persian communities shaping entire retail corridors.

Businesses that market to Richmond Hill as if it were a single English-speaking suburb are invisible to half their own street. The competitive structure is just as distinct: commerce lives in plazas — hundreds of them — where forty units share one parking lot and no storefront gets discovered by accident. Professional and health services cluster densely around the Yonge corridor and compete on reviews and referrals; restaurants compete with Markham's food scene next door; and every advertiser in the city pays GTA-grade auction prices where Toronto brands bid on the same eyeballs.

It's a market that punishes mediocre marketing faster than anywhere else we work. The problems
