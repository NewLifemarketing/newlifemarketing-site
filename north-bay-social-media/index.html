North Bay Social Media

The Far Half Of The Catchment

North Bay Social Media earns its cost doing one thing nothing else here can do: reaching the people in a catchment more than an hour wide who will never see a map result with your name in it, because proximity decides that result and they are not near you.

The gap it fills

North Bay Social Media Reaches Where The Map Cannot

Local results weight proximity heavily, and a business has one address.

Somebody searching from forty minutes out gets a map result built around where they are standing. However good your profile is, it will not place a business in town in front of them, and no amount of work changes that.

A paid audience has no such constraint. It can be drawn to match the communities you genuinely serve, including the ones at the far edge, and the cost of reaching somebody out there is the same as reaching somebody downtown.

That is the strongest argument for a budget here and it is rarely the one in a proposal, which usually talks about brand awareness. Awareness is hard to judge; covering the half of your catchment that search cannot reach is a specific job with a specific test.

The test is one extra column at the point of enquiry: where the customer actually was. Over a quarter it answers the only question the budget needs to settle.

What a quote should separate

Four Jobs Behind One North Bay Social Media Invoice

The figure on an invoice usually covers four things with very different prices and very different value.

Deciding what to say

Occasional thinking, not monthly work. Charged every month it is billing for a decision made once, and it is the least visible line in any retainer.

Making the material

Photographs and film of real work. A genuine cost that is routinely assumed to be free, which is why accounts run out of things to publish.

Publishing and replying

Cheap, useful and highly visible on a report, which is why it tends to stand in for the whole arrangement.

Buying attention

Money reaching people who do not follow you. The only line that produces somebody new, and the first to shrink when a blended fee is negotiated down.

Four numbers rather than one, and the comparison between two suppliers becomes possible for the first time. A supplier who will not split them has not decided which of the four you are buying.

Who you are reaching

Two Audiences With Nothing In Common

A catchment this wide contains two groups who want opposite things from the same account.

The first lives close enough to pass your premises and already has a rough idea who you are. For them an account is maintenance: it confirms you are trading, shows recent work, and answers the small questions that would otherwise be a phone call.

The second is forty minutes out and has never heard of you. Nothing in your account is familiar to them, which means the post that works is the one that explains rather than the one that updates, and the two read very differently.

Most accounts here are written entirely for the first group, because the first group is who the owner knows. The posts land, the engagement looks healthy, and nothing reaches anybody new.

Splitting that is not a creative problem. It is a budget decision: the explaining posts need paid distribution aimed outward, and the maintenance posts need none at all.

The quiet constraint

You Will Run Out Of Things To Say Before Money

In a catchment this size the limiting factor is material, and it is rarely the one being budgeted for.

A business can fund a campaign for a year and still have nothing new to put in it after six weeks. That is the actual ceiling here, and it arrives long before the budget does.

What produces material is not a content calendar; it is a habit of capturing work as it happens. The finished install, the problem nobody expected, the thing that arrived damaged and was dealt with properly — all of it is interesting and none of it survives being remembered a fortnight later.

So the useful arrangement is a rule rather than a schedule: anything worth telling somebody about gets photographed at the time, by whoever is standing there, and sorted out afterwards.

A business that does that for a month has more usable material than a year of posting retainers would produce, and it costs nothing but the discipline.

Measurement

One Column Nobody Adds

The platform will tell you plenty. None of it says whether the far half of the catchment is responding.

Engagement, reach and follower counts all describe the audience the platform assembled, which in a wide catchment is overwhelmingly the people nearest you — the ones who would have found you anyway.

The number that matters is how far away the people who enquired actually were, and it does not exist unless somebody writes it down. One column, filled in at the point of enquiry, answers the only question the budget is really asking.

Read it quarterly rather than monthly, because at these volumes a month contains too few enquiries to show a pattern and too many opinions to settle one.

And keep the column across years. The useful signal in a catchment like this is whether the average distance is moving, which no single quarter can show.

Our part

Distribution, And The Material To Feed It

A short offer, honestly bounded.

We run paid social against cost per enquiry and film the material it runs on. We manage over $70,000 a month in advertising spend for Ontario businesses and the owner runs the paid media personally.

What we would not take on in north bay social media

North Bay Social Media work that means writing in the owner’s voice is the part that cannot be outsourced honestly, and we would not take it. In a city where people know each other, a borrowed voice is detectable and it costs more than it buys.

We are in Minesing, several hours south, so filming here involves travel, which we price as its own line. We do not sell daily posting retainers, community management or inbox monitoring.

Why pay for reach when we already rank well?

Because ranking does not reach the whole catchment. Local map results weight proximity heavily, so one address cannot appear for somebody searching from forty minutes out, and the catchment here runs over an hour across two districts. A paid audience can be drawn to match where you actually serve, at the same cost per person as downtown.

What should a quote separate?

Posting, strategy and distribution. They have different prices, different frequencies and very different value: distribution produces the enquiries and is the line that shrinks when a blended monthly fee is squeezed. A supplier who cannot split them has not decided which of the three you need.

Should the account be the business or the owner?

Usually the owner, or at least a voice that reads as a person. A workable arrangement is the owner recording a voice note or sending three photographs from a job, somebody else turning it into the post, and the owner reading it before it goes out. The voice stays theirs and the hour disappears.

It worked and then stopped. Why?

Usually creative fatigue rather than a failing campaign. In an audience this size people see a piece of creative repeatedly and cost per result climbs while nothing in the account has changed. Decide at the start how many distinct pieces a quarter needs, film them in one visit, and hold some back.
