Placements, Not Platforms

People search for Instagram ads, Facebook ads and Meta ads in roughly equal measure here, and all three describe the same account, the same targeting tools and the same billing.

What differs is the placement, which is a setting rather than a purchase. The same campaign can run in a feed, in stories, in reels or on a partner network, and which of those performs best is an empirical question nobody can answer in advance.

That matters commercially. If a supplier quotes separately for each, you are paying twice for one setup, and you are also being promised a prediction that the system itself does not make.

The useful version is one account, one budget, and placements decided by what the reporting shows after a fortnight rather than by what somebody preferred at the start.

It is also the easiest thing in a proposal to verify before signing. Ask what placements the supplier intends to run and why, and if the answer is a preference rather than a plan to test, you are being sold an opinion as a service. The honest answer at the start of any account is that nobody knows yet, and the fortnight of data that settles it costs the same either way.

What actually differs

The Creative Is The Targeting

Placement choices matter far less than what the material is and how it is cut.

Vertical, always

A landscape video in a vertical placement wastes most of the screen, and the screen is the whole of the attention you bought. Decide it before filming, not in the edit.

Captions, checked

A meaningful share of viewing happens with the sound off. Auto-generated captions left unchecked produce the wrong words in the one element people actually read.

The point in two seconds

Not a logo and not a title card. Whatever the piece is about has to be visible before a thumb moves.

Real footage rather than stock

The only material a competitor cannot buy from the same library. In a city this size it is also the only thing that reads as a specific business rather than a category.

Those four decide performance more than any placement setting, and all four are taken before filming. Taken afterwards they mean editing the same material twice.

There is a practical test for all four before anything is filmed. Put the draft on a phone, hold it at arm’s length, mute it, and look away for two seconds. If you cannot tell what it is about when you look back, the piece will not work in a feed no matter how good the rest of it is, and finding that out before a shoot costs nothing.

Where it earns

Three Jobs Worth Paying For

Each is testable, and one of them is missing from nearly every small account.

Retargeting first. The audience has already visited your site, reaching them again costs a fraction of reaching a stranger, and in a considered purchase it is where a meaningful share of conversions actually happens.

Reaching people before they search second. Search intercepts a decision already underway; this reaches the much larger group who have not started, which is the one thing a search account cannot do in any form.

Recruitment third, and almost nobody runs it. The audience tools are the same and the creative is different, and in a competitive labour market one filled vacancy is frequently worth more than a quarter of enquiries.

Each needs a stated end or a fixed job, because without one a campaign becomes a permanent line item and the first anybody reviews it is a year later, by which point nobody remembers what it was supposed to prove.

The geography

What An Instagram Ads Agency Vaughan Should Do With Five Communities

A city-wide audience reports one average and hides everything useful.

Vaughan’s population is spread across five communities, and for a business with a physical location they are not equally reachable. A single city-wide audience produces one blended cost per enquiry that cannot be acted on.

Splitting the reporting costs nothing and takes an hour. A campaign performing well in one community and poorly in another is a common pattern and entirely invisible without the split.

The southern boundary matters too: an audience drawn generously reaches into a much larger market where costs are higher and the viewer has closer alternatives, and nothing announces the crossing.

Review it quarterly against where the customers who actually paid were, which is the only record that settles it.

Measuring it

Cost Per Enquiry, On A Rolling Quarter

The platform offers several figures that cannot be acted on, and two that can.

Engagement rate is a percentage of an audience the platform chose. Reach is purchasable, so a reach figure with no spend beside it says nothing. Followers are the least predictive number on the page.

What works is cost per enquiry split by community, and asking arriving customers how they heard of you. The second is the only line in the report that comes from your business rather than from a platform.

Read both across three months rather than one. Demand creation shows up later than interception, so an account judged monthly will be cut in the month before it would have started working.

And watch creative fatigue explicitly: a rising cost per result with nothing changed in the account is the audience having seen it rather than the campaign failing. Plan for it by deciding at the start how many distinct pieces a quarter needs, and holding some back rather than opening with everything.
