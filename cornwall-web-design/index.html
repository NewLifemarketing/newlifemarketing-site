Cornwall Web Design

Two Trades, One Small Market

The cornwall web design cluster carries both vocabularies in almost equal measure — design and development, fifty searches each — which in a market of 270 searches means the two trades are being asked for by the same small group of businesses, usually without knowing they are different.

Design is presentation: a site that explains what you do, collects enquiries and perhaps sells a catalogue through a platform that already exists. Development is software: something on the site has to compute, integrate or decide.

In Toronto those are separate industries with separate suppliers and a cluster of 17,890 searches to divide between them. Here they are 270 searches and frequently the same two or three local suppliers.

The practical consequence for a buyer is that the quote you receive may be priced for either, and the words in the brief will not tell you which. The test is narrow: does anything on the site have to think? If not, you are buying design, and the development vocabulary in the cluster is a red herring.

Several of the phrases here also carry "ontario" or "canada", which is a searcher being careful about the other Cornwall. Say the province early on your own pages; it costs a few words and most competitors do not.

The most used element

The Phone Number Is The Conversion

On a local services site it is tapped more often than every form submission combined.

It belongs in the header on every page, as a link rather than as text, so that a thumb on a phone dials it instead of selecting it. That one detail is routinely missed on sites that cost five figures.

It belongs in the footer as well, because somebody scrolling to the bottom has finished reading and is deciding. And it belongs near the bottom of any page describing work somebody is ready to buy.

Put the hours beside it. A number with no indication of when it is answered produces calls at nine in the evening that nobody picks up, and a caller who concludes the business is closed.

Where calls go to a mobile that is often on site, say so and offer a second route. Honesty about availability converts better than an unanswered ring, which is the only impression a missed call leaves.

Structure

Cornwall Web Design Needs One Page For Each Thing You Sell

The commonest structural fault, and the one that quietly limits everything afterwards.

A single services page listing six offerings can rank for one of them at best. Six pages, each about one thing, can rank for six, and each can say enough to be worth reading rather than a paragraph.

It also changes what a visitor does. Somebody who wanted one specific job lands on a page about that job, with examples of it and a price indication, rather than on a list where their thing is the fourth bullet.

The usual objection is that it looks repetitive to whoever built it. It does not look repetitive to a visitor, who sees exactly one of those pages and never knows the others exist.

Where there is genuinely not enough to say about one of the six, that is a useful finding rather than a reason to merge. A service nobody can describe in four hundred words is usually a service nobody is really buying.

What a small-town site has to do

Three Jobs, In Order Of How Often They Are Missed

Say where you go

The city holds 47,845 people and the census agglomeration 61,415, so a fifth of your potential customers are outside the boundary. "Do you come out this way" is a top-three question and answering it on the page removes a round of email.

Show the work

Photographs of actual jobs rather than stock. In a town where a reputation travels by word of mouth, the site’s job is to confirm what somebody already heard, and a stock image does the opposite.

Make contact easy on a phone

A tappable number, a form that is short, and hours that are current. Unglamorous, and it is where most small-town sites lose enquiries they already earned.

None of these requires a large budget. All three are regularly absent from sites that cost a great deal.

The pricing question

Shown, Banded, Or Withheld

There are three honest answers and only one wrong one, which is pretending the question was not asked.

Showing a price works where the thing is standard and you are not the cheapest — it filters out the people who were only ever going to ask, which is most of the enquiry volume that wastes a week.

Banding works for almost everything else: a typical job runs from this to that, here is what moves it. Visitors accept a range readily and resent its absence, because the alternative is a phone call whose only purpose is to find out whether they can afford you.

Withholding is defensible where every job is genuinely different, but then say that in those words and describe what the first conversation covers. Silence is read as expensive, and the people who read it that way do not ring to check.

The decision is commercial rather than technical, and it belongs to the owner rather than to whoever builds the site. It is also the single question most likely to be left unanswered until the week of launch.

Our scope

What We Would Quote For, And What We Would Not

Short, and said before a meeting rather than halfway through one.

We build and rebuild presentation sites where the page list is decided against the search clusters each page is meant to own, the redirects are written before launch, and the words are planned as part of the project rather than chased afterwards. We also buy the advertising those pages receive, managing over $70,000 a month in spend for Ontario businesses.

Where cornwall web design is not what you need

Where the current site is sound and the enquiries are not arriving, cornwall web design work is the wrong purchase and a rebuild will not change the result. The twenty-minute phone test above settles which situation you are in, and we would rather run it with you than quote for the wrong project.

We do not build online shops, booking platforms, membership systems or anything with its own users outside the business. We are based in Minesing, several hours west, and this work is done remotely.

How much should a website cost here?

The honest answer is a range, and the way to narrow it is to write the page list first. Two quotes against the same list of pages are comparable; two quotes against a conversation are not, and the difference between them is usually scope rather than quality.

Do we need web design or web development?

Design, unless something on the site has to think — a calculator, a portal, an integration with a system you depend on. The cluster here contains both vocabularies at the same volume, which means the words in a brief will not tell you which you are being quoted for. Ask directly.

Will a new site bring us more enquiries?

Only where the present one is losing them, and that is testable in twenty minutes before spending anything. Open it on a phone outdoors, try to dial the number with a thumb, send yourself a test enquiry and see where it lands. Most of what a rebuild would fix shows up in that test, and some of it can be fixed without one.

What if our web designer disappears?

Settle it on the first day rather than the last. The accounts the site depends on should be registered to a company email address you control, and you should be able to log into each one today without asking anybody. Where that is already not true, fixing it is an afternoon now and a dispute later.

That one question settles whether this is design or development, and it settles most of the price with it.
