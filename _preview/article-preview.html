Where Ontario SMBs Are Actually Spending Their Marketing Budget in 2026

Marketing Budgets/SMB Trends/Ontario

NewLife Research Posted on July 30, 2026

This analysis draws on data from 10 sources, spanning industry benchmark reports, platform economic impact studies, and Statistics Canada business survey data.

Ontario small and mid-sized businesses are putting a bigger share of revenue behind marketing than at any point since 2022 — and the channel mix behind that spend has shifted hard toward paid social, paid search, and SEO. Here's what the numbers actually show about where the budget is going and what it's producing.

We'll explore:

  • How Much Ontario SMBs Are Actually Spending
  • Where the Budget Is Going: Channel Mix
  • The Budget Growth Trajectory Since 2022
  • Where New Customers Actually Come From

How Much Ontario SMBs Are Actually Spending

Ontario small and mid-sized businesses now put an average of 9.6% of gross revenue toward marketing in 2026, up from 6.2% in 20221. That's not a handful of aggressive spenders pulling the average up — three out of four surveyed businesses report a higher marketing budget this year than last2.

The increase tracks closely with how competitive local search and social feeds have become. Businesses that held their budget flat through 2023 and 2024 are, on average, reporting the slowest customer growth in this year's data.

Where the Budget Is Going: Channel Mix

The channel mix has shifted decisively toward paid social and paid search, with SEO retainers holding a steady third place rather than losing ground to paid channels.

Figure 1: Average Monthly Marketing Spend by Channel — Ontario SMBs, 2026

Source: 3

Meta Ads and Google Ads together account for just under half of the average monthly budget, at $2,310 and $2,040 respectively3. SEO retainers average $1,180 a month, but businesses running SEO for three-plus years report a meaningfully lower blended customer acquisition cost than businesses relying on paid channels alone4.

The Budget Growth Trajectory Since 2022

The shift isn't just about where the money goes this year — it's about the trend line. Year-over-year budget growth has been consistent since 2022, tracking closely with the share of SMBs that now operate from a documented marketing plan rather than an ad-hoc one5.

Figure 2: Marketing Budget Growth — Average Ontario SMB, Indexed to 2022

Source: 5

Where New Customers Actually Come From

Budget growth only matters if it changes outcomes, and on that front the data is direct: when new customers are asked how they first heard about a business, Google Search and social media now account for nearly two-thirds of responses, comfortably ahead of referral and walk-in traffic6,7.

Figure 3: Where New Customers Say They First Found the Business, 2026

Source: 6

Businesses that connect paid media, organic search, and a CRM into one measurable pipeline convert new inquiries at nearly twice the rate of businesses running channels independently of one another8. That gap correlates closely with whether a business operates from a documented plan9 and whether its ad platforms are actually wired back into its CRM10 — the spend total matters less than whether it's organized into a system.
