The market you are hiring in

Software Developer Waterloo: A Competitive Labour Market, Not A Supply Problem

There are plenty of developers here. The question is what you can offer against the alternatives.

26,000+
active co-op students at the University of Waterloo

University of Waterloo, Co-operative Education

121,436
people in the City of Waterloo

Statistics Canada, 2021 Census of Population

The co-operative education programme alone places more than 26,000 students, which makes this one of the densest concentrations of software talent in the country. Supply is not the constraint.

What is constrained is interesting work. A developer choosing between a product company building something hard and a non-technical business that needs an internal tool maintained is making an easy decision, and salary alone rarely reverses it.

So a business outside the technology sector hiring a software developer waterloo is competing on something other than money, and the honest list of what that can be is short: autonomy, a problem that is genuinely interesting, or a schedule that suits somebody who wants one.

The shape of the capacity

Two Days Of Someone Experienced Beats Five Of Someone Cheap

The budget question is usually asked as how many people, and that is the wrong axis.

A business with one internal system and a short list of improvements does not need a full week of anybody. It needs a few hours of judgement, reliably, from somebody who has built this kind of thing before and will say which half of the list is not worth doing.

Hiring full-time to get that produces the opposite: a capable person with more hours than work, who fills the gap by building things nobody asked for. Those things then need maintaining, and within two years the role justifies itself by the complexity it created.

Part-time senior capacity is harder to find and harder to retain, which is why it gets ruled out early. It is also what most firms of this size actually need, and in a labour market with this much senior talent in it the arrangement is genuinely available.

The test is the list. If twelve months of work exists and keeps being added to, hire. If the list is six items and shrinking, buying days is cheaper and reversible.

If you hire

Three Things To Insist On From Day One

Each costs nothing at the start and is expensive to retrofit.

A repository you control

In the company’s account, with more than one administrator. A business that discovers its source lives in a personal account has discovered it does not own its software.

Documentation written as work, not afterwards

A short document per system: what it does, what it connects to, how to run it locally, what breaks it. Thirty minutes a week while the knowledge is fresh, rather than a week nobody ever schedules.

A second pair of eyes, however junior

Even a part-time co-op student reviewing changes removes the single point of knowledge. The review quality matters less than the fact that somebody else has read the code.

Assessing somebody

How To Interview A Software Developer Waterloo When You Cannot Judge The Code

You are not going to assess the technical work. There are three things you can assess.

Ask them to explain a past project to you, in your language, including what went wrong. Somebody good will describe a mistake and what it cost; somebody to avoid will describe a flawless project, because flawless projects do not exist and the answer tells you what you will be told when something breaks.

Ask what they would advise you not to build. A developer whose first instinct is to reduce your scope is protecting your money, and that instinct is the single most reliable signal available to a non-technical buyer.

Then ask for one reference from a client whose project ended, not one still running. The useful question for that reference is whether a different developer could pick the work up, and what the handover looked like.

Three questions, none technical, all answerable on a first call. They sort suppliers better than any portfolio does.

Before either route

Most Backlogs Are A Decision Queue, Not A Capacity Queue

Worth checking, because hiring against the wrong diagnosis is expensive and slow.

Go through the list of outstanding work and mark each item with what is actually blocking it. A surprising share will be waiting on somebody to decide something rather than on anybody to build anything.

Those items do not move when capacity arrives. They move when somebody with authority spends an afternoon ruling on them, and a new developer sitting idle in week three is an expensive way to discover that.

A second group will be items that nobody would miss. Written down a year ago, still on the list, never once raised since — those are not a backlog, they are a record of old enthusiasm.

What is left after both passes is the real queue. It is frequently a third of the original list, and a third of a list is a very different hiring decision from the whole one.

What the shortened list tells a software developer waterloo candidate

It also changes the conversation with whoever you approach. A list of six specific items with a stated order is a brief somebody can quote against; a general statement that there is a lot of work is a brief that attracts the wrong applicants and tells the right ones nothing.

Do the two passes before the first conversation rather than after. The exercise costs an afternoon, it is the same afternoon either way, and doing it first is the difference between a focused search and an expensive one.

The build, which is quoted once, from hosting and third-party services, which are monthly and are somebody else’s bill, from maintenance, which is the one nobody budgets and the one that never stops. Then ask what you end up holding: the code, in a repository you can reach; the accounts, in your name; the data, exportable in full. A build you cannot leave with is a subscription with a large deposit.

How A Software Developer Waterloo Project Actually Runs

Five steps, and the first one is arithmetic rather than software.

01

Step 1 of 5

Hours

Not features. How many hours a week does the current way cost, and who spends them. A project that cannot answer that is a project with no way of knowing whether it worked.

02

Step 2 of 5

Decide

Most problems are solved by configuring something that exists. Building is for the part that is genuinely yours, and that part is almost always smaller than it looks at the start.

03

Step 3 of 5

The joins

Where this has to talk to what you already run. The joins are where the cost and the risk both live, and they are the part a feature list never mentions.

04

Step 4 of 5

Build small

Something in real use within weeks rather than a specification that takes a quarter to agree. Nobody knows what they want until they have used a version of it.

05

Step 5 of 5

Hand over

The code, the accounts, the data, and a written answer to who maintains it. Without those, you have rented a system and the rent is open-ended.

Straight answers

Software Development FAQ

Straight answers before you ever book a call.

Should we hire a co-op student?

Often yes, and specify the handover before they start rather than in their final week. The pattern that fails is four months of genuinely good work followed by a system nobody else can change. A repository you control, a short document per system, and somebody else reading the code are the entire fix and they cost nothing at the beginning.

Why is it hard to hire here when there are so many developers?

Supply is not the constraint; interesting work is. A developer choosing between a product company building something hard and a business that needs an internal tool maintained is making an easy choice. Outside the technology sector you are competing on autonomy, problem interest or schedule rather than on salary alone.

Is a contractor cheaper than an employee?

Per hour, no. Per outcome, frequently, for bounded work, because you buy the weeks you need rather than a year of capacity. The comparison that matters is not the rate but the total: what gets finished, by when, and what it costs to keep running afterwards.

Who owns the code?

Whatever the contract or employment agreement says, so read it before rather than after. You want the source in a repository the company controls, documentation good enough for another developer to continue, and any hosting in the company’s name. These are five-minute decisions at the start and arguments at the end.
