That shapes what gets sold. A social media marketing agency toronto businesses find this way is quoted on output: so many posts a month, so many stories, a content calendar, a monthly report. All of it is production, and production is the half that is easy to count.

What is missing is the only question that determines whether any of it is seen, and it is missing in every Ontario market we look at. Organic reach on a business page has been falling for a decade and is now close enough to nothing that it should be planned for as nothing. A post with no money behind it reaches a fraction of the people who already follow you, and essentially nobody who does not.

The two halves

Production Is Half The Job And All Of The Invoice

A retainer is almost always priced entirely on the half that can be counted, and that is the half that does not reach anybody.

Once distribution is paid for, the production half changes shape too. The thing that moves results is not a better post, it is more distinct attempts: different openings, different formats, different claims, enough of them that the platform has something to choose between. Twelve polished posts a month is fewer attempts than it sounds.

This is the actual argument for social media management toronto businesses should be having with a supplier. Not how good the work looks, but how many genuinely different things are being tried, and what is behind the ones that start working.

Where to spend

What Social Media Marketing Services Toronto Budgets Should Buy

What usually gets sold here

A fixed number of posts, a calendar, community management and a monthly report counting likes and followers. Everything in it is countable and none of it is reach. Social media marketing companies toronto buyers compare on price are usually comparing production volume, which is the part of the job that does not determine the result.

What we do instead

Decide the distribution budget first, even if it is small, because it sets how much production is worth making. Then make more distinct attempts rather than fewer polished ones, measure past the click, and let what works decide the next month rather than a calendar written in advance.

The B2B case

B2B Social Media Toronto Businesses Keep Getting Wrong

The commonest objection is that social does not work for business buyers. It is half right, and the half it gets wrong is expensive.

Measure the right thing

A b2b social media toronto account judged on direct enquiries will be switched off while it is working. The honest measure is whether people arrive at the first meeting already knowing what you do.

One channel, properly

Being thin on four platforms is worse than being real on one. A social media manager toronto firms share across five accounts is producing presence rather than effect.

Content marketing is not social

A content marketing agency toronto buyers hire for articles and a social supplier are solving different problems. They work together well and substituting one for the other is a common and expensive mistake.

Even if it is small

How much is behind the work decides how much work is worth making. A small amount spent deliberately beats a large amount of production nobody sees, and it is the decision that makes every later number interpretable.

Questions

Social In Toronto: Common Questions

Do we need to pay to promote our posts?

Yes, and it should be decided before the content is planned rather than after. Organic reach on a business page has fallen for a decade and is now close enough to nothing that it should be planned for as nothing. A post with no money behind it reaches a fraction of your existing followers and essentially nobody else. The distribution budget determines how much production is worth making, which is why it is the first decision rather than the last.

How many posts a month should we be getting?

It is the wrong question, and it is the one every proposal answers. What moves results is the number of genuinely distinct attempts: different openings, formats and claims, enough that the platform has something to choose between. Twelve polished posts is fewer attempts than it sounds. Ask instead how many different things will be tried, and what happens to the ones that start working.

Does social work for business-to-business here?

Not for generating the enquiry, which is what it usually gets judged on and why it usually gets switched off. It works for being shortlisted: buyers look you up before the first meeting, an empty account is a signal, and it reaches the people who were never in the room but have to agree. Judged on direct enquiries it fails. Judged on whether people arrive already knowing what you do, it is one of the cheaper things you run.

How many platforms should we be on?

One, properly, until that is working. Being thin across four accounts is worse than being real on one, and a shared manager spread across five clients and five platforms produces presence rather than effect. Pick the one where your buyers actually are, which is a question with an answer rather than a matter of taste, and expand only when the first one is genuinely working.

Is this the same as content marketing?

No, and substituting one for the other is a common and expensive mistake. Content marketing produces things people seek out: articles, guides, tools that answer a question somebody typed. Social distributes and builds familiarity with people who were not looking. They work well together and they are different jobs with different measures, and a proposal that merges them usually delivers the cheaper one.
