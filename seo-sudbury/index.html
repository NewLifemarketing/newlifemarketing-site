The Capital Of Northern Ontario Buys Differently

Greater Sudbury is the largest city in Northern Ontario — around 166,000 people, the mining capital of the country, and the retail, healthcare and education hub for the entire northeast. That hub status is the opportunity. Sudbury businesses don't just sell to Sudbury — they pull customers from hours in every direction, from Espanola to Sturgeon Falls, because this is where the big-box stores, the specialists and Health Sciences North are.

A retailer or clinic that owns Sudbury's search results effectively owns a regional catchment several times the city's size. The economy underneath is distinct: the nickel-mining supply chain feeds hundreds of industrial and trade businesses; Laurentian University, Cambrian College and Collège Boréal cycle thousands of students through the city; a strong francophone community makes bilingual marketing a genuine edge; and a lakes-everywhere geography — 330 of them inside the city limits, Ramsey Lake at the centre — spreads the population across communities that behave like separate towns.

Marketing that treats Greater Sudbury as one homogeneous audience wastes most of its budget crossing that map. The problems

Sudbury FAQ

Do you actually work in Sudbury, or just say you do? We work Northern Ontario for real — our crew has produced and launched campaigns across the region, including for Aaron's, whose Northern Ontario locations include Sudbury; the Sudbury campaign creative sits in our portfolio. Northern engagements are built around efficient production trips: one or two on-site days become a month of content, and the ads and CRM run from home base every day.

Greater Sudbury is huge geographically. How does targeting work? Precisely — that's the advantage of paid media here. We build campaigns around the communities that matter to your business: the core, the Valley, Lively, Chelmsford, Val Caron — rather than spraying one radius over 3,200 square kilometres.

For trades, that geographic discipline is the whole budget. Half my customers are francophone. Can you handle that? Greater Sudbury is one of Ontario's most bilingual markets and campaigns can be structured to reflect it — audience splits and creative variants by language, so a bilingual business shows up as one.

We scope this on the strategy call based on how your customers actually buy. What industries do you focus on in Sudbury? The ones that anchor the city: trades and industrial services around the mining supply chain, retail serving the whole northeast catchment, healthcare-adjacent and professional services, and restaurants competing for the Laurentian, Cambrian and Boréal crowd.
