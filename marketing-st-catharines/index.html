Marketing St Catharines

An Older Market

Marketing St Catharines is bought in the oldest market we work in — a median age of 45.9 against 40.7 for the province, with 23.6% of the metropolitan area aged 65 or older — and almost every piece of general marketing advice is written for somebody younger.

What the demographics change

Marketing St Catharines: The Audience Is Five Years Older

Three figures, and together they change the medium more than the message.

people in St Catharines

45.9

Niagara Region Community Dashboard, 2021 Census

23.6%
of the St Catharines–Niagara metropolitan area aged 65 or older

Statistics Canada, 2021 Census of Population

A five-year gap in median age sounds small and is not. It changes which channels are actually used, how long somebody will spend deciding, how much they want to speak to a person, and how much tolerance there is for an interface that assumes familiarity.

The practical effects are unglamorous and mostly about removing friction. A phone number that is tappable and prominent rather than hidden behind a form. Type that is readable without pinching. A form with four fields rather than eleven. Hours that are current, because a wasted trip is remembered.

None of that is a concession. It is simply designing for the market that exists rather than for the one in a template, and it tends to improve results for everybody else too.

The mistake worth avoiding is the opposite assumption: that an older market is not online. It plainly is, and in Niagara it is the larger share of the people with money to spend, which makes designing them out of a site an expensive habit rather than a stylistic one.

What to change first

Four Frictions Worth Removing

All four are cheap, and all four are measured in enquiries rather than impressions.

Make the phone the first option

A prominent, tappable number. In this market a meaningful share of people who will buy would rather speak to somebody, and a form-only contact page quietly turns them away.

Say what happens next

Who calls back, how soon, and what they will ask. Uncertainty is the commonest reason an enquiry does not get made, and one sentence removes it.

Make the text readable

Larger body type, real contrast, and no critical information inside an image. It costs nothing at build time and it is expensive to retrofit.

Keep the hours and the address correct

Everywhere they appear. A wasted trip in a market this size is remembered and repeated, and it is entirely avoidable.

Four changes, none of them a campaign, and together they usually move enquiries more than a quarter of advertising would.

What to buy

Three Things That Compound Here

Each works slowly and keeps working, which suits a market that decides slowly.

A correct Business Profile, with categories, service area, hours and photographs that match what you do now. It decides a large share of local results, it is free, and the phone call it produces is the enquiry this market most readily makes.

Reviews collected as a process rather than an intention. In an older market a recent review from somebody local carries unusual weight, because the decision is being made carefully and with a preference for evidence over novelty.

And one page per thing you sell, named the way customers name it, with the price range and the exclusions written down. A buyer taking their time reads more before deciding, which makes thin pages a real cost here.

All three survive a change of supplier, which is the test worth applying to anything bought in a market this size.

There is a fourth that costs nothing and gets skipped more often than any of them: writing down which page owns which search, so two of your own pages never compete for the same result. Where that happens a search engine picks one of them, usually not the one you would have chosen, and nothing reports the problem because no error has occurred.

What advertising is for

Where A Marketing St Catharines Budget Earns Its Place

Eighty searches a month for a supplier tells you not to build a business on local paid search.

A budget answers questions quickly that would otherwise take two quarters: does this offer land, does this page convert, which words do the people who actually enquire use. Each is settled in a fortnight for a modest sum.

In this market it is also the fastest way to learn how long the decision takes. A longer gap between first click and enquiry is normal here, and an account judged on same-session conversions will understate what it is doing by a wide margin.

Retargeting matters more here than in a faster market for exactly that reason. If the decision takes three weeks, being visible during those three weeks is the campaign, and it costs a fraction of reaching a stranger.

What a budget should not do is substitute permanently for the profile and the pages, which keep earning after the card is declined.

Measuring it

Count Phone Calls As Enquiries

The commonest measurement error in this market is counting only what a form reports.

Calls, tracked and counted

If the phone is the preferred channel and only form submissions are counted, every report will understate the result and the wrong campaigns will get cut.

How they heard of you

Asked of every arriving customer and written down. It is the only line in the report that comes from your business rather than from a platform.

Read both over a rolling quarter, and expect a longer lag between the first visit and the enquiry than general advice assumes. At these volumes one month cannot distinguish a good one from a bad one in any case, and an account judged monthly gets cut and restarted on noise rather than on evidence.

Our scope

A Narrow Offer, Said Plainly

Four things, and a list of what we do not do.

Paid media on Google and Meta managed against cost per enquiry. Search work built one cluster at a time so two of your own pages never compete. Video and photography of real work. And the systems underneath, so an enquiry can be traced to what produced it. We manage over $70,000 a month in advertising spend for Ontario businesses and the owner runs the paid media personally.

What a marketing st catharines engagement should hand back

A marketing st catharines engagement should leave you holding the registry of which page owns which phrase, the profile audit with its corrections applied, and whatever was filmed. The thing that survives a change of agency is the only thing worth paying for twice.

Not public relations, not events, not brand identity from scratch. We are in Minesing, a couple of hours away, which matters for filming, where travel appears as its own visible line rather than folded into a fee.

Does an older market mean fewer people online?

No, and assuming otherwise is the expensive mistake. The market is plainly online; what differs is how it behaves — a stronger preference for speaking to somebody, a longer decision, and less tolerance for an interface that assumes familiarity. The fixes are friction removal rather than a different channel.

What should we change first?

Make the phone prominent and tappable, say what happens after an enquiry, make the text readable without pinching, and keep hours and address correct everywhere. Four changes, none of them a campaign, and together they usually move enquiries more than a quarter of advertising.

Why do our reports look worse than our phone does?

Because the phone is probably the preferred channel and only form submissions are being counted. If calls are not tracked, every report understates the result and the wrong campaigns get cut. Count calls as enquiries and the picture usually changes substantially.

How long should we expect decisions to take?

Longer than general advice assumes, which matters for how you judge a campaign. An account measured on same-session conversions will understate what it is doing, and retargeting is worth more here than in a faster market because being visible during a three-week decision is the campaign.
