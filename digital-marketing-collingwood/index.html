Digital Marketing Collingwood

Faster Than The Plan

Digital Marketing Collingwood has an unusual problem for a town of 24,811 people: this was the fifth fastest-growing census agglomeration in Canada, and a market changing at that rate makes an annual marketing plan a document about a town that no longer exists by the time it is approved.

How fast it is moving

Growth of that order does not just add customers. It changes who the customers are, what they expect, what they will pay and who else is competing for them, and it does all of that continuously rather than in a step.

The practical consequence is that assumptions age faster here than they do anywhere else in this region. A price positioning decided three years ago, a service area drawn five years ago, a competitor list from before the last wave of arrivals — all three are likely to be wrong in ways nobody has checked.

Most of that is invisible from inside the business, because each individual month looks much like the last. It only shows up when somebody goes back and compares what they believed with what is now true.

Which makes a short annual re-survey more valuable here than any individual campaign, and it is the thing almost nobody does.

The thing to do yearly

Four Assumptions Worth Re-Checking Every Year

Each takes under an hour and each one quietly goes stale in a market moving this fast.

Who is actually buying

The mix of residents, visitors and people who own property here but live elsewhere shifts year to year. A plan written for one of the three will slowly stop working as the balance moves.

Who you are competing with

In a fast-growing town new suppliers arrive constantly and some of them are better funded than anything that was here before. A competitor list older than a year is a guess.

What you charge against what the market now pays

Prices set before a growth wave are frequently below what newer arrivals expect to pay, and the business reads that as being busy rather than as leaving money behind.

How far you travel

The built-up area is physically larger than it was. A service area drawn years ago either excludes customers you would serve or includes drives you no longer want.

Those four are not a campaign and they do not need a supplier. They are the input every campaign afterwards is built on, and in a market growing at this rate they are the only part that genuinely expires.

What to buy

Three Things That Keep Working While The Town Changes

Each compounds, and none of them has to be rewritten when the market moves.

A correct Business Profile, with categories, service area, hours and photographs that match what you actually do now. It decides a large share of local results and it is free, and in a growing town it goes out of date faster than anybody expects.

One page per thing you sell, named the way customers name it. Five services on one page ranks for none of them; five pages cost the same to write and produce five results, and they keep earning while the market changes around them.

And reviews collected as a process rather than an intention: a specific moment in the job when somebody asks, and a short link that makes it a thirty-second task. In a town where a rising share of buyers are new and have nobody to ask, recent reviews do the work a recommendation used to.

All three survive a change of supplier, which is the test worth applying to anything bought in a market that moves this quickly.

Who the buyer became

What Digital Marketing Collingwood Should Re-Examine Every Year

In a market changing this fast, last year’s customer description is a liability.

Who is buying, what they are worth, what they ask about first, and what they compared you with. Four answers that were true two years ago and may not be now.

The symptom of getting this wrong is quiet: pages written for a customer who has been replaced, advertising aimed at a concern nobody has any more, and a slow decline nobody can attribute.

Checking it costs an afternoon with the last fifty invoices and twenty minutes of asking whoever answers the telephone what people are asking now.

Do it in the same month each year, with the previous year’s answers beside the new ones. The comparison is the valuable part, and it only exists if the first version was written down.

Who else they considered

Ask What They Nearly Did Instead

One question that tells you more than any competitor research.

Asked of a new customer, it produces the actual comparison set — which is frequently not the businesses you think of as competitors, and sometimes is not a business at all but doing nothing.

Where the honest answer is doing nothing, the whole argument changes: the page has to establish that the problem is worth solving before it argues about who should solve it.

Where a specific competitor keeps appearing, that is the one to read carefully — their pages, their prices, what they say that you do not.

Ten answers is enough to see the pattern, and collecting them costs nothing because somebody is already speaking to these people.

Ask it of the ones who did not buy as well, where you can. The reason somebody chose elsewhere is the single most useful sentence available to a business, and it is almost never requested because nobody enjoys the conversation.

Our scope

A Narrow Offer, Said Plainly

Four things, and a list of what we do not do.

Paid media on Google and Meta managed against cost per enquiry. Search work built one cluster at a time so two of your own pages never compete. Video and photography of real work. And the systems underneath, so an enquiry can be traced to what produced it. We manage over $70,000 a month in advertising spend for Ontario businesses and the owner runs the paid media personally.

What a digital marketing collingwood engagement should hand back

A digital marketing collingwood engagement should leave you holding the registry of which page owns which phrase, the profile audit with its corrections applied, and whatever was filmed. In a market that changes this quickly, the written record is what stops the next supplier starting again from nothing.

Not public relations, not events, not brand identity from scratch. We are in Minesing, about an hour away, which keeps filming travel modest and we price it as its own visible line regardless.

Why does growth change how we plan?

Because the assumptions underneath a plan expire faster than the plan does. This was the fifth fastest-growing census agglomeration in Canada, which means who is buying, who is competing, what the market pays and how far the built-up area reaches all move continuously. A short annual re-survey is worth more here than any individual campaign.

What should we re-check every year?

Who is actually buying across residents, visitors and part-time owners; who you are now competing with; what you charge against what the market now pays; and how far you genuinely travel. Each takes under an hour, none needs a supplier, and all four are the input everything else is built on.

Is 90 searches a month worth anything?

It depends entirely on what a customer is worth. A handful of enquiries a month is transformative for a business selling large jobs and marginal for one selling small ones. Work out the value of a customer first; in a town this size it decides whether any of this is worth buying.

Our campaign faded after a few weeks. Why?

Usually because the audience has seen it. Twenty-four thousand residents is a finite pool, and a flat permanent spend through it produces a slow decline that looks like the market cooling. Bursts with a reason behind them, and new material planned in advance, work better than a constant drip.
