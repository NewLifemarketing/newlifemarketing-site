Peterborough CRM Setup: Location And Season Are The Whole Question

Both are obvious once stated and both are routinely left out of the form.

A third of the people in this area live outside the city, down roads that take twenty-five minutes. Two jobs at the same price are not the same job when one of them costs an hour of driving, and nothing in a default CRM configuration will ever tell you which was which.

Season is the second. Parks Canada opens the waterway in mid-May and closes it in mid-October, and for a large share of businesses here the year has a shape dictated by that. A record that does not say when somebody bought cannot tell you whether your quiet February is normal or a warning.

Both are one extra field on a form and one extra column in a report. The reason they are usually missing is not difficulty; it is that nobody was asked what the system would be used to decide.

That question is the actual configuration work, and it takes an hour.

What to capture

Four Fields Worth Arguing About

Everything else can be added later. These four are painful to backfill.

Where they are

Not a mailing address — the place the work happens, and ideally the drive time. It is what turns a revenue report into a profit report.

How they heard of you

Asked out loud, written down, every time. In a dispersed catchment most business arrives by routes no tracking parameter survives, and this is the only record of them.

When they bought

With the season, not just the date. Against a published calendar it becomes the most useful column you have for planning next year.

What they did not buy

The quote that went quiet, and why. It is the cheapest list of future customers any business owns and almost nobody keeps it.

Four fields, each a few seconds at the point of entry, and together they answer most of the questions a business this size actually has.

What it should do on its own

Automation Earns Its Place In The Quiet Months

The point is not fewer staff. It is that the things everybody means to do actually happen.

An enquiry acknowledged within minutes rather than at the end of the day. A quote that follows itself up twice without anybody remembering. A review request that goes out at the moment the job finishes, which is the only moment it works.

Each of those is a decision somebody intends to make and forgets, and each is worth more than any report the system produces. They are also the parts that survive a busy season, which is exactly when they matter.

The seasonal version is the one most businesses here never build: a message to last year’s customers before the season opens. The list already exists, the cost is nothing, and it reliably outperforms advertising aimed at strangers because these people have already bought once.

That single sequence is frequently the entire return on setting the system up properly.

Where it goes wrong

Where A Peterborough CRM Setup Usually Goes Wrong

Four failures, and not one of them is a software problem.

Too many stages. Fourteen columns on a board is a board nobody updates, and a board nobody updates is a spreadsheet with a monthly bill attached. Five stages that each mean something somebody can act on beats fourteen that describe a process nobody follows.Too many required fields. Every field somebody must fill in before they can save a record is a reason not to save the record. Four fields, and the rest optional, is how a system survives a busy week.Built by somebody who will never use it. The person who configures it and the person typing into it on a Friday afternoon are rarely the same person, and only one of them knows which steps are impossible on a phone, in a van, between jobs.No decision about what happens to the old data. Three years of enquiries in an inbox, a spreadsheet and somebody\u2019s notebook. Decide before the switch what moves, what gets archived and what is genuinely abandoned, because afterwards nobody will.

Those four account for almost every abandoned system we are asked to look at, and all four are decided before any software is chosen.

Doing it in the right month

Set It Up Before The Season, Not During

The install happens while somebody has an hour, which here is a narrow window.

A system goes in properly when the people who will use it can be asked what they need from it. For a business serving this catchment, that is not July, and anyone who has tried to roll one out mid-season knows exactly how it ends.

The right sequence is to decide the fields in winter, run them for a few weeks on paper if necessary, and have the habit established before the first busy week arrives. A habit formed in a quiet month survives a loud one; the reverse never happens.

It also means the first full season produces a complete year of data rather than a partial one, and a partial year cannot answer the question everybody wants answered, which is whether this year was better than last.

There is a second reason the quiet months are the right ones. Whatever you decide to capture has to be entered by whoever is standing in front of a customer, and that person has an opinion about which of the four fields is worth the seconds it costs. Asking them in February produces a form they will actually complete; imposing one in July produces a field that is left blank and a report that quietly lies for a year.

That is a small timing decision with an outsized effect, and it is free.

Our scope

What We Set Up And What We Leave Alone

Narrow, and said before a meeting rather than during one.

We build the capture-to-record path: forms that reach the system, the fields that make reporting possible, the automatic acknowledgements and follow-ups, and the tracking that connects an enquiry back to what produced it.

What a peterborough crm setup project should hand back

A peterborough crm setup project with us should leave you holding the field definitions, the automations in plain language, and an export you can take anywhere. A system you cannot leave is not a system you own, and in a market with this many suppliers that matters more than the feature list.

We do not take on bookkeeping, payroll or inventory systems, and we would not replace an industry package that already works. Naming that now is cheaper for both sides than discovering it in month three.

What A Peterborough CRM Setup Quote Should Separate

The build, which happens once, from the licence, which is monthly and goes to whoever makes the software, from support, which is somebody answering when it breaks. Then ask the two questions that decide whether you own this: whose name are the accounts in, and can the data be exported in full without asking anybody. A system you cannot leave with is one you are renting.

Straight answers

Peterborough CRM Setup FAQ

Straight answers before you ever book a call.

Which CRM should we use?

Almost always the one already included in something you pay for, until you can name a decision it cannot support. The choice of system matters far less than whether four fields are captured consistently and whether one person owns it. Most failures here are configuration and habit, not software.

What should we record that we probably are not?

Where the customer actually was, how they heard of you, which part of the season they bought in, and what they did not buy. The first turns a revenue report into a profit report in a catchment where a third of the market is outside the city. The last is the cheapest list of future customers you own.

When should we set it up?

In the quiet half of the year. Parks Canada ran the 2026 navigation season from 15 May to 12 October, and for much of this catchment that is the commercial year. A habit formed in a quiet month survives a busy one; a system rolled out in July does not survive at all.

What is the single highest-return automation?

A message to last year’s customers before the season opens. The list already exists, the cost is nothing, and people who have bought once reliably outperform strangers. Most businesses here have never built it, and for many it pays for the whole setup in one season.
