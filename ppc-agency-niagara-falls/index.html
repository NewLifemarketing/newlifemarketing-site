Somebody typing your name has already chosen you. If a platform listing appears above your own site, the booking that follows carries a commission on a customer you had already won, which is the most expensive click in the entire market.

Defending it is cheap, because relevance is on your side: your own name points at your own site more obviously than at anybody else’s, and the cost per click reflects that.

It is also measurable in a way most advertising is not. The direct share of bookings is a number you already have, every point it moves is worth the commission on that share, and unlike most marketing figures it does not need a platform’s cooperation to produce.

That is the first job. Everything else a budget can do here is smaller, and most proposals start with the smaller things because they are easier to describe as growth. Defending what you already have does not sound like a campaign, which is exactly why it keeps being left undone.

What else a budget does

Three Jobs After The Name

Each is testable, and none of them is permanent.

Reaching visitors before they arrive

They are deciding weeks ahead from somewhere else and are not searching yet, so search cannot reach them. A seasonal, geographically targeted campaign can, and almost nobody runs one properly.

Answering a question

Does this offer land, does this page convert, which words do the people who actually book use. A fortnight settles each, and the answers direct unpaid work that would have been guesswork.

Retargeting

A booking decision takes days and the audience has already visited your site. Reaching them again costs a fraction of reaching a stranger, and in a considered purchase it is where a real share of conversions happens.

Each needs a stated end or a fixed job, because without one a seasonal campaign quietly becomes a permanent line item and nobody notices until the following winter, by which point several months of spending have gone to an audience that was no longer there.

What the page has to do

What A PPC Agency Niagara Falls Needs From The Landing Page

Advertising magnifies whatever the page already does, and here the page has a specific competitor.

Somebody arriving from an advertisement has just seen the same business on a platform with photographs, prices, availability, reviews and a two-tap booking. Your page is being compared with that, whether or not anybody intended it to be.

So it needs the same things, visible without scrolling: what it costs, whether it is available, what is included, and how to book in as few steps as possible.

And it needs a visible reason to book direct. Not a large one — a rate, an inclusion, flexibility — but unmistakable, because the reader is one tap from an alternative they already trust.

Test the booking yourself on a phone, start to finish. A direct path that takes six screens loses to a platform that takes two, however good the offer is, and no bidding change recovers that.

One practical note before any of this is designed. Open your own listing on a platform and your own site side by side on a phone, and write down every question the listing answers that your page does not. That list is the brief, it takes ten minutes, and it is almost always shorter and cheaper to act on than whatever a redesign would have proposed.

Before any budget

Three Numbers, One Afternoon

In a commission market the arithmetic is unusually clear once it exists.

What a booking is worth

Net of commission where it applies. The gap between a direct booking and a platform one is the real value of moving the channel mix, and it is frequently larger than people expect.

What share convert

From click to enquiry to booking. The number most likely to be guessed, and a guess moves the defensible budget by a factor of two.

The maximum defensible cost per booking

Which follows arithmetically, and which can then be given to the account as the thing to optimise toward rather than left to a default.

A supplier who quotes a monthly figure before asking for those three is quoting a fee rather than proposing a plan.

Judging it

Direct Share, On A Rolling Quarter

The reporting period matters as much as the number in a seasonal market.

Cost per click is an input and conversion rate is a property of the page. The figure that means something here is the share of bookings arriving direct, because it converts straight into commission saved.

Read it across three months rather than one, and compare against the same months last year rather than the ones before. In a seasonal economy a month-on-month chart measures the season rather than the work.

Keep asking guests how they heard of you. A visitor market generates a great deal of business through routes no tracking parameter survives, including the recommendation that happened in a car park and the sign somebody noticed on the way past.

And set an end condition for anything seasonal before it starts: a date, a number, or an answer. Without one, a summer campaign runs into February and nobody notices until the invoice.
