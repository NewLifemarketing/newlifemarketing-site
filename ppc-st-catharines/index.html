The Call Is The Conversion

PPC St Catharines accounts routinely look worse than they are, for one correctable reason: in a market with a median age of 45.9 a large share of the people who respond pick up the phone, and an account counting only form submissions is grading itself on the smaller half.

The measurement problem

Call tracking is cheap, takes an afternoon to set up, and is missing from most small accounts. Without it, every campaign that drives phone calls looks like a campaign producing nothing, and the usual response is to switch it off.

That failure compounds because automated bidding learns from whatever it is told counts. An account optimising toward form submissions in a market that prefers to call will systematically push spend toward the audiences least likely to be the ones you want.

So the first piece of work in any account here is not a keyword decision. It is making sure the thing being optimised toward is the thing you actually want, and in this market that is a phone call.

Done properly it frequently reverses the conclusion of the last twelve months of reporting, which is an uncomfortable conversation and a cheap one compared with another year pointed in the wrong direction.

What else is different

A Longer Decision Changes The Account

Three consequences, all of which make a default setup understate performance.

Same-session conversion understates

If the decision takes two weeks, measuring the click that happened to be last will credit the wrong campaign and starve the one that started the process.

Retargeting is the campaign

Being visible during a longer decision is where a modest budget does most of its work, and it costs a fraction of reaching somebody new.

Audience windows need setting

The default retargeting window is rarely the right one. It should match how long your buying cycle actually takes, which is a question only your own records answer.

None of those requires more budget. All three require somebody to look at how long your customers actually take and configure the account to match, which is an afternoon of work that almost never gets done, and which no platform will prompt anybody to do.

What the landing page must do

Make Phoning Obvious

Advertising magnifies whatever the page already does, and in this market the page has one job above the rest.

A prominent, tappable number near the top, repeated at the bottom. Not an image, not hidden behind a contact tab, not below a form that asks for eleven fields.

Then say what happens next: who answers, how soon, and what they will ask. Uncertainty is the commonest reason somebody does not ring, and one sentence removes it.

Make the text readable without pinching, and put the critical information — hours, address, service area, price range — in text rather than inside a graphic. All of it costs nothing at build time.

Test it the way a customer does: open the page on a phone away from the office, try to call without scrolling or zooming, and see how long it takes before anything is readable. Ten minutes, and it catches most of what a campaign would otherwise pay to discover slowly over a quarter. Do the same with the form, and check that whatever it sends actually reaches somebody who reads it rather than a mailbox nobody has opened since the person who set it up left.

Before any budget

Three Numbers, One Afternoon

They make the budget conversation short and the reporting meaningful.

What a customer is worth

Over the whole relationship rather than the first job. Without it, cost per enquiry is a figure with nothing to compare it against.

What share of enquiries convert

Including calls. In a market that phones, leaving them out of this calculation produces a conversion rate that is wrong in a flattering direction and a budget built on it.

The maximum defensible cost per enquiry

Which follows arithmetically, and which can then be given to the account as the thing to optimise toward.

A supplier who quotes a monthly figure before asking for those three is quoting a fee rather than proposing a plan. The three take an afternoon with your own invoices and they are the only honest basis for deciding whether this channel is worth entering at all in a thirty-search market.

What goes wrong

How A PPC St Catharines Account Drifts

Three failures, and the first two are invisible in a monthly report.

Calls uncounted, so the campaigns that work look like the ones to cut. That is the big one and it is entirely fixable in an afternoon.

Automated campaign types left to choose their own placements and geography inside whatever budget they are given. They will find the cheapest impressions available, and the cheapest impressions are cheap for a reason.

And a boundary that drifts. A radius drawn generously from here reaches across the region into markets with their own closer suppliers, and nothing in the account announces that crossing. Review it quarterly against where paying customers actually were.

The habit that catches all three is the same: read the search terms report and the geographic report side by side every month and ask one question of each line, which is whether you would have chosen to pay for it.

Anything that fails that test becomes a negative keyword or an excluded location that week, and the account gets tighter every month instead of looser. It takes about twenty minutes and it is the single most reliable predictor of whether an account is being managed or simply left running.
