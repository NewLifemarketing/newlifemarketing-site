Website Design Aurora

Write For Somebody New

A replacement buyer knows what the service costs, what it involves and what went wrong last time. They want a price and an availability date, and a long explanatory page irritates them.

A first-time buyer knows none of that. They want to understand what they are buying before they make contact, and they will read a page that explains it properly. In a town adding people at this rate, that second reader is a large share of the traffic.

Which means the usual advice — shorter, punchier, more calls to action — is the wrong advice here. The page that wins explains the thing, shows the work, gives a price range honestly, and asks for contact at the end rather than in the first screen.

It also means the site has more jobs than a brochure. It is the introduction a word-of-mouth recommendation would have been in a settled town.

The regional problem

Say Which Town You Serve

The Regional Municipality of York contains nine municipalities, and a buyer scrolls past most of them without reading.

The reader’s first question is whether you come out this way. A site that does not answer it in the first screen is a site somebody leaves, and in a region of nine municipalities that question is constant.

Answering it is free: name the towns you actually serve in a sentence a person would write, set the Business Profile service area to match, and make sure the directory listings agree with both.

The temptation is to list all nine to widen the net. That produces enquiries you have to refuse and reads as vague to the people you actually want, and it weakens the pages for the towns that matter.

The same applies to page structure. One page naming three towns competes properly for none; one page about one town, written from that town’s own facts, wins one.

Navigation

Five Things In The Menu, Not Fifteen

Every item added makes the others harder to see, and the list grows by consensus.

A menu is a set of priorities, and a business that cannot choose five puts up twelve. The visitor then reads none of them properly and uses the one word they recognise, which is usually not the one you most wanted.

The test is simple: cover the menu and ask somebody what this business does. If the answer depends on reading the list, the pages underneath are doing too little and the menu is compensating.

Things that belong in a footer rather than a menu: policies, careers, accreditations, the newsletter. People look for those at the bottom and nowhere else, and moving them there frees the places that matter.

On a phone it matters more again, because the menu is hidden behind a button and every extra item is a line of scrolling before somebody reaches the thing they came for.

The page nobody plans

The About Page Is Where Website Design Aurora Work Is Won

It is the second most visited page on most small business sites and the last one written.

Somebody reads it when they are close to deciding and want to know who they would be dealing with. That is a sales moment, and it is usually answered with a paragraph about being passionate and customer-focused.

What actually works is specific: who runs this, how long, what they did before, where they are based, what a customer will experience. A photograph of actual people rather than a stock image of a meeting.

Length is not the issue; evasiveness is. A page that could describe any company in the country tells a reader that the honest version was avoided, and they draw their own conclusion.

For a business selling to people who will have somebody in their home or on their property, this page frequently decides it. It deserves the attention that goes to the front page and receives a fraction of it.

Forms

Every Field You Add Costs You Enquiries

The arithmetic is unforgiving and it runs the opposite way to most people's instincts.

Each additional question reduces the number of people who finish. A form asking for a name, a way to reach them and a sentence about the job gets completed; the same form with eight fields gets abandoned by people who were ready to buy.

Everything else can be asked in the reply. You will be speaking to them anyway, and a question asked by a person is answered more readily than the same question asked by a box.

Required fields deserve particular suspicion. A required postcode on an enquiry form stops somebody who does not know it yet, and the cost of that is invisible: they leave, and nothing records that they were there.

Where a long form is genuinely needed — a detailed quote request, a booking — make it the second step, after a short one. The people who start something are far likelier to finish it.

And test the thing on a phone, with a thumb, outdoors. A form that works on a desk and fights a thumb on a bright screen is a form that collects a fraction of what it should, and nobody discovers that from the office.

Our scope

What We Build And What We Would Hand On

Narrow on purpose, so a wasted meeting is avoided.

We build and rebuild presentation sites with the structure decided against the search clusters each page is meant to own, the redirects written before launch, and the content planned as part of the project rather than chased afterwards.

When website design aurora work should go elsewhere

Website Design Aurora work that is really an application — a portal, a calculator, something that has to compute — needs a specialist and a different commercial model, and we will say so rather than take it. We also do not take on open-ended builds with a forming requirement.

We are based in Minesing rather than Aurora, which matters for photographing premises and for nothing else on this list. Where a job genuinely needs somebody who can drop in next week, a local supplier is the better buy.

How much should a website cost here?

The spread is wide and it is set by how much is bespoke, what it connects to and who writes the content. A quick way to narrow it: decide whether anything on the site has to compute something specific to your business. If not, you are in the lower part of the range regardless of how the site looks.

Should the site be short and punchy?

Not in a town growing at this rate. A large share of the audience is buying this kind of service for the first time and is trying to understand it before making contact, and a short page assumes knowledge they do not have. Explain the thing, show the work, then ask for contact.

Will a new site bring more enquiries?

Only if the current one is losing them, which is checkable before you spend anything. Look at whether the number is tappable on a phone, whether the form works, whether pages load, and whether there is a page for each thing you sell. If all four are fine, the problem is upstream and a rebuild will not fix it.

Can you work with our existing developer?

Yes, and where there is one the usual split is that they own anything that computes and we own the presentation layer and the search structure. The thing to agree up front is who owns the URL map, because that is the seam where rebuilds go wrong and it needs one owner rather than two.
