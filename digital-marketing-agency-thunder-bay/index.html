The Thunder Bay market

The Hub Of The Northwest, Half A Province From Anywhere

Thunder Bay is the regional capital of northwestern Ontario — about 110,000 people on Lake Superior's shore under the Sleeping Giant, and the commercial hub for a catchment that stretches hundreds of kilometres in every direction.

Like Sudbury in the northeast, hub status is the prize: the port and grain elevators, the regional hospital, Lakehead University and Confederation College make this the place the northwest drives to for everything serious. A Thunder Bay business that owns its category's search results owns customers from Nipigon to Fort Frances.

We know this market from the ground, not from a map. Our crew spent the road-trip days here filming excavation sites, store walkthroughs and finished builds — and what stood out is how personal commerce still is: owners on first-name terms with customers, referrals carrying real weight, and a visible gap between the businesses investing in how they show up online and everyone else. That gap is the opportunity.

The problems

What Thunder Bay Businesses Told Us

Referrals don't reach the catchment

The customers driving in from the region have never heard your name — they search, compare and decide from a phone before they leave home.

Trades demand outpaces follow-up

Construction and renovation inquiries stack up while crews are on site. We watched it firsthand — the fastest quote wins the job.

Considered purchases need conversations

Furniture, appliances, contracts — northwest buyers want to talk first. Ads that don't open a conversation just decorate the feed.

Southern agencies won't show up

Remote-run campaigns with stock creative read as fake in a city this genuine. Real footage of real local work is the trust shortcut.

Seasonal building windows

The construction season is compressed — marketing has to fill the pipeline before the ground thaws, not after.

Recruiting is marketing too

Every growing TBay business we met was also hiring. The employers with a live, professional presence win the applications.

Across the city

Two Downtowns, One Market

Thunder Bay is famously a tale of two cities — Port Arthur's north core and Fort William's south core, stitched together by the Intercity retail corridor between them — and customers still shop along those lines.

The north core carries the waterfront district, restaurants and event traffic; the south core anchors its own loyal neighbourhoods; and Intercity is where the region comes to spend — the big-box gravity well every retailer competes against. Around it all sits the catchment that makes TBay matter: Nipigon, Marathon, Geraldton, and west along the highway toward Dryden and Fort Frances — customers who research everything online because the drive is measured in hours. Our shoot days here covered both cores and the job sites between them; the targeting reflects how the city actually moves.

What the first 30 days look like: strategy call with Cruz or Keegan and a written plan in week one; tracking, CRM and campaign builds start immediately — and because our crew already cycles through Thunder Bay for existing clients, your first shoot day joins the next production trip rather than waiting for one.
