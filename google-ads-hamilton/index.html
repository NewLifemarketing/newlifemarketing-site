Google Ads Hamilton

Renting A Position

Google Ads Hamilton is bought for one honest reason in a contested city: it rents a position on the first screen that would take two years of unpaid work to earn, and whether that rent is worth paying is a single piece of arithmetic almost no account has done.

The three numbers are what a customer is worth over the whole relationship, what proportion of enquiries become customers, and therefore the maximum you can pay for an enquiry and still be ahead.

They take an afternoon with your own invoices and they are the only honest basis for any budget. Without them, cost per enquiry is a figure with nothing to compare it against, and every report becomes a matter of opinion.

They also answer the question that actually matters in a contested market: whether you can afford to be here at all. In some trades the arithmetic says yes comfortably; in others it says the clicks cost more than the customers are worth, and that is a useful answer rather than a disappointing one.

A supplier who quotes a monthly figure before asking what a customer is worth is quoting a fee rather than proposing a plan.

Where the money goes

What You Are Paying For In A Management Fee

Three structures, and they create different incentives for the person running the account.

A percentage of spend

The commonest and the most conflicted. The supplier earns more when you spend more, which is exactly backwards in a market where the right advice is frequently to spend less and narrow the targeting.

A flat monthly fee

Honest about what it is and vulnerable to neglect. An account needing an hour a week and an account needing an hour a quarter cost the same, so the quiet one stops getting the hour.

A fee tied to enquiries

Aligned, and harder to administer. It also requires both sides to agree what counts as an enquiry before anything starts, which is a conversation worth having regardless of the model you choose.

None of the three is wrong. What is wrong is not knowing which you are on, and a surprising number of businesses do not, because the line on the invoice says management.

What the account needs

Four Things That Decide The Cost Per Enquiry

None of them is the bid, and all four are usually neglected in a small account.

The page the click lands on

Advertising magnifies whatever the page already does. A slow page or a buried phone number raises the cost per enquiry more than any bidding change will lower it.

Negative keywords

The list of what you are not. In a small account a handful of irrelevant phrases can absorb a meaningful share of a month before anybody notices, and nobody notices because nothing reports it.

The search terms report

What people actually typed, as opposed to what you asked for. It is the most useful document in the account and the one least often opened.

Retargeting

The audience has already visited and demonstrated interest. Reaching them again costs a fraction of reaching a stranger, and in the small accounts we are shown it is the single most common thing missing entirely.

Those four are worth more attention than the budget figure. Doubling the spend on a broken page doubles the waste; fixing the page changes every click that follows.

The local layer

Where Google Ads Hamilton Targeting Should Stop

Dundas, Ancaster and Stoney Creek are searched as separate markets, and the amalgamated boundary is not a service area.

A campaign set to the municipal boundary treats a business in one of those communities as equally reachable from all of them, which is not how customers behave and not how the travel works.

The better configuration names the places you genuinely serve and prices the travel beyond them as its own line. It produces a smaller, cheaper campaign that converts better, and it stops the quiet failure where a quarter of the budget buys clicks from people you would rather decline.

It also matters for the ad text. A business that names the specific community in the ad earns a click from somebody who was about to check whether you come out there, and loses the click from somebody you could not serve, which is a saving rather than a loss.

Review the boundary every quarter against the one record that settles it: where the customers who actually bought were.

How the account is arranged

Fewer Campaigns Than You Think

Small accounts are routinely split into so many parts that none of them gathers enough data.

Every campaign has its own budget and its own learning. Split a modest budget across six and each one is running on a handful of clicks a day, which is too little to optimise and too little to read.

One or two campaigns, each with a small number of tightly grouped phrases, outperforms an elaborate structure at this scale almost every time.

Separate things only when they genuinely need different budgets or different locations — an urgent service and a planned one, two towns with different values. Not for tidiness.

The tell is a structure that looks like an organisation chart. Accounts are frequently arranged to match how a business thinks about itself rather than how its customers search, and the second is the only one the auction cares about.
