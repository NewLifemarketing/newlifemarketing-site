Search help is looked for nine times as often as paid help in this city, for a channel that is not nine times smaller. The explanation is how each is bought: businesses decide to do search work and go looking for somebody, while advertising is usually either run in-house or attached to something else.

Which makes this an unusual page to arrive at. You are either comparison-shopping deliberately, which is rare and sensible, or something has gone wrong with an account that already exists.

Difficulty of 12 says the same thing from the other direction: almost nobody is competing to be found for this, which makes the position cheap to take and the market itself quiet.

So the useful question is narrow. What would you pay to find out quickly, that would otherwise take months to learn?

What the money is for

Speed, Not Reach

Where the unpaid position is winnable, advertising has a narrower and more defensible job.

A page here can rank with competent work, which means advertising is no longer the only way to appear. It is the fastest way, and the two are different products.

So the honest use of a budget is to answer questions quickly that search work would answer slowly. Does this offer land. Does this page convert. Which words do people who actually enquire use. Is this service worth a page of its own.

Each is settled in a fortnight for a modest sum, and each answer directs months of unpaid work that would otherwise have been guesswork. In a city where the search cluster runs to 56 phrases, knowing which four to write first is worth real money.

Used permanently as a substitute for the pages, an account is an expensive rental in a market you could have bought.

Where accounts leak

Four Costs That Do Not Show In The Interface

None of these is an error. All of them are money.

Search terms nobody reads

The keyword list is what you bid on; the search-terms report is what people typed. The gap between them is the largest recoverable cost in most accounts and it takes under an hour a month to read.

Brand spend blended into the total

Bidding on your own name converts beautifully and mostly buys clicks you would have had anyway. Inside one blended cost per acquisition it flatters the whole account.

One page for several campaigns

A specific search sent to a general page converts badly at any price, and it is the cheapest thing on this list to fix.

A geography set once

A radius drawn around a downtown in a city of 656,480 covers a great deal of ground and excludes some of the industrial and commercial areas where your customers actually are.

What self-run accounts get right and wrong

What A PPC Agency Brampton Would Change In An Existing Account

In a city where most advertising is run in-house, the accounts we are shown have a consistent shape.

They are usually structurally reasonable. Somebody sensible set them up, the campaigns map roughly to the services, and the conversion tracking exists. The problems are rarely construction problems.

What is missing is attention. The search-terms report has not been read in months, the schedule has not been revisited since setup, and no landing page has been written for any campaign. Those are hours rather than expertise, and hours are what an owner running a business does not have.

So the first deliverable should be a list of findings with a number against each, produced from read access in an hour, before anybody writes a proposal. In a market of 410 searches that is also a differentiator, because almost nobody does it.

And where the honest finding is that the account is fine and the problem is the offer or the page, that is what we would say. It costs the fee and it is the thing that earns the relationship.

Measurement

Two Figures, And Only One Of Them Is In The Account

The platform can tell you what an enquiry cost. It cannot tell you what one was worth.

Cost per enquiry is produced automatically and means very little on its own. Judged against nothing, a figure of eighty dollars sounds expensive and three hundred sounds alarming, and both judgements are made on instinct.

The figure that gives it meaning sits in the business rather than the account: of the enquiries that arrived last quarter, what share became work, and what was that work worth. Two numbers, available from records most firms already keep.

Put together they produce a ceiling — the most an enquiry can cost before the advertising stops paying for itself. Every later argument about budget, targeting and fee is settled by comparing against that one number.

Work it out before committing to anything. An account run without it is being judged on how its figures feel, which over a year is how a profitable campaign gets cut and an unprofitable one gets extended.

It also changes who the conversation is with. A supplier arguing for more budget against a known ceiling is making a checkable claim; the same supplier arguing for more budget against no ceiling at all is making a sales pitch, and neither of you has any way to settle it.

We run our own account. What would an agency add?

Attention rather than access. Most self-run accounts we are shown are structurally reasonable and have an unread search-terms report, a schedule nobody has revisited and no campaign-specific landing page. Those are hours rather than expertise, and hours are what an owner running a business does not have.

What is a sensible budget here?

Enough to produce a few hundred clicks within a month so the account has data to optimise against. The firmer constraint is your maximum defensible cost per enquiry, which comes from what a customer is worth rather than from a benchmark. Below the data threshold, managing the account is guesswork.

Should we advertise or do search work?

Both, in that order, for different reasons. Advertising settles within a fortnight whether the offer and the page work and which words convert. Search work then makes the same traffic stop costing per click. In a 56-phrase search cluster, the paid test is also how you decide which pages to write first.

Can you take over an existing account?

Yes, and read access with an assessment beats rebuilding, because an account with history carries learning that is expensive to recreate. The usual finding is not poor construction but a geography, a schedule or a match type set once and never revisited, plus brand spend blended into the headline number.
