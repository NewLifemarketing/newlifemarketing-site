A calendar, not a cadence

Kingston Runs On An Academic Year

Queen’s University, St Lawrence College and the Royal Military College all refill the city at the same time. For the businesses that serve them, that is the whole marketing year.

The standard Ontario social media plan is a cadence. Three posts a week, every week, forever, measured monthly. It assumes the audience is a constant and the only variable is how often you speak to them. For a large slice of Kingston that assumption is simply false.

A business serving students has an audience that arrives in a fortnight, makes most of its habit-forming decisions in the following three weeks, and then spends eight months doing what it decided in September. Reaching somebody in November with a message that would have worked in August is not a smaller win. In many categories it is no win at all, because the habit is already somebody else’s.

So social media marketing kingston businesses buy should be planned as a calendar with peaks, not as a weekly rhythm. The budget concentrates where the decisions are made, and the quiet months get the cheap, sustainable version rather than the same spend producing less.

The two plans

Even Spend Against Concentrated Spend

Same annual budget, two distributions. In a city with a seasonal edge as hard as Kingston’s they do not produce remotely similar results.

The practical consequence is that July is the most important month in a Kingston plan and almost nothing gets published in it. That is when the photography happens, the video gets edited, the offers get decided and the arrival sequence gets written. Kingston social media work that starts in August is already late.

It also changes how a Kingston account is judged. A monthly report comparing October to September will always look like a collapse, and it is not one. The comparison that matters is this September against last September, which almost no reporting template produces by default.

Where the effort goes

What Social Media Services Kingston Businesses Should Buy

What usually goes wrong in Kingston

A twelve month retainer with an even monthly deliverable, agreed in February, which means the September window is served by exactly the same effort as the February one. Nobody is at fault and the plan was never shaped like the city. Social media for kingston businesses sold as a flat monthly service has this problem built into the invoice.

What we do instead

Work out when the year is actually decided, which for many here is late August and for others is a tourist summer or a spring trade season. Then weight the plan accordingly, make the content in the quiet months, and spend the paid budget only when the audience is in the city.

The other Kingston

Not Every Business Here Runs On September

The argument above is for Kingston businesses serving students. Plenty of Kingston firms have a completely different peak, and the same principle applies to theirs.

Find your four weeks

Look at last year’s revenue by month rather than at a marketing calendar. The peak is usually obvious and it is frequently not where the plan assumes. A kingston social media agency that has not asked for that number is guessing.

Make it in the quiet Kingston months

Photography, video and offers prepared when nobody is around and published when everybody is. This is the entire mechanical trick and it costs nothing extra, only foresight.

Compare year on year

This September against last September, never against August. Social media kingston reporting that compares consecutive months in a seasonal city will tell you something alarming and meaningless every autumn.

Questions

Social Media In Kingston: Common Questions

Should we post less in the quiet months?

Publish less, work the same. The quiet months are when the photography, the video and the arrival sequence get made, which is why it is worth keeping the work going even though the output drops. What should fall away is filler published to satisfy a cadence, because in a city that empties out it is reaching a fraction of the audience at full cost. Keep a light sustaining presence so the account never looks abandoned, and put the real effort into production.

Our engagement collapses every October. Is something wrong?

Almost certainly not. In a Kingston running on an academic year, comparing October to September will show a decline every single time and it is a feature of the calendar rather than a failure of the work. The comparison that means something is this September against last September. We set the reporting up that way from the start, because otherwise every autumn is spent explaining a chart that was always going to look like that.

We do not serve students. Does this apply?

The specific timing does not, the principle does. Kingston contains at least four different trading calendars: student arrival, the visitor season, institutional budget cycles, and genuinely year-round resident trade. Only the last of those suits an even monthly plan. The first step is always to look at revenue by month for the last two years and build the plan around whatever that shows, which for a surprising number of businesses is not where they assumed it was.

Is paid advertising worth it here?

Inside the window, very much so, and outside it rather less. Local paid reach is inexpensive in Kingston, which makes it tempting to run continuously, but spending it in a month when a large part of your audience is in another city is simply buying impressions from people who cannot visit. Concentrating the same budget into the weeks when decisions are being made is usually the single largest improvement available, and it requires no extra money at all.

When should we start planning for September?

June, and by July the production should be happening. Starting in August means the most important four weeks of the year are served by whatever could be assembled at short notice, which is reliably the weakest content of the year arriving at the moment it matters most. The work in June and July is unglamorous and nothing publishes from it, which is exactly why it gets skipped and exactly why it is the difference.
