The local read

What Marketing In Haliburton Actually Has To Deal With

There is no single centre to be visible in. The catchment is a scatter of lakes, roads and small communities, and a business here is reachable rather than passed.

That changes what "local" means in practice. A radius drawn around an address covers water, forest and a great many people who will never drive that way, which is the commonest reason an advertising budget here produces a flattering reach figure and very few enquiries.

The useful version is a named list of the communities that actually produce customers, built from your own records rather than from a map. Most businesses have never done that count and are surprised by it: one or two places produce most of the work and several that feel important produce almost none.

The second consequence is that the audience divides into two groups with nothing in common. Permanent residents choose on convenience and reputation and will hear about you from somebody. Seasonal owners arrive with no local network, decide from a phone, and are making the decision weeks before they are physically here.

Those two need different things from the same business. The first needs to be reminded you exist; the second needs everything explained, including how far you travel and what you will not do. A site written entirely for one of them quietly loses the other, and it is almost always the second that gets lost.

And the practical constraint: a connection out here is not what it is in a city. Anything heavy fails before it loads, and the people it fails for simply leave without anything recording that they were there.

What to count

Three Figures Worth More Than Any Dashboard

At the volumes a business here works at, most reporting is noise. Three numbers, kept by hand, answer everything a budget decision turns on.

How many enquiries arrived, by month. Written down by whoever takes the call, including the ones that came by telephone, which no advertising report can see and which in most trades here are the majority.

Where each one says it came from, asked out loud and recorded as said. It is the only record covering the routes nothing else sees — a mention at a counter, a neighbour, a vehicle somebody noticed on a road.

And what a customer turned out to be worth. That one only the business can supply, and without it every other figure is a number with nothing to compare it against.

Read the three across a rolling quarter against the same quarter a year earlier. A single month out here contains too few enquiries to show anything and too many opinions to settle anything.

The absent half of the market

A Share Of Your Customers Are Not Here Most Of The Year

Seasonal owners are a different kind of customer, and almost nothing about a standard local marketing plan is aimed at them.

They plan from somewhere else, usually weeks ahead, and they are searching for a supplier rather than remembering one. Word of mouth, which carries most local business here, reaches them barely at all.

So the things that persuade them are the things nobody local needs: a clear statement of how far you travel, what a call-out actually involves, whether you will take a small job, and how quickly somebody can be there. Each of those is a question they cannot ask a neighbour.

Timing matters more for this group than for anybody else. The decisions about the season are made in the weeks before people arrive, which is exactly when most businesses here are still quiet and not advertising.

They are also the group most likely to keep a supplier for years once they have found one, because the cost of finding another at a distance is high. The first job is worth more than it looks.

None of this requires a separate campaign. It requires a page that answers the absent reader’s questions in plain words, and that page is usually the one a business has never written.

Getting started

From First Call To Live Campaigns In Haliburton

01 · Count where customers actually come from

Your own last fifty, by community. It takes an afternoon, nobody has done it, and it routinely rules out half of what a map-based targeting radius would have paid for.

02 · Decide which audience the site is written for

Resident, seasonal owner, or both in a stated order. A page trying to speak to both without deciding reads as speaking to neither.

03 · Fix the free things first

Listing, hours including the seasonal exceptions, photographs, and anything on the site that fails on a weak connection. All of it costs nothing and moves faster than anything paid.

04 · Build before people arrive

Live in the weeks the decisions are made rather than the weeks people are here, because the absent half of the market has already chosen by then.

05 · Keep the record

One sheet: every enquiry, the date, and what they say when asked how they found you. Across a year it is the only document that can tell a seasonal dip from a real decline.

Straight answers

Haliburton Marketing FAQ

How wide should our targeting actually be?

Narrower than a map suggests. Draw it from the communities your own records say produce customers rather than from a radius, because a circle out here covers a great deal of water and forest and a lot of people who will never drive your way.

What does digital marketing cost for a Haliburton business?

It is scoped rather than packaged. What a business needs depends on its margins, its catchment and how much of its work comes from seasonal owners rather than residents. We scope it on the call and price it plainly, and the written plan is yours either way.

Do seasonal customers need something different?

Yes, and it is the half most sites leave out. Somebody planning from elsewhere cannot ask a neighbour, so they need the practical answers written down: how far you travel, what a visit involves, whether you take small jobs, and how soon somebody could be there.

Our site is fine. Why are we not getting enquiries?

Check it on a phone, outdoors, on mobile data rather than on a laptop in the office. Out here the connection is the constraint, and a page that takes five seconds to appear loses most of the people it was built for without anything recording that they were there.

Will you film at our location?

Where production is part of the scope, yes. We would plan one visit to produce a year of material rather than returning for each piece, because the drive is the expensive part rather than the filming.

Who actually does the work?

The people named on our leadership page. Nothing is offshored or subcontracted without telling you first, and you have direct contact with the crew running your account rather than a queue.
