In a large city budget buys new people. In a town this size it buys the same people more often.

Start from 213,700 residents. Take the age band your offer actually suits, the households that could plausibly buy, and whatever interest signal is relevant, and the reachable audience for most local businesses lands somewhere in the tens of thousands.

That is enough to run a campaign and not enough to keep one fresh. The pattern is consistent: three or four weeks of good results, then cost per result climbing while nothing in the account has changed. Nothing has broken. The people inclined to act have acted.

A Facebook ads agency Oakville businesses stay with is one that planned for week five before week one, which means budgeting for more than one piece of creative. That line is the one most often missing from a quote.

What an expensive click says

Eleven Dollars Is A Clue About The Competition

The cost per click on this town's social cluster is high for a market of its size, and the reason is informative.

Semrush records $11.10 on the local social cluster, which is well above what the same category costs in most Ontario towns. Cost in an auction is a function of who else is bidding and how much they can afford, not of how many people live there.

What that figure describes is a market where the businesses advertising are, on average, selling something with a high enough value per customer to justify an expensive click. Professional services, home improvement at the larger end, specialist retail, health.

Two practical consequences. If your own value per customer is lower than that, you are bidding in an auction set by people who can outspend you, and the answer is a narrower audience rather than a bigger budget. If your value per customer is higher, the click price is not your problem and you are probably underspending.

Either way, the figure to work out before anything else is what a customer is worth. Without it the click price is just a number to flinch at.

What works here

Three Uses That Justify A Facebook Ads Agency Oakville Budget

Each of these does something search cannot, which is the only defensible reason to pay this much for attention.

Retargeting, first and always

The people who visited and did not act are a small, warm audience that costs a fraction of prospecting. It is the least glamorous thing in the account, the most reliable, and the thing most often not running at all.

Showing work that photographs well

Renovation, landscaping, dentistry, anything where the result is visual. A clip of the actual job outperforms every adjective available and cannot be copied by a competitor using the same stock library.

Reaching a defined area repeatedly

A new location, a seasonal push, an event with a date. A finite audience is a disadvantage for prospecting and an advantage here: you can reach most of a neighbourhood several times for a knowable amount.

What a quote should contain

Four Lines To Insist On

The gaps in a proposal are more informative than its contents.

Creative volume, as a number

How many pieces per month and who makes them. If the answer is one, the campaign has a four-week life in this audience and nobody has said so.

A frequency threshold with a response

The number at which something changes, and what changes. "We will monitor it" is not a plan.

Media spend separated from fee

Blended into one number, the media is what gets squeezed when the fee is under pressure, and you will not see it happen.

What they would switch off first

Every proposal has a weakest line. An agency that cannot name its own has not thought of the proposal as a budget.

What a Facebook ads agency Oakville owners trust will say unprompted

That in a town this size the campaign needs new material every month, and that if there is no budget for making any, the account will decay regardless of how it is managed.

The offer

The Ad Is Rarely The Thing That Is Wrong

When a campaign underperforms, the creative gets blamed and the offer is usually the culprit.

An advertisement can only ask for what the business is willing to give. If the only available action is "contact us", the campaign is asking a stranger for a commitment they have no reason to make yet, and no amount of production quality changes that arithmetic.

What changes it is an ask proportionate to the relationship. A price guide, a short assessment, a dated offer, a thing they can have without speaking to anybody. The purpose is not cleverness; it is to give the large majority who are not ready today a reason to identify themselves so they can be reached again cheaply.

That second audience is where the economics of a small market improve. Prospecting into a finite town is expensive and saturates. Reaching people who have already raised a hand is cheap and does not.

So before any budget is agreed, it is worth asking what somebody who is interested but not ready is supposed to do. If the honest answer is nothing, that is the first thing to build, and it costs less than a month of media.

Our part

We Film As Well, Which Is The Point

In a market constrained by creative rather than by money, production is the bottleneck being removed.

We run Meta accounts against cost per enquiry, and we film the material they run on. One shooting day of real work produces enough cuts to keep a small-town audience from going stale for months, and the marginal cost of the fifth cut is a fraction of the first.

We manage over $70,000 a month in advertising spend for Ontario businesses, and the owner runs the paid media personally. We are based in Minesing rather than Oakville, so filming here involves travel, which we price rather than fold into a fee.

What we do not do is daily posting, community management or inbox monitoring. Those are real jobs and we are not built for them.

Why did our results get worse after a month?

Almost always frequency rather than a fault. In an audience this size the people inclined to respond have done so inside four weeks, and the remaining impressions reach people who have already decided not to act. The account looks unchanged because nothing in it changed. New creative and a rebuilt audience are the fix, and they work best when planned beforehand.

How much should we spend here?

Work out the reachable audience, decide how many times somebody needs to see the work before acting, and the budget falls out of that. Spending below the level where anybody sees you twice produces nothing and teaches nothing. Spending far above the level that saturates the audience buys repetition you did not want.

Is Instagram separate?

It is the same buying platform, so it is one decision rather than two. Run both, look at where results actually came from after a month, and move the weight. In this town the split tends to follow age and category more than anything else, and guessing it in advance is less reliable than letting a month of data answer.

Can you use footage we already have?

Yes, and we would before making anything new, because existing material carries performance history you have already paid for. The usual finding is not that the footage is poor but that there is one piece of it, which is a volume problem rather than a quality one and a cheaper thing to fix than most people expect.
